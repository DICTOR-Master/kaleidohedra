Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore

The gaps of the Ogdoecontahedron packing: an exact fill, limbs that cross in golden rhombi, why a canonical golden fill shatters, and the one adapter, the GBPC, that every golden fill needs

Draft 3 · 16 October 2026 · DICTO DOI: on publication · Licence CC BY 4.0 · Data, scripts, exact checks and renders are included with this record and at https://github.com/DICTOR-Master/kaleidohedra (branch research: studies/2026-10-16-ocd-pyrochlore; shared checks in studies/_lib).
The pyrochlore networks of the Ogdoecontahedron, the questions of what fills their gaps (partners, bridges, limbs, adapters, the golden fill), the use of DICTO's own pieces, and the names Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore and GBPC are DICTO's. Computation, exact arithmetic and renders were done with Claude (Anthropic) as a research assistant; the obstruction and the adapter of §6 were found by Claude Fable, with DICTO's go.
Abstract. Copies of the DICTO Ogdoecontahedron joined face to face on their hexagons form a body-centred cubic packing that fills (9225 − 3915√5)/608 ≈ 77.43% of space (DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23404688). This paper is about the remaining 22.57%. Exactly, in ℚ(√5): each copy's share of the gap is its Voronoi cell, a truncated octahedron, minus the solid, and cones from the centre over the 72 free faces cut it into 72 convex pieces of 3 kinds whose volumes add up with remainder 0. Asking what could fill the gap more naturally, DICTO proposed partners, bridges, hexagonal limbs and adapters. The limbs are tilted hexagonal prisms, one per free hexagon, which cross in pairs at the centres of the cell's square faces; their crossing zone has exactly two golden-rhombus faces, the faces of the rhombic triacontahedron and the golden rhombohedra. From there a golden fill suggests itself: golden rhombohedra, DICTO's Cleo-Icos as adapters from hexagons to golden planes, and the Bodhi pieces, which are all bounded by golden-rhombus planes. Each copy's golden envelope is a rhombic triacontahedron; cut at the joints, the envelopes divide the packing into 77.4% solid, 11.8% non-golden shell and 10.7% golden region. But the golden region does not divide naturally into Bodhi pieces: cut canonically by every golden plane, even in DICTO's five-fold chorus arrangement, it shatters into 8,027 pieces (exact), with only five Bodhi pieces among them. So we ask what any golden fill must contain. Exactly: the golden region's walls are golden planes except for 24 small star-tip triangles per cell, which lie in the three-fold joint planes and meet the neighbouring golden faces at 20.905° (cos² = φ²/3), so golden rhombohedra and Bodhi pieces alone can never fill it. One adapter removes the obstruction: the GBPC (Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore Corner), a low triangular pyramid shaped like the rhombic triacontahedron's three-fold corner and the golden conjugate of DICTO's Cleo-Ico, 24 per cell, overlapping nothing. What remains has only golden walls and five-fold edges, and no local obstruction is left. But it has no face-to-face tiling by golden rhombohedra: for one size, its walls fix the edge at a rational multiple of φ and a Galois-conjugate count then demands a negative number of oblate rhombohedra (exact computation; the counting step is a proof sketch); and a face-to-face tiling cannot mix sizes. Whether it tiles other than face to face is open. Float explorations are labelled as such.

1. The packing and its gap

The DICTO Ogdoecontahedron (OCD) is the polar of the DICTO Hecatodihedron: 20 regular hexagons and 60 kites, exact in every coordinate. Joined face to face on a tetrahedral set of four hexagons, copies form a diamond network whose joints lie on a pyrochlore lattice; two such networks interpenetrate into a body-centred cubic (bcc) packing, every copy in the same orientation and touching eight neighbours through eight of its hexagons (DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23404688). The pyrochlore lattice of corner-sharing tetrahedra is familiar from frustrated magnets (e.g. Okamoto et al. 2013), and bcc packings of icosahedral units are familiar from crystal chemistry: the 1/1 cubic approximants of icosahedral quasicrystals pack Tsai-type icosahedral clusters on a bcc lattice (Takakura et al. 2007; Deguchi et al. 2015). Dense packings of single polyhedra are surveyed by Torquato and Jiao (2009) and Damasceno, Engel and Glotzer (2012); the gaps that a polyhedral packing leaves, and how they might be filled, are the subject here, as they were for tetrahedra in Conway and Torquato (2006). The other 12 hexagons and all 60 kites face the gap.

The gap, exact. Each copy's Voronoi cell in the bcc packing is a truncated octahedron V, one of Fedorov's five parallelohedra (see Garber 2019), of volume 512/729 + 256√5/729. The OCD lies inside V and touches V's eight hexagons, which are the joints. So each copy's share of the gap is V − OCD = 18728/13851 − 6296√5/13851 ≈ 0.335695, 22.57% of space. Cones from the centre over the OCD's 72 free faces (12 hexagons and 60 kites) cut it into 72 convex pieces, and the pieces add up to the gap exactly, remainder 0 in ℚ(√5). They are of 3 kinds (Table 1, Figure 1). None of their faces is a rhombus.

2. Partners, twins and bridges (float)

  • A partner. The 12 free hexagons face the six square-face centres of the cell in fours (two from each copy on that cube axis). Giving each gap piece to its nearest square-face centre collects exactly one third of the gap per site, but the union is not convex: its hull is twice its volume. No rhombi appear where partners meet.
  • Twinning. Turning a copy 90° about a cube axis puts it in the other golden frame and maps the eight joint directions onto themselves, so twinned and plain copies still pack at 77.43%. At a joint between a plain and a twinned copy the two hexagons share plane, centre and size but are turned 15.52° against each other; their contact becomes a 12-gon covering 94.4% of the hexagon.
  • An octahedral bridge. At each square-face centre the planes of the eight facing free hexagons enclose a closed solid of 8 corners and 8 faces (4 triangles, 4 quadrilaterals), volume 0.021229, colliding with no copy. Three per copy fill 19.0% of the gap. At the cell corners (the tetrahedral sites) the facing planes do not close.

3. Limbs, and where they cross in golden rhombi

All copies share one orientation and are centrally symmetric, so the copy at a lattice point c has a hexagon exactly parallel, opposite and equally turned to each of our free hexagons. Sweeping a free hexagon onto it gives a hexagonal prism, a limb, right for the joints and oblique otherwise. Each free hexagon has exactly one limb that hits no other copy: length 0.522476, 2.196 times the hexagon edge, tilted 79.19° from the hexagon normal, its sides parallelograms of 0.2379 × 0.5225 (float). The 12 free hexagons give 6 limbs per copy, in 3 crossing pairs, one pair per cube axis, crossing at the square-face centres. Choosing one limb from each pair gives 3 collision-free limbs per copy, in 8 ways, filling 12.9% of the gap.

The crossing zone, exact. Where two limbs cross they share a convex zone, as pyrochlore tetrahedra share corners. Rebuilt from the exact network in ℚ(√5), with nothing rounded, the zone has 12 corners, each the exact meeting point of three limb planes, and 10 quadrilateral faces. Exactly two of them are golden rhombi: planar, four equal sides, diagonals in the ratio φ, the face of the rhombic triacontahedron and of both golden rhombohedra. Their edges run along five-fold axes (float). With each zone counted once, all six limbs fill 23.8% of the gap per copy (float). Where the limbs cross, the tips of the Cleo-Icos of §4 also meet; trimmed by the symmetric planes between the four facing hexagons, the four tips close into a ring, each touching its two neighbours face to face (Figure 2).

4. Towards a golden fill (float)

The golden rhombi of the crossing zone point to a golden fill: pieces bounded only by golden-rhombus planes, which are perpendicular to the 15 two-fold axes.

  • Golden rhombohedra. The prolate and oblate golden rhombohedra, the unit cells of the three-dimensional Penrose tiling and of icosahedral quasicrystal models (Levine and Steinhardt 1984, 1986; Socolar and Steinhardt 1986; Henley 1991), have only golden-rhombus faces. Grown greedily outward from one zone's golden rhombi, accepting only bricks that collide with nothing, 300 bricks (152 prolate, 148 oblate) grew before the cap, edge 0.049892. Golden rhombohedra make flat surfaces only on golden planes; the OCD's hexagons are perpendicular to three-fold axes and its kites to no symmetry axis, so bare bricks meet them in a saw-tooth. By symmetry there are only two junction angles: on a hexagon 20.905°, exactly cos² = φ²/3, where the adapter is a low three-sided cap shaped like a rhombic triacontahedron's three-fold corner, the GBPC of §6; and 11.6407° on a kite (float), a shallow wedge.
  • DICTO's pieces as adapters. The AXE, CLEO and FUJI pieces (DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23351291) have three-fold and five-fold faces, but none a golden plane. The Cleo-Ico (the icosahedron's face pyramid, apex at its centre; Theaetetus, DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23378070) has a three-fold base and three two-fold sides at 69.09° to the base: a ready-made adapter from hexagon to golden planes. One stands on each free hexagon, base on alternate corners, all 12 with golden sides, together 27.3% of the gap. Their tips meet the neighbours' tips at the square-face centres (Figure 2c). The Cleo-Ico is not the adapter the joints need (§6): its squared side-to-base ratio is (5 + √5)/8, the golden conjugate of the GBPC's (5 − √5)/8. Its place is the free hexagons.
  • The Bodhi pieces. Every face of all five Bodhi pieces (DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23311353) is perpendicular to a two-fold axis: every face lies in a golden-rhombus plane, so they sit flush with golden rhombohedra and each other (they are not zonotopes: the Vajra is a bipyramid).
  • The golden envelope. The 30 golden planes pushed in until they touch the OCD enclose a rhombic triacontahedron, volume 1.398921: Kepler's golden zonohedron (Kepler 1619; on its stellations Messer 1995; on golden zonohedra Hart 1999). Neighbouring envelopes poke through each other at the joints; cut at the eight joint planes they no longer overlap, and the two cut faces at a joint are triangles turned 180°, a six-pointed star around the joint hexagon.
  • The three-way split of the packing: OCD 77.4%; the non-golden shell inside the cut envelopes 11.8%, for adapters or resin; the golden region between the envelopes 10.7%, for Bodhi pieces and golden rhombohedra (Figure 3).

5. The golden region shatters (exact)

Does the golden region divide naturally into Bodhi pieces? A canonical answer cuts it by every golden plane that crosses it, so the result does not depend on the order of the cuts. In the cubic packing the region is crossed by golden planes in many directions that do not line up, and a first, non-canonical cut already gave 1,624 small pieces. The cubic lattice does not carry the golden module that makes the Bodhi Seed's pieces simple (the same cubic–icosahedral tension that relates cubic approximants and twins to icosahedral order: Pauling 1985; Prodan et al. 2017; Baake and Grimm 2013), so we repeated the cut in DICTO's five-fold chorus arrangement, where the copies sit on five-fold-symmetric positions (DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23396361). There, exactly in ℚ(√5): the 41 central chorus sites are exact lattice points; 100 distinct envelope planes cross the central copy's cell; cutting by every one gives 16,204 cells, which sum exactly to the cell (2440/2187 + 1288√5/2187, remainder 0); 8,027 of them form the golden region, exact volume (22420 − 8972√5)/2187 ≈ 1.078188; the cut is exactly five-fold symmetric. The pieces fall into 561 kinds, and only 5 are Bodhi pieces, one five-fold orbit of the fine Vajra (Figure 4).

Conclusion. Even in the five-fold arrangement the golden region does not divide into Bodhi pieces; under the canonical cut it shatters. The Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore's golden fill needs either resin or a deliberate, non-canonical choice of Bodhi pieces and golden rhombohedra.

6. What a golden fill must contain (exact)

The canonical cut shatters, so we ask instead what any fill of the golden region G must contain. Per cell, G has exact volume (5984 − 2624√5)/729, 10.75% of space; each cut envelope K is a rhombic triacontahedron cut at its 8 joint planes, and no two overlap.

The obstruction. Every wall of G lies on an envelope, whose faces are 30 golden faces and 8 triangular joint faces. Across each joint face exactly one neighbour touches, its face the same triangle turned 180°. The two overlap in the central hexagon, exactly 2/3 of the triangle; the other 1/3 is three equilateral tips, which face the gap. So per cell G has 24 non-golden walls, the tips, in the four joint-plane directions, whose normals are the three-fold axes along the cube diagonals. Any dissection of G into finitely many pieces must cover each tip, a planar region of positive area, by piece faces lying in its own plane; a face in any other plane meets it only in a line. Every face of a golden rhombohedron and of each Bodhi piece is perpendicular to a two-fold axis, never to a three-fold one. So golden rhombohedra and Bodhi pieces alone cannot fill G. More sharply, where a tip meets the neighbour's golden face, G has a convex edge along a two-fold line opening at 20.905° (cos² = φ²/3), smaller than any golden piece's dihedral angle (36° or more).

The GBPC. On each tip, the joint plane and the golden planes through the tip's three edges bound a tetrahedron: base the tip, three golden sides, squared base and side edges 8/27 − 8√5/81 and 20/81 − 8√5/81, volume (−44 + 20√5)/2187. Its base over side, squared, is 2 + 2√5/5, the long diagonal of a golden rhombus over its edge: it is the rhombic triacontahedron's three-fold corner, cut through the corner's three neighbours. DICTO named it the GBPC, Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore Corner (Figure 5). Its squared side-to-base ratio, (5 − √5)/8, is the golden conjugate of the Cleo-Ico's (5 + √5)/8: it is the central pyramid of the great icosahedron, as the Cleo-Ico is of the icosahedron. All 24 per cell are congruent, and an exact test over 254 nearby pairs finds no overlaps.

What remains. G′ = G minus the GBPCs has exact volume 704/81 − 928√5/243 per cell, 10.22% of space. Its walls are all golden, and every edge runs along a five-fold axis, the edge direction of the golden rhombohedra. Its 48 convex edges per cell all open at 36° (float), the acute edge of the oblate rhombohedron, so no local obstruction is left.

No one-size fill. G′ is exactly space minus the union of the uncut rhombic triacontahedra: each overlaps its 8 joined neighbours (volume (−704 + 320√5)/729 each, no three overlapping), and each GBPC is the part of an envelope's corner beyond the joint plane that lies outside the neighbour. Three exact tests ask whether G′ tiles face to face by golden rhombohedra of one edge length λ. Corners: the 15 golden plane directions cut the space round each of the 48 corners per cell into 120 chambers, and every free cone is exactly a union of rhombohedron corner cones; no corner forbids a tiling. Module: all corners and their lattice translates lie in one coset of the Ammann–Kramer module at λ = φ/9 (in units of the cell), and at φ/(9k); this test alone can forbid nothing. Walls: summing corner signs over each of the 30 walls per cell (6 golden rhombi and 24 L-shaped hexagons), a dissection invariant that holds for any arrangement of faces, every wall is satisfied exactly when λ is a rational multiple of φ, coarsest φ/9. Count: applying the conjugation a + b√5 ↦ a − b√5 to the tiles (a perp-space volume count, checked exactly on one triacontahedron and on G′, where it gives 704/81 + 928√5/243) forces, per cell, 66 prolate and −30 oblate rhombohedra at λ = φ/9 (times k³ at φ/(9k)). The oblate count is negative at every allowed scale, so G′ has no face-to-face tiling by golden rhombohedra of one size, periodic or not. The computations are exact; the counting step (a homotopy argument with an O(N²) bound) is a proof sketch not yet checked independently. The same count reproduces the known Ammann–Kramer ratio of prolate to oblate, φ : 1, a check of the method on a known case.

No face-to-face fill of any sizes. A face-to-face tiling of G′ by golden rhombohedra cannot mix sizes: tiles sharing a face share its edges, all edges of a golden rhombohedron are equal, and G′ is connected. With the one-size count, G′ has no face-to-face tiling by golden rhombohedra of any sizes (exact; the counting step is a proof sketch). Only tilings that are not face to face remain. For one size the wall test still forces the edge length to be a rational multiple of φ|v|; only the oblate has the 36° dihedral angle, so oblates must line the 48 convex edges, but the free 36° wedges continue past the ends of those edges, so edge lengths set no condition (exact).

Not face to face. A greedy exact build with golden rhombohedra of edges φ|v|/9 and φ|v|/18 fills 46.5% of G′ (184 tiles per bcc cell, overlap-free, exact; a float check of 200,000 points agrees) before no further corner admits a tile. Two exact constraints hold for any arrangement: every tile with a face on a wall of G′ has its edge in ℚφ·|v| (a Dehn-type argument on the walls, whose corners all have coordinates in ℚφ), and a periodic tiling cannot use only such edges, since its count requires −10/243 for the oblates. The Hadwiger edge invariant gives no obstruction: it vanishes on G′, because each pair of joined envelopes overlaps in exactly one oblate golden rhombohedron of edge 2φ|v|/9, so G′ is, as a valuation, a combination of zonotopes. Whether G′ has a tiling that is not face to face, perhaps with Bodhi pieces, remains undecided (study by Fable, studies/2026-10-16-ocd-pyrochlore/FABLE-GBPC-TILING.md, -2 and -3).

The larger region. If the non-golden shell is included (22.6% of space), every copy's 60 kites face the gap, and kite normals lie on no symmetry axis; by the same area argument any fill needs pieces with faces in the kite planes, which golden pieces, Bodhi pieces and Cleo-Icos do not have.

7. Further observations (float)

  • The Hecatodihedron wraps the OCD. Scaled to 4/9 of its unit-sphere polar size, the Hecatodihedron passes all 102 of its faces through the OCD's corners (its 90 tangent faces through the 90 sphere corners, its 12 pentagons through the 12 outer corners): the dual nesting, as an icosahedron inside a dodecahedron. In the packing each such Hecatodihedron reaches past its cell and overlaps those of its eight joined neighbours.
  • The densest lattice packing found (0.789260, DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23404688) leaves holes that form one connected labyrinth (voxel flood fill at two resolutions), not separate pockets.

8. Conclusion

The Ogdoecontahedron packs as a cubic crystal, but its faces, and the junctions between its copies, are icosahedral. The gap of its bcc packing makes that tension concrete. Cut from the centre, the gap falls exactly into 72 pieces of three kinds, a clean answer but not a golden one: none of its faces is a rhombus. Golden structure appears only where the copies' own geometry is extended into the gap. The limbs, one tilted hexagonal prism per free hexagon, cross at the square-face centres, and their crossing zone carries exactly two golden rhombi. That is the seed of a golden fill, and DICTO's pieces carry it further: the Cleo-Ico turns a hexagon into golden planes, golden rhombohedra grow from the zone, and the Bodhi pieces fit flush with both. Each copy's golden envelope, a rhombic triacontahedron cut at its joints, divides space cleanly into solid, shell and golden region.

What the golden region does not do is divide itself. Cut canonically by every golden plane, it shatters, in the cubic packing and, exactly, even in the five-fold chorus, where only five of 8,027 pieces are Bodhi pieces. The golden module and the cubic lattice do not share enough planes for the fill to choose itself. This mirrors the crystal chemistry: icosahedral clusters on a bcc lattice make a cubic approximant, not a quasicrystal, and the golden order lives only locally, at the junctions. The cubic frame leaves exactly one kind of trace inside the golden region: the 24 star tips per cell, in its three-fold joint planes. They forbid a purely golden fill, and the GBPC, the golden conjugate of DICTO's Cleo-Ico, is exactly the piece that covers them.

So the Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore has an exact frame (the packing, its gap, the golden crossing zones, the envelopes and the GBPCs) and one sharpened question inside it: the region left after the GBPCs has only golden walls and five-fold edges, yet golden rhombohedra of any sizes cannot fill it face to face; only a fill that is not face to face remains possible. Until a fill is built the golden fill remains a deliberate choice, with Bodhi pieces and golden rhombohedra where they fit and resin elsewhere. It can be explored in the Kaleidohedra app as the world Golden Bodhi Pyrochlore Networks (kaleidohedra.dictospheres.com/?open=bodhiPyro). Open: does G′ tile with golden rhombohedra not face to face (any sizes, with or without Bodhi pieces); an independent check of the counting step; a rhombic division of the 72-piece gap; a collision-free set of Cleo-Ico adapters on the free hexagons with their tips resolved; and a physical build of one cell.

9. Relation to known work, and status

Known and credited: the diamond and pyrochlore lattices and the bcc lattice with its truncated-octahedron Voronoi cell; golden rhombohedra and the rhombic triacontahedron as the classical golden zonohedra; the cone decomposition of a gap from a centre; the icosahedral irrational twin behind the five-fold chorus (Ranganathan, Srivastava and Lord 2006); the golden rhombohedra and zonohedra and the cubic approximants cited above; the densest lattice packings of polytopes (Betke and Henk 2000). DICTO's earlier papers supply the Ogdoecontahedron and its packings (#31), the five-fold chorus (#30), the Cleo-Ico (Theaetetus, #29), and the Bodhi pieces (the Bodhi Seed). Not found in our reference library and web searches (16 October 2026): the exact gap fill of the Ogdoecontahedron packing, its limbs and their golden crossing zone, and the golden fill's canonical shattering. Exact: §1's gap and its 72 pieces; §3's crossing zone and its two golden rhombi; §5's canonical cut; §6's obstruction, the GBPC and its fit, and G′'s volume and walls.
