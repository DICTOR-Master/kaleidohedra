Where the Vacuus Meets Known Mathematics
A map of the classical theories our results touch, and of the gaps between them

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com · ORCID 0009-0001-2620-2036
Working map · 27 November 2026 · for DICTO's reading. The question is DICTO's: everything done with the Vacuus and the Resonus turns up inside separate classical theories; connect them and look for the empty places. Drafting with Claude (Anthropic).
How to read this map. “Known” is written only from a source we opened ourselves; each source carries its status: opened (text read), record only (title, abstract or a secondary page read, not the work itself), not opened. A “gap” is a question we did not find answered in the sources checked; it is not a claim that it is new. Nothing here goes into a paper until the gap is searched properly and the result is proved.

The Vacuus and the Resonus are made from the most basic parts of a polyhedron: its faces, its edges, its centre and its polar. Every big theory of polytopes is made from the same parts. So it is no surprise that our results keep landing inside known theories. The chance lies between them: where one of DICTO's constructions is the only thing that joins two theories that do not know each other.

2. The gaps, ranked

Ranked by how many separate theories one answer would join, and by how close our tools already are.

  1. Hollow-straw criterion. Joins four theories (scutoids, foam T1 processes, kinetic hulls, fullerenes and fair dice) in one exact statement. We already have the exact machinery from the C116 proof. Next step: run the same exact test on other pairs (C60 with a 32-face Aequus, C80 with 42 faces) and look for the rule.
  2. The hull of a polytope and its polar. Joins Schlegel diagrams, the Cayley embedding and the segmentochora; would make every Vacuus-Solid a 4D object. Next step: test the Archimedean and Catalan solids exactly, then attempt the proof of the general condition.
  3. Dehn zero exactly when space-filling for these pieces. Joins Hilbert's third problem and tiling. Next step: four cuts (needs the faster search).
  4. Which arrangement cells make Aequi. Next step: finish the 4,168, then look for a pattern in the 48,988.

Before any gap is called new: a proper literature search for that one question (sources listed and opened), then an exact proof.
