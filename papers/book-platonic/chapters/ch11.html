Chapter 11. The Residents

New chapter (draft of 11 November 2026), from #108 The Atlas of the Chains (§5, Observation 5); checks in studies 2026-11-11-platonic-ch11 (snub24.py) and 2026-11-11-orbit-polytopes (residents_exact.py).

In three dimensions only the cube fills space alone, and of the pairs only the tetrahedron with the octahedron (Part Two). One dimension up the room curves: the cells of a four-dimensional polytope fill the three-dimensional sphere that bounds it, and there the five behave differently. Four of them fill curved space alone; the icosahedron, the outsider of the Opening, does not, and needs the tetrahedron. And all five live together, among the corners of one family of polytopes only: the family of the Queens, DICTO's names for the 600-cell (the White Queen) and the 120-cell (the Red Queen) (#63, #65).

What this chapter shows. (1) Four of the five fill the three-sphere alone, as the cells of the six regular 4-polytopes; the icosahedron never (Schläfli; Coxeter). (2) Removing the 24 corners of an inscribed 24-cell from the 600-cell leaves the snub 24-cell, whose cells are 24 regular icosahedra and 120 regular tetrahedra (Gosset 1900; Conway and Guy 1965; checked). (3) The 600-cell's corners hold 120 dodecahedra, 240 icosahedra, 600 cubes, 3000 tetrahedra and 900 octahedra, and the 120-cell's 1560, 2400, 7200, 34 200 and 8100, all exact over ℚ(√5). (4) Of all the polytopes built by the reflection groups from seeds of zeros and ones, only the Queens' family houses all five (observed, #108, Observation 5).

These are the six regular 4-polytopes (Schläfli; Coxeter 1973). No regular 4-polytope has icosahedral cells: the icosahedron's dihedral angle, 138.19°, is too wide, since three of them round an edge already exceed 360°, so they cannot close up even in curved space.

11.2 The icosahedron needs the tetrahedron

Proposition 11.1 (Gosset 1900; Conway and Guy 1965). Remove from the 600-cell's 120 corners the 24 corners of an inscribed 24-cell. The other 96 corners span the snub 24-cell, whose cells are 24 regular icosahedra and 120 regular tetrahedra.

Checked by building the 600-cell's corners, removing those of its 24-cell, and taking the hull of the rest: 24 icosahedra and 120 tetrahedra, every one regular; the full 600-cell gives its 600 tetrahedra (snub24.py, FINAL ok). So the icosahedra are carved out of the White Queen: each sits round one removed corner, where twenty of the 600-cell's tetrahedra met. The same twenty tetrahedra round a corner, built in flat space, are pairwise disjoint and leave 14.1 % of their hull open (#99 §3, Chapter 10): the three-dimensional trace of the curvature that closes them in four dimensions.
