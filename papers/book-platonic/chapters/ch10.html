Chapter 10. Plato's Five in the Octet

From #99 Plato's Five in the Octet (§§1, 3) and #8 The Euclid–Kepler–Pacioli Cell (§§2, 5); pointer to #41 The DICTO Jewel; study 2026-11-06-crossed-nest.

The five solids do not fill space together, but a lattice can hold all five at once. Chapter 1 found one: the EKP cell, a simple cubic lattice whose cells nest dodecahedron, cube, stella octangula, octahedron and icosahedron, with dodecahedra that cross the cell walls. The octet, the honeycomb of tetrahedra and octahedra, holds them more simply: inside every octahedron sits Pacioli's icosahedron, inside that the dual dodecahedron, then Euclid's cube, then Kepler's tetrahedron, and every tetrahedron of the octet holds the same nest at half the size. The nest's cube is exactly the octahedron's dual cube, and the nest goes on for ever, a third smaller at each turn (#99).

What this chapter shows. (1) In the octet of edge 1 every octahedron holds O ⊃ I ⊃ D ⊃ C ⊃ T with edges √2/φ², √2/(3φ), √2/3 and 2/3, the cube being the octahedron's dual cube, and every tetrahedron holds the same nest at half size: all five in every cell of a structure periodic in three directions (Theorem 1 of #99). (2) The nest repeats inward and outward by exactly 1/3 per turn, the golden factors cancelling; the shorter loop cube ⊃ icosahedron ⊃ dodecahedron ⊃ cube repeats by φ/3 (Theorem 2 of #99). (3) Supercells from the 120-cell, the 600-cell and the middle slice; the Dodeca-13 fits inside its own central dodecahedron at exactly 1/3 (Proposition of #99). (4) In the EKP cell, dodecahedra on alternate cells are the densest lattice packing, and Kepler stars with icosahedra share only corners, the EKP network (#8 §5).

10.2 The EKP cell as a lattice

The EKP cell of Chapter 1 is a lattice in its own right; its measured properties and the arrangements that can be taken from its thirteen points without overlap are those of #8.

The same cell carries the DICTO Jewel: Euclid's dodecahedron on a cube carved by the stella octangulas of its six neighbouring cubes, with twelve thick Penrose rhombi as windows and volume exactly 3/16 when a block of 2 × 2 × 2 cells has edge 1; Jewels and stella octangulas fill space in the rock-salt pattern, 3 : 1 by volume (#41). It is told in full in Space-Fillers and Lattice Moves, Chapter 5, and #8, #99 and #92 appear there among the plates of its Atlas.

10.3 From the studies: crossing the walls

What made the EKP cell work, DICTO noted, was the overlap of cells: Euclid's dodecahedra cross the cube faces, so the roofs continue through the lattice and meet as Pacioli's rectangles. Study 2026-11-06-crossed-nest asked the same of the octet, in floating point with the thresholds checked to machine precision. Grow every Pacioli icosahedron of the octet of edge 1 about its own centre by a factor s. At s = 1 its eight faces lie in the octet's layer planes and its three golden rectangles lie in the cube planes through the octahedral sites, so the rectangles of all those cells share one plane, as in the EKP cell. Neighbouring icosahedra first touch at s = √5/2; the centres of the tetrahedral holes are reached at s = 3/2; and at s = φ the grown icosahedra cover all of space, the last points covered being the octet's own corners. Coplanar faces of neighbouring icosahedra meet edge to edge, as EKP's roofs do, only deeper in the overlap: the eight layer-plane faces at s = 1 + √5/2, the twelve golden faces at s = φ². Grown dodecahedra touch at s = 3/2 and their coplanar faces meet at s = 3. These thresholds have not yet been checked exactly.
