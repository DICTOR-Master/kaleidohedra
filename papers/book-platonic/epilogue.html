Epilogue. What has been shown

The book set out to take the five solids apart, put them together, fill space with them and find where they live. Inside them it found Euclid's, Pacioli's and Kepler's inscriptions closing into one periodic cell (Chapter 1), exactly two golden cuts on every solid, the cube's alone needing √2 (Chapter 2), face pyramids that fill space only as the cube's or as the tetrahedron's with the octahedron's, and that rebuild their solids face to face (Chapter 3), the walls between those pyramids, whose angles are read from the polar solid and whose discs from the mirrors (Chapter 4), and five round bodies of smallest width one (Chapter 5). Filling space, the icosahedron came home in shells of AXE, CLEO and FUJI (Chapter 6) and in octahedra with Admiral Caps; with Hart's Dogstar beside the dodecahedron (Chapter 7) every mix of the five filled space (Chapter 8), then all mixes on one cubic grid (Chapter 9), and all five sat nested in every cell of the octet, for ever by thirds (Chapter 10). In four dimensions four of the five fill curved space alone and all five live together only among the corners of the Queens (Chapter 11); a ladder of crystals carries the nest towards five-fold order, and on the hidden side of the OG the regular icosahedron and dodecahedron appear exactly (Chapter 12); beyond four dimensions the golden line stops (Chapter 13).
