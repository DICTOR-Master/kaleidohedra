The four solids DICTO named on 31 October 2026 (DICTO Solids v7) were given the treatment for this book, with the scripts of Chapters 2 and 3 unchanged (study 2026-11-01-star-cores-book). These results are new here and not yet in a paper.

All four are convex and fall in group B of Chapter 2: under the centre rule each star fills its halo, and the core does not come back to the shape itself. With polar spikes at ρ² = φR² the halos are 6.798, 5.837, 5.245 and 4.570 times the solids (Cube Pearl, Golden Clasp, Hamlet, Ophelia), and the layers of 80, 120, 1172 and 1722 corner and edge pieces make up the halo minus the star to within 3 × 10⁻¹⁵ of its volume, as the Main theorem of Chapter 2 requires; no two pieces overlap in the first three (the overlap test is not run on layers of 1200 pieces or more).

Their cores cycle exactly with period two. Cube Pearl and Golden Clasp enter the cycle at the first turn, on solids of 6 squares and 8 hexagons of the octahedral pair, and two turns scale them by t²/3, as the Theorem of Chapter 3 gives for face planes on the axes of the octahedral group. Hamlet and Ophelia enter at the second turn: their first core has 30 squares, 20 hexagons and 12 decagons, and from then on the cores have 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons, the icosahedral pair, scaled by t²(5 + 2√5)/15 every two turns.

Built upon with the valley rule, Cube Pearl and Golden Clasp keep growing: over steps two and three Cube Pearl adds ×1.72 at t = φ and ×1.91 at t = 2, the Golden Clasp ×1.71 at t = φ and ×1.45 at t = 2. Cube Pearl's sharpest edge is exactly 120°, the bound of the valley law for step two at t = 2; the Golden Clasp's is 125.26°. Hamlet and Ophelia, with over 200 faces, were not built upon, as in Chapter 2; their sharpest edges are 160.15° and 161.10°.
