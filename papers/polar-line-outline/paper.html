0. The words (DICTO's)

  • The Hopscotch: the framework, walking a line of polar pairs from wall to wall.
  • Antipol: a shape together with its polar, facing each other; each square of the Hopscotch is an antipol.
  • Hop: from a value to the same value again: along a slider from one zero of the determinant to the next (a wall to the next wall), or around a cycle from a value to its return (one full turn of a golden cycle, one gnomon step).
  • Step: a hand-over in which the new bonds' emergence comes before the old bonds' dissolution: for a stretch both exist (the gradual case of #53).
  • Jump: emergence and dissolution at the same value: everything changes at once (the switch of #53, Theorem B). #53's Proposition D tells step from jump by one number: τA + τB < 0 is a step, = 0 a jump.
  • The turn: at the end of the line one turns and comes back (DICTO): the fixed point of the polarity, e.g. √3 for the core turn μ ↦ 3/μ; the return visits the mirrored events in reverse order.

1. The framework in one statement

Take a convex shape and a fixed quadric (a sphere, a paraboloid, or a hyperboloid). The quadric pairs every point with a plane, and so every shape with its polar. Add one slider μ: a scale, a weight, or a viewpoint. Then every DICTO construction is a family of polar pairs along the slider, and every event in it (a switch of bonds, a golden cut, a nesting, a perfect yantra) is a value of μ where one determinant vanishes. The determinants are linear or low-degree in μ, which is why the events land on rational or golden numbers.

Three quadrics, three faces of one idea:

  • Sphere: polar reciprocity of solids: P(S), S*, the golden sweeps, the nestings.
  • Paraboloid: power diagrams and weighted lattices: the bonds, the switch points, the holes.
  • Hyperboloid (projective view): maps that keep a circle or sphere: the viewpoint principle of the yantra.

4. What the framework predicts

  1. Every event of a DICTO sweep lies in the field of its determinant, so its degree can be bounded before any search. to verify on the known events.
  2. The fixed point of a polar turn maximises the containment constant: true on the cube–octahedron sweep (√3); test the icosahedral sweep.
  3. For the one-piece aim: an aperiodic stacking is a polar line at an irrational slope (as cut-and-project is a lifting). to verify; this would link the aim to the framework.
  4. The yantra's conditions are walls of one determinant family, explaining why the DICTO Yantra's slopes multiply to 2/φ³. to verify.
