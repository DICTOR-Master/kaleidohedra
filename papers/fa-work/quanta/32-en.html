#89Golden Cuts in Andreini's Fillings

Cut every cell of a classical filling of space the same way, and the corners cut off round each meeting point close into one small hole. DICTO proposed, from the Rhombiverse app, that cut cubes and small octahedra fill space. They do at every depth, one octahedron per cube, face to face (proved in #89); Andreini found the pattern in 1905 with truncated cubes, and at the two golden depths it is a filling by the Greater, or the Lesser, Golden Cut Cubes with regular octahedra, which is a slice of a four-dimensional honeycomb, the rectified tesseractic honeycomb. Three more of Andreini's patterns have golden members, checked by volume: the Golden Cut Rhombic Dodecahedra with cubes and tetrahedra; the Golden Cut Tetrahedra with Icosaxes, or with Unborn Axes, and cuboctahedra, the octet cut at its golden depths, which leads back to the Golden Split (#48); and Kelvin's truncated octahedra cut so that their squares, or their hexagons, are golden, with small tetragonal disphenoids. Each filling comes in a greater and a lesser version, one depth at a time. The patterns are Andreini's and no new filling is claimed: what is new is which golden solids sit in them. No new pair fills space on its own as Icosaxes and Unborn Axes do.
