The Web of Bonds: How the DICTONIC Bonds Hand Over

(working title — DICTO decides)

Theorem: both DICTONIC loops are weighted Voronoi (power) diagrams of one fixed crowd of points of two kinds, with one weight slid between the kinds; the bonds are the edges of the dual weighted-Delaunay web; the bond between unlike pieces never breaks inside a loop, while the two like bonds hand over, gradually in the golden loop and all at once, at the octet, in the Delian loop

The ideas are DICTO's: the DICTONIC SOLIDS and the DICTONIC BONDS and their names, and the web of bonds, a network of bonds that "should create a web of lattices that change with the rhythm of the loop" (DICTO, 25 October 2026); computation, exact proofs and drafting with Claude (Anthropic).
In short. DICTO's two loops of space-filling pairs, the golden loop of paper #48 and the Delian loop of paper #51, each slide one pair of solids through a whole family of shapes. Draw one line, a bond, between every two pieces that share a face, and the lines make a web. We show that each loop is really one fixed crowd of points of two kinds, the piece centres, and that the only thing that moves is one number, a weight given to one kind against the other. Every piece is the region nearer (in this weighted sense) to its own point than to any other. Then the web is exactly the web of the classical weighted Delaunay construction. As the weight slides, the bond between unlike pieces never breaks. The two like bonds hand over: in the golden loop gradually, both alive all the way and equal at Kelvin's cell; in the Delian loop all at once, at the octet, where every rhombus of a hidden rhombic dodecahedron trades its long diagonal for its short one. Read physically, the weight is a ball radius squared: the golden loop is the caesium chloride structure, the Delian loop the fluorite structure, and the range where the balls can touch is bounded exactly by Pauling's classical radius-ratio limits.
Main theorem. For every x in [0, ∞] the golden network is the power diagram of the body-centred cubic sites with weight difference (φ²/4)(x − 1)/(x + 1); for every t in [0, 1] the Delian network is the power diagram of the fluorite sites with weight difference (3/8)(2t − 1). The web is the 1-skeleton of the dual regular subdivision, with exact bond areas. In the golden loop all three bond types live on the whole open loop, with constant face lattice; the two like bonds have area ratio x² and are equal only at x = 1. In the Delian loop the unlike bond lives exactly on (0, 1), oct–oct exactly on [0, 1/3), tet–tet exactly on (1/3, 1]; t = 1/3, the octet, is the only parameter at which both pieces exist and only one bond type is present. Theorem 2 (the balls): the weight is a squared radius; with unlike neighbours touching, ρ = (x + 2)/(2x + 1) and ρ = t/(1 − t), and the touching windows end exactly at Pauling's limits √3 − 1 and √(3/2) − 1. All results are exact (371 + 61 checks, none failed).

1. Where it started

DICTO's DICTONIC world in Kaleidohedra shows a web: one line for every face two pieces share. DICTO's idea was that this web should itself be a lattice that changes with the rhythm of the loop. We asked what that web is, exactly.

Paper #48 [1] cut the gaps between icosahedra at the golden point and placed the resulting pair, the Icosaxes and the Unborn Axes, in a one-parameter family T(x), T(1/x) of truncated octahedra (Koca's orbit [10]). Paper #51 [2] cut the octet by one sliding plane per tetrahedron–octahedron pair and found the Delian bond, Samson and Delilah. DICTO called the solids the DICTONIC SOLIDS and the pairs the DICTONIC BONDS, and in the app each family runs as a loop. On 25 October 2026 DICTO proposed that the network of bonds should create a web of lattices that change with the rhythm of the loop. The app draws that web (Figure 5): one line per shared face, thicker for a larger face. Float runs on a small network suggested that bonds trade places along each loop. Two exact studies [S1, S2] answer what the web is, and this paper reports them.

2. The two loops

Both loops are pairs of pieces that fill space. Each piece sits on a point; the points never move. Only the shapes change.

Golden loop. T(x) is the convex hull of all permutations of (±(1 + x), ±x, 0), scaled so that its squares lie at distance L/2 from its centre, with L = φ (paper #48, Theorem 4). T(x) sits on the big sites B = φℤ³, T(1/x) on the small sites S = φℤ³ + (φ/2)(1, 1, 1), for x from 0 to ∞. We write s = x/(1 + x) in [0, 1]: then T(x) is the set of signed permutations of (1, s, 0)·L/2, and T(1/x) the same with 1 − s in place of s. At x = 1 both are Kelvin's truncated octahedron [20]; at x = τ they are the Icosaxes and the Unborn Axes.

Delian loop. The pieces of paper #51 at slider t in [0, 1], with a = 3(1 − t)/2: the tetrahedron pieces sit on Q = (ℤ + ½)³/√2, the octahedron pieces on O = {n ∈ ℤ³ : n₁ + n₂ + n₃ odd}/√2, octet edge 1. At t = 0 the octahedron pieces are Kepler's rhombic dodecahedra, at t = 1/3 the octet, at t = 1 the tetrahedron pieces are cubes; in between lie the two equal-edged members, the half-and-half member and the Delian bond (Samson and Delilah).

Power diagram. Given sites ci with weights wi, the power cell of ci is the set of points p with |p − ci|² − wi ≤ |p − cj|² − wj for all j: a Voronoi cell in which each site is given a head start [3, 4]. Two cells meet in a plane perpendicular to the line between their sites (the power bisector). The cells are convex and fill space. Their dual, the weighted (regular) Delaunay subdivision, has an edge between two sites exactly when their cells share a face [3].

3. Main theorem

Each loop is one fixed crowd of points with one weight slid between the two kinds. The web of bonds is the classical dual web. The unlike bond never breaks; the like bonds hand over.

Main theorem. (i) For every x ∈ [0, ∞] the golden network is the power diagram of the body-centred cubic sites B ∪ S with wB − wS = (φ²/4)(x − 1)/(x + 1) = (L/2)²(2s − 1). For every t ∈ [0, 1] the Delian network is the power diagram of Q ∪ O with wQ − wO = (3/8)(2t − 1). At t = 0 the Q cells are single points, at t = 1 the O cells are single points.
(ii) The web of bonds is the 1-skeleton of the dual regular subdivision, and the bond faces and their areas are those of Table 1.
(iii) Golden: gradual hand-over. For 0 < x < ∞ all three bond types are present and the face lattice is constant. Big–big rises strictly from 0 and small–small falls strictly to 0; their ratio is x², the diagonals of the two square faces (sL and (1 − s)L) add up to the lattice spacing φ, and the two areas are equal only at x = 1. The whole picture is symmetric under x ↔ 1/x.
Delian: hand-over at the octet. Oct–tet is present exactly on (0, 1), oct–oct exactly on [0, 1/3), tet–tet exactly on (1/3, 1]. t = 1/3 is the only parameter at which both pieces exist and every face is of one bond type (there the web is bipartite). The ends t = 0 and t = 1 carry one bond type only because one piece has shrunk to a point. The golden loop never has a single bond type: at x = 0 there are two (big–small and small–small), at x = ∞ two (big–small and big–big).

Why: choose the weights so that every flat face of every piece is exactly the plane of equal weighted distance between the two points it separates. Then each piece is its point's weighted cell, and the rest is the classical duality.

Proof. (i) With the weights in (i), every facet plane of every piece of the app is the power bisector of the two sites that share it, for every s ∈ (0, 1) and every t in each of the regimes (0, 1/3) and (1/3, 1), as an identity in the parameter [S1, A2]. Every corner of the piece satisfies every other power inequality against every site within a fixed distance, with slack ≥ 0 on the whole open regime: the slacks are linear in s or t and have no root inside [S1, A1]. The facet planes' own vertex enumeration gives back exactly the piece's corners, and their normals sum to 0 with rank 3, so they bound a bounded region; together with A1 this is piece = power cell. A uniform distance bound shows that sites farther away cannot cut the cell [S1, A7]. The regime boundaries and the ends x = 0, ∞, t = 0, 1/3, 1 are checked by building the power cells from scratch among the fixed sites, with no use of the app's pieces, in exact arithmetic, and comparing with the app's pieces; at t = 0 and t = 1 the vanishing kind's cell is a single point [S1, B1]. (ii) Power diagrams are dual to regular subdivisions [3], so two sites are bonded exactly when they span an edge of a dual cell. The face of each bond is computed in closed form [S1, A3] and checked at every sample, rational and irrational [S1, B2], and against the app's own web to 10⁻⁹ [S1, C2]. (iii) The monotonicity, birth and death points and the corner at 1/3 follow from the closed forms [S1, A6]; the ratio of the like bonds is s²/(1 − s)² = x², so they are equal only at x = 1; the square diagonals are sL and (1 − s)L, which add to L = φ. The swap: s ↔ 1 − s flips the sign of the weight, and the translation by (φ/2)(1, 1, 1) exchanges B and S, so the diagram at 1/x is the diagram at x translated with big and small exchanged; hence big–big(1/x) = small–small(x) and big–small is unchanged. The bond types present in each regime are read off the incidences of the dual cells (Corollary 3) [S1, B6]. ∎

Corollary 4 (the weight line). For fixed sites, the weights that give one and the same dual subdivision form the chambers of the secondary fan [5, 6]. Each loop's weight runs along a segment. The golden segment stays inside one chamber except at its ends; the Delian segment crosses exactly one wall, at t = 1/3, and the regular subdivision on that wall is the rhombic dodecahedra.

Corollary 5 (the Delian bond). At the Delian bond, wQ − wO = 3/8 − 22/3/4 ≈ −0.02185: close to, but not at, the unweighted Voronoi point t = 1/2. The ratio of its two bond areas is oct–tet / tet–tet = 21/3√3 / (4 − 2·21/3).

Remark (the weights in earlier work). These weights agree with paper #51's cube Remark, w = (x − 1)/(4(x + 1)) at cube edge 1, and with the octet check of #51's study; the theorem proves them for every parameter, not only at samples. The golden volumes are (4/3)(1 + u)³ − 4u³ in units of (L/2)³, with u = s for big and u = 1 − s for small, always adding to L³; the Delian volumes are those of paper #51.

5. The physical reading: balls of two sizes

A weight in a power diagram can be read as the square of a ball's radius: the face between two touching balls passes through their point of contact. Read this way, the two loops are two classical crystal structures, and the range where the balls can touch ends exactly at Pauling's radius-ratio limits.

Theorem 2 (the balls). (a) The radical tessellation of a packing of balls (ci, ri) is the power diagram with wi = ri² + C, for any common constant C; a power diagram therefore fixes the radii only up to ri² → ri² + C. (b) The golden sites are the caesium chloride structure (B2, Wyckoff 1a and 1b, lattice constant a = φ in app units); the Delian sites, shifted by (1, 0, 0)/√2, are the fluorite structure (C1: the oct sites on the face-centred cubic 4a, the tet sites on 8c, all the tetrahedral holes; a = √2). (c) If unlike neighbours touch, the radius ratio is ρ = rsmall/rbig = (x + 2)/(2x + 1) = (2 − s)/(1 + s) on the golden loop and ρ = rtet-site/roct-site = t/(1 − t) on the Delian loop; so t is exactly the share of the unlike bond length taken by the tet-site ball. (d) The touching window (unlike balls touching, like balls not overlapping) is x ∈ [(√3 − 1)/2, √3 + 1], ρ ∈ [√3 − 1, (√3 + 1)/2] on the golden loop, and t ∈ [1 − √(2/3), 1/√3], ρ ∈ [√(3/2) − 1, (√3 + 1)/2] on the Delian loop. Its ends are Pauling's radius-ratio limits [9]: √3 − 1 ≈ 0.732 for 8-coordination, and √(3/2) − 1 ≈ 0.225 for 4-coordination. (e) Without the touching condition, some non-overlapping ball packing realises every x ∈ [0, ∞], but only the t ∈ [1/6, 2/3].

Why: the face between two balls is where their weighted distances agree, and that is the power bisector with weight r². Touching fixes the sum of the two radii, the weight fixes the difference of their squares, and the two together fix each radius.

Proof. (a) The radical plane of two balls is the set where |p − ci|² − ri² = |p − cj|² − rj², that is, 2p·(cj − ci) = |cj|² − |ci|² + wi − wj, which depends on the weights only through their differences [S2, A1a, A1b]; for touching balls it passes through the contact point [S2, A1c] (classical: [4, 7]). (b) Exact coset comparison with the Wyckoff positions [S2, A6]. (c) Let d be the unlike nearest distance and Δ the weight difference of the Main theorem. Then r1 + r2 = d and r1² − r2² = Δ give r1 − r2 = Δ/d. Golden: d = (√3/2)φ and Δ/d² = (2s − 1)/3, so rbig/d = (1 + s)/3 and rsmall/d = (2 − s)/3. Delian: d² = 3/8 and Δ/d² = 2t − 1, so rtet/d = t [S2, A2, A4]. (d) Golden: the big balls touch (2rbig = a = φ) exactly when (1 + s)/√3 = 1, at x = √3 + 1, ρ = √3 − 1, and the small balls touch at x = (√3 − 1)/2 by the swap. Delian: the oct balls touch (2rO = 1) at t = 1 − √(2/3), ρ = √(3/2) − 1, the tet balls (2rQ = 1/√2) at t = 1/√3, ρ = (√3 + 1)/2 [S2, A2e, A2f, A4c, A4d]. (e) Put u = r1 + r2; then r1,2 = (u ± Δ/u)/2, both radii grow with u and ρ moves monotonically towards 1, so the feasible u form an interval, nonempty exactly when the larger radius √|Δ|, with the other radius 0, fits. Golden: |Δ| ≤ (a/2)² = φ²/4 always. Delian: −Δ ≤ (1/2)² exactly when t ≥ 1/6, and Δ ≤ (1/(2√2))² exactly when t ≤ 2/3 [S2, A3, A4f]. ∎

Corollary 6 (the stops as balls). Under the contact map: golden x = 0 gives ρ = 2 and x = ∞ gives ρ = 1/2, both outside the window, where the only packing has the other kind as points; Kelvin (x = 1) forces ρ = 1, equal balls; x = τ gives ρ = √5/φ² = (3√5 − 5)/2 ≈ 0.8541, inside. Delian: the octet t = 1/3 gives ρ = 1/2, the Delian bond ρ = (3/2)·21/3 − 1 ≈ 0.8899, the unweighted Voronoi point t = 1/2 gives ρ = 1, all inside; t = 0 and t = 1 admit no packing at all; t = 1/6 and t = 2/3 are the end packings (touching face-centred cubic balls with the tet sites as points, and touching simple-cubic balls on the tet sites with the oct sites as points) [S2, A5].

Corollary 7 (an area identity). In the Delian contact model the oct–tet triangle has area (3√3/2)·roct² for t ≥ 1/3 and 6√3·rtet² for t ≤ 1/3 [S2, A7]. So compounds with the same oct-site radius have the same oct–tet area: BaF₂, Na₂O and K₂O, all with roct = 1.42 Å, share 5.239 Å².

Cubic ZrO₂ lies far outside the window (r⁺/r⁻ = 0.61), which fits the general knowledge that cubic zirconia is not stable at room temperature unless doped.

6. Limits of the hard-ball reading

To be updated from the scale-anomaly study (2026-10-25, running). The known misfits, as found in [S2]:

The balls are a reading of the geometry, not a model of the crystals. Where real crystals and hard balls disagree, the geometry stands and the reading stops.

UO₂ (r⁺/r⁻ = 0.725) and CeO₂ (0.703) are stable fluorites, yet with effective ionic radii they fall outside the touching window: both the hard-ball contact model and Pauling's rule miss here. With crystal radii (CR = IR ± 0.14) both fall inside, so the verdict depends on the radius convention; under crystal radii UO₂, CeO₂ and ZrO₂ move into the window (t = 0.521, 0.528 and 0.559). BaF₂ has r⁺/r⁻ = 1.08 > 1, where Pauling's rule would prefer a higher coordination; the model, which tests only overlap, has no objection. Na₂O (0.697) and K₂O (0.965) lie far above the 0.414 upper limit for 4-coordination, yet both are antifluorites; the model allows them, as it has no upper "rattling" limit. Li₂O moves under crystal radii from t = 0.2935 to t = 0.363, past the octet, so its side of the octet stop is not a conclusion; for every other compound the side is the same under both conventions. CsCl moves from x = 1.126 to 1.432 under crystal radii, the other halides towards x = 1. The radical faces are not the distance shells: in fluorite with t > 1/3 the cation has 12 cation neighbours at a/√2 but no cation–cation face; the anions' cubes separate the cations. β-PbF₂ is placed, but ambient PbF₂ is the orthorhombic α (cotunnite type) form.

Limits of the model: real ions are not hard balls; a power diagram fixes only r₁² − r₂², and the radius ratio needs the contact assumption; the real lattice constant is set by the crystal, not by r₁ + r₂, and a placement from measured lattice constants was not made; the halide radii are CN VI proxies; the radii come from a transcription of Shannon's table. Left out: NH₄Cl (no NH₄⁺ entry in the transcription), the metallic B2 alloys CuZn, NiAl, FeAl, CoAl (metallic radii unverified), Rb₂O (no Rb⁺ CN IV entry).

7. Conclusion

DICTO's web of bonds is a lattice that changes with the loop, as he proposed: the classical dual of a weighted Voronoi diagram. In the golden loop it changes only in size; in the Delian loop it changes its shape once, at the octet.

Both DICTONIC loops are one fixed crowd of points with one weight slid between two kinds, and the web of bonds is the dual weighted-Delaunay web. Its rhythm differs between the two loops. In the golden loop the dual never flips: the like bonds trade size in the ratio x², equal at Kelvin's cell, and the whole loop is symmetric under x ↔ 1/x. In the Delian loop the dual flips once, at the octet, where every rhombus of a hidden rhombic dodecahedron exchanges its long diagonal for its short one, and the octet is the one member whose web joins only unlike pieces. Read as balls, the loops are the caesium chloride and fluorite structures, and Pauling's classical limits mark where the balls can touch. Open: the scale-anomaly study (§6); the keyed bond's web; a web search before any claim of novelty.

8. What is known, and what is new

Power diagrams, their duality with weighted Delaunay subdivisions, radical tessellations of ball packings, the two crystal structures and Pauling's rule are all classical. What we did not find is their application to these two loops.

Known. Power (Laguerre, weighted Voronoi) diagrams and their duality with regular triangulations [3, 4]; regular subdivisions change only at the walls of the secondary fan [5, 6]; radical tessellations of packings of balls of two or more sizes [7], and Voronoi–Dirichlet and radical polyhedra in crystal chemistry and coordination [18, 19, 21]; two unequal truncated octahedra on a body-centred lattice as Laguerre cells (general; noted in paper #48 [1]); the octet, rhombic dodecahedron and cube connections [11]; the T(x) orbit [10]; ionic radii [8] and the radius-ratio rule [9]; caesium chloride type and other binary crystals of colloids and nanoparticles whose phase is set by the size ratio [12–17]. Paper #51's cube Remark [2] and its octet check already gave the two weights at samples. Voronoi's theory of Delaunay subdivisions of lattices (Erdahl [22]) is related, but treats lattices, not two kinds of weighted sites.

Not found, in the sources listed in Appendix A: the closed-form bond areas of both loops; the contrast between the golden loop (no flip) and the Delian loop (one flip, at the octet); the like-bond ratio x² and the diagonal sum φ; the sequence of dual cells along each loop; the octet as the only member with a bipartite web; the contact maps ρ = (x + 2)/(2x + 1) and ρ = t/(1 − t); the touching windows bounded exactly by Pauling's limits; the Delian packing range 1/6 ≤ t ≤ 2/3. These are elementary consequences of standard theory and may well be known in some form; "not found" is limited to those sources, and a web search is still needed before any claim of novelty.
