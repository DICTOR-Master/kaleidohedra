The DICTO Jewel

Euclid's dodecahedron carved by its neighbours' stella octangulas: an exact solid with twelve Penrose windows, its lattice, its clusters and rhombohedron, and the DICTO Jewel Cube and Jewel Cube Key, which fill space 5 : 3

The Jewel, its stella-octangula counterpart, its name, its clusters, the Jewel Cube and the name Jewel Cube Key are DICTO's (findings #10, #10b and #15 on dictospheres.com); computation, exact proofs and drafting with Claude (Anthropic), which also found the Jewel Cube Key as the space between Jewel Cubes.
Abstract. Put Euclid's dodecahedron on a cube, as roofs on its six faces, and carve away the stella octangulas of the six neighbouring cubes: what remains is the DICTO Jewel, with 38 corners and 60 faces, twelve of them thick Penrose rhombi (the windows) and 48 triangles, and volume exactly 3/16 of the Jewel Cube defined below (or 12 on cells of edge 2). With stella octangulas on the other cells, Jewels fill space as a rock-salt pattern, 3 : 1 by volume [2]; we prove this exactly: each stella's cube is the stella and six carved roofs of exactly 1/96 each. The Jewels form two clusters, a tetrahedral one (4 Jewels, 228 faces) whose copies share out all Jewels, and an octahedral one (6 Jewels round a sealed stella hole) that tiles space with loose stellas on exactly 12 lattices of index 12; eight Jewels on the corners of the face-centred cubic cell, meeting on 18 windows, form a rhombohedron round one stella. Eight Jewels on a 2 × 2 × 2 block of cells, overlapping inside, make one solid, the DICTO Jewel Cube (5/4: a cube with 24 roofs); the space between Jewel Cubes is another single solid, the DICTO Jewel Cube Key (3/4: eight stellas and twelve double roofs), whose 24 recesses hold the Cubes' 24 roofs. Cubes and Keys fill space 5 : 3, the rock-salt pattern of the Jewel one level up, and every Cube meets each of its 12 neighbours on exactly two windows. As a solid the Jewel is pyritohedral, so it twins like the Ogdoecontahedron [5]; its packing cannot, because a (111) mirror does not keep the cube lattice. All claims are exact in ℚ(√5) except where marked float.

1. The Jewel (exact)

Frame. As in the EKP cell [1] and [2]: cells are cubes centred on a cubic lattice; a cell is even or odd by the parity of its index sum, and the even cells form a face-centred cubic lattice. All values below are at the scale where the Jewel Cube (§4) has edge 1, so a cell has edge 1/2 and volume 1/8; the scripts use cells of edge 2, where every volume is 64 times larger.

Construction. Euclid builds the regular dodecahedron on a cube by setting a roof on each face [3]: the hull of the face and a ridge of length 1/φ times the cell edge, at height (φ − 1)/2 times the cell edge; each roof is (1 + √5)/192 and the dodecahedron (5 + √5)/32. Each roof reaches into the next cell, where the stella octangula (Kepler's two tetrahedra on the cube's corners [4], volume 1/16) takes away (√5 − 1)/192 of it, leaving a carved roof of exactly 1/96. The Jewel is its cell and its six carved roofs: 1/8 + 6/96 = 3/16.

Its surface. 38 corners: the cube's 8, the 12 ridge points, the 6 face centres, and 12 points that complete the windows. Its 60 faces are 12 windows, one across each cube edge, lying in the dodecahedron's face planes, and 48 triangles in the stellas' planes; 38 − 96 + 60 = 2. Each window is a thick Penrose rhombus: side (√5 − 1)/4 = 1/(2φ), long diagonal 1/2 (the cell's edge), angle 108° at the ridge corners (cos 108° = (1 − √5)/4, exact) and 72° at the cube corners [6]. The Jewel's corners are centrosymmetric, have the three-fold turns and the three coordinate mirrors, and no mirror across a (111) plane: it is pyritohedral.

2. The Stella–Jewel Lattice (exact)

Jewels on the even cells and stella octangulas on the odd cells fill space, 3/16 : 1/16 = 3 : 1 [2]. The first check (finding #10b) sampled points; here it is exact. Take an odd cell. The six Jewels on its face-neighbours each reach into it with one carved roof (their ridges are Jewel corners). These six roofs lie in the cell, any two meet only in a face (the intersection of the uncarved roofs has volume exactly 0), and each carved part is 1/96. With the stella they give 1/16 + 6/96 = 1/8, the cell: so an odd cell is exactly its stella and six carved roofs, an even cell is the core of its Jewel, and the Jewels and stellas tile space.

3. Clusters and the rhombohedron (exact)

Neighbours. Two Jewels on neighbouring even cells (one step along a face diagonal) share exactly one window; Jewels two cells apart along an axis share none.

The tetrahedral cluster (finding #15): four Jewels on mutually neighbouring even cells. Each pair shares one window, so its surface has 4 × 60 − 2 × 6 = 228 faces (192 triangles, 36 windows), and all four meet at one point, their cells' common corner; volume 3/4. Anchored on the cells whose three indices are all even, these clusters use every Jewel exactly once.

The octahedral cluster (finding #15): the six Jewels round an odd cell. Each shares a window with its four neighbours in the ring (12 windows), so its outer surface has 288 faces (240 triangles, 48 windows), and inside it the 48 walls of the Jewels' carved roofs enclose the stella-shaped hole, sealed but for its eight tips; volume 9/8, or 19/16 with the hole filled. Octahedral clusters tile space together with loose stellas on exactly 12 lattices of index 12 (exact integer search over all lattices of that index), for example the one with Hermite basis (1,0,3), (0,1,5), (0,0,12) in cell units; each period holds one cluster and five loose stellas.

The rhombohedron [2]: eight Jewels on the corners of the face-centred cubic cell. Its faces have 60° angles, so the Jewels meet not only along its 12 edges but also along the 6 short face diagonals: 18 windows. All six face-neighbours of the odd cell at its centre are among its corners, so that cell's stella is sealed inside: 8 Jewels and a stella, 25/16 = 8⅓ Jewels.

4. The DICTO Jewel Cube and the DICTO Jewel Cube Key (exact)

The Jewel Cube. Put a whole Jewel on each of the eight cells of a 2 × 2 × 2 block, on the even and the odd cells alike. Inside the block they overlap; outside it, each of the 24 outer cell faces carries its Jewel's roof. As one solid, the DICTO Jewel Cube is the block (edge 1) with 24 carved roofs on its faces: 1 + 24/96 = 5/4, six and two-thirds Jewels.

The Jewel Cube Key. Put Jewel Cubes on alternate blocks, rock-salt fashion (the blocks of even index sum). Each other block is then entered by 24 roofs, four through each face, from its six neighbouring Cubes; what is left is one solid, the DICTO Jewel Cube Key: 1 − 24/96 = 3/4, the volume of four Jewels. It is eight stella octangulas, one per cell, joined by twelve double roofs across the inner cell faces: 8/16 + 12 × 2/96 = 3/4. Its 24 recesses are exactly the Cubes' 24 roofs, so Cubes and Keys fill space, 5 : 3.

The same pattern one level up. A cell with six carved roofs added is a Jewel (1/8 + 6/96), with six taken away a stella (1/8 − 6/96); a block with 24 carved roofs added is a Jewel Cube, with 24 taken away a Jewel Cube Key; and both levels sit in the rock-salt pattern [7].

Windows. The Cubes sit on a face-centred cubic lattice of blocks. Each Cube meets each of its 12 neighbours on exactly two whole windows (24 in all, exact), and no two Cubes' roofs overlap. The Jewel Cube is a union of overlapping Jewels, which is why it is defined as one solid.

5. Twins

By the twin rule [5], a centrosymmetric solid with a three-fold axis has as its mirror image in a plane at right angles to that axis the same solid turned 60°. The Jewel is such a solid, so as a solid it twins like the DICTO Ogdoecontahedron. Its packing does not: in [8] a twin through a (111) layer of the Stella–Jewel Lattice left about 4% of sampled points uncovered and 4% covered twice (float). The reason is exact and simple: the mirror in a (111) plane takes the cubic lattice of cells to a different lattice (the step to the next cell, (1, 0, 0) in cell units, goes to (1/3, −2/3, −2/3)), so the mirrored cells cannot meet the cells on the other side face to face. The Ogdoecontahedron's packing twins because its cells, octahedra, are mirrored into the next octahedra of the gyrated octet; the Jewel's cells are cubes, and a mirrored cube does not continue the cubic lattice.

6. Prior art and what is claimed

  • Classical parts. The dodecahedron as a cube with six roofs is Euclid's (Elements XIII.17) [3]; the stella octangula is Kepler's [4]; the thick rhombus is Penrose's [6]; the rock-salt arrangement is that of sodium chloride [7]; the EKP cell holds them together [1].
  • Lalvani. US 4,723,382 [9] builds periodic and non-periodic structures from polygonal members whose edges all lie on the fifteen two-fold axes of the icosahedral group. All 48 window edges of the Jewel lie on those axes, but none of its other 48 edges do (lalvani_edges.py, float), so the Jewel, and with it the stella and the lattices here, fall outside that edge condition (our reading, not legal advice).
  • Already published. The Jewel's definition, its volume and the Stella–Jewel Lattice are in [2], with the cluster networks; the Jewel and the lattice were first released with the Kaleidohedra app (findings #10, #10b, #15 [10]).
  • Claimed here: the exact proofs of the Jewel's surface, volume and symmetry and of the Stella–Jewel tiling; the clusters as exact solids, with the 12 lattices of octahedral clusters; the 18 windows of the rhombohedron; the DICTO Jewel Cube and the DICTO Jewel Cube Key, their 5 : 3 tiling, the two-window contacts and the pattern one level up; and the exact reason the packing does not twin.

In the sources checked (the full text of the 80 library sources listed in the appendix, and web searches on 20 October 2026) we did not find the Jewel, its clusters, the Jewel Cube or the Jewel Cube Key.
