The DICTO Jewel

Euclid's dodecahedron carved by its neighbours' stella octangulas: an exact solid with twelve Penrose windows, its lattice, the DICTO Jewel-Tetra, Jewel-Octa and Jewel-Rhombi and how they fill space, and the DICTO Hexa and Hexa-Key, all exact

The Jewel, its stella-octangula counterpart, its name, its clusters (and the Jewel-Tetra, Jewel-Octa and Jewel-Rhombi as single solids, and their names), the Hexa, the Hexa-Key and their names are DICTO's (findings #10, #10b, #15 and #18 on dictospheres.com); computation, exact proofs and drafting with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. Put Euclid's dodecahedron on a cube, as roofs on its six faces, and carve away the stella octangulas of the six neighbouring cubes: what remains is the DICTO Jewel, with 38 corners and 60 faces, twelve of them thick Penrose rhombi (the windows) and 48 triangles, and volume exactly 3/16 of the Hexa's cube defined below (or 12 on cells of edge 2). With stella octangulas on the other cells, Jewels fill space as a rock-salt pattern, 3 : 1 by volume [2]; we prove this exactly: each stella's cube is the stella and six carved roofs of exactly 1/96 each. DICTO's clusters become single solids: the DICTO Jewel-Tetra (4 Jewels, 228 faces, 3/4), whose copies share out all Jewels, and the DICTO Jewel-Octa (6 Jewels and the stella that exactly fills their hole, 19/16), which tiles space with loose stellas on exactly 12 lattices of index 12. Together they sit in the pattern of the octet truss, eight Jewel-Tetras round each Jewel-Octa, but as overlapping solids, not as a tiling: without overlap at most one stella in six can be inside an Jewel-Octa, and the 12 lattices reach that bound. The third, the DICTO Jewel-Rhombi, is eight Jewels on the corners of the face-centred cubic cell, meeting on 18 windows, with the stella sealed inside (396 faces, 25/16). Each of the three tiles space with loose stellas (on 4, 12 and 25 lattices); none can without stellas, since Jewels are only three quarters of space. The DICTO Hexa and DICTO Hexa-Key [12] (5/4 and 3/4, filling space 5 : 3, two windows between neighbouring Hexas) are proved exactly. As a solid the Jewel is pyritohedral, so it twins like the Ogdoecontahedron [5]; its packing cannot, because a (111) mirror does not keep the cube lattice. All claims are exact in ℚ(√5) except where marked float.

1. The Jewel (exact)

Frame. As in the EKP cell [1] and [2]: cells are cubes centred on a cubic lattice; a cell is even or odd by the parity of its index sum, and the even cells form a face-centred cubic lattice. All values below are at the scale where the Hexa's cube (§4) has edge 1, so a cell has edge 1/2 and volume 1/8; the scripts use cells of edge 2, where every volume is 64 times larger.

Construction. Euclid builds the regular dodecahedron on a cube by setting a roof on each face [3]: the hull of the face and a ridge of length 1/φ times the cell edge, at height (φ − 1)/2 times the cell edge; each roof is (1 + √5)/192 and the dodecahedron (5 + √5)/32. Each roof reaches into the next cell, where the stella octangula (Kepler's two tetrahedra on the cube's corners [4], volume 1/16) takes away (√5 − 1)/192 of it, leaving a carved roof of exactly 1/96. The Jewel is its cell and its six carved roofs: 1/8 + 6/96 = 3/16.

Its surface. 38 corners: the cube's 8, the 12 ridge points, the 6 face centres, and 12 points that complete the windows. Its 60 faces are 12 windows, one across each cube edge, lying in the dodecahedron's face planes, and 48 triangles in the stellas' planes; 38 − 96 + 60 = 2. Each window is a thick Penrose rhombus: side (√5 − 1)/4 = 1/(2φ), long diagonal 1/2 (the cell's edge), angle 108° at the ridge corners (cos 108° = (1 − √5)/4, exact) and 72° at the cube corners [6]. The Jewel's corners are centrosymmetric, have the three-fold turns and the three coordinate mirrors, and no mirror across a (111) plane: it is pyritohedral.

2. The Stella–Jewel Lattice (exact)

Jewels on the even cells and stella octangulas on the odd cells fill space, 3/16 : 1/16 = 3 : 1 [2]. The first check (finding #10b) sampled points; here it is exact. Take an odd cell. The six Jewels on its face-neighbours each reach into it with one carved roof (their ridges are Jewel corners). These six roofs lie in the cell, any two meet only in a face (the intersection of the uncarved roofs has volume exactly 0), and each carved part is 1/96. With the stella they give 1/16 + 6/96 = 1/8, the cell: so an odd cell is exactly its stella and six carved roofs, an even cell is the core of its Jewel, and the Jewels and stellas tile space.

3. The Jewel-Tetra, Jewel-Octa and Jewel-Rhombi (exact)

Neighbours. Two Jewels on neighbouring even cells (one step along a face diagonal) share exactly one window; Jewels two cells apart along an axis share none.

The DICTO Jewel-Tetra (DICTO's tetrahedral cluster, finding #15, as one solid): four Jewels on mutually neighbouring even cells. Each pair shares one window, so its surface has 4 × 60 − 2 × 6 = 228 faces (192 triangles, 36 windows), and all four meet at one point, their cells' common corner; volume 3/4. Every set of four mutually neighbouring Jewels is of this kind, one per cell corner. Anchored on the cells whose three indices are all even, Jewel-Tetras use every Jewel exactly once, with the stellas loose between them.

The DICTO Jewel-Octa (DICTO's octahedral cluster, finding #15, as one solid): the six Jewels round an odd cell, and that cell's stella. Each Jewel shares a window with its four neighbours in the ring (12 windows); inside, the 48 walls of the Jewels' carved roofs enclose a stella-shaped hole, sealed but for its eight tips, and since the odd cell is exactly its stella and the six carved roofs (§2), the stella fills the hole exactly. The solid has an outer surface of 288 faces (240 triangles, 48 windows) and volume 6 × 3/16 + 1/16 = 19/16. Jewel-Octas tile space together with loose stellas on exactly 12 lattices of index 12 (exact integer search over all lattices of that index), for example the one with Hermite basis (1,0,3), (0,1,5), (0,0,12) in cell units; each period holds one Jewel-Octa and five loose stellas.

The octet pattern (DICTO's octet network [10]). The Jewels' cells form a face-centred cubic lattice; its tetrahedral holes are the cell corners and its octahedral holes the odd cells, so Jewel-Tetras and Jewel-Octas sit exactly where the octet truss has its tetrahedra and octahedra. Each Jewel lies in 8 Jewel-Tetras and 6 Jewel-Octas, and each of an Jewel-Octa's 8 faces (three mutually neighbouring Jewels) is completed to a Jewel-Tetra by exactly one Jewel outside. It is not an octet tiling: neighbouring Jewel-Tetras and Jewel-Octas share Jewels, and what fills space is still the Jewels and the stellas. Without overlap it cannot be one either: each Jewel-Octa takes six Jewels and one stella, and there are as many Jewels as stellas, so at most one stella in six can be inside an Jewel-Octa; the 12 lattices above reach that bound, leaving five stellas in six loose.

The DICTO Jewel-Rhombi (the rhombohedral cluster of [2], as one solid): eight Jewels on the corners of the face-centred cubic cell, which is the Jewel-Octa with the two Jewels at the ends of its long diagonal. Its faces have 60° angles, so the Jewels meet not only along its 12 edges but also along the 6 short face diagonals: 18 windows. All six face-neighbours of the odd cell at its centre are among its corners, so that cell's stella is sealed inside and fills the hole exactly, as in the Jewel-Octa. As one solid it has 396 faces (336 triangles, 60 windows; 8 × 60 − 2 × 18 − 48) and volume 8 × 3/16 + 1/16 = 25/16, 8⅓ Jewels.

How they fill space. The fillers are always the Jewels and the stellas; the pieces are ways of grouping them. In the Stella–Jewel Lattice there are as many stellas as Jewels, and Jewels are only three quarters of space, so no pieces made of Jewels alone can fill it: the stellas have to come too. Each of the three pieces fills space with copies of itself and loose stellas (stellas not inside any piece) on a lattice, and we found every such lattice (exact integer search; a period holds as many stellas as Jewels):

Grouped with sharing instead, the pieces make networks: Jewel-Tetras sharing single Jewels corner to corner form a pyrochlore network (DICTO's Kagome network, finding #16 [10]; corner-sharing tetrahedra are the classical pyrochlore lattice), and Jewel-Tetras with Jewel-Octas form the octet pattern.

4. The DICTO Hexa and the DICTO Hexa-Key (exact)

DICTO's Hexa and Hexa-Key and their 5 : 3 filling are published in [12]; here they are proved exactly.

The Hexa. Put a whole Jewel on each of the eight cells of a 2 × 2 × 2 block, on the even and the odd cells alike. Inside the block they overlap; outside it, each of the 24 outer cell faces carries its Jewel's roof. As one solid, the DICTO Hexa is the block (edge 1) with 24 carved roofs on its faces: 1 + 24/96 = 5/4, six and two-thirds Jewels.

The Hexa-Key. Put Hexas on alternate blocks, rock-salt fashion (the blocks of even index sum). Each other block is then entered by 24 roofs, four through each face, from its six neighbouring Hexas; what is left is one solid, the DICTO Hexa-Key: 1 − 24/96 = 3/4, the volume of four Jewels. It is eight stella octangulas, one per cell, joined by twelve double roofs across the inner cell faces: 8/16 + 12 × 2/96 = 3/4. Its 24 recesses are exactly the Hexas' 24 roofs, so Hexas and Hexa-Keys fill space, 5 : 3.

The same pattern one level up [12]. A cell with six carved roofs added is a Jewel (1/8 + 6/96), with six taken away a stella (1/8 − 6/96); a block with 24 carved roofs added is a Hexa, with 24 taken away a Hexa-Key; and both levels sit in the rock-salt pattern [7].

Windows. The Hexas sit on a face-centred cubic lattice of blocks. Each Hexa meets each of its 12 neighbours on exactly two whole windows (24 in all, exact), and no two Hexas' roofs overlap. The Hexa is a union of overlapping Jewels, which is why it is defined as one solid.

5. Twins

By the twin rule [5], a centrosymmetric solid with a three-fold axis has as its mirror image in a plane at right angles to that axis the same solid turned 60°. The Jewel is such a solid, so as a solid it twins like the DICTO Ogdoecontahedron. Its packing does not: in [8] a twin through a (111) layer of the Stella–Jewel Lattice left about 4% of sampled points uncovered and 4% covered twice (float). The reason is exact and simple: the mirror in a (111) plane takes the cubic lattice of cells to a different lattice (the step to the next cell, (1, 0, 0) in cell units, goes to (1/3, −2/3, −2/3)), so the mirrored cells cannot meet the cells on the other side face to face. The Ogdoecontahedron's packing twins because its cells, octahedra, are mirrored into the next octahedra of the gyrated octet; the Jewel's cells are cubes, and a mirrored cube does not continue the cubic lattice.

6. Prior art and what is claimed

  • Classical parts. The dodecahedron as a cube with six roofs is Euclid's (Elements XIII.17) [3]; the stella octangula is Kepler's [4]; the thick rhombus is Penrose's [6]; the rock-salt arrangement is that of sodium chloride [7]; the EKP cell holds them together [1].
  • Lalvani. US 4,723,382 [9] builds periodic and non-periodic structures from polygonal members whose edges all lie on the fifteen two-fold axes of the icosahedral group. All 48 window edges of the Jewel lie on those axes, but none of its other 48 edges do (lalvani_edges.py, float), so the Jewel, and with it the stella and the lattices here, fall outside that edge condition (our reading, not legal advice).
  • Already published. The Jewel's definition, its volume and the Stella–Jewel Lattice are in [2], with the cluster networks and the rhombohedral cluster; the Jewel, the lattice, the tetrahedral and octahedral clusters and the octet network were first released with the Kaleidohedra app (findings #10, #10b, #15 [10]); the Hexa, the Hexa-Key, their 5 : 3 filling and the two windows between Hexas are in [12].
  • Claimed here: the exact proofs of the Jewel's surface, volume and symmetry and of the Stella–Jewel tiling; the DICTO Jewel-Tetra and Jewel-Octa as exact single solids (the stella filling the Jewel-Octa's hole exactly), the DICTO Jewel-Rhombi as one solid with its 18 windows; all lattice tilings of the three with loose stellas (4, 12 and 25); the octet pattern and its bound of one stella in six; exact proofs of the Hexa, the Hexa-Key, their 5 : 3 filling and the two-window contacts; and the exact reason the packing does not twin.

In the sources checked (the full text of the 80 library sources listed in the appendix, and web searches on 20 October 2026) we did not find the Jewel, its clusters, the Hexa or the Hexa-Key outside DICTO's own records [2, 10, 12].
