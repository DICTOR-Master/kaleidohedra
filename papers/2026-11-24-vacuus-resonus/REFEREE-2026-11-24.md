## Findings, in order of severity

### A. Overclaims and novelty (fix before release)

**1. Abstract, "fails only for the truncated tetrahedron (Theorem A)".**
Problem: as written this is a general statement and it is false; it holds only inside the catalogue.
Evidence: any tetrahedron truncated at its four corners to unequal depth has its 4 + 4 faces in parallel pairs and is not centrally symmetric; so has the octahedron with four large and four small triangles. §4.2 itself says "occurs once" of the 215 pairs.
Fix: "and in the catalogue fails only for the truncated tetrahedron".

**2. Contributions, item (5) under "New here": the plate of every ridge of the permutohedron and the count A402191.**
Problem: both were published in #105 (11–12 November) and in the OEIS entry (14 November), so they are not new in this paper.
Evidence: #105 states the 65 slices, the normal by blocks, the coincidence rule and "the count N(n) in every dimension (Theorem 3)", brute force n = 3..9; OEIS A402191 (approved, author line 14 November 2026) links #105, Theorem 3. §6 of this paper says so itself ("new (#105 [2], Theorem 3 …)").
Fix: move to "known and used": "from #105: the plates and their number A402191". Keep as new here only what is: the derivation from the pencil lemma, parts (iv) and (v), Theorem 3B with the type-B count, and the (3, 2, 1) example.

**3. Contributions, item (4) under "New here", and Theorem 8(e).**
Problem: reading #110's walls as two Resoni is a new name for a statement #110 already makes, and the 60 + 300 directions have an earlier source that this paper does not cite.
Evidence: #110 §2 defines the cross walls as "the hyperplanes through O parallel to a pair of opposite cells that are not already [mirrors]", says "for the dual 120-cell the cell-parallel sections are the 60 mirrors, so the dual pair has one wall system of 360", and credits the 60 and 300 normal directions to Waegell and Aravind's Kochen–Specker sets from the 600-cell and the 120-cell.
Fix: list (4) as "a naming, not a result" (as the paragraph "What is not new here" nearly does), and cite Waegell and Aravind there.

**4. Abstract and §1, "We prove that these two frameworks of planes are one object seen from two sides"; abstract and §8, "the topology of their complexified complement", "the shape of what is left".**
Problem: two rhetorical claims go beyond what is proved.
Evidence: (a) Theorem 4 shows both are read off the polar P* (corners and edge directions); neither determines the other: the cuboctahedron, truncated cube and truncated octahedron have the same Resonus (7 planes) and Vacuus of 4, 18 and 15 plates (Table 1). (b) Theorem 9 gives the Betti numbers only; the Poincaré polynomial does not fix the topology of the complement.
Fix: (a) "both are read off the polar P*"; (b) "the Betti numbers of their complexified complement" in the abstract, §8 opening and the closing sentence.

**5. Contributions, items (1), (7) and "Theorems A–D" in (6): strength of "new".**
Problem: several items listed as new are immediate or classical, and one is a tautology.
Evidence: Theorem 2 (pencil lemma) is the standard fact that the hyperplane through a codimension-2 flat and a point is the member of the pencil through that point. Theorem 4 is one line from the polar (Ziegler, Theorem 2.11) plus Theorem 2. Theorem A(d) is the classical corollary of Minkowski's uniqueness theorem. Theorem B restates the two definitions; its proof is one sentence. For zonotopes the Resonus is a known object: the facet normals of the zonotope of an arrangement are its one-dimensional flats, so the Resonus is the adjoint arrangement (the paper already notes, after Kühne, that the resonance arrangement is the adjoint of the braid arrangement; Kühne cites Aguiar and Mahajan 2017, §6.3.12). That covers Theorem 5 and Proposition 3.
Fix: list (7) and Theorem B under "also given, not claimed as new"; call Theorem B a remark; say in §2.1 or §7.2 that for a zonotope the Resonus is the adjoint of its arrangement, with the reference (open Aguiar–Mahajan to confirm the definition); keep "new" for the name, the general (non-zonotope) construction, the catalogue, Theorems 6–7, 8(f), 3B, C, D.

**6. Theorem C2 and Proposition 3: prior art missing for the icosahedral and octahedral unions.**
Problem: the simplicial arrangements found are classical and are presented without credit; Question 2 may already be answered.
Evidence: the 31 planes at right angles to the 6 + 10 + 15 axes of the icosahedron are Buckminster Fuller's "31 great circles of the spherical icosahedron" (Synergetics, 1975/1982; Wikipedia article of that name gives the 15 + 10 + 6 split). The 15 mirrors plus the 6 planes polar to the five-fold axes are called "the icosahedral arrangement A(21,2)" in a recent arXiv abstract (T. S. R. Silva, arXiv:2610.28874, abstract only). Cuntz and Labbé (arXiv:2009.14152, found by search, not opened) give normals and invariants for all known simplicial arrangements up to 37 lines, which is past the "beyond Cuntz's range" of §7.2 and is the place to settle A(31,1).
Fix: credit Fuller for the 31 (and check his 13 = 3 + 4 + 6 for the octahedral case); open Grünbaum's catalogue text and Cuntz–Labbé for A(21,·), A(25,·), A(31,1) and state the identification or keep Question 2 with these sources named.

### B. Errors of statement or wording (small)

**7. Proposition 1, proof.** "the sides (0, ±1, φ)–(0, ±1, −φ)… are the pairs at distance 2" names the long sides (length 2φ). Fix: "the short sides (0, 1, ±φ)–(0, −1, ±φ) have length 2, the edge length; the long sides (0, ±1, φ)–(0, ±1, −φ) have length 2φ".

**8. §4.3 Observation, "the bicupolas and birotundas with an equator of edges in a mirror parallel to an end face (J27–J31, J34)".** For J29 and J31 the equator plane is not a mirror (symmetry D4d and D5d). Evidence: CSV `both_on_mirrors` is 0 for J29 and J31, 1 for J27, J28, J30, J34; own computation gives J29 shared planes 9, none a mirror. The phrase also explains one plane of 7, 9, 11, 6, 11. Fix: "with an equator of edges through the centre, parallel to an end face (a mirror in J27, J28, J30, J34)", and say that further planes are shared.

**9. §3, near misses: J84 given as an example of "a plate holding a two-fold axis".** J84's two shared sheets are its two mirrors (CSV: shared 2, on mirrors 2; own coordinates: three edges in each mirror plane). Fix: move J84 to the mirror case or drop it.

**10. Abstract, subtitle, §4, Contributions: "163 polyhedra".** §4.1 says the rows hold 159 distinct polyhedra (the cube and the octahedron each occur three times). Fix: "163 rows (159 distinct polyhedra)" at first use, or drop the duplicate rows from the count.

**11. §4.1, the prism family is not defined.** The Vacuus counts of a prism or antiprism depend on its height, and those of a bipyramid or trapezohedron on its proportions. Fix: one sentence: prisms and antiprisms with regular faces (uniform), bipyramids and trapezohedra their polars about the centre (as the duplicates in finding 10 imply).

**12. §4.1 against §8: "215 polyhedron–centre pairs" and "510 arrangements"; "74 arrangements"; "one of 14 such records".** 510 = 2 × 255 counts both centres for all 92 Johnson polyhedra, although 40 have one centre and the Resonus is the same about both (Theorem A(a)). The 14 records are 7 arrangements, each listed twice (`emptiness.log`: J1, J8, J31, J35, J54, J80, J81, corner and volume). Fix: give distinct counts, or say "counted about both centres".

**13. Data and reproducibility; meta line.** The paper's own scripts and logs (`scripts/emptiness.py`, `figures_v1.py`, `fig4_resonus.py`, `make_parts.py`, `data/*.log`) are not in the tree at commit e9537abd (the paper folder there holds one file), and the meta link points to `/studies` only. Fix: at release cite the commit that holds the paper folder and name the folder.

**14. Theorem C2, list of non-simplicial cases.** "{3} (4, 14), {3,2} (10, 72), {5} (6, 32), {3} (10, 92) or {5,3} (16, 152)" mixes the two groups, with {3} twice. Fix: "for Oh: {3}, {3,2}; for Ih: {5}, {3}, {5,3}".

**15. §7.2, "the even–even half of it".** For n even the Vacuus holds 2^(n−2) − 1 of the 2^(n−1) − 1 Resonus planes (3 of 7), not half. Fix: "the even–even part".

**16. §7.2, notation "A′n".** Kamiya, Takemura and Terao write the restricted arrangement A⁰ = A⁰_m (restriction to H₀), §3; Lemma 5.3 is as cited (polynomials and 6, 32, 370, 11292 agree coefficient for coefficient; m ≤ 8). Fix the symbol.

**17. Contributions and status box, "Pacioli's golden rectangles".** §2.2 reports that the rectangles were not found in Pacioli. Fix: "the golden rectangles often attributed to Pacioli".

**18. §2.2, "often attributed to Pacioli … (Livio, Burger and Starbird [30], not opened)".** The attribution rests on two unopened books; a search (Wikipedia "Golden rectangle"; Wolfram Demonstrations, which credits Cromwell, Polyhedra, 1997, p. 71; others) found no opened source that makes it. Fix: open one source that makes the attribution and cite it, or write "sometimes attributed" with the source seen.

**19. §7.2, "Its regions were first counted in physics … (Evans [31])".** Evans [31] is not in "Sources checked"; the priority word "first" rests on the OEIS entry. Kühne cites a different Evans paper (1995). Fix: "counted in physics as generalized retarded functions (Evans [31], as cited in OEIS A034997)".

Sources checked: silent on many cited references.** No status is given for [9], [10], [11], [13], [17], [18], [19], [22], [23], [24], [26], [27], [28], [31], although precise places are cited (Ziegler Theorem 2.11 and Example 0.10; Hohlweg–Lange–Thomas §1; Björner–Brenti without a place). **22. Reference [4].** The OEIS name ends "… that are spanned by its ridges (faces of codimension 2)."; the draft stops at "ridges". Trivial.

**23. Table 3 row order.** Tetrahedron, octahedron, cube, icosahedron, dodecahedron; Table 1 and Figure 3 use dodecahedron, icosahedron, cube, tetrahedron, octahedron. Fix: one order.

## Prior art: searches made for each "new" claim (free sources)

- The Resonus construction (central hyperplanes parallel to the facets): web searches on facet-parallel central hyperplanes, facet normals, polar corners, adjoint, zonotope; "Resonus" with mathematics, polytope, arrangement, geometry. No named general construction found; for zonotopes it is the adjoint arrangement (finding 5); for the icosahedral case Fuller's 31 great circles (finding 6).
- The polar link (Theorem 4): no separate statement found; it follows at once from the polar (Ziegler). New as a statement only.
- The two mirror laws (Theorems 6, 7): one search (reflection swapping adjacent facets, mirror through a ridge and the centre); nothing found. Elementary; "new as statements" is fair.
- 120-cell uniqueness (Theorem 8(f)): searches on the 600-cell's corners as the H4 root system (Wikipedia "H4 polytope", "600-cell"; arXiv:1005.5492; arXiv:1912.06156). The fact behind (a) is classical; the uniqueness sentence was not found stated. Fair to claim as a remark.
- A402191: OEIS entry opened; it is the paper's own earlier work (#105), see finding 2.
- The pencil lemma: standard linear algebra; not new (finding 5).
- Theorem A: (d) is the classical Minkowski corollary; (a)–(c), (e) are immediate. Theorem B: a restatement. Theorem C2 / Proposition 3: classical arrangements (finding 6); the classification by axis classes as a table was not found. Theorem D: follows from Theorem 4; not found elsewhere.
- Also seen and worth one line in the paper: Geis, "On simplicial arrangements in P³(R) with splitting polynomial" (arXiv:1902.11185, abstract only), for the observation in §8 that the simplicial cases split.

Sources listed above were found by search or opened in part as stated; none was bought.

## DICTO's decision on the referee report (25 Nov 2026)
Point 1, in his words: "yes credit where due for vacuus and Resonus". The paper claims only what is still new; everything known is credited at the point of use (Aguiar–Mahajan via Kühne for the zonotope Resonus as the adjoint arrangement; Fuller's 31 great circles; Waegell–Aravind for the #110 walls; #105 for A402191; the standard pencil lemma and Theorem B). Apply all findings 1–24 for v2.
