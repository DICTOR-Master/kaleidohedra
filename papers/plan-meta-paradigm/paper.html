1. The meta-arc

DICTO's rule for a book (13 November) is applied to the shelf: a beginning that is a question, a middle that climbs with each part handing a question to the next, a summit, and an end that returns to the beginning, transformed. The shelf's question: what stays in place when the parts of space change places?

This is decision 1 at the end.

What each book hands the next (arc order; this is not the order of release, which is in Section 6).

  1. Quanta of Space hands on the atoms and the opening question: what are the pieces of space, and what do they do?
  2. The DICTOnary of Atoms hands on the names: one name per shape, the earliest name first, used unchanged in every later book.
  3. Space-Fillers and Lattice Moves hands on the sorting (alone, together, held) and the first exchange: a lattice move carries one filling into another. Its question for the next: what joins the partners?
  4. The Platonic Solids hands on the five in the order D · I · C · T · O, the duality that pairs them (D ↔ I, C ↔ O, T with itself), and the outsider: the icosahedron fills no space alone. Where does it live?
  5. DICTONIC Bonds hands on the bond as a walk along a line: each solid T(x) fills space with its reciprocal T(1/x), and bonds hand over at switch points. Its epilogue, "From the walk to the law", passes the Rule of Halves to the stackings.
  6. HexOct: The Hexagonality Law hands on two letters, c and h, the laws that are straight lines in the share of h, the first never-repeating order made from ordinary pieces (the Fibonacci stacking), and Narcissus, the piece that is its own mirror partner.
  7. The Golden Cycle hands on the nest that returns to itself, the shells turned inside out, and with Kyushindo Cores (#43) the first theorem of the turn.
  8. Star Cores hands on the pairing of a star with its core, and core cycles: the exchange seen as spikes and hollows.
  9. The Golden Ladder hands on the golden cuts as slices of higher-dimensional solids, rung by rung to E8, and the twin side reached by √5 ↔ −√5.
  10. Alicia in Wonderland: The Red & White Queens hands on the two dual queens, the 600-cell and the 120-cell, and the line between them: the home of five-fold order, and the stage on which the summit is set.
  11. The Permutohedron Paradigm hands on the neighbour swap as the unit of all the others, the scutoid as that swap seen in a slice, and, in its last chapter, the road from the permutohedron to the associahedron, whose canonical form is a scattering amplitude (Arkani-Hamed, Bai, He and Yan 2017): the door to the laws.
  12. A Brief Geometry of Space hands back to the first book: the same pieces, a sharper question, and a list of what is proved, what is near and what is open.

Sample opening paragraph for the shelf (for the shelf map page and the opening of Brief Geometry; DICTO's to change).

Two people sitting side by side change seats. Nothing has been added and nothing taken away, yet the row is a different row; do it again and the first row is back. These twelve books are about that move and about what it leaves in place. They begin with pieces: the few solids that fill space alone, together, or held by their shape alone. They climb through the ways the pieces change places: neighbour with neighbour, cell with cell, layer with layer, a shape with its mirror image, faces with corners, one end of a line with the other, a number with its twin. At the top stands a solid made of every order of five things, and in it the scutoid, the shape of the cells of living skin (Gómez-Gálvez and colleagues, 2018), which DICTO carried into four dimensions. And they come back to the pieces with one question: is it at the still points of these exchanges that geometry gives its laws?

Sample closing paragraph for the shelf (the last paragraph of Brief Geometry; written for the proposition as it stands today, open; to be rewritten to whatever its tests show).

We began with two neighbours changing seats and a handful of pieces. Every level of the climb had its exchange, and at every level something stood still: the piece that is its own mirror partner, the tetrahedron that is its own dual, the solid whose faces all touch one sphere, the tiling that is its own partner, which is the quasicrystal. Some of these still points carry theorems, proved in the books named. Some carry near misses, measured and reported as misses. The sentence that would join them all, that geometry gives its laws at the fixed points of its swaps, is DICTO's proposition; it has been put to the test here and it is not proved. The pieces of the first book are the same pieces. What has changed is the question we put to them: not only what fills space, but what stays when everything in it changes places.

Sources of the four still points in the closing paragraph: Narcissus (#98); the self-dual tetrahedron (classical); the turn's fixed solid (The Turn Law, held draft, Theorems 1 and 2; Conjecture 1 beyond the regular solids is open); the Ammann–Beenker tiling as the only self-partnered tiling on its line (The Turn Law, held draft, Theorem 3). The last two stand on a draft that is not released; if the draft changes, the paragraph changes with it.

3. Swap, borrow, duplicate

Three kinds of presence for a result. Home: the one book that tells it in full and keeps it current. Retelling: a deliberate duplicate of one to four pages, in the borrowing book's voice, headed "retold from" with the home named. Pointer: one line. Duplication is used where the arc needs the reader to hold the result in hand at that moment, and only there. 5. Gaps the meta-paradigm exposes

Missing links between books, each with a one-line test. None was run for this plan. Each test can fail; a failure is reported as it falls and the map is corrected.

#gapone-line test
G1The proposition has no test record on the branch (the swaps study folder is absent).The study exists with its table of levels fixed before any outcome, one row per level (swap, fixed point, law found there or not), and a final OK/FAIL line.
G2Not every "swap" on the map is known to be a swap. Lattice moves (book 3), bonds handing over (5), star ↔ core (8) may be moves that do not undo themselves.For each level write the map and check on one worked example per book that doing it twice gives back the start; a level that fails is renamed a move and leaves the proposition's scope.
G3The Golden Cycle and the core cycles are cycles, not fixed points; the Platonic book's clockwork returns after four steps, and Paradigm Theorem 5.4 has a cycle of four shells.Compare the two four-step maps on the truncated icosahedron: same map or not; and state the cycle as a fixed point of the fourth step only if the step is exact in ℚ(√5).
G4Layers: c ↔ h sends the share h to 1 − h, but no book says what is special at h = 1/2.Evaluate the counts of #57, #61 and #84 at h and 1 − h: is any count symmetric about, or extreme at, h = 1/2?
G5Three reciprocal maps on lines live in three books: T(x) ↔ T(1/x) (Bonds), μ ↦ 3/μ (Star Cores, #81), μ ↔ c/μ (the Turn Law).Put all three on the cube–octahedron line in one variable: are they the same involution, and is the fixed solid the same? (Kelvin's cell does not have all faces on one sphere, so at least two may differ; that is worth knowing.)
G6Space-Fillers Ch 3 builds five classical space-fillers by moves; Paradigm §14.4 finds Fedorov's five by sliding. The two lists are not matched.One table of both lists by name and face count: which solids are in both.
G7Kyushindo's three principles head Paradigm Ch 13 (Change, Rhythm, Harmony for bell changes) and Brief Geometry Ch 7 (step, hop, jump).Lay the two assignments side by side word for word: Change = the step in both?
G8Numbers ↔ duality: on the hidden side of the OG a dual pair appears (icosahedron at step 2, dodecahedron at steps 3 and 7; Platonic Ch 12). No book says whether the twin map and the polar commute.On the stored step data, exact in ℚ(√5): twin of the polar = polar of the twin?
G9The summit's centre is unknown to the climb: "scutoid" appears once in Space-Fillers and not at all in HexOct or Queens.Word search of each book after its next version: every book names the neighbour swap at least once, by pointer.
G10Aim 3 has no swap of its own: Narcissus is fixed by the mirror, yet it forces no stacking, and the hunt is paused.State in one sentence which swap a forcing piece would have to be fixed by (the layer swap is ruled out for local rules by the obstruction of Brief Geometry Ch 3); open, to be said as open.

6. Reading order and order of release

For a newcomer (six books, in this order): Quanta of Space, the essay only → The Platonic Solids → Space-Fillers and Lattice Moves → HexOct: The Hexagonality Law → The Permutohedron Paradigm → A Brief Geometry of Space. The DICTOnary of Atoms stays at hand throughout. Side roads, taken when the main road points to them: DICTONIC Bonds (from Space-Fillers), The Golden Cycle and Star Cores (from The Platonic Solids), The Golden Ladder and Alicia in Wonderland: The Red & White Queens (before the Paradigm, for the fourth dimension). The full arc order is the map's order, 1 to 12.

Order of release of the unpublished four: no change. 1 Space-Fillers and Lattice Moves, 2 The Permutohedron Paradigm, 3 The Platonic Solids, 4 A Brief Geometry of Space. The architecture supports the agreed order: the Platonic book points to Space-Fillers (Chapters 5, 6, 8) and to the Paradigm (the Mirror Law, the balance law), so both must exist first; Brief Geometry leans on all three and on two held drafts. The arc order differs from the release order only in that a reader meets the Platonic book before the summit; that is a matter for the shelf map page and needs no change of release.
