DICTO 13-Atom Clusters

Twelve around one, seven ways: six golden clusters and the periodic one, with the pieces that fill between them

The clusters, their names (Lotus seed, DESHI, UNITY, VENUS, DICTO-Star, Bodhi seed, the piece names, DICTO 13-Atom Clusters), the question whether the rhombic dodecahedron's 13 fills space like the Hexa, and the choice to round it while keeping it keyed are DICTO's. Computation and checking with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. Twelve equal spheres can touch a thirteenth and no more (Schütte and van der Waerden 1952), and twelve atoms round one make the icosahedral cluster of liquids and metals (Frank 1952). We set seven polyhedral thirteens side by side, each a centre and twelve copies on its five-fold or two-fold axes: the Icosa-13 (Lotus seed), DESHI, the Dodeca-13, UNITY with its closed form VENUS, the DICTO-Star, the Bodhi seed, and the rhombic dodecahedron's 13 (RD 13). For each we give how the twelve touch the centre and one another, and the kits that fill between them: 16 kits in all, from 13 to 213 pieces, every piece a closed solid with flat faces. Two of the golden clusters close exactly into a larger copy of their own form (the Icosa-13 in an icosahedron of edge φ², the Bodhi seed in a triacontahedron of edge φ²); the others leave dimples by design. The RD 13 is the periodic member: of the 183 sublattices of index 13 in the face-centred cubic lattice, 8 have the cluster as a complete set of representatives, so blocks of 13 rhombic dodecahedra fill space by translation alone; the 8 are one arrangement, turned. Moving every corner of that honeycomb by the same rule in every block, with every face kept flat and every cell convex, the block's surface can be cut from 59.40 to 47.47 (minimum piece thickness 0.15), its roundness 36πV²/S³ rising from 0.365 to 0.715, while the blocks still fill space and interlock. The block tiling is the face-centred cubic case of a radius-one perfect code; we did not find it stated, but the setting is classical (Golomb and Welch 1970).

3. The periodic member: RD 13

Rhombic dodecahedra are the Voronoi cells of the face-centred cubic lattice (here the integer points with even coordinate sum, neighbours √2 apart, cells of volume 2). A cluster of 13 cells, a centre and its twelve neighbours, fills space by translation exactly when its 13 lattice points are a complete set of representatives of a sublattice of index 13. Every such sublattice is the kernel of a map x ↦ w·x mod 13; of the 183 choices of w up to scale, 8 separate the 13 points. Their shortest translations have length √10, along directions such as (3, 1, 0), and the 8 are one arrangement in eight orientations, all related by turns of the cube (so it has no left- and right-handed forms): the three-dimensional counterpart of the seven-hexagon flower tiling of the plane. So the RD 13 is the one cluster here that tiles space on its own, with no partner (the DICTO Hexa needs its Key).

DICTO asked for the block to be made rounder in the way the Hexa reads as one block, with its outside still keyed. Every corner of the honeycomb was given a displacement depending only on its class modulo the block lattice (39 classes), so the blocks still tile exactly; every face was kept flat (four corners coplanar), every cell convex with its corners at least a set thickness inside each face plane, and the block's outer surface minimised (its volume is fixed at 26, so least surface is roundest). A single rhombic dodecahedron has roundness 0.741, so at thickness 0.15 the block of thirteen reads nearly as round as one cell. The pieces keep the rhombic dodecahedron's combinatorics (12 four-sided faces, 14 corners) with flat but no longer rhombic faces; the centre and the two kinds of outer piece come with their mirror images, six outer pieces of each hand. Requiring all thirteen pieces equal in volume reaches only 0.431, from differing starts. Flat faces are exact to 10⁻¹², and the thirteen volumes sum to 26 in every case.

4. Kits to build

All 16 kits are buildable piece by piece in the Polyhedraverse app (DICTO's pieces). Each kit is stored as a plan, every piece's shape, place and turn about the centre. Choosing a kit puts its centre down; the plan is lined up with the build by the turn and place that match the most built pieces, and the next layer still open is shown as outlines, from the centre out. The kits use 25 new pieces (among them AXE, FUJI, Cleo, Hasu, VAJRA, Vajra (fine), Chatra, Mudra, Crown Jewel, Diadem L and R, and the RD 13 pieces) and reuse catalogue solids (dodecahedra, icosahedra, J11, J63, the icosidodecahedron, the triacontahedra) for 221 placements. The DICTO 13-Atom Clusters world of the Kaleidohedra app shows every kit with pull-apart, shell-by-shell and see-through views.

5. Checks

Every piece of every kit: closed, consistently wound, faces flat (worst 1.5 × 10⁻⁵, from five-place coordinates; 10⁻¹³ for the RD 13). Kit volumes as tabled; the closed kits equal their hulls. Every placement of every kit plan reproduces its piece exactly (corners within 10⁻⁴), and every kit builds to completion following its outlines from a centre placed at an arbitrary turn and place. The RD 13 tiling: the 13 lattice points are distinct modulo the sublattice (8 of 183 sublattices), and the rounded blocks keep volume 26 with every corner class moved once. Scripts: scripts/dump-thirteens.mjs, build-thirteens-data.py, build-kits13.py, kit-shapes.py; studies/2026-10-13-rd13-block.

6. Relation to known work

Twelve spheres round one and the thirteen-sphere problem: Schütte and van der Waerden (1952). The thirteen-atom icosahedral cluster: Frank (1952); its shells as a dense non-crystallographic packing: Mackay (1962). Icosahedral scaling and packing of icosahedra and dodecahedra: Gray (2002). Clusters of triacontahedra: Kabai. Tilings of a lattice by translates of a point and its neighbours are radius-one perfect codes; in the cube lattice they exist in every dimension (Golomb and Welch 1970; see Etzion 2011, Campello et al. 2015), while radius two fails in three dimensions (Gravier, Mollard and Payan 1998). We did not find the face-centred cubic case, the RD 13 tiling, stated, though it is simple enough that it may be known; we claim no novelty for it. We found no description of the rounded, keyed RD 13 block, or of these seven clusters with their kits side by side. The search is not exhaustive.
