# Audit of paper #118, Version 1 (24 November 2026)

"Icosahedral Cores in Golden Rhombohedra: Scutoid Cells for the Ammann–Kramer–Neri Tiling", DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24491406.
Source audited: `papers/2026-11-22-amber-ammann/paper.html` as released. Study: `studies/2026-11-21-amber-ammann` (commit 8d5653d7).
Nothing was published or changed in the paper; this file is for the conversation with DICTO.

## What needs correcting

1. The abstract says the odd edges alone give conditions with no common solution; the computation used every loop of walls, not only the odd edges, so it should say "the conditions from all loops of walls have no common solution".
2. In the proof of Theorem 3, "the loops closed by all 998 walls" is wrong: 597 walls close loops, and their holonomies are 27 identity, 368 improper and 202 proper; the 428 counts the 401 tree walls as identity loops.
   - Note, 24 November 2026 (items 1 and 2): the odd-edge conditions were then solved alone, exactly over ℚ(√5), with the same unknown x₀ and frames (`studies/2026-11-21-amber-ammann/scripts/odd_edges.py`, `logs/odd_edges.log`). The 552 odd edges give 552 mirror-plane conditions (96 distinct planes); they have no common point (rank 3, inconsistent). Certificate: three odd edges of one rhombic face, two round which 5 cells meet and one round which 3 meet; the two 5-cell planes force z = 0, the 3-cell plane forces z = −(1+√5)/2. So the abstract's wording "the odd edges' conditions alone have no common solution" is true and item 1 is withdrawn; the proof of Theorem 3 should cite this computation instead of the all-loops one (this also supplies the computation item 3 asked for). The counts of item 2 are confirmed: 998 walls = 401 tree walls (all identity) + 597 loops, and 597 = 27 identity + 368 improper + 202 proper.
3. The sentence in Theorem 3 that "the planes of the different edges have no common point" was never computed on its own; it should be removed or replaced by the all-loops statement.
4. The proof of Theorem 1 (c) says a tiling with a period has rational frequencies; that holds only with three independent periods, so "no period" needs the standard cut-and-project fact (Moody's survey, which I opened) in place of the frequency argument.
5. The abstract says the scutoid layer has exactly the symmetries of the tiling; only translations were proved, so it should say "exactly the periods (translations) of the tiling".
   - Note, 24 November 2026 (item 5, rotations and mirrors): checked exactly in ℚ(√5) (`studies/2026-11-21-amber-ammann/scripts/sym_check.py`, `logs/sym_check.log`, ending FINAL ok).
     (a) The paper's tiling (shift γ = (1234/10007, −567/10009, 891/10037), window the triacontahedron π′([−½,½]⁶) in its 20 parallelepipeds) has no symmetry but the identity: no rotation, mirror, inversion, screw, glide or translation. Proof: an isometry x ↦ gx + t of the tiling maps edges to edges, so g permutes the 12 edge vectors ±e_i and is one of the 120 elements of I_h (computed); it acts on ℤ⁶ by a signed permutation, and its Galois conjugate g′ acts on the e_i′ by the same one, so g′ carries each window piece to the piece of the image triple. t joins two vertices, so t = π(k) with k in ℤ⁶, and the image tiling is the one with shift g′⁻¹(γ − π′(k)). Two shifts on no window boundary give the same tiling only when they are equal, since the windows are open and π′(ℤ⁶) is dense (it is closed under multiplication by φ′³ = 2 − √5, computed; not under φ′). γ lies on no window boundary anywhere in the infinite tiling (a denominator test for all 15 face normals), so the isometry is a symmetry exactly when γ − g′γ = π′(k). Solved in the basis e₁′, …, e₆′ of ℚ⁶: only the identity gives an integral k, and then k = 0.
     (b) A point symmetry g needs γ − g′γ in π′(ℤ⁶), so a generic shift has none; icosahedral symmetry about a point needs a special shift. The obvious one, γ = 0 (fixed by every g′), lies on a window boundary (exact witness: triple 012, n = (−1, −1, −1, 0, −1, 0)), so the rule does not fix a tiling there. (Setting: Moody, arXiv:math/0002020, §3 and §5; Baake and Grimm, arXiv:1205.3633. The argument in (a) is written out here, not quoted.)
     (c) The finite 402-tile patch: an isometry of it fixes the barycentre of the tile centres and has its linear part in I_h; all 120 were tested exactly: only the identity.
     (d) The layer (Rollerball, S₀, centroid seeds; the 20 exact runs): for all 120 g in I_h and all 20 triples A, g maps the 72 pieces of the cell of A onto the 72 pieces of the cell of gA, compared facet by facet in exact coordinates (the 12 stabiliser elements of one triple of each type, and one g for each other triple, which together give all 2400 cases). So for every tiling T of Theorem 1 each symmetry of T is a symmetry of the layer. Conversely, an isometry of the layer maps cores to cores and the pieces on a core to the pieces on its image, so each cell to a cell: it is a symmetry of T. The proof of Theorem 1 (c) carries over from translations to all isometries once this equivariance is cited.
     Verdict: "exactly the symmetries of the tiling" can stand, as a statement about every tiling T of Theorem 1, if the proof cites (a)–(d); for the tiling actually used, both groups are the identity alone, and the paper should say so, so that no reader expects rotations or mirrors. Not checked here: the other four cores of Proposition 4, whose Theorem 1 (c) claim needs the same equivariance run.
   - Note, 24 November 2026 (item 5, the other four cores): the equivariance check (d) run for every valid setting of Table 2 (Fullerene Star S₀ and 6/5 S₀ projected; icosidodecahedron 6/5 S₀ both seed rules; icosahedron S₀ and 6/5 S₀ projected, S₀, 6/5 S₀ and own centroid; dodecahedron S₀, 6/5 S₀ and own centroid, 6/5 S₀ projected; 13 settings, 20 orientations each): in every one, all 120 g carry the pieces of each cell onto those of the image cell, exactly in ℚ(√5) (`scripts/sym_check.py` with core arguments, `logs/sym_check_cores.log`, ending FINAL ok). So Theorem 1 (c), symmetries included, holds for all five cores of Proposition 4.
6. The near miss for the Fullerene Star with centroid seeds is blamed on the core being small at the common scale, but it also fails at its own tight scale, so that explanation must go.
   Note, 24 Nov (the true reason; study amber-ammann, `scripts/buried_hex.py`, `logs/buried_hex.log`, exact in ℚ(√5), FINAL ok). (a) The centroid of each of the 20 hexagons lies strictly inside the convex hull of the other 79 face centroids (it sits in the hollow between the pyramids); the centroids of the 60 triangles do not. So for each hexagon the points nearer its centroid than any other centroid form a closed, bounded pocket, and the hexagon gets an outer region only if the mould surface cuts that pocket. (b) For each hexagon and each cell, σ = the smallest scale at which the pocket lies inside the mould, found by six linear programmes with exact certificates (the three active bisectors meet in a point satisfying all 79 inequalities exactly; the mould normal is a non-negative combination of those three exactly). For s > σ the hexagon gets no region; for s < σ it gets one of positive area. (c) Prolate cell (all 10 orientations): σ = (−83 + 55√5)/4 ≈ 9.996 for the two hexagons on the cell's long three-fold axis, facing the two sharp corners; 24 − 7√5/2 ≈ 16.174 for six; (13 + 41√5)/4 ≈ 26.170 for twelve. Since s₀(prolate) = (15 + 3√5)/2 ≈ 10.854 > 9.996, the two axis hexagons get no outer region at the tight scale and so at every admissible scale s ≥ s₀; at S₀ = (15 + 9√5)/2 ≈ 17.562 and 6/5 S₀ the six more join them (8 empty). Oblate cell: σ = (13 + 41√5)/4 for twelve and (109 + 27√5)/4 ≈ 42.343 for eight, all above s₀(oblate) = S₀, so no face is empty for s₀ ≤ s < (13 + 41√5)/4. (d) These predictions reproduce all 30 logged centroid-seed results exactly (every empty-cell list, every orientation, own s₀, S₀, 6/5 S₀). (e) No scale works with centroid seeds in the prolate cell (proved by (c)). Projected seeds lie on the mould and so never leave a face empty; with them the prolate cell is valid at S₀ and 6/5 S₀ but fails at its own s₀ (four kinds of piece cross themselves, logs `C1_*_own_cproj.log`). Proposed wording for §4: "The Fullerene Star with centroid seeds fails in the prolate cell at every scale: each hexagon's centroid lies in the hollow between the pyramids, so the points nearest to it form a closed pocket, and for the two hexagons facing the cell's sharp corners that pocket lies wholly inside the mould even at the tightest fit (exactly, the pocket fits from scale (−83 + 55√5)/4, below s₀ = (15 + 3√5)/2), so those faces get no outer region. Projected seeds, which lie on the mould, avoid this."
7. Figure 3's caption says the white dots are the 30 Y-vertices of each cell; in the pulled-apart view each Y-vertex is drawn on every piece that carries it, about 180 dots per cell.
8. Figure 4's colour words disagree: the caption says yellow and pale blue, the figure's subtitle says sand and slate, and its legend shows a slate swatch for lines drawn pale blue.
9. Section 7 says Frettlöh's note gives the cut-and-project description used in Section 2; it does not, it treats Danzer's tetrahedra and only says the rhombohedral tiling comes from Z⁶.
10. Section 2 says the construction is "in Kramer and Neri's form"; that cannot be checked (their paper could not be opened), and Section 7 says the patch was written from scratch, so the words should go.
11. The Mackay reference has the wrong pages: the Russian original is Kristallografiya 26 (1981) 910–919, and the English translation is Sov. Phys. Crystallogr. 26 (1981) 517–522; it was reprinted in Steinhardt and Ostlund, The Physics of Quasicrystals (1987).
12. The three "standard, cited without a source opened" facts (frequencies from window volumes, repetitivity, no period) now have an opened free source: Moody, "Model Sets: A Survey", arXiv:math/0002020. Cite it at each point of use.
13. The history of the tiling can be cited from opened free sources: Baake and Grimm, arXiv:1205.3633, for Ammann, Mackay and Kramer–Neri, and Senechal's slides for Ammann's 1976 letters to Gardner. Kramer–Neri and Mackay should then be marked "not opened".
14. "How it was found" says the mirror obstruction was found that evening; the log and the commit put it at about 2 pm on 21 November, and the exact reruns behind every "Exact" mark were made on 22 November.
15. Remark 5 uses another author's method without credit: the Voronoi diagram on a plane is a power diagram, a fact due to Aurenhammer (1987). It needs a credit where it is used, from a source that can be opened (not yet found).
16. Name, for DICTO to decide: since the term rule of 24 November, the pieces are called scutoid frusta, but the paper and Figures 3–4 say "true scutoids" and "scutoids" 33 times.

One addition, not an error: the abstract's "across every prolate–oblate wall they do not" is true for every tiling. All 120 possible prolate–oblate wall configurations occur in the patch. A one-line reason could be added.
  - Note, 24 November 2026 (checked, `studies/2026-11-21-amber-ammann/scripts/wall_configs.py`, `logs/wall_configs.log`, 8 settings, each ending FINAL ok): the upgrade holds, for every face-to-face tiling by the two cells, Penrose or not. (a) A wall configuration is an unordered pair of half-walls (A, l, s): a cell given by its triple A among the 20, and its face spanned by the two vectors of A other than e_l, on side s; the two cells lie on opposite sides of the common face. In a face-to-face tiling by translates of the 20 cells, every shared face is such a pair, and the pair fixes both cells relative to the face. The list follows from geometry alone, so it needs no fact about cut-and-project tilings: on each of the 15 face planes, on each side, there are exactly 2 prolate and 2 oblate placements (exact). (b) So there are exactly 15 × 2 × 2 = 60 prolate–oblate pairs of placements, each in two mirror positions across the face: 120 in all (also 60 prolate–prolate and 60 oblate–oblate). The 998 walls of the patch all lie in this list, and all 120 prolate–oblate configurations occur among its 618 prolate–oblate walls. (c) The pieces of a cell depend only on its triple, the core, the scale and the seed rule, and move with the cell under translation, so the patterns on the two sides of a wall depend only on its configuration. Compared directly for each configuration, relative to the face centre, exactly in ℚ(√5): the patterns differ in all 120, in each of the 8 settings of the fm_* logs. (d) Any orientation: all 15 face rhombi are congruent, and each cell's full symmetry group has order 12 (8 vertices, distance-preserving permutations counted exactly), the same as its stabiliser in I_h. So across a given face, on a given side, a copy of a cell can sit in exactly 6 × 4 / 12 = 2 ways, and both already occur among the 20 orientations. Every prolate–oblate wall in any face-to-face tiling by congruent copies of the two rhombohedra is therefore congruent to one of the 120. With the pieces carried by each copy (well defined by the stabiliser check, item 5 (d)), the patterns differ across every prolate–oblate wall. Strongest true statement: in every face-to-face tiling of space by the prolate and oblate golden rhombohedra, in any arrangement, the pieces do not meet top to top across any prolate–oblate wall, in each of the 8 settings. This does not cover tilings that are not face-to-face, or settings that were not computed. Side finding, not for the P–O claim: the same-type claim (prolate–prolate and oblate–oblate walls agree) does not carry over in the same way. Of the 60 same-type configurations of each kind, 30 are mirror pairs (they agree, by (ii)) and 30 are two translates of one cell stacked across a face. Agreement counts 30/60 for each kind in every setting, so the stacked ones disagree. They do not occur in the patch, and whether they occur in some cut-and-project tiling was not checked (LEAD).
