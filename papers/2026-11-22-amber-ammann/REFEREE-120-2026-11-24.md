# Referee report: the 120 prolate–oblate wall configurations (paper #118), 24 November 2026

Independent adversarial review of one claim. Materials read: the paper's definitions (§2, Theorem 1), the note at the end of the
extra-finding section of `AUDIT-2026-11-24.md`, `scripts/wall_configs.py`, `scripts/facematch_exact.py`, the seed and outer-pattern
part of `scripts/exact_cell.py`, `scripts/aligned.py`, and `logs/wall_configs.log` (study 2026-11-21-amber-ammann).

## The claim

In every face-to-face tiling of space by the prolate and oblate golden rhombohedra, Penrose or not, across every prolate–oblate wall
the Scutoid-Frusta pieces of the two cells do not meet top to top; in each of the 8 settings of the paper's wall checks.

## Verdict: correct, with gaps

No step fails. The gaps are of statement and scope, listed in §3; none changes the result. Two findings are stronger than the claim (§4).

## 2. Is "every face-to-face tiling" justified?

Yes, with the induction of step (4). The list of 120 comes from geometry alone, and the patterns depend only on the configuration, so
nothing about cut and project is used. The fact that all 120 occur in the patch is not needed for the claim (it shows the list is not
larger than necessary).

## 3. Gaps

1. **The global step is not written.** The note argues wall by wall ("every wall is congruent to one of the 120"). For an arbitrary
   tiling one must also say what "all cores the same way up" means. It follows from step (4): all cells share one six-axis star, the
   core with its five-fold axes on that star is unique (its symmetry group is the group of the star), and this agrees with "pieces
   carried by each copy" because the pieces are invariant under the cell's full group of order 12. One sentence closes it.
2. **Scope of the settings.** The 8 settings all have both cells at one common scale. The "own scale" settings of Table 2 (two core
   sizes) and settings not computed are not covered. The note says so; the paper's sentence must too.
3. **Meaning of "do not meet top to top".** What is proved is that the two outer patterns on the wall differ as partitions of the
   wall. See §4 (a) for the stronger statement that is also true.
4. **Independence.** The exact comparison shares its pattern code with the wall check it extends. The sampled check above is
   independent of that code, but uses the same core coordinates and the same scales from the study (rebuilt separately in the audit,
   double precision). The exact result and the sampled result agree in every one of the 8 × 240 cases.
5. **Same-type walls: the paper's sentence needs a scope.** See §5.

## 4. Stronger than claimed (checked here)

(a) Exact, all 8 settings: in every one of the 120 prolate–oblate configurations the two patterns have **no region in common at all**
(0 common polygons; for example 16 and 16 regions for the Rollerball, 17 and 22 for the Fullerene Star, none shared). So not one piece
top on one side coincides with a piece top on the other.

(b) See §5: the open lead of the note is closed.

## 5. The side finding about same-type walls

Confirmed: of the 60 same-type configurations of each kind, 30 are two different cells that are mirror images in the wall (patterns
agree, all 8 settings) and 30 are two translates of one cell stacked across a face (patterns differ, all 8 settings; exact rerun and
sampled check). Reason for the stacked ones: the cell and its core are centrally symmetric, so the pattern seen from the far side is the
near pattern turned by a half-turn about the face centre, and the patterns do not have that symmetry.

The lead ("do stacked pairs occur in some cut-and-project tiling?") is closed here, exactly in ℚ(√5): **they never occur** under the
paper's rule. The tiles n + C_A and n + e_l + C_A (l in A) are both present only if e_l′ lies in the open set Σ_{b∉A} (−1, 1) e_b′.
Writing e_l′ in the basis e_b′ (b∉A), for all 20 triples and all 3 choices of l, the absolute coefficients are (1, φ, φ) or
(1/φ, 1/φ, 1): one coefficient is exactly 1 in all 60 cases, so e_l′ is on the boundary and the two open windows do not meet, for
every shift γ. So in every tiling of the paper's cut-and-project class each same-type wall is a mirror pair and the patterns agree.

For tilings outside that class the same-type statement is false in general: the tiling by translates of a single cell, which the
hypothesis of Theorem 1 admits, has only stacked walls. The sentence "across a wall shared by two cells of the same type the patterns
line up" must be limited to the cut-and-project tilings (or to walls whose two cells are mirror images).

## 6. Statements that are safe to print

- In every face-to-face tiling of space by the prolate and oblate golden rhombohedra, with the cores at one common scale, the outer
  patterns of the two cells differ across every prolate–oblate wall, in each of the 8 settings computed; indeed they share no region.
- In every 3D Penrose tiling of the paper's cut-and-project class, two cells of the same type that share a wall are mirror images in
  it, and their patterns agree (8 settings).
- Not covered: tilings that are not face to face; the own-scale settings; settings not computed.
