# #62 (working title) Shadows of the Fourth Dimension: Deltahedra that Join Differently

Status: GROUNDWORK (2026-10-27). Waits for the six large 600-cell slices (studies/2026-10-27-4d-shadows). DICTO's ideas: 4D shadows represent the new deltahedron family better than the regular one; variants differ in shape AND attachability; halves to pin down Shadow-17.

1. Opening (no label): the four 4D solids made of triangles (5-, 16-, 24-, 600-cell) cast 3D shadows and slices with triangle faces; they echo the deltahedra, but bent, and they join differently.
2. Background: the 8 convex deltahedra; variants (same pattern, other shape: Tsuruta et al. 2014); five-pyramids study: gluing DICTO's pieces gives no new deltahedron (772 solids, all known kinds) — why the 4D route.
3. Method: exact shadows, vertex layers, hyperplane slices in Q(√5), every direction (vertex-, edge-, face-, cell-first); 223 records, 27 all-triangle.
4. Result 1: no non-cell shadow/slice is a regular deltahedron; 2 convex variants (5-cell edge-first ~ J12; 16-cell edge-first ~ octahedron = square bipyramidal honeycomb cell, credited).
5. Result 2, attachability: the variants join differently (three round an edge at 120°; space-filling alone).
6. Result 3: 600-cell patterns of non-convex deltahedra: 12-dented icosahedron, augmented dodecahedron, excavated dodecahedron (known); Shadow-17 = two J11 base to base (halves method; DICTO's starting piece).
7. Result 4: the six large slices (pending).
8. Note: the true vertex-first sections of the 600-cell (edge midpoints included) vs the vertex-shell hulls often shown.
9. Bipyramid obstruction: n-gonal bipyramids, n ≥ 6, have no deltahedral form (4 patterns).
10. Prior art and sources checked. 11. Conclusion.
