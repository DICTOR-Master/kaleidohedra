# #63 (working title) The White Queen 600-cell: an exact atlas of its slices and shadows

Name: DICTO 2026-10-27 ("six impossible things"; "White Queen 600 cell"). Status: GROUNDWORK; built after #62 with the 4D study's exact tools.

Epigraph: "Why, sometimes I've believed as many as six impossible things before breakfast." — the White Queen, Lewis Carroll, *Through the Looking-Glass, and What Alice Found There* (1871), ch. 5 "Wool and Water".

## Dodgson (Lewis Carroll) references to weave in (each where it fits; all checked 2026-10-27)
- **Through the Looking-Glass** (1871): the epigraph; the looking-glass = the mirror ζ → 1 − ζ / reflections used throughout (shadows seen in a glass; mirror images of slices at ±h).
- **Sylvie and Bruno Concluded** (1893), ch. 7 "Mein Herr": **Fortunatus's Purse**, three handkerchiefs sewn so that "whatever is inside that Purse is outside it" — a projective plane, an object that cannot be built in three dimensions; Carroll's own picture of a higher-dimensional object only partly realisable in our space: the theme of shadows of the fourth dimension. (Text: Lit2Go chapter 7; Standard Ebooks; NKU handout on the projective plane.)
- **Condensation of Determinants** (Proc. Royal Society, read 17 May 1866) and **An Elementary Treatise on Determinants** (1867): Dodgson condensation; exact rational/ℚ(√5) linear algebra is how the atlas is computed and checked.
- **Euclid and His Modern Rivals** (1879): Dodgson's defence of Euclid's exact, proved geometry — the standard the atlas keeps (every slice proved, none sampled).
- **Curiosa Mathematica** Part I, *A New Theory of Parallels* (1888); Part II, *Pillow Problems Thought Out During Sleepless Nights* (1893).
Sources for the list: ProofWiki "Mathematician: Charles Lutwidge Dodgson"; Oxford Mathematical Institute, "Lewis Carroll" (maths.ox.ac.uk PDF); BSHM "Dodgson" (bshm.ac.uk PDF); MacTutor (St Andrews) DNB and DSB entries; IMA "The Mathematical World of Charles L. Dodgson"; Lit2Go Sylvie and Bruno Concluded ch. 7.
