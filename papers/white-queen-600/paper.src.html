The White Queen 600-cell

An exact atlas of the slices and shadows of the 600-cell, and Humpty Dumpty put back together

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com
Paper #63 · Version 1 · __DATE__ · __DOI__ · Licence CC BY-SA 4.0
The ideas are DICTO's. Computation and proofs with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. The 600-cell is the four-dimensional solid built from 600 regular tetrahedra, twenty round every vertex. DICTO asked for a definitive, proved picture of it. We give a complete exact atlas of its three-dimensional slices in its four main directions (through a vertex, the middle of an edge, the centre of a triangle and the centre of a cell). Every slice is computed in exact numbers a + b√5. Between two neighbouring vertex heights a slice keeps one pattern of faces, so one cut per gap, together with the cuts at the vertex heights, gives every slice there is: 62 patterns on one side of the centre (9, 17, 21 and 15 in the four directions). Exactly twelve of them are made only of triangles, and we name each one. The largest slices have 144 vertices. With the four shadows of the 600-cell, this completes the atlas. Its most unusual member is Humpty Dumpty, the 48-vertex slice rebuilt with all 92 triangles equilateral: one of six large slices that ask for an equal-edged form, and the only one of the six that has one with its full symmetry.

1. Begin at the beginning

“Begin at the beginning,” the King said gravely, “and go on till you come to the end: then stop.” — Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, ch. 12

That is the plan of this paper: start at the centre of the 600-cell and cut it, height by height, until the cut leaves it at the far tip. DICTO's question was whether the 600-cell could be assembled into a definitive picture, every part proved. Our previous paper (#62, Shadows of the Fourth Dimension) asked which of its slices are deltahedra; here we map all of them. The title is DICTO's: the 600-cell belongs to the White Queen of Through the Looking-Glass, who practised believing impossible things (§5).

2. Method

The 600-cell is taken with its 120 vertices at distance 2 from the centre: the 8 permutations of (±2, 0, 0, 0), the 16 points (±1, ±1, ±1, ±1) and the 96 even permutations of (±φ, ±1, ±1/φ, 0), where φ = (1+√5)/2. Its edges have length √5 − 1. All arithmetic is exact in ℚ(√5). For each direction d we sort the vertices by height x·d. A slice at height h is the convex hull of the vertices at that height and of the points where edges cross it; hulls are proposed in floating point and every face is then verified exactly.

Lemma (one pattern per gap). Between two neighbouring vertex heights, the slice has the same pattern of faces at every height. Reason: no vertex lies in the open gap, so the same edges cross every cutting space in the gap, and the same triangles and tetrahedra of the 600-cell are cut. Hence the cuts at the vertex heights and one cut inside each gap give every slice pattern.

Heights are measured as x·d, with d the vertex, edge midpoint, triangle centre or cell centre itself, so heights compare within one direction only. The 600-cell is centrally symmetric, so the far half of each direction mirrors the near half; we list the near half, from the centre to the tip.

5. Six impossible things

“Why, sometimes I've believed as many as six impossible things before breakfast.” — the White Queen, Through the Looking-Glass, ch. 5

Six of the all-triangle slices are large (28 to 72 vertices) and match no known deltahedron: the slices with 72, 32 (edge-first), 30, 28, 42 and 48 vertices. #62 asked of each whether it can be rebuilt with every edge equal while keeping its symmetry, and solved all six exactly. Five are impossible. The sixth, the 48-vertex slice, is possible, and is Humpty Dumpty. Equal-edged forms of the five with less than their full symmetry have not been searched; they are queued as a separate study.

6. Humpty Dumpty

“All the King's horses and all the King's men / Couldn't put Humpty Dumpty in his place again.” — Through the Looking-Glass, ch. 6

The cell-first slice at height 1 + √5/2 is a convex, egg-like solid of 48 vertices and 92 triangles, with edges in a ratio up to (5+√5)/2. Rebuilt with all edges equal and its tetrahedral symmetry kept, it has exactly one form: Humpty Dumpty (DICTO's name; Slice-48 in #62). It has 138 equal edges and dihedral angles from 81.75° to 243.20°, so it folds in and out where the egg was smooth; its nearest non-adjacent parts are 0.069 apart at edge 1, and its embedding was confirmed with exact orientation tests. It is put back together, but not in its old place: the equal triangles force a new shape.

“When I use a word,” Humpty Dumpty said in rather a scornful tone, “it means just what I choose it to mean—neither more nor less.” — ch. 6

We use the name in that spirit: Humpty Dumpty means exactly this solid.

7. The four shadows

The shadows (projections along the four directions) are convex. The vertex-first shadow has 42 vertices and 80 triangles, the same pattern as the central slice (#62). The edge-first shadow has 52 vertices, 60 triangles and 20 squares; the face-first shadow 56 vertices, 84 triangles and 12 squares; the cell-first shadow 44 vertices, 72 triangles and 6 squares.

8. Prior art and credit

Alicia Boole Stott (1900; encouraged by P. H. Schoute) studied series of three-dimensional sections of the regular four-dimensional polytopes and made complete sets of models of the sections of the 600-cell and the 120-cell; two sets of 600-cell section models are kept at the University of Groningen, and a further set is at the Faulkes Institute, Cambridge. Coxeter's Regular Polytopes treats the polytopes and the vertex-first sections. The directions and number of sections in Boole Stott's series are not stated in the sources we checked, and we did not read her paper; her work is the precedent for this atlas. What we did not find in the sources checked: the exact atlas in all four directions with the gaps between vertex heights, the list of all-triangle slices, and Humpty Dumpty.

9. Conclusion

The 600-cell has 62 slice patterns on each side of its centre in its four main directions, all now computed exactly; twelve are made only of triangles, and each of them is identified. Together with the four shadows this is a complete, proved atlas of the White Queen's solid. Among the six large all-triangle slices that challenge an equal-edged rebuild, Humpty Dumpty is the one that can be put back together.
