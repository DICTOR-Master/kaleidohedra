4. The catalogue of 10 725

Version 1 counted the dice of the frame groups. Here every smaller group with one fixed point is crossed with the same 122 face directions, and the list grows to 10 725 fair dice, each one certified.

Scope. Every die in the catalogue has its faces on the 122 frame directions: the axes of one cube and of both icosahedra that fit it (6 + 8 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 24 + 12 + 12 + 24 directions, §3). Its depths are fixed by fairness (every face seen from the centre under 4π/N, Theorem A), and its full symmetry group fixes only the centre, so the centre is the centroid. The catalogue is complete for the searches stated, and only for them:

  1. Nos. 1–1232 (Version 1 and the two-placement dice): every orbit set of the frame groups Ih, Oh, O, Td, Th and T, on one icosahedron placement (149 dice) and on both (1083).
  2. Nos. 1233–1235, the Aequi Permutahedra: the duals of the three permutahedra, fair by symmetry (§7).
  3. Nos. 1236–10725, catalogue 2 (9490 dice): every subgroup of Oh with one fixed point that does not contain T, up to conjugation (D6h, D4h, D3h, D3d, D2h, D2d, D4, D3, C4h, S6, C2h, S4, D2 as full groups), on both icosahedron placements, every set with two kinds of face (m = 2) for every class, m = 3 for every class of order at least 6 and m = 4 for D3d (5935 dice); then three batches on one placement with higher limits (3555 dice). Every one of the 6924 orbit sets of the first batch gave exactly one fair die. 66 dice met again under another group are counted once (the earlier number is kept).

Not searched, and so not claimed: larger m for the small groups, and directions outside the 122. Theorem B (§2) shows fair dice exist far beyond this frame; the catalogue is the part of that infinite family that is listed, numbered and certified.

5. Certification

A computed die could be a rounding error that looks fair. Each of the 10 725 comes with a proof that a fair die exists in a small box around the listed depths, and only one.

Theorem 5 (every listed die is fair; computer-assisted proof). For each of the 10 722 computed dice of the catalogue there is exactly one fair depth vector in an explicit box around its listed depths. For Nos. 1–1232 the published 60-digit depths lie within 5·10⁻⁶⁰ of it; for catalogue 2 the refined depths published with the data lie within 5.2·10⁻⁷⁶ of it. (The three Aequi Permutahedra are fair by symmetry.)

Method (study 2026-11-24-aequi-certify-extend). The face normals are exact vectors over ℚ(√5), turned into balls at 320 bits (Arb, through python-flint). Each die is described by one depth per face orbit of its full group. Over a box of depths the combinatorial type is fixed and certified: the slack of every plane at every corner is linear in the depths, so its minimum over the box is exact. Corners where more than three planes meet are proved structural in ℚ(√5) (188 dice) or treated on split boxes (3 dice). Then Krawczyk's test on the fairness equations proves one zero in the box. A tight run (box radius 10⁻⁴⁰ for Nos. 1–1232; Newton-refined boxes for catalogue 2) gives the bounds; a wide run gives each die's uniqueness half-width (data/certify-table.csv).

Independent rebuild (study 2026-11-24-aequi-catalogue-2, app_build.py). Every one of the 9490 dice of catalogue 2 was rebuilt from its orbits and depths for the picker. Each face's solid angle was checked against 4π/N to within 10⁻⁹. The full symmetry group was computed from the solid itself, and its order, face orbits and corners were compared with the catalogue: 9490 of 9490 agree.

7. Designations

Some dice share a history or a property worth a name; three designations mark them in the picker.

Fair by symmetry (m = 1): every face is carried to every other by a symmetry of the solid, Diaconis and Keller's first kind of fair die [Diaconis–Keller]. The face-transitive solids are classical and are credited as such.

The Aequi Diaconis–Keller (m = 2; 1088 dice: 56 among Nos. 1–1232 and 1032 in catalogue 2). Diaconis and Keller showed that a die can be fair by continuity: let one kind of face grow at the expense of another, and somewhere the chances pass through equality [Diaconis–Keller]. Every die of the catalogue with exactly two kinds of face is fair for this reason, and the name is given in tribute to Persi Diaconis and Joseph B. Keller. The tribute and the name are ours; they are not claimed to be endorsed by them. Their argument concerns the chance of landing on a face; here fairness is equal solid angle from the centre (§1), so the name honours the idea of continuity between two kinds of face, not a claim about the physical rolling of these dice.

The Aequi Permutahedra (Nos. 1233–1235): Ini-misa #1233, the tetrakis hexahedron (24 faces); Ini-miyo #1234, the disdyakis dodecahedron (48); Ini-migo #1235, the disdyakis triacontahedron (120). They are the duals of the permutahedra of the groups A3, B3 and H3, one face for each element of the group, fair by symmetry. Rolling such a die from face to face across an edge applies one mirror of the group.

9. Names

Each die is named by reading its number in goroawase syllables, one syllable for each digit (the first reading of the digit, the next reading when the first would repeat the syllable before it), and written with its number after a hashtag: Minana #377. From four syllables on, a hyphen follows the second syllable: Ini-mimu #1236, Goko-goko #5555, Io-nanigo #10725. The 10 725 names are distinct, and each decodes to its own number. The Aequi picker (https://kaleidohedra.dictospheres.com/aequi/) shows every die under its name, with its group, kinds of face, depths, Vacuus and Resonus.
