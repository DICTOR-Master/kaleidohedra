Preface

A space-filler is a solid whose copies pack together with no gaps, like bricks. Most solids cannot do this alone, but some can in pairs: two solids that, together, fill space. DICTO calls such a pair a DICTONIC bond. There are four: the Golden bond, Icosaxes and Unborn Axes; the Keyed bond, Lotus Jewel Axes and Dodecaxes (both Chapter 3); the Delian bond, Samson and Delilah (Chapter 4); and the Twinned Delian bond, Sonsam and Lahdeli (Chapter 8). The DICTONIC bonds are the founding members of DICTO's family of DICTO Equilibrium Space-Fillers, the solids at the distinguished points of space-filling continua, to be set out in a later paper.

In the web papers (from Part III on) the word bond on its own means something smaller: a shared face between two neighbouring pieces. The bonds together make the web, and the web shows how a filling changes as its pieces change shape.

DICTO proposed that the bond papers are one series building to the Rule of Halves. This book follows that path. Part I, Filling, starts from the question of which solids fill space together: every mix of the five Platonic solids (dodecahedron, icosahedron, cube, tetrahedron, octahedron) does, with a kit of seven connectors (Chapter 1) and classical space-fillers come exactly from one setting of three moves (Chapter 2). Part II, The bonds, cuts the gaps of a filling at the right place and finds the first pairs: the golden point of the icosahedra (Chapter 3) and the cut through the octet where Samson and Delilah have equal volume (Chapter 4). Part III, The web, shows that the golden and the Delian families are each one fixed set of points of two kinds with one weight slid between the kinds, and follows which faces are shared as the weight slides, there and in ten more crystal networks (Chapters 5 and 6). Part IV, The weight walk's bonds, follows the pairs that the walk reaches: David and Goliath in the A15 alloy (Chapter 7) and Sonsam and Lahdeli on the twinned octet of the hexagonal stacking (Chapter 8); Chapter 9 returns to the golden pair and closes twenty Icosaxes into one solid, the Icosaxes Seed, with eight Icosaxes round one Unborn Axes as the blocks of a network. Part V, The law, tells the whole series as one route (Chapter 10) and then turns the same weight on every way of stacking balls at once to find the Rule of Halves (Chapter 11): every piece is borrowed, whole or in half, from the two pure stackings.

Each chapter is one published paper, kept word for word, with the paper and the DICTO DOI it comes from. Figures are renumbered by chapter (Figure 4.2 is Figure 2 of Chapter 4). The sources checked, the data and code, and the references of every chapter are gathered in the Appendix, chapter by chapter.
