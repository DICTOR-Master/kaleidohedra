The 3D Hat: Where It Stands

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · one-page plan, 12 November 2026 · aims 1–3 joined: one piece, any stacking; the bridge between lattice and non-repeating order; geometry giving laws
DICTO: aims, ideas and names. Computation, checks and drafting with Claude (Anthropic).
Draft for DICTO's read. A working plan, not for release; the keyed-shape tests (step 1) are running.

The goal. One piece, a cross of the rhombic dodecahedron (RD) and its hexagonal partner (TRD) such as Narcissus, that fills space alone and can only do so without ever repeating, ideally forcing a non-repeating stacking of close-packed layers (a Fibonacci word of c and h).

The RD bridge (12 Nov)Tsiokos's chair (Chair44) with Kamenetsky's certified marking, carried exactly onto the RD packing: 118 marked RDs, cut down to 62 (each RD knows how the chairs it touches are turned, up to quarter turns about one axis, and which of its cubes share a chair). Certified strongly aperiodic: only the 30 real chair contacts are possible, whole chairs are forced, and Kamenetsky's Theorem A (reproduced by us) applies. All layers stay c.
Keyed shapes (12 Nov)Simple bumps and dents on the 62: contacts stay certified, but whole chairs are not forced. Richer sets (90, 118) are being tested as shapes.
The golden slice (Paradigm Ch. 12)Slicing the 4D permutohedron filling in the golden direction gives a non-repeating filling with cells of exactly 35 combinatorial kinds but endlessly many shapes.

The route from here

  1. Shape. Turn a certified marked RD set into bump-and-dent shapes that force it exactly (refine the types until faces that fit a common partner fit the same partners), then cut the count down again, as from 118 to 62.
  2. Fewer pieces. Merge marked RDs into larger pieces along the hierarchy (one chair = 7 cubes), so that the hierarchy is carried by fewer, larger pieces; Grogan's keyed chairs (aiXiv 2610.01307) are the template.
  3. The stacking. The c/h aim needs a carrier that is not one lattice (hcp is not); Kamenetsky lists this as his open point 2. Candidate: the HexOct layer tiles (#75) carrying a chair hierarchy in a vertical plane, so that the rows are the layers.
  4. The window. Cut the golden slice's 35 kinds down to finitely many shapes by the window method; finitely many shapes with a non-repeating order is the cut-and-project bridge of aim 1.

Next concrete step: step 1 on the 90- and 118-type sets (running), then exact keying of the smallest set that certifies as shapes.
