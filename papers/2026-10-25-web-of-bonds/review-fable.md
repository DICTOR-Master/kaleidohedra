## Points

1. **MAJOR** — §5 "Real compounds on the loops", 2nd sentence after the first paragraph: *"For every compound, 'in the window' is the same as 'r⁺/r⁻ ≥ 0.732', Pauling's 8-coordination limit [S2, B3]."*
   **Problem:** false as written for the antifluorites in the paper's own Table 3: Li₂O has r⁺/r⁻ = 0.59/1.42 = 0.4155 and Na₂O 0.99/1.42 = 0.697, both < 0.732, and both are "in". The study's check B3 (`compounds.py` l.177–181) is explicitly restricted to "CsCl-type and fluorite" and tests cation/anion only for those; for antifluorite the record's `pauling` rule is the CN 4 window [0.225, 0.414), which Na₂O and K₂O fail while being "in". The equivalence that is true is: in window ⇔ r(8-coordinated ion)/r(4-coordinated ion) ≥ √3 − 1, i.e. r_oct-site/r_tet-site ≥ 0.732, which is r⁺/r⁻ for fluorite and r⁻/r⁺ for antifluorite (the lower Delian end 0.2247 is never active for these twelve compounds, all ρ ≥ 0.4155).
   **Fix:** "For every caesium chloride type and fluorite compound, 'in the window' is the same as r⁺/r⁻ ≥ 0.732, Pauling's 8-coordination limit; for the antifluorites the same inequality holds with the ion on the 8-coordinated site in the numerator, r⁻/r⁺ ≥ 0.732 [S2, B3]." (Also make B3's label in the study say which compounds it covers, or extend it.)

2. **MAJOR** — Corollary 4 (the weight line) and §8 Known: *"the weights that give one and the same dual subdivision form the chambers of the secondary fan [5, 6] … The golden segment stays inside one chamber except at its ends; the Delian segment crosses exactly one wall, at t = 1/3."*
   **Problem:** in GKZ / De Loera–Rambau–Santos the cones of the secondary fan correspond to regular subdivisions; the *chambers* (full-dimensional cones) correspond to regular *triangulations* only. The golden dual for 0 < x < ∞ is a triangulation (disphenoids), so "one chamber" is right. The Delian duals are never triangulations: octahedra (6 O) for t < 1/3 and square pyramids (1 O + 4 Q) for t > 1/3 (Corollary 3), because the tied weights keep six oct sites co-power-spherical. So the Delian segment lies in lower-dimensional cones on both sides of 1/3, not in chambers, and it does not "cross a wall" between two chambers; it passes from one non-maximal cone through a smaller one (the rhombic-dodecahedron subdivision) into another. The underlying fact (one change of subdivision, at 1/3) is correct and verified.
   **Fix:** "the weights that give one and the same dual subdivision form the cones of the secondary fan [5, 6], its chambers being the triangulations; … The golden segment stays inside one chamber except at its ends; the Delian segment lies in two non-maximal cones (its dual cells are not simplices, because the tied weights keep six oct sites co-spherical) that meet exactly once, at t = 1/3, in the cone of the rhombic dodecahedra." Same wording in §8 ("change only at the walls" is fine there).

3. **MINOR** — §5 Agreements and §8 Known, Wang et al. [25]: *"built the caesium chloride type binary hard-sphere crystal at size ratio √3 − 1 and found both radical polyhedra 14-faced (6 squares + 8 hexagons)"*.
   **Evidence (AIP page, read this session):** Wang et al. build four binary crystals they call FCC, HCP, BCC and SC: large spheres (diameter 1) on each lattice with a small sphere filling each pore. Their "SC" crystal is the caesium chloride *arrangement* (simple cubic large spheres, a small sphere in every cubic pore), with coordination 8 (small) / 14 (large) and "the face number of RPs, either for small spheres or large spheres, are all 14 … 6 quadrangle planes and 8 hexagon planes". The phrase "caesium chloride type" and the explicit value √3 − 1 are the paper's reading, not theirs (the pore-filler diameter in a touching simple cubic packing is √3 − 1; the extracted text garbles the expression). The result and the identification with x = √3 + 1 are right.
   **Fix:** "Wang et al. [25] built a binary hard-sphere crystal of large spheres on a simple cubic lattice with a small sphere in every cubic pore (their 'SC' crystal: the caesium chloride arrangement at the pore-filling size ratio √3 − 1) and found …".

4. **MINOR** — §2 Golden loop, §5, Corollary 6, Figures 1, 2, 4, Appendix B: **τ is never defined** in this paper. It is used for the stop x = τ and in "x = τ ≈ 1.618", while φ is used everywhere else for the same number. Paper #48 wrote "Let φ = (1 + √5)/2 = τ". A reader of #52 alone cannot know τ = φ.
   **Fix:** in §2, first use: "at x = τ (= φ, the name of the stop in paper #48 and the app) they are the Icosaxes and the Unborn Axes".

5. **MINOR** — Corollary 3, table caption: *"volumes in units of … the unit cube of paper #51's tetrahedron pieces for the Delian loop"*.    **Fix:** "volumes in units of (L/2)³ for the golden loop and, for the Delian loop, of the cube that the tetrahedron piece becomes at t = 1 (edge 1/√2 at octet edge 1)".

6. **MINOR** — §3 "The classical members": *"Kelvin's cells (x = 1), the rhombic dodecahedra (t = 0) and the cubes (t = 1) are ordinary Voronoi diagrams."* At t = 0 and t = 1 the weight difference is ∓3/8, not 0; the tilings are the ordinary Voronoi diagrams *of one kind of site alone* (O at t = 0, Q at t = 1), the other kind having shrunk to points (as Main theorem (i) says).
   **Fix:** "… are ordinary Voronoi diagrams (of the surviving kind of site alone at t = 0 and t = 1)."
