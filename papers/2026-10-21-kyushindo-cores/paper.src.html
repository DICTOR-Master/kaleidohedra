The ideas (shells of towers in a Fibonacci rhythm round the CLEO sphere; their polar; the OG and OTT cut into rays from the centre; turning the OG's rays out with the golden step) and the names Cleo Towers, Polar Cleo Towers, Hex Aktis, Deltoid Aktis, OG, OTT, Oktaktis, Triaktis, Tetraktis, the OG turned out and the Kyushindo series are DICTO's; computation, exact proofs and drafting with Claude (Anthropic). The rule of each step is that of Universal PiTet [1], the three-dimensional antipedal construction; its reverse is Smarandache's face pedal polyhedron [2, 9].
Abstract. DICTO's Kyushindo series builds outward from an exact core, shell by shell, with towers. One beat is one step of the Universal PiTet rule [1], T(S) = {x : v·x ≤ v·v for every corner v of S}; shells fuse 1, 1, 2, 3 and 5 beats, and each shell is cut into towers, one over each face of the solid inside it (the cone from the centre over that face, cut off by the solid outside). We prove in exact arithmetic in ℚ(√5): the DICTO Cleo Towers, round the CLEO sphere (the icosidodecahedron), are five shells of 32, 30, 32, 32 and 60 towers, each kind of tower one shape and each shell filled exactly. Reciprocation about the sphere of radius² 15 − 5√5 takes beat k to beat 5 − k, and turns them into the DICTO Polar Cleo Towers: shells of 5, 3, 2, 1 and 1 beats from the centre, with 30, 32, 30, 60 and 32 towers. The DICTO Ogdoecontahedron (OG) cut from its centre gives the OG core of 80 Aktis: 20 DICTO Hex Aktis and 60 DICTO Deltoid Aktis, each set one shape, taking (65 − 3√5)/110 and (45 + 3√5)/110 of the OG. The DICTO OTT gives a core of 38 OTT rays: 8 DICTO Oktaktis, each exactly a Cleo-Icos and lying in a hexagon plane of the OG, 24 DICTO Triaktis and 6 DICTO Tetraktis. DICTO's OG turned out flips the 80 Aktis outward at 1/φ of their height, tips at φ times the foot points of the OG's faces: then beat k is exactly φk times the k-th solid of the OG's downward family [2], every solid holds the one before, and the shells of 1, 1 and 2 beats hold 80, 132 and 140 towers of 2, 3 and 3 shapes, each shell filled exactly. The boundary solids change their surface pattern from shell to shell; on the TQH the trapezoids pair into 30 ribbons on the two-fold axes, which DICTO sees as a possible new shell structure. The shells of 3 and 5 beats of the turned-out sequence are still being computed. All claims are exact except where marked.

1. Beats, shells and towers

The beat. As in Universal PiTet [1]: for a convex solid S centred at the origin, T(S) = {x : v·x ≤ v·v for every corner v of S}. Each face of T(S) passes through a corner of S at right angles to the line from the centre. A corner v of S stays on the surface of T(S) exactly when w·v ≤ w·w for every corner w of S (otherwise the face of w cuts it off). This is not true of every convex solid, but it holds, exactly, for every station stepped from in this paper: the ID, RT, GTI and FPD, and so for all their scaled copies, since the condition does not change with scale (it holds too for the OG, TPH, TQH and TQHD, but not for the TQP, where 60 of the 140 corners would be cut off). In the plane this is the classical antipedal construction about a point; T is its three-dimensional form [1, 10], and the reverse step P, whose corners are the foot points of S's face planes (the points of the planes nearest the centre), is Smarandache's face pedal polyhedron [9], run from the OG in [2]. DICTO calls one step a beat.

The rhythm. DICTO's idea: shells of towers as tall as possible, in a Fibonacci rhythm. Shell n fuses Fn beats, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, so the shells end at beats 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 7 | 12, and the beats inside a shell are passed through, not drawn.

The towers. A shell between an inner solid A and an outer solid B ⊃ A is cut into one tower per face of A: the cone from the centre over that face, cut off by B, without the pyramid from the centre to the face (these are the shell pieces of [1]). A tower is one shape per symmetry orbit of faces; we prove for every shell below that the towers on each kind of face form one orbit of the 60 exact rotations of the icosahedral group (so they are congruent), have one exact volume, and add up to the volume of the shell with remainder exactly 0; we also check that the inner and the outer solid of every shell are each fixed by all 60 rotations. Corners are proved as in [1]: floating point only guesses which faces meet at a corner; every corner then solves its faces exactly and lies inside every face, every face holds at least three corners, and every edge lies in exactly two faces with V − E + F = 2.

2. The DICTO Cleo Towers (exact)

The core is DICTO's CLEO sphere [7, §5.5]: the icosidodecahedron (ID), cut from its centre into 20 CLEOs [6] on its triangles and 12 pentagonal pockets. Here it has circumradius φ (edge 1), the frame of [1]. Its beats are the stations of Universal PiTet: S0 = ID, S1 = RT (rhombic triacontahedron), S2 = DICTO GTI (Golden Truncated Icosahedron), S3 = DICTO FPD (Flat Pentakis Dodecahedron), and S4 = c·ID with c = 10/φ⁴ = 35 − 15√5 ≈ 1.459: one turn of four beats returns the ID exactly c times larger (checked again here), so Sk+4 = c·Sk for every k [1].

3. The DICTO Polar Cleo Towers (exact)

DICTO asked what the polar of the Fibonacci shells would give. Take the sphere through the GTI's corners, radius² ρ² = 15 − 5√5 = c·φ²: 10/φ⁴ times the CLEO sphere's radius². Polar reciprocation about it takes every beat to another beat: polar(Sk) = S5−k, checked exactly for k = 0, …, 5 (ID ↔ c·RT, RT ↔ c·ID, GTI ↔ FPD; this is the self-polarity of [1, Proposition (iv)]); since polar(c·S) = polar(S)/c, it holds for every k. The boundaries 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 7 | 12 of the Cleo Towers go to 5 | 4 | 3 | 1 | −2 | −7: read from the centre, shells of 5, 3, 2, 1 and 1 beats, the most beats deepest. The core is S−7 = RT/c², which cuts from its centre into 30 rhombic rays.

4. The OG core of 80 Aktis (exact)

DICTO's idea: cut the DICTO Ogdoecontahedron (OG) [3], with its 20 regular hexagons and 60 kites, into its 80 face pyramids, every tip at the centre, as the CLEO sphere is cut into CLEOs and pockets. DICTO named them Aktis (Greek, ray): 20 DICTO Hex Aktis on the hexagons and 60 DICTO Deltoid Aktis on the kites. Values are in the frame of [4], where the OG has 102 corners and volume (−1000 + 1240√5)/1539 ≈ 1.151868.

  • The 80 Aktis fill the OG exactly (volumes add up with remainder 0; neighbouring Aktis meet only in faces, as pyramids over different faces of a convex solid from an inside point must; the first 60 of the 180 neighbouring pairs were also intersected exactly).
  • The 20 Hex Aktis are one shape, and the 60 Deltoid Aktis are one shape (corner matching: tip to tip, base corners in order, all distances equal).
  • Hex Aktis: volume (−2 + 2√5)/81 ≈ 0.030520, height √((6 + 2√5)/27) ≈ 0.622782; the 20 take (65 − 3√5)/110 ≈ 52.99% of the OG.
  • Deltoid Aktis: volume (−4 + 8√5)/1539 ≈ 0.009024, height √((42 + 2√5)/109) ≈ 0.652955; the 60 take (45 + 3√5)/110 ≈ 47.01%.

The kites lie farther out than the hexagons, so the Deltoid Aktis are the taller rays.

5. The OTT core of 38 rays (exact)

The DICTO OTT (Octahedrally Truncated Triacontahedron) [5] is the rhombic triacontahedron cut by its octahedron at all 8 key sites: 24 corners, 38 faces (8 equilateral triangles in the octahedron's faces, 24 half-rhombi and 6 whole rhombi), volume (−608 + 320√5)/81 ≈ 1.327676, pyritohedral symmetry. DICTO's idea: cut it, like the OG, into its face pyramids from the centre, the OTT rays, which DICTO named Oktaktis (on the octahedron cuts), Triaktis (on the half-rhombi) and Tetraktis (four-sided, on the whole rhombi). The Oktaktis are Cleo-Icos. DICTO noticed that the Oktaktis looks like a Cleo-Icos; we prove it exactly: each Oktaktis is exactly similar to the Cleo-Icos [7, §5.6], the pyramid from an icosahedron's centre to one face: height²/edge² = φ⁴/12 and side²/edge² = (φ² + 1)/4, as for the icosahedron. They lie in the OG's hexagon planes. In the frame of [4], where both solids are given, each of the OTT's 8 octahedral triangles lies exactly in one of the 20 hexagon planes of the OG; so an Oktaktis has exactly the height of a Hex Aktis, √((6 + 2√5)/27). Since the OG lies inside the OTT [5], each of those 8 hexagons of the OG lies in an octahedral triangle of the OTT; more exactly, the hexagon's circumradius equals the triangle's inradius (squared, (6 − 2√5)/27), and three corners of the hexagon are exactly the midpoints of the triangle's edges, so each hexagon touches its triangle at those three points.

6. The OG turned out: the golden step (exact)

The idea is DICTO's: turn the inside towers out and start a new sequence. Each of the 80 Aktis is flipped outward over its own face, and DICTO chose the golden step: the Aktis shortened to 1/φ of its height before it is flipped, so its tip lies at φ times the foot point of its face plane (the point of the plane nearest the centre). The next solid is the hull of the 80 tips; on it the same is done again, one beat at a time.

What it builds. The hull of the tips of a solid S is φ·P(S), where P is the reverse step of [2], and P commutes with scaling. Hence beat k is φk times the k-th solid of the OG's downward family [2]: the OG, φ·TPH, φ²·TQH, φ³·TQP, φ⁴·TQHD (DICTO's Triangle-Pentagon Hecatontatriacontadihedron, Triangle-Quadrilateral-Hexagon Polyhedron, Triangle-Quadrilateral-Pentagon Polyhedron and Triangle-Quadrilateral-Hexagon Diacosihedron). This is checked exactly: at each beat the corners are exactly the flipped tips, and each solid is fixed by the 60 rotations of the icosahedron. In the frame of [2] (ID circumradius φ):

7. The surface patterns and the lines (exact)

DICTO observed that every shell so far has a different pattern on its surface, and that "the lines defining the shells denote the structures". We read each boundary solid two ways, both exactly: by its faces (the orbits of the 60 rotations, and how faces of one kind join edge to edge into groups) and by its lines (the edge network). The boundaries are few solids: the Cleo Towers end at ID | RT | GTI | ID | FPD | ID (each 10/φ⁴ to a power larger), the Polar Cleo Towers at RT | GTI | RT | FPD | ID | RT, and the OG turned out at OG | TPH | TQH | TQHD (each φ to a power larger; the TQP is passed through inside shell 3). Table 5. The boundary solids. "Apart": no two faces of that kind share an edge. A group is "on" an axis when a rotation about that axis maps it to itself. A direction is "along" an axis when its edges are parallel to it. A central belt is a closed circuit of edges in one plane through the centre.

Faces. No two boundaries of one sequence in a row have the same faces. In the OG's sequence each boundary solid has its own grouping: kites in fives (OG), triangle pairs on the two-fold axes (TPH), trapezoid ribbons on the two-fold axes (TQH), and trapezoid rings of five on the five-fold axes (TQHD), which repeat the rings of the TQP passed through inside shell 3.

Lines. No edge runs on straight through a corner, as convexity requires (a corner never lies between two points of the solid). The lines that line up across the surface are the parallel families. On every solid but the RT and the OG, 15 families point along the two-fold axes (on the RT, a zonohedron, the 6 families point along the five-fold axes); the OG alone has no edge along any axis. Only the ID has central belts: its six decagonal equators, as is classical.

The ribbons (DICTO's observation). On the TQH of the OG turned out (beat 2, φ²·TQH) the 60 four-sided faces are congruent isosceles trapezoids, with parallel sides of squared length (38367 + 17019√5)/13448 and (6615 + 2925√5)/3362 (≈ 2.3839 and 1.9781), legs ≈ 0.4343, corner angles ≈ 62.15° and 117.85° and height ≈ 0.3840. They pair up across their shorter parallel sides: 30 shared edges, every trapezoid in exactly one pair, each pair centred on a two-fold axis (a half-turn about it swaps the two), two pairs per axis, one at each end. So each ribbon is drawn by three parallel lines, its two outer sides and the seam, bent ≈ 23.28° across the seam (float). The 15 edge families of the TQH that point along a two-fold axis are exactly these lines: 6 per family, the two ribbons at the two ends of one two-fold axis, all running along a second two-fold axis. Shell 3 stands a long blade-like tower on each trapezoid, so each ribbon carries two blades (Figure 7). In the TQP and the TQHD the trapezoids join along their legs instead, into rings of five on the five-fold axes (each trapezoid meeting two others); of the nine solids only the TQH has ribbons. DICTO sees in the ribbons a possible new shell structure; we record the observation and claim nothing beyond the checks above.

8. Prior art and what is claimed

  • Known and credited. The step T is the three-dimensional antipedal construction about the centre, and its reverse P is Smarandache's face pedal polyhedron [9, 10] (as recorded in [1, 2]); reciprocation about a sphere is classical [17]. Solids nested round one centre go back to Kepler's Mysterium cosmographicum [11]; icosahedral shells round a centre are found in Mackay's packing of equal spheres [12], in the clusters of icosahedral quasicrystals [13] and in the nested icosahedral point sets of viruses and carbon onions [14, 15]; Bergman's continuous spherical truss surrounds a core of twenty tetrahedral pyramids with endless layers [16]; Kramer filled space round a centre with cells that recur τ³ larger [18]. Cutting a solid into pyramids from its centre is as old as Hypsicles' "Book XIV" of Euclid, whose icosahedron pyramids are the Cleo-Icos [7, 19]; the Cleo-Icos is also the unit tetrahedron of Mackay's icosahedron [7, 12]. The Fibonacci numbers and the golden ratio are classical.
  • Already published by DICTO. The UP rule, its four stations and its first shells' pieces, and the chain's self-polarity [1]; the OG [3] and its downward family [2]; the OTT [5]; CLEO [6]; the CLEO sphere and the Cleo-Icos [7] (all listed in [8]). Shells 1 and 2 of the Cleo Towers and shell 4 of the Polar Cleo Towers are shells of [1].
  • Claimed here: the Fibonacci fusing of beats into tower shells and the exact towers of the fused shells (Cleo Towers, shells 3–5); the Polar Cleo Towers as exact tower shells, with the Fibonacci boundaries carried by the polarity of [1]; the OG core of 80 Aktis and the OTT core of 38 OTT rays as exact sets of one shape per kind, with their shares; the Oktaktis as Cleo-Icos lying in the OG's hexagon planes; and the OG turned out with the golden step: beat k = φk times the k-th downward solid, the nesting at every beat built, and the exact towers of its first three shells; the surface patterns and edge networks of the boundary solids (Table 5), including the TQH's 30 ribbons.

In the sources checked (the full text of the 77 library sources with a text layer listed in the appendix, searched on 21 October 2026; the library's link-only entries were not searched) we did not find tower shells in Fibonacci beats, the Polar Cleo Towers, the Aktis or ray cores of the OG and the OTT, the OG turned out, or the ribbons of the TQH.

Outlook (DICTO's idea, untested). DICTO notes that these shells may mirror material structures, as geodesic forms did before C60 [24] and carbon nanotubes [25] were found. Nested icosahedral shells do occur in nature: in the clusters of quasicrystals and their approximants (Mackay [12], Bergman [22], Tsai [13]), in metal and boron nanoclusters, and in virus capsids (Caspar and Klug [23]; see also [26]). Whether the shells of this paper correspond to any real structure has not been tested; a checkable next step is to compare their corner counts and radius ratios with published sequences of cluster shells.

The series name

Kyushindo is the martial art of Kenshiro Abbe [20, 21]. DICTO gives its three principles as Change 万物流転, Rhythm 律動 and Harmony 調和, and his brief for this series was "rhythm, harmony and change in the construction of towers and cores". Here rhythm is the Fibonacci beats, harmony the exact golden fits, and change that each shell's form differs from the last (§7).
