The Chain of Karma

Endless golden growth of the regular icosahedron from whole regular icosahedra, and a self-building kit for its gap pieces

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com

The Lotus seed, the Chain of Karma, the FUJI tip + mirror rule and the names Lotus seed, AXE, FUJI and CLEO are DICTO's. Design study and checking with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. A regular icosahedron enlarged by φ² can be filled exactly by twelve regular icosahedra of edge 1, one of edge 1/φ at its centre, and three gap pieces: 60 AXE, 20 FUJI and 20 CLEO (the Lotus seed). Every icosahedron inside is again a regular icosahedron, so each can be replaced by a Lotus seed of its own size, and the rule repeats without end, upward and downward: the Chain of Karma. Steps 1–3 were built and checked (113, 1,569 and 20,497 pieces, volume exact); after n steps there are 13ⁿ icosahedra in n + 1 sizes, and their share of the volume falls by (12 + φ⁻³)/φ⁶ ≈ 0.682 per step. Dehn invariants show that the gap pieces cannot be rebuilt from copies of the kit at a single size, but that with mixed φ-sizes no such obstruction remains. Two gap pieces are known tiles (AXE and CLEO are the Mosseri–Sadoc tetrahedra t6 and t4), but those tetrahedra do not grow by φ on their own, because an equilateral face grown by φ cannot be tiled by their smaller faces. FUJI, a hexagonal pyramid whose apex is a cube corner, supplies the missing face: its base hexagon plus three equilateral triangles makes an equilateral triangle φ or φ² times larger, which is how every face of the Lotus seed is covered. For FUJI itself we give a growth rule: φ·FUJI = FUJI + a FUJI of size 1/φ turned over + a side ring, where the side ring has exactly three FUJI volumes and Dehn invariant zero. Whether the whole kit rebuilds itself (search in progress) would give a hierarchical icosahedral space-filling that keeps whole regular icosahedra at every scale. The FUJI rule holds for any pyramid, and with it the dodecahedron grows the same way: a dodecahedron is a small icosahedron in twelve pentagonal pyramids with 30 AXE and 20 CLEO, and each growth cycle ends as a regular dodecahedron round a Chain-grown icosahedron; the dodecahedral counterpart with whole dodecahedra is due to Al Raisi, Koca, Koca and Koc (2026), who note that icosahedra cannot be done with their tiles. We relate it to the periodic octet frame of the icosahedral octet through pyritohedral symmetry, as a route from periodic approximants to the aperiodic limit.
