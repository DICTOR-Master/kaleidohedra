The Chain of Karma

The icosahedron and the dodecahedron as endless shells of each other

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com
DRAFT 2026-10-11 · not for release · Licence CC BY 4.0 (on release) · Data, renders and checks to be included with the record.
The Lotus seed, the Chain of Karma, the FUJI tip + mirror rule and the names Lotus seed, AXE, FUJI and CLEO are DICTO's. Design study and checking with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. We show that the regular icosahedron and the regular dodecahedron can each be filled exactly as a shell around the other, and that the two shells alternate without end. A dodecahedron of edge 1 is an icosahedron of edge 1/φ inside a shell of 12 pentagonal pyramids, 30 AXE and 20 CLEO; an icosahedron of edge φ² (the Lotus seed) is that dodecahedron inside a shell of 12 J11 (each an icosahedron with one pyramid cap removed), 30 AXE and 20 FUJI. Thus the nesting I(1/φ) ⊂ D(1) ⊂ I(φ²) ⊂ D(φ³) ⊂ … grows by φ³ per cycle, and each shell has one piece for every vertex, edge and face of the dual pair (12, 30, 20): the pyramids point at the small icosahedron's corners, the dodecahedron's faces cut each outer icosahedron into a pyramid and a J11, AXE lines both shells, CLEO the inner and FUJI the outer. The pieces are exact, at edge 1: AXE is a tetrahedron with five edges 1 and one 1/φ (two equilateral faces, two golden triangles 1, 1, 1/φ); CLEO is a triangular pyramid on an equilateral base of side 1/φ with side edges 1; FUJI is a pyramid of height 1/√3 on a flat-ended (truncated) triangle with sides 1 and 1/φ alternating and all angles 120°, with side edges 1, three equilateral and three golden faces, and a cube corner at its apex; the pentagonal pyramid has all edges 1. Every dihedral angle of the kit is, modulo 180°, an integer combination of two angles, α = arccos(√5/3) = 41.81° and δ = arctan(3 − √5) = 37.38°: the icosahedron's own is 180° − α = 138.19°, and FUJI's faces meet its base at α on the long sides and δ on the short. Because every icosahedron inside is again whole, each can be replaced by its own Lotus seed: the Chain of Karma, built and checked to 20,497 pieces, in which every stage closes as an exact icosahedron or dodecahedron. The gap pieces cannot be rebuilt from copies of themselves at one size (Dehn invariant), and AXE and CLEO, which are Mosseri–Sadoc tetrahedra, cannot be rebuilt from that tile set alone. Instead, we give a growth rule for every pyramid, and so for AXE, CLEO, FUJI and the pentagonal pyramid: φ·P = P + a copy of size 1/φ turned over + a side ring with three times P's volume and Dehn invariant zero. By Sydler's theorem the ring is always equivalent to a cube, so the kit regenerates itself up to a periodic remainder; whether the ring can be cut into kit pieces remains open. A second exact cycle passes through the icosidodecahedron, the fullerene cage and the rhombic triacontahedron (icosahedron φ² ⊃ triacontahedron ⊃ cage ⊃ dodecahedron ⊃ icosidodecahedron ⊃ icosahedron 1/φ, each layer filled by a few piece types); it shares the dodecahedron with the Lotus seed, so the halves of the two structures can be exchanged at every scale. The nesting of the dual pair at golden ratios goes back to Pacioli and Kepler and appears at every φ-power in Gray (2002); a different dodecahedral growth, with whole dodecahedra inside, is due to Al Raisi, Koca, Koca and Koc (2026). The exact shells and their endless alternation are new.

6. A second cycle: the Spheres of Dharma

A second exact, endlessly repeating cycle passes through the icosidodecahedron, the fullerene cage and the rhombic triacontahedron: icosahedron φ² ⊃ triacontahedron ⊃ cage ⊃ dodecahedron ⊃ icosidodecahedron ⊃ icosahedron 1/φ. It shares the dodecahedron of edge 1 and both icosahedra with the Lotus seed, and each half of either structure fills its side of that dodecahedron exactly, so the two can be exchanged half by half at every scale. It is described in its own paper (DICTO, Spheres of Dharma, #23).

7. The 600-cell, flattened

DICTO's route into four dimensions began with the DICTO Dodeca-13 (#13): a dodecahedron with twelve more on its faces, the 120-cell's first shell flattened, its 10.3° gaps being the flattening. The Lotus seed is the same for the 600-cell, the 120-cell's dual, as the icosahedron is the dodecahedron's. Seen down one vertex, the 600-cell (circumradius 1, edge 1/φ) has shells of 1, 12 (icosahedron, radius 0.587785), 20 (dodecahedron), 12 (icosahedron), 30 (icosidodecahedron), …, alternating icosahedron and dodecahedron as the Chain's shells do. Two matches are exact (cell600_match.py): the Lotus seed's small icosahedron is the 600-cell's first shell (edge 1/φ, circumradius 0.587785), and the centres of the Lotus seed's 13 icosahedra are the 600-cell's centre vertex and its 12 neighbours, scaled by φ² (radius 1.538842, spacing φ). The 600-cell is the icosahedral "12 around 1" packing that closes only in curved four-dimensional space; in flat space twelve around one leaves gaps (geometrical frustration, Sadoc and Mosseri). The Lotus seed reads as a flattened piece of the 600-cell, with AXE, FUJI and CLEO as exactly what flat space needs where curved space closes by itself, and the Chain of Karma repeats that flattening at every scale. The match is in projection; we do not claim the Lotus seed is a projection of the 600-cell.

The fullerene chain

DICTO (2026-10-11): the Lotus seed shows a fullerene architecture. Checked (fullerene_surface.py): its surface is 60 triangles of edge 1, grouped round the 12 corners as pentagonal caps, and 20 flat-ended triangles (DICTO's term: triangles with their corners cut flat, sides 1 and 1/φ, the FUJI bases) in the places of the hexagons, so the Lotus seed has the face pattern of C60, the buckyball (Kroto et al. 1985), with flat-ended triangles for its hexagons: a truncated icosahedron whose cut corners are filled back by whole icosahedra. Chain step 2 keeps the 12 caps, has flat-ended triangles in two sizes (60 small, 20 of sides φ and φ²) and 60 pairs of triangles folded over the edges: fullerenes inside fullerenes, every piece a triangle, whole or flat-ended. Counted as hexagons it has C180's 80, but it is not a Goldberg polyhedron (Goldberg 1937). The nesting recalls carbon onions, nested icosahedral fullerene shells (Ugarte 1992).

9. Self-similar, repeating, not periodic

Scaling is the growth step: the icosahedron and dodecahedron shells repeat by scaling, φ³ per cycle, and the counts follow it exactly (13ⁿ icosahedra after n steps of the Chain, gap pieces in the fixed proportion AXE : FUJI : CLEO = 3 : 1 : 1), as the growth matrix's largest (Perron–Frobenius) eigenvalue and its eigenvector say. Fit is the exact fit: each shell fills the space round the other, with volumes and Dehn invariants balancing. Non-periodicity is that no sliding of the whole lands it on itself. The three are linked: because the scale factor is irrational, a sliding repeat would force repeats at φ³, φ⁶, … times every period, which no single lattice of periods allows; and the same scaling makes every local arrangement recur.

Status. Proved: the shells, the exact fits and the counts. The Chain as built has a centre, so it is trivially not periodic. For a tiling of all space by a finite kit at bounded sizes, two further properties are expected once the kit closes (the FUJI and pyramid side rings, section 4): non-periodicity, via unique composition (Solomyak 1998), and repetitivity (every finite patch recurring within a bounded distance of every point).

10. Relation to known work, and status

Nesting of the icosahedron and dodecahedron. Golden nestings of the two go back to Pacioli and Kepler. Gray (2002) describes icosahedra at every φ-power, each containing a central icosahedron at φ⁻³ and twelve at φ⁻² (the arrangement of the Lotus seed's icosahedra), and dodecahedra that "scale pack" with them; the gaps are not filled. The Sierpinski icosahedron keeps the twelve corner icosahedra at 1/φ² and leaves the gaps empty. Al Raisi, Koca, Koca and Koc (2026) grow the dodecahedron by τⁿ with whole dodecahedra inside, using modified Mosseri–Sadoc tiles, and note that icosahedra cannot be treated this way with those tiles.

Tiles. AXE and CLEO are the Mosseri–Sadoc tetrahedra t6 and t4 at scale 1/φ (Mosseri and Sadoc 1982; Kramer 1982); the six tetrahedra admit no stone inflation (Papadopolos and Ogievetsky 1999, 2000), and Koca et al. (2020) state that they do not inflate the icosahedron past edge τ. Danzer's ABCK tetrahedra (1989), with Dehn invariant zero, and Sydler's theorem (1965) frame the remainder of the pyramid rule.

Nested icosahedral shells in matter. Concentric icosahedral shells are central to the structure of real materials and viruses, as point arrays: Mackay's icosahedral sphere packing (1962) and Frank's icosahedral order in liquids (1952); the Tsai-type cluster of icosahedral quasicrystals and their approximants, concentric dodecahedron, icosahedron and icosidodecahedron shells inside a rhombic triacontahedral envelope (Takakura et al. 2007; Deguchi et al. 2015; Ishimasa 2019); and icosahedral viruses (Caspar and Klug 1962), whose capsids and genomes follow nested icosahedral point arrays at fixed radial ratios, obtained from affine extensions of the icosahedral group or by projection from a six-dimensional lattice (Keef and Twarock 2008, 2009; Twarock, Valiunas and Zappa 2015), the same framework applying to nested fullerene cages (Dechant et al. 2014). The Chain of Karma differs in kind: its shells are solid and filled exactly, with whole regular icosahedra among the pieces, and the icosahedron and dodecahedron alternate as shells of each other without end.

Status. In the sources above and those in the study folder we found no description of this solid, exact, alternating filling. The search is not exhaustive; the claim of novelty is limited to it.

Remarks

The following are DICTO's own readings of the work, given as remarks; they are not mathematical claims.

On the two shells: "It is like yin and yang: neither one nor the other." Each solid holds the other at its centre, and each is the other's shell.

On the 600-cell: "It does strike me as remarkably similar to a 4D polytope."

On section 9: "The pattern that doesn't repeat is exactly the pattern that is repeating." DICTO relates the Chain to Kyushindo, the philosophy of Kenshiro Abbe, with its three principles 万物流転 banbutsu ruten (all things in constant flux), 律動 ritsudō (rhythmic motion) and 調和 chōwa (harmony): change, rhythm and harmony, each contained in the others. In section 9's terms they correspond to non-periodicity, scaling and exact fit.
