The Chain of Karma

The icosahedron and the dodecahedron as endless shells of each other

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com
DRAFT 2026-10-11 · not for release · Licence CC BY 4.0 (on release) · Data, renders and checks to be included with the record.
The Lotus seed, the Chain of Karma, the FUJI tip + mirror rule and the names Lotus seed, AXE, FUJI and CLEO are DICTO's. Design study and checking with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. We show that the regular icosahedron and the regular dodecahedron can each be filled exactly as a shell around the other, and that the two shells alternate without end. A dodecahedron of edge 1 is an icosahedron of edge 1/φ inside a shell of 12 pentagonal pyramids, 30 AXE and 20 CLEO; an icosahedron of edge φ² (the Lotus seed) is that dodecahedron inside a shell of 12 J11 (each an icosahedron with one pyramid cap removed), 30 AXE and 20 FUJI. So I(1/φ) ⊂ D(1) ⊂ I(φ²) ⊂ D(φ³) ⊂ … grows by φ³ per cycle, and each shell has one piece for every vertex, edge and face of the dual pair (12, 30, 20): the pyramids point at the small icosahedron's corners, the dodecahedron's faces cut each outer icosahedron into a pyramid and a J11, AXE lines both shells, CLEO the inner and FUJI the outer. Because every icosahedron inside is again whole, each can be replaced by its own Lotus seed: the Chain of Karma, built and checked to 20,497 pieces, in which every stage closes as an exact icosahedron or dodecahedron. The gap pieces cannot be rebuilt from copies of themselves at one size (Dehn invariant), and AXE and CLEO, which are Mosseri–Sadoc tetrahedra, cannot be rebuilt from that tile set alone. We give instead a growth rule for every pyramid, and so for AXE, CLEO, FUJI and the pentagonal pyramid: φ·P = P + a copy of size 1/φ turned over + a side ring with three times P's volume and Dehn invariant zero. By Sydler's theorem the ring is always equivalent to a cube, so the whole kit regrows itself up to a periodic remainder; whether the ring can be cut into kit pieces is being searched. The nesting of the dual pair at golden ratios goes back to Pacioli and Kepler and appears at every φ-power in Gray (2002); a different dodecahedral growth, with whole dodecahedra inside, is due to Al Raisi, Koca, Koca and Koc (2026). New here are the exact shells and their endless alternation.

6. The 600-cell, flattened

DICTO's route into four dimensions began with the DICTO Dodeca-13 (#13): a dodecahedron with twelve more on its faces, the 120-cell's first shell flattened, its 10.3° gaps being the flattening. The Lotus seed is the same for the 600-cell, the 120-cell's dual, as the icosahedron is the dodecahedron's. DICTO (2026-10-11): "it does strike me as remarkably similar to a 4D polytope." Seen down one vertex, the 600-cell (circumradius 1, edge 1/φ) has shells of 1, 12 (icosahedron, radius 0.587785), 20 (dodecahedron), 12 (icosahedron), 30 (icosidodecahedron), …, alternating icosahedron and dodecahedron as the Chain's shells do. Two matches are exact (cell600_match.py): the Lotus seed's small icosahedron is the 600-cell's first shell (edge 1/φ, circumradius 0.587785), and the centres of the Lotus seed's 13 icosahedra are the 600-cell's centre vertex and its 12 neighbours, scaled by φ² (radius 1.538842, spacing φ). The 600-cell is the icosahedral "12 around 1" packing that closes only in curved four-dimensional space; in flat space twelve around one leaves gaps (geometrical frustration, Sadoc and Mosseri). The Lotus seed reads as a flattened piece of the 600-cell, with AXE, FUJI and CLEO as exactly what flat space needs where curved space closes by itself, and the Chain of Karma repeats that flattening at every scale. The match is in projection; we do not claim the Lotus seed is a projection of the 600-cell.
