Background for you (not for pasting)

Facts you could put in your own words: a layer is hexagonal (h) when its two neighbours have the same position, cubic (c) otherwise (Jagodzinski); here layer n is h exactly when A003849(n) = 1, so the types follow the Fibonacci word c -> ch, h -> c; each step is +1 or -1 mod 3, a cubic layer repeats the step and a hexagonal one reverses it; first layer A and first step forward (the other choices permute 0, 1, 2); hexagonal layers never touch and their share is 1/phi^2 (#69); the first 16 terms, ABACBCACBABCACBC, are #69's 16-layer approximant.

(Python)
def fib_stacking(N):   # a(0..N-1)
    w = 'c'
    while len(w) < N: w = ''.join('ch' if t == 'c' else 'c' for t in w)   # Fibonacci word
    a = [0, 1]; s = 1
    for k in range(1, N-1):
        if w[k] == 'h': s = -s          # a hexagonal layer reverses the step
        a.append((a[-1] + s) % 3)
    return a[:N]
print(fib_stacking(130))

%C A085002 A fractal sequence.
%C A085002 From _Michel Dekking_, Apr 24 2018: (Start)
%C A085002 Usually an integer sequence is called 'fractal' if it has the self-generating properties of a morphic sequence, i.e., the letter to letter projection of a fixed point of a morphism. Indeed, take the alphabet {1,2,...,8} and the morphism eta defined by
%C A085002     eta:  1->5, 2->7, 3->8, 4->6, 5->53, 6->71, 7->82, 8->64.
%C A085002 Then eta has fixed point
%C A085002     x = (5,3,8,6,4,7,1,6,8,2,5,7,1,6,4,7,5,3,...).
%C A085002 Let pi be the projection morphism
%C A085002   pi(1)=1, pi(2)=0, pi(3)=1, pi(4)=0, pi(5)=1, pi(6)=0, pi(7)=1, pi(8)=0.
%C A085002 Then pi(x) = (a(n)).
%C A085002 To prove this, one may use my paper "Iteration of maps by an automaton".
%C A085002 The two maps are phi_a and phi_b defined by
%C A085002     phi_a(0) = 1, phi_a(1) = 0, phi_b(0) = 0, phi_b(1) = 1.
%C A085002 The substitution is the Fibonacci substitution sigma given by
%C A085002     sigma(a) = b, sigma(b) = ba.
%C A085002 Since the first differences of the lower Wythoff sequence are given by the Fibonacci substitution 1->2, 2-> 21, it follows from replacing 1 with a and 2 with b that (a(n)) is generated by iterating the two maps phi_a and phi_b according to the fixed point babba...of sigma. The two maps phi_a and phi_b are commuting bijections on {a,b}, exactly as in the Example on page 85 of "Iteration of maps by an automaton". It follows as in that example that (a(n)) is generated by the projection of a fixed point of a substitution on an alphabet of 8 letters, and a simple computation as on that page yields the morphism eta. (End)

%F A085002 Proof that this sequence is the parity sequence of the lower Wythoff sequence:
%F A085002 if n*phi/2 = M + e, with 0 < e < 1, then 2*floor(phi*n/2) = 2M, and
%F A085002 floor(phi*n) = floor(2M+2e) = 2M or 2M+1.
%F A085002 So floor(phi*n) - 2*floor(phi*n/2) = 0 if floor(phi*n) is even, and equals 1 if floor(phi*n) is odd. (End)
