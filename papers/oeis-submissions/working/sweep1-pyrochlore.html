Exampleoptional

Ready to paste — choose one

1
a(2) = 6 because a walk of 2 steps returns only by going out along one of the 6 bonds at the site and coming straight back.
a(3) = 12 because a walk of 3 steps returns only by going round a triangle; the site lies on 6 triangles, and each can be walked in 2 directions.
2
For n = 2, there are 6 closed walks, one for each of the 6 neighbors of the site.
For n = 3, there are 12 closed walks: the site lies on 6 triangles, and each triangle can be walked in 2 directions.

A400949: your own approved entry; its %H line shows the link form the editors accepted.
%S A400949 0,0,24,48,480,3432,25704,194256,1509072,11820000,93722376,750276792,
%N A400949 Number of closed non-backtracking walks of length 2n (cyclically non-backtracking, i.e., tailless) starting and ending at a fixed atom of the diamond network.
%C A400949 A walk v_0, v_1, ..., v_{2n} = v_0 along bonds is non-backtracking if v_{i+1} != v_{i-1} for all i, including v_1 != v_{2n-1}. a(n) = (1/|V|) trace(B^(2n)) for the Hashimoto (non-backtracking edge) matrix B of the diamond network; walks of odd length do not exist since the network is bipartite.
%C A400949 a(1) = a(2) = 0 because the shortest cycles of diamond are its six-rings.
%C A400949 The same numbers hold at every atom of lonsdaleite (hexagonal diamond, 2H) and of the tetrahedral network of every close-packed stacking (polytypes 4H, 6H, 9R, ..., periodic or not), although their coordination sequences differ (e.g., A008253, A008264): this is DICTO's Loop Equality, found in #74 for lengths 6 to 34 on five stackings and proved for all lengths in #102 (Theorem 1). The corresponding statement for all closed walks (A002895) is due to Nagle (1966, ice) and Thorpe (1972, periodic polytypes).
%H A400949 DICTO, <a href="https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23730421">Spin Ice and Kagome Magnets on Every Stacking</a>, Zenodo (2026). See Section 3.
%H A400949 DICTO, <a href="https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24037664">Every Site Hears the Same: the Loop Equality Is a Theorem</a>, Zenodo (2026).
%H A400949 DICTO, <a href="https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23731359">HexOct: The Hexagonality Law, with the Window Law, the Rule of Halves & the Swap Rule</a>, Zenodo (2026). See Chapter 13.
%H A400949 M. F. Thorpe, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1665973">Random walks in polytype structures</a>, J. Math. Phys. 13 (1972), 294-299.
%F A400949 Sum_{n>=1} a(n)*x^n/n = -2*log((1-x)*(1+3*x)) + Sum_{k>=1} A002895(k)*(x/(1+3*x)^2)^k/k (Ihara-Bass formula for a 4-regular net).
