DICTO Spheroid-13 RD

Thirteen rhombic dodecahedra as one rounded, keyed block that fills space by translation alone

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com
DRAFT, not released · 13 October 2026 · Licence CC BY 4.0 · Data, scripts, renders, nets and checks: studies/2026-10-13-rd13-block at https://github.com/DICTOR-Master/kaleidohedra.
The question whether the rhombic dodecahedron's thirteen fills space as the DICTO Hexa does, the rounded, keyed quality of the block, the choice of thicknesses and the name DICTO Spheroid-13 RD are DICTO's. Computation and checking with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. A rhombic dodecahedron with its twelve face neighbours, thirteen cells of the face-centred cubic (FCC) honeycomb, fills space by translation alone: of the 183 sublattices of index 13 in the FCC lattice, exactly 8 have the thirteen lattice points as a complete set of representatives, and the 8 are turns of one arrangement, with block translations of length √10 (neighbour spacing √2). Moving every corner of the honeycomb by a displacement that depends only on its class modulo the block lattice keeps the blocks tiling exactly; with every face kept flat, every cell convex with a minimum thickness, and the block's outer surface minimised, the thirteen-cell block becomes nearly round while its outside stays keyed. At minimum thickness 0.15 its roundness 36πV²/S³ is 0.715 (the plain block 0.365, a single rhombic dodecahedron 0.741, the block lattice's own Voronoi cell, the roundest tile with these translations, 0.748). The block has the tiling's full symmetry (order 6: three turns and inversion), so it uses three piece shapes, a centre and two outer kinds of six, each outer kind three pieces and their mirror images. Each block meets 14 others across all 84 of its outer faces; the centres never meet. Four thicknesses are given (0.08 to 0.35), with printable nets at one scale per set. The block tiling is the FCC case of a radius-one perfect code; we did not find it stated, and claim no novelty for it.

1. The thirteen fills space

Take the FCC lattice as the integer points with even coordinate sum; neighbours are √2 apart and each Voronoi cell, a rhombic dodecahedron, has volume 2. A block of 13 cells, a centre and its twelve neighbours, fills space by translations exactly when its 13 lattice points are a complete set of representatives of a sublattice of index 13. Since 13 is prime, every such sublattice is the kernel of x ↦ w·x mod 13 for some w ≠ 0 (in lattice coordinates), 183 in all up to scale; for exactly 8 the thirteen values are distinct. The 8 sublattices are carried into one another by the 24 turns of the cube, so the tiling is one arrangement in eight orientations, with no left- and right-handed forms. One of them has basis (−3, 0, 1), (0, −1, 3), (1, −3, 0) and Gram matrix [[10, 3, −3], [3, 10, 3], [−3, 3, 10]], covolume 26; its shortest vectors are 6 of length √10, then 2 of √12 (along a three-fold axis) and 6 of √14. The cube symmetries that keep it, the point group of the tiling, have order 6: three turns about that axis and the inversion. It is the three-dimensional counterpart of the seven-hexagon flower tiling of the plane.

2. Rounding the block, keeping it keyed

DICTO specified the block's rounded quality: a block that reads as one rounded form, as the DICTO Hexa reads as one block, with its outside still keyed. To realise it, every corner of the honeycomb was given a displacement depending only on its class modulo the block lattice (39 classes, 117 unknowns), so a corner shared by two blocks moves once and the blocks still tile exactly. Constraints: each of the 78 face classes stays flat (its four corners coplanar), and each cell stays convex with every corner at least a set thickness inside the plane of each face not containing it. Objective: the block's outer surface, which at fixed volume 26 is the same as maximising roundness. Sequential quadratic programming from five small random starts reached the same block at every thickness.

3. The pieces

Every piece keeps the rhombic dodecahedron's combinatorics, 14 corners and 12 four-sided faces meeting as in the rhombic dodecahedron, but the faces are now general flat quadrilaterals and the dihedral angles, all 120° in the rhombic dodecahedron, spread. The block has the tiling's full symmetry (order 6), so the twelve outer cells fall into two kinds of six, and in each kind the inversion pairs three pieces with their three mirror images.

4. How the blocks meet

Inside a block 36 faces are shared: the centre's 12 with the twelve, and 24 among the twelve (the cuboctahedron's edges). All 84 outer faces of a block lie against neighbouring blocks, none left open: 54 against the six nearest blocks (√10 away), 12 against the two along the three-fold axis (√12) and 18 against six more (√14). So each block meets 14 others, the same neighbours as the lattice's 14-faced Voronoi cell. All 84 outer faces belong to outer pieces: the centres never meet one another. Each centre is an island wrapped by its own twelve, and the outer pieces of all blocks form one connected keyed network.

5. Making it

Printable A4 nets of the centre and of both outer kinds, with their mirror images, are included for thicknesses 0.15 and 0.35 (ten sheets), each set at one scale so the pieces fit together: 24.0 mm per unit at 0.15 and 27.7 mm at 0.35, giving blocks about 10 cm and 12 cm across. Cut lines are solid, fold lines dashed, glued edges numbered alike, with tabs. In the Polyhedraverse app (DICTO's pieces) the block is a kit built from the centre out with outlines showing each next piece; the Kaleidohedra app shows it whole, pulled apart, and as seven blocks filling space.

6. Checks

The 13 lattice points are distinct modulo each of the 8 sublattices and coincide modulo the other 175. In every rounded block, faces are flat to 10⁻¹² and the thirteen volumes sum to 26 exactly; every corner class moves once, so blocks tile; the block's corner set is invariant under the six symmetries; all 84 outer faces are matched by faces of neighbouring blocks. Five starts agree at each thickness. Every kit placement reproduces its piece to 10⁻⁴. Scripts: round.py, analysis.py, pieces.py, compare.py, render.py and nets.mjs in studies/2026-10-13-rd13-block.

7. Relation to known work

A tiling of a lattice by translates of a point and its nearest neighbours is a radius-one perfect code. In the cube lattice such codes exist in every dimension (Golomb and Welch 1970; product constructions in Etzion 2011; Campello et al. 2015 on the lp metrics), while radius two fails in three dimensions (Gravier, Mollard and Payan 1998). We did not find the FCC case, the 13-cell block tiling, stated; it is simple enough that it may be known, and we claim no novelty for it. Twelve equal spheres round one is the kissing configuration of the FCC packing (Schütte and van der Waerden 1952 for the bound). We found no description of rounding a space-filling cluster with flat faces while keeping it keyed, of the DICTO Spheroid-13 RD, or of its pieces. The search is not exhaustive.
