The Spiked Truncated Icosahedron

A doubling cycle and a golden cycle grown from the truncated icosahedron's own pyramids

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · contact@dictospheres.com · https://dictospheres.com
Paper #78 · Version 1 · DRAFT 30 October 2026 · DICTO DOI: (on publication) · Licence CC BY-SA 4.0
The ideas and names are DICTO's. Computation and proof with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. The truncated icosahedron (TI) is the midpoint cell of DICTO's path between the White and the Red Queen (#76) and a station of his golden cycles. DICTO proposed filling its core with pentagonal and hexagonal pyramids, and then starting a new golden cycle from TI with those pyramids turned outwards, a spiked core. Cut from its centre, TI is exactly 12 pentagonal and 20 hexagonal pyramids; the hexagonal one carries on every base edge half the icosahedron's dihedral angle, the angle of the Cleo-Icos, because the hexagons of TI lie in the face planes of an icosahedron three times its edge. Turned outwards, the pyramids give the spiked TI. Main theorem. Put the tip of each spike at s times the centre of its face. For s = 2 (each spike the mirror image of the pyramid inside) and for s = φ (each spike 1/φ of its height), TI, the spiked TI, the hull of the 32 tips and TI s times larger form one turn of an endless cycle, each layer filled exactly by tetrahedra of three kinds over the corners and the two kinds of edge: for s = 2 a doubling cycle, the spiked TI twice the volume of TI as DICTO's Cleo star is twice the icosahedron's; for s = φ a golden cycle, the spiked TI φ times TI and one turn φ³ in volume. The hull of the tips is a pentakis dodecahedron, not the Catalan one. The spike tips touch none of the twelve stations of DICTO's golden cycle (#54) at any golden scale, so the two cycles are new and separate. All volumes are exact; 333 pieces per turn are free of overlap.

1. Introduction

A truncated icosahedron can be cut from its centre into pyramids, one on every face, as Aristotle's pyramids cut the Platonic solids. Turn the pyramids outwards and it grows spikes. Wrap the spikes and the solid comes back, larger.

The truncated icosahedron appears twice in DICTO's work. In the Spheres of Dharma (#23) and in the twelve-station golden cycle (#54) it is a station between the rectified truncated icosahedron and the Hecatodihedron; in the path between the queens (#76) its four-dimensional counterpart, the bitruncated 600-cell, is built from 120 truncated icosahedra and 600 truncated tetrahedra. Paper #77 showed that, face to face, the five Platonic face pyramids keep to their solids. DICTO proposed the same cut for TI, and then a new golden cycle that begins, like his Cleo star (#35), with the pyramids turned outwards. This paper carries out both.

2. The truncated icosahedron from its centre

With edge 1, the truncated icosahedron TI (corners: the even permutations of (0, ±1, ±3φ), (±1, ±(2 + φ), ±2φ), (±φ, ±2, ±(2φ + 1)), halved) has volume (125 + 43√5)/4. Cut from its centre it falls into the pyramids of Table 1, exactly.

Proposition 1. The hexagonal pyramid of TI is the Cleo-Icos of edge 3 with its three corners cut off; the truncated tetrahedron's hexagonal pyramid is the tetrahedron's face pyramid of edge 3 with its corners cut off. Proof. Truncating a solid keeps the planes of its faces: the hexagons of TI lie in the face planes of the icosahedron of edge 3, and the truncated tetrahedron's in those of the tetrahedron of edge 3. A face pyramid's angle along an edge of its base depends only on the face plane and the edge, so the angles are those of the Cleo-Icos and of the tetrahedron's pyramid (#44, #77). ∎

Base to base, the hexagonal pyramid of TI on the truncated tetrahedron's makes a hexagonal bipyramid of height (√6 + √(42 + 18√5))/4 = 2.87966: the meeting of the two midpoint cells of #76, as the tetrahedron's pyramid meets the octahedron's in the octet. Face to face the four pyramids keep to their solids, as in #77: round a side edge only one pentagonal and two hexagonal pyramids close (111.381° + 2 × 124.309° = 360°; for the truncated tetrahedron 67.115° + 2 × 146.443°), and the angles round an edge exclude TI from every face-to-face tiling by the two solids (the only edge star of up to five solids is 2 × 109.47° + 2 × 70.53° of truncated tetrahedra; the closest one with TI misses by 1.57°).

3. Main theorem: the spiked TI and its two cycles

Turn every pyramid outwards and the solid becomes a star. The tips of the star mark the faces of a larger one, and the space between them fills with small tetrahedra of only three kinds.

Let cf be the centre of face f of TI (it is the foot of the perpendicular from the centre, because the faces are regular). For s > 1 the spiked TI, TI(s), is TI with a pyramid over every face whose tip is s·cf; its spikes are the inward pyramids scaled in height by s − 1, so TI(s) has volume s·TI. Let K(s) be the convex hull of the 32 tips, and s·TI the truncated icosahedron scaled by s; its face centres are exactly the tips.

Theorem. For s = 2 and for s = φ: (a) TI ⊂ TI(s) ⊂ K(s) ⊂ s·TI, and K(s) is a pentakis dodecahedron: 32 corners (the tips), 60 congruent triangles, each over one corner of TI. (b) The layer K(s) minus TI(s) is filled exactly by 60 corner tetrahedra, each a corner v of TI with the three tips over the faces at v, and 90 edge tetrahedra, each an edge vw of TI with the two tips over the faces at vw (60 over pentagon–hexagon edges, 30 over hexagon–hexagon edges). (c) The layer s·TI minus K(s) is filled exactly by 60 corner tetrahedra (a corner of s·TI and the three face centres there) and 90 edge tetrahedra (an edge of s·TI and the two face centres beside it). (d) Scaling by s repeats the turn without end, so the four stations form a cycle: for s = 2 a doubling cycle, for s = φ a golden cycle.

Proof and checks. The tips are s times the face centres, so they are the face centres of s·TI, which gives (a) together with the computed hull: its 60 face planes are all at one distance, each through one pentagon tip and two hexagon tips over one corner of TI. Each corner tetrahedron of layer 1 has its base on a face of K(s) and its apex at the corner of TI below; each edge tetrahedron is bounded by the two spike faces over its edge and by two faces of the neighbouring corner tetrahedra; so the pieces close round every spike edge and every hull edge. The volumes of each layer add up to the layer to 10−12 for both s (corners exact in ℚ(√5); volumes in double precision), and all 333 pieces of a turn, the core and the spikes are pairwise free of overlap by the separating-axis test (faces and edge cross products of every pair of convex pieces whose bounding spheres meet). This is (b) and (c); (d) is the scaling. ∎

4. Doubling and golden, and the Cleo star

Mirroring a face pyramid in its face puts the tip at twice the face centre: that is the doubling cycle, and the spiked TI is then exactly twice TI, as DICTO's Cleo star, the icosahedron with its twenty Cleo-Icos turned outwards, is twice the icosahedron (#35, #54). The golden ratio enters when the spikes are 1/φ of the pyramids' height. The two cycles behave differently from the Cleo star in one respect: the Cleo star's twenty tips are the corners of a dodecahedron, D′, which is how it joins DICTO's twelve-station cycle; the 32 tips of the spiked TI lie on two spheres (the pentagon tips 1.0265 times further out than the hexagon tips, the inverse of the Catalan pentakis dodecahedron's proportion), and they touch no station of the twelve-station cycle at any scale φk, k = −2, …, 4 (study data contacts.json). The spiked TI therefore starts a cycle of its own, beside the golden cycle rather than on it.

5. What is new and what is known

  • Known, credited: cutting a convex solid from its centre into face pyramids; raising pyramids on the faces (Kepler's stellations, the Kleetope construction), and the pentakis dodecahedron as the Kleetope of the dodecahedron; the truncated icosahedron and truncated tetrahedron (Archimedes, Kepler); the bitruncated 600-cell of #76 (Boole Stott 1910).
  • New, as far as the sources checked show: Proposition 1 (the two hexagonal pyramids carry the Cleo-Icos's and the tetrahedron pyramid's angles), the hexagonal bipyramid of the two midpoint cells, the exclusion of the pyramids of TI from face-to-face tilings, and the Theorem: the doubling and golden cycles of the spiked TI with their exact fills.
  • Not decided: which other factors s give a cycle with the same pieces (only s = 2 and s = φ were checked), and whether the golden cycle can be joined to the twelve-station cycle by an intermediate solid.

6. Conclusion

DICTO's idea holds both ways. The core of TI fills exactly with pentagonal and hexagonal pyramids, and the hexagonal ones carry the Cleo-Icos's angle. Turned outwards they make a spiked core from which two new cycles grow, a doubling one and a golden one, each repeating without end with one core, 32 spikes and 300 tetrahedra of six kinds per turn.
