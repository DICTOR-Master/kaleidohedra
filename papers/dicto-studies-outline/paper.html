Volume I: The Five Solids. An outline, 10 November 2026

DICTO · dictospheres.com · 10 November 2026
For DICTO, not for publication: a first outline of a new book (a new record, so nothing is published without DICTO's word). Scope as DICTO set it on 10 November: everything specific to the five Platonic solids, including what cannot be done; the rhombic dodecahedron and the cuboctahedron go into the free-scale paper of #47 instead. Built from the 266 study folders: 143 are about the Platonic solids; 114 of these already feed published papers, 29 have never been written up. Chapter contents below are the candidate results, each with its study; the order and the title are DICTO's to set.

The idea

The papers each prove one theorem. The studies behind them found much more: exact results that never needed a paper of their own, results that are complete only together, and careful proofs that something cannot be done. DICTO STUDIES gathers them for the five Platonic solids, one chapter for each solid, its variants and its partners (DICTO, 10 November), in the order of the name: D · I · C · T · O. Each chapter ends with the solid's partner table: fills space alone, with one partner, with two, never.

How it reads (DICTO, 10 November)

Reordered from the papers into one story, fun to read and a bit lighter on equations: each result told in words and pictures first, the exact statement in a short box, and always traceable back to the original paper (a marker such as → #47 with its DICTO DOI on every result, and the study behind it in the notes). The proofs stay in the papers; the book shows what was found and why it matters.

Prologue. Aristotle's pyramids (re-edited, DICTO: "definitely in there")

On the Heavens III.8: "the pyramid and the cube" fill space; read as the regular tetrahedron this is false. The five face pyramids, one per solid, are the first pieces every later chapter uses. 2026-10-21-aristotle-pyramids (#44), 2026-10-27-five-pyramids

1. The dodecahedron and its variants

  1. Euclid's dodecahedron: the cube of edge φ with six roofs; the roof as a piece.
  2. The Dogstar, rediscovered independently by DICTO in the void of the dodecahedron packing (G. Hart, 1996, first); dodecahedra on alternate cubes; its volume exactly φ/2. #42; 2026-11-10
  3. The growth-rule chain (#81): period 4, scale 35 − 15√5 = 10/φ⁴; the five regimes of the depth. 2026-11-09-b1-walls
  4. The DICTO Dodeca-13 and its hulls; the Queen geodes and sectors. 2026-10-09-dodeca-13; 2026-10-31-queen-* (not yet written up)
  5. Partners and the impossible: never with pentagonal pyramids, Cleo-Icos and Antonius only (θD, θI, π independent).

2. The icosahedron and its variants

  1. Pacioli's golden rectangles inside the icosahedron (DICTO): its twelve corners are the corners of three perpendicular golden rectangles; the same frame seats it in the octahedron (Pacioli) and in Euclid's cube.
  2. The Cleo-Icos (DICTO's rediscovery) and Antonius: the golden rhombohedron, the Cleo-Icos in the literature. #20, #44; 2026-10-14-cleo-icos-reading
  3. The Chain of Karma shell: icosahedron + 12 pentagonal pyramids + 30 AXE + 20 CLEO = the dodecahedron; the flat Karma layer. #22; 2026-11-10-di-bridge
  4. In the octahedron: six Admiral Caps (each exactly φ/12). Icosahedra in a filling of space: one surrounded by Cleo-Icos and Antonius alone; the octet route; Dehn (one icosahedron balances ten Antonius). 2026-11-10-icosahedra-fill
  5. Clusters and networks; the Cleo shells. 2026-10-10 studies; 2026-10-27-cleo-shells
  6. The impossible: wrapped icosahedra on the octet's regular places (spacings 3.89 – 6.30), the gyrated octet's grid condition.

3. The cube and its variants

  1. Euclid's cube in the dodecahedron (same circumsphere); the cube's right-angled corner tetrahedra; cubes as cubes beside the Dogstars. 2026-11-10-free-scale-mixes
  2. The growth-rule chain (#80): period 4 from step 1, exact on 3/4 < s < 1; the sample depth 5/7 in the only non-closing window. 2026-11-09-b1-walls

4. The tetrahedron and its variants (the classical chapter)

  1. The regular tetrahedron: not alone (Aristotle's error; the Dehn invariant); partners octahedron (octet, every stacking) and truncated tetrahedra.
  2. The classical space-filling tetrahedra: Hill (1895), Sommerville (1922/23; the tetragonal disphenoid), Baumgartner, Goldberg's three infinite families (1974), Senechal's survey "Which tetrahedra fill space?" (1981). DICTO's Bain disphenoids (#3) against Sommerville's (to check).
  3. DICTO's tetrahedra: the quarter Q (Aristotle's face pyramid), the corner tetrahedra (also the octahedron's face pyramid), AXE and CLEO.
  4. Partner classification of every tetrahedron in the kit (main theorem of the chapter).

5. The octahedron and its variants

  1. The half octahedron P and the octahedron's face pyramid; the octet and its stackings; the crossed nest (stella octangula in EKP). 2026-11-06-crossed-nest
  2. The octahedron on the dodecahedron's roof ridges (edge φ²/√2) and Pacioli's icosahedron in it; the golden-core octets (OCD caps, Penrose core, golden envelope). 2026-10-18 studies

6. All five together: the EKP cell, the networks and every space-filling

  1. The EKP cell (Euclid–Kepler–Pacioli, → #8): all five solids nested in one periodic cell; its network and the Jewel windows (→ #10).
  2. Plato's Five in the Octet (→ #99): every octahedron of the octet holding the nested chain octahedron ⊃ icosahedron ⊃ dodecahedron ⊃ cube ⊃ tetrahedron.
  3. Every mix of the five fills space (→ #47): the 31 mixes with a kit of seven connectors.
  4. The space-filling gallery: every space-filling of the five solids and their pieces in DICTO's work, gathered in one place, each with its paper.

7. One world

Every mix at free scale (two worlds, one face-to-face seam, six connectors); then one world for all: each solid inside a skin that makes it a dodecahedral cell, every mix on one cubic grid; sphere-unit sizes. 2026-11-10-free-scale-mixes, 2026-11-10-one-world

8. Shells and cycles of the solids

Fused shells opened by large facets; the new cycles after #43. 2026-10-13-fused-shells; 2026-10-21-new-cycles
