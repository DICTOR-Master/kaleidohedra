Epilogue. What has been shown

Conclusion

The book set out to show that one classical solid, the permutohedron, reorganises a large part of DICTO's geometry, and it has. In three dimensions the truncated triangle is the permutohedron of three values and every face of every permutohedron is a truncated triangle or a rectangle (Chapter 1); the golden loop of DICTONIC is exactly the set of four-value permutohedra with the cube's full symmetry, each filling space with its reciprocal in one way only (Chapter 2); and the weight that drives the web of bonds is the spacing of the four values, d = (g − m)/(g + m) (Chapter 3). The paradigm also showed where it stops: DICTO's rhombic dodecahedron taken apart closes into the chamfered cube, whose hexagons are elongated, not truncated triangles, so it is not a permutohedron, yet with cube pyramids it fills space (Chapter 4). Scutoids turned out to be exchanges of neighbours seen in a slice (Chapter 5).

In four dimensions the permutohedron of five values carries two stories. Its Vacuus has 65 central slices of four kinds, and at its heart five DICTO 1:2 Truncated Octahedra slot together along ten regular hexagons (Chapter 6); its sweep is a scutoid tissue counted by the Eulerian numbers, and every simple 4-polytope sweeps the same way, by its h-vector, with the exchanges balanced between the Queens (Chapter 7). The Coxeter chain tells clean tissues from rosettes (Chapter 8) and faces on mirrors from faces off them (Chapter 10), and the walls of the Swap Rule of close packing are exchanges one dimension up (Chapter 9). Part Three carried both stories into every dimension (Chapter 11), cut the 4D filling in the golden direction into a filling of 35 kinds of cell that is non-periodic in one direction only, repeating along a plane, and whose window version has 60 tile shapes (Chapter 12), and found the permutohedron in the oldest walk through all the orders of a set, English change ringing, where the plain hunt is the only shortest way from rounds to backrounds for every number of bells (Chapter 13). Finally it moved the walls instead of the values: the permutohedron's family runs from Kelvin's cell to the associahedron, whose canonical form is a scattering amplitude, and in three dimensions its members that fill space by sliding are exactly Fedorov's five (Chapter 14).

Open problems

(1) Which rosette tissues without symmetry balance, beyond the 16-cell law N1 + N5 = NQ + 2 (Chapter 8)? (2) Extend the Swap Rule below λ = −9s/8 and below s = 1/8, where a piece reaches two gaps, and close the windows of the Boulder (#80, #81) and the exact form of s* (Chapter 9). (3) Is there a golden filling that repeats in no direction at all? Theorem 12.2 shows that no cut of codimension one in a golden direction gives one (Chapter 12). (4) Among the polytopes of the seven reflection chains, Vacuus slices off the mirrors that carry three or more faces occur in four dimensions only in the permutohedron and the runcinated 5-cell (#108, Observation 4); which polytopes outside the chains have them (Chapters 6 and 10)? (5) Does the 13-COA fill space with octahedra and tetrahedra, and which equal-edged members do the A4, B4, F4 and H4 cones hold? For n ≥ 5, which members of the family are parallelohedra (Chapter 14)?
