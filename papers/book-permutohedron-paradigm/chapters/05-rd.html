DICTO cut rhombic dodecahedra in half in Polyhedraverse and put fourteen halves together. The result looks like a permutohedron, with squares where Kelvin's cell has squares and hexagons between them, and it is not one: its hexagons have their long sides opposite, and none of its edges is a swap. This chapter proves what the build shows and finds the place where the paradigm stops.

Chapters {{ch:o1}} to {{ch:o3}} found the solids of the series among the permutohedra. This chapter follows a build that leads out of the family. The rhombic dodecahedron is a cube with a square pyramid on each of its six faces, and cut through six of its corners it falls into two halves (a Hemi RD, volume 1 when the whole has volume 2). The cut face is an elongated hexagon: sides 1, √3/2, √3/2, 1, √3/2, √3/2, the long sides opposite each other, the very hexagon that will appear on the chamfered cube. The build in Polyhedraverse starts from one Hemi RD and attaches thirteen more to its faces. Three things show in the result: its hexagons are irregular, with the long sides opposite; its six open squares could be filled with pyramids cut from a cube; and those squares echo the squares of the permutohedron. This chapter proves them and shows where the echo holds and where it stops.

Theorem {{S:o4.1}} (DICTO's build; exact). The fourteen half rhombic dodecahedra (Hemi RDs: a rhombic dodecahedron cut through six of its corners falls into two of them) of the build, one Hemi RD with thirteen more attached to its faces (Figure {{f:rdapart}}), have, as convex hull, the zonohedron Z with generators the unit vectors e1, e2, e3 and the four half body diagonals ½(±1, ±1, ±1): the chamfered cube, with 6 squares of side 1 and 12 elongated hexagons (sides 1, √3/2, √3/2, 1, √3/2, √3/2), and volume 15. The build fills Z except for six cube pyramids (base the unit square, height ½, volume ⅙ each), one under each square.

Proof. The volume of a zonotope is the sum of |det| over its triples of generators: 1 (the three axes) + 6 (two axes with one diagonal) + 6 (one axis with two diagonals) + 2 (three diagonals) = 15. The build is read exactly from its world export: each stored rotation is, to 10−15, a matrix with entries 0 and ±1 (orthogonal, determinant 1) and each stored position a multiple of 1/10, and the Hemi RD's own corners are tenths; so every corner of the build is rational. In rational arithmetic: each piece has volume exactly 1; the 91 pairs of pieces have disjoint interiors (each pair is separated by an exact plane, a face normal of one piece or the cross product of an edge direction of each); every corner of the build lies in Z + c, c = (0, 1/5, 1/5) the centre of the build, and every corner of Z + c is a corner of a piece, so the convex hull of the build is exactly Z + c, with squared hexagon sides 1, 3/4, 3/4, 1, 3/4, 3/4. The six cube pyramids under the squares of Z + c lie in Z + c and have interiors disjoint from every piece and from one another (99 more exact separations). Pieces and pyramids then fill a volume 14 · 1 + 6 · ⅙ = 15 = vol Z inside Z + c without overlap, so they fill Z + c (hemi_exact.py, FINAL ok). ∎

Corollary {{S:o4.2}}. The chamfered cube is not a permutohedron. By Chapter {{ch:o1}}, every 2-dimensional face of a permutohedron is a truncated triangle (sides alternating) or a rectangle; the hexagons of Z are elongated hexagons, their long sides opposite each other, not alternating, so they are not truncated triangles. The difference is one of symmetry: a regular hexagon has both a third-turn and a half-turn; the truncated triangle keeps the third-turn and loses the half-turn (opposite sides unequal), the elongated hexagon keeps the half-turn (opposite sides equal and parallel, two mirrors, like a rectangle) and loses the third-turn. Every face of a zonohedron has the half-turn, so the chamfered cube's hexagons had to be elongated.

Theorem {{S:o4.3}} (exact). Copies of Z (the chamfered cube of Theorem {{s:o4.1}}) at the points of L = {(a, b, c) ∈ 2ℤ³ : a + b + c ≡ 0 (mod 4)} (twice the face-centred cubic lattice, cell volume 16) and unit cubes at L + (2, 0, 0) fill space. In the pieces of the build: per lattice point, 14 Hemi RDs and 12 cube pyramids (six in the dents of Z, six making the cube between six neighbours).

Proof. No two pieces overlap: each of the 487 pairs within reach is separated by an exact plane (a face normal of either piece or the cross product of an edge direction of each), checked in rational arithmetic. Each lattice point carries volume 15 + 1 = 16, the volume of a cell of L, so the pieces have density 1; a packing of convex bodies with density 1 leaves no gap. ∎ (exact_fill.py)

Remark 4.4. Z alone does not fill space by translations: round a hexagon's long sides its zone has 8 faces, and a convex body that tiles by translations has every belt of 4 or 6 faces (Venkov 1954; McMullen 1980). The cubes are needed.

Where the echo holds. The six squares of Z sit on the cube axes exactly where the 3D permutohedron has its six squares, and in the 4D permutohedron those squares are where a second kind of cell, the hexagonal prism, joins, as here a second kind of piece, the cube, does. The hexagons differ: twelve elongated hexagons on the rhombic dodecahedron's face directions here, eight truncated triangles on the body diagonals there. The echo is in the squares, not in the hexagons.

Worked example: the volume ledger. The chamfered cube is a zonohedron with seven generators, the three unit axes and the four half body diagonals ½(±1, ±1, ±1). Its volume is the sum of |det| over its triples of generators: 1 (the three axes) + 6 (two axes with one diagonal: twelve triples, ½ each) + 6 (one axis with two diagonals: eighteen triples, twelve of them ½ and six flat) + 2 (three diagonals: four triples, ½ each) = 15. The build's pieces give 14 Hemi RDs of volume 1 and 6 cube pyramids of ⅙: 14 + 1 = 15, the same.

No: the chamfered cube's edges run along directions that swap no two values, and the paradigm stops there. But the swap itself does not stop: inside cells it becomes a trade of neighbours.
