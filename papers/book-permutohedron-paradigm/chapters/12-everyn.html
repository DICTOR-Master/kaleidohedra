Six values, seven, a hundred: the orders of n values are the corners of a permutohedron in n − 1 dimensions, and its swaps and its central slices are counted by formulas that hold for every n. The swap that reverses to itself comes back in every even dimension, but only in four is it a trade of neighbours.

The permutohedron of five values carried this book's two four-dimensional stories, its Vacuus (the central slices spanned by its ridges) and its sweep. Both continue in every dimension.

The Vacuus in every dimension

The construction works for the permutohedron of 1, …, n in dimension n − 1: its ridges (faces one dimension below the cells) are the ordered splits into three blocks, and each spans one central slice with the centre.

Theorem {{S:o11.1}} (the Vacuus count; #105, Theorem 3). The ridges of the permutohedron of 1, …, n lie in exactly N(n) central hyperplanes, where
N(n) = Σa<c n!/(a! b! c!) + T(n), the sum over a, b, c ≥ 1 with a + b + c = n, and T(n) = 2n−1 − 1 for n odd, 2n−2 − 1 for n even.

Proof. By the normal formula, a split with a ≠ c gives a slice whose normal takes three different values; the normal is unique up to scale, its level sets are the blocks, its one negative level is B₂, and the two positive levels are B₁ and B₃ in either order, so only the split and its reverse give the slice, and no two-valued slice can equal it: the first sum. A split with a = c gives a normal with two values, c₁ on S = B₁ ∪ B₃ (of even size 2a) and c₂ on the middle block T; the ratio c₁ : c₂ = −b : 2a depends only on the sizes of S and T, so the slice is fixed by the unordered pair {S, T}, and every pair with |S| even and at least 2 occurs. When n is odd exactly one of S, T has even size: 2n−1 − 1 pairs. When n is even, |S| = 2a is even so |T| is even too, and the split (a, b, a) gives the same slice as (b/2, 2a, b/2) with S and T exchanged: the pairs with both parts even, (2n−1 − 2)/2 = 2n−2 − 1. The two-valued slices are the hyperplanes Σi∈S xi = 0 through the centre (centred coordinates), parallel to facets: members of the resonance (all-subsets) arrangement (Kamiya, Takemura and Terao 2011; Billera et al. 2012; Kühne 2023). The three-valued slices are not of that kind. In closed form, with cn the central trinomial coefficient (1, 1, 3, 7, 19, 51, …; OEIS A002426), the splits with a = c number An = cn − 1 − [n even]·C(n, n/2), and N(n) = (3n − 3·2n + 3 − An)/2 + T(n). Brute force over every ridge for n = 3, …, 9, with each slice found by exact elimination, gives the same numbers (vacuus_general.py). ∎

In two dimensions the Vacuus of the hexagon is its three long diagonals; in three, the 3D permutohedron's 36 edges lie on 15 planes through its centre. So do the 36 edges of the DICTO 1:2 Truncated Octahedron (the permutohedron of 1, 2, 4, 5): its 15 cards are three octagons in the three coordinate planes, slotted at right angles like the three squares of the octahedron Vacuus, each carrying 4 edges, and twelve hexagons carrying 2 each (Figure {{f:o11.1}}; vacuus3d_12.py, exact). Neither N(n) nor its three-valued part (0, 12, 50, 210, 707, 2380, 7506) is in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (searched 11 November 2026 for 3, 15, 65, 225, 770, 2443 and for 12, 50, 210, 707, 2380); we make no stronger claim of novelty.

The sweep in every dimension

Proposition {{S:o11.2}}. Sweep the permutohedron of 1, …, n by f(x) = u · x with u1 < … < un. A corner has as many edges below as there are values k standing to the left of k + 1, so the corners with i edges below number the Eulerian number A(n, i): 1, 4, 1 (n = 3); 1, 11, 11, 1; 1, 26, 66, 26, 1; 1, 57, 302, 302, 57, 1; 1, 120, 1191, 2416, 1191, 120, 1 (n = 7). By #104, Theorem 1′, a corner with i edges below is the bistellar i → (n − 1 − i) move of the slice, so these numbers count every kind of exchange the sweep makes.

Proof. As for Proposition {{s:o5.1}}: exchanging k (at position p) and k + 1 (at q) changes f by up − uq, negative exactly when p < q; the orderings counted by how many k precede k + 1 are counted by the Eulerian numbers (Petersen 2015). Listed directly for n = 3 to 7 (sweep_all_n.py). ∎

Worked example: N(4) = 15. For n = 4 the splits into three blocks with a ≠ c are (1, 1, 2) and (2, 1, 1) in the sum over a < c, giving 4!/(1! 1! 2!) = 12. Since n is even, T(4) = 2² − 1 = 3. So N(4) = 15: the 36 edges of Kelvin's cell lie on 15 planes through its centre, the fifteen cards of Figure {{f:o11.1}}, three octagons and twelve hexagons.

The swap in higher dimensions. In the permutohedron of n values every reversal of the sweep exchanges the corners with i and n − 1 − i edges below; the Eulerian numbers are symmetric, A(n, i) = A(n, n − 1 − i), and a self-reverse class exists exactly when the dimension n − 1 is even (for n = 7, the 2416 corners with three edges below). By Theorem {{s:o7.1}}′ those corners trade lower faces, not contacts of cells; the pure scutoid swap of Chapter {{ch:centre1}} stays four-dimensional.

The Vacuus and the sweep are counted for every n, and every simple d-polytope sweeps by its h-vector. The fillings by permutohedra repeat; cut at a golden tilt, they do not.
