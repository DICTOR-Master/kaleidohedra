### Critical Review of Section 2 (Theorem 3)

**1. Unsubstantiated Computational Scale and Methodology**
*   **Exact Quote:** "about 11 million checks"; "every corner was re-solved symbolically and every plane's slack proved non-negative on the whole cell... exact Bernstein sign tests over the s-interval".
*   **Problem:** **Unsupported / Missing Detail.** The claim of "11 million checks" is a massive computational burden that is not accompanied by a description of the algorithm, the specific software (e.g., Maple, Mathematica, Sage, C++ with FLINT), or the complexity of the symbolic expressions. Without a description of how the "exact Bernstein sign tests" were applied to a two-parameter family of polynomials over a 2D domain, the proof is a "black box" assertion. The phrase "re-solved symbolically" is vague; solving for corners of a power diagram with linear weights in $s, \lambda$ yields rational functions, but verifying non-negativity of slacks over a 2D cell requires quantifier elimination or specific sign-invariance algorithms, not just "tests."
*   **Concrete Fix:** Provide a pseudocode summary of the verification algorithm, specify the exact algebraic complexity (degree of polynomials in $s, \lambda$), and cite the specific computational geometry library used. Alternatively, provide a smaller, fully reproducible example (e.g., for a single stacking word) with the full symbolic output.

**2. Ambiguity in "Each Wall is a Swap" and the "Six Stacking Words"**
*   **Exact Quote:** "built at one sample point for all six stacking words of #61's map"; "Each wall is a swap."
*   **Problem:** **Unclear / Missing Credit.** The text assumes the reader knows what the "six stacking words" are and how they map to the "Swap Rule" in reference #61 (which is not provided). Furthermore, the statement "Each wall is a swap" is a strong geometric claim that conflates a *boundary* in the parameter space $(s, \lambda)$ with a *combinatorial event* (a T1 transition) in the spatial tiling. While likely true, the proof sketch does not explicitly link the vanishing of a slack variable (a wall) to the specific combinatorial swap of neighbors described in Theorem 1. It assumes the correspondence without proving that *every* wall corresponds to a *single* swap and not a more complex degeneration.
*   **Concrete Fix:** Explicitly list the six stacking words and define the "swap" event in the context of the power diagram (e.g., "a wall corresponds to the moment a vertex of the dual graph crosses an edge"). Clarify that the "11 million checks" included verifying that the combinatorial change at each wall is exactly a single swap, not a simultaneous multi-cell flip.

**3. Hidden Assumption of Finite Neighborhood and Genericity**
*   **Exact Quote:** "On each cell of the map... every corner was re-solved symbolically"; "The classical parts... are credited below".
*   **Problem:** **Hidden Assumption / Gap.** The proof relies on the assumption that the local configuration of the power diagram (the "clash cell") is determined solely by the "four-layer keys" and that the "finite neighbourhood" of the hole does not interact with distant layers in a way that changes the combinatorics within the $[1/8, 1/4]$ range. The text states "four-layer keys do not determine it" below $-9s/8$, implying that for the valid range, the 4-layer assumption holds. However, this is a non-trivial topological claim about the decay of influence in the power diagram. The proof does not demonstrate *why* the 4-layer truncation is sufficient to capture the global topology of the swap.
*   **Concrete Fix:** Add a lemma or argument justifying why the combinatorial type of the power diagram for a hole is invariant under the addition of layers beyond the 4th layer within the specified parameter range. This is critical for the "exact computation" to be valid for the infinite lattice.

**4. Overclaim of "Newness" and Lack of Prior Art Context**
*   **Exact Quote:** "the scutoid reading appears to be new (sources checked at the end)... No source links scutoids to polytopes, to slices of 4-polytopes, or to the h-vector."
*   **Problem:** **Overclaim / Unsupported.** The claim that "no source links scutoids to polytopes" is a negative assertion based on a web search performed on a future date (2026). This is methodologically weak. The concept of "scutoids" (cells changing topology in a sweep) is mathematically equivalent to the study of *bistellar flips* (or Pachner moves) in the context of polytope slicing, a well-established field (cited in the "Sources" section as Buchstaber, Ziegler, Santos). The paper frames a known combinatorial phenomenon (bistellar flips in 4D) as a "new" geometric discovery ("scutoid reading") without rigorously distinguishing the *geometric* novelty (the specific power diagram context) from the *combinatorial* novelty.
*   **Concrete Fix:** Reframe the contribution to emphasize the *geometric realization* of these flips in the specific context of the "Swap Rule" and close packing, rather than claiming the combinatorial link is new. Acknowledge the established literature on bistellar flips and clarify that the "scutoid" terminology is a specific application to this physical model, not a new mathematical definition of the flip itself.

**5. Incomplete Definition of the "Clash Cell" and Parameter Range**
*   **Exact Quote:** "the clash cell below $\lambda = -9s/8$ and $s < 1/8$ are open"; "for every $s$ in $[1/8, 1/4]$".
*   **Problem:** **Gap in Proof.** The proof explicitly excludes the region $s < 1/8$ and $\lambda < -9s/8$ as "open" (unproven). However, the theorem claims validity for the *entire* range $[1/8, 1/4]$. The transition at $s=1/8$ is a boundary where the "four-layer keys" might fail or the "clash cell" definition might change. The proof does not address the behavior *at* $s=1/8$ or the continuity of the "scutoid" definition across this boundary.
*   **Concrete Fix:** Provide a continuity argument or a separate verification for the boundary case $s=1/8$ to ensure the "exact computation" holds up to the limit, or explicitly state that the theorem is restricted to the open interval $(1/8, 1/4]$ if the boundary is singular.

**6. Verification of "Rational Corners" and "Linear Planes"**
*   **Exact Quote:** "every power-diagram plane has a normal linear in s and an offset linear in s and $\lambda$, and every corner is rational in $(s, \lambda)$".
*   **Problem:** **Potential Error / Oversimplification.** In a power diagram (weighted Voronoi), the bisector between two sites $p_i, p_j$ with weights $w_i, w_j$ is defined by $2(p_i - p_j) \cdot x = |p_i|^2 - |p_j|^2 + w_i - w_j$. If the weights $w$ are linear in $s, \lambda$ and the sites $p$ are fixed (or linear), the plane equation is indeed linear. However, the *corners* (intersections of 4 planes in 4D) are solutions to a linear system. If the coefficients are linear in $s, \lambda$, the solution (corner coordinates) is a *rational function* (ratio of polynomials), not necessarily a polynomial. The text says "rational in $(s, \lambda)$", which is correct, but the subsequent claim that "slack is affine in $\lambda$" requires that the corner coordinates (which are rational) do not introduce non-linearities that break the affine nature of the slack when substituted back. If the corner coordinates have denominators depending on $\lambda$, the slack (a linear function of the corner) becomes a rational function, not affine. The proof must ensure the denominators do not vanish or that the "affine" claim refers to the numerator after clearing denominators.
*   **Concrete Fix:** Clarify the algebraic structure of the slack function. If the corner coordinates are rational, the slack is rational. The "affine in $\lambda$" claim is only valid if the denominator is constant or if the slack is defined as the numerator of the inequality. The proof must explicitly state that the sign of the slack is determined by the numerator, which is affine in $\lambda$ for fixed $s$.

### Verdict
The proof of Theorem 3 relies on a massive, unverified computational black box and makes unjustified assumptions about the sufficiency of 4-layer truncations and the algebraic simplicity of the slack functions, rendering the "exact" nature of the proof currently unconvincing without significant elaboration.
