The Golden Ladder · Rung 0
From a Point to the Triangle of Mirrors

DICTO (artist, Osaka, Japan) · dictospheres.com · A study companion to The Golden Ladder (DICTO DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23966554) · Sample, version 2 · 8 November 2026
The lessons form a ladder, with mnemonics as bridges, to be climbed up and down. Drafting with Claude (Anthropic).
Draft for DICTO's reading: sample of the format, simpler second try (one idea per step, no formulas). Not a record.

Thirteen small steps from a dot on paper to the first rung of the Ladder. Each step: one idea, one picture, one phrase to remember, what comes next, what it rests on, and something to try with your hands. Going down, read from 13 back to 1.

1 · A point

A point marks a place. It has no size.

A point is where.

↑ Next: two points give a line.
↓ Rests on: nothing: this is the ground.
✋ Try: Put a dot on paper. Put a second dot.

2 · A line

Two points fix exactly one straight line between them.

A line is between.

↑ Next: where two lines meet, they make a turn.
↓ Rests on: two points.
✋ Try: Join your two dots with a ruler. Try to draw a second straight line through both: you cannot.

3 · A turn

Where two lines meet they make an angle: how far you turn from one to the other. A full turn is 360°. Half a turn makes a straight line, 180°. A quarter turn is the corner of a sheet of paper, 90°.

Full 360, half 180, quarter 90.

↑ Next: three points, three lines and three turns make a triangle.
↓ Rests on: two lines meeting.
✋ Try: Stand up and turn round once, half, a quarter.

4 · A triangle

Any three points not on one line make a triangle. You can choose them in endless ways, so there are infinitely many triangles.

Three points, three lines, three corners.

↑ Next: so many triangles: which ones are really different?
↓ Rests on: points, lines, turns.
✋ Try: Draw three triangles that look as different as you can.

5 · Same shape

Make a triangle bigger, as a photocopier does, and its corners stay the same. Two triangles with the same corners have the same shape. Shape is what counts, not size.

Bigger, same shape.

↑ Next: what do the corners of every triangle have in common?
↓ Rests on: the triangle.
✋ Try: Photocopy or trace a triangle larger. Compare the corners.

6 · The corners make a line

Tear the three corners off any paper triangle and put them side by side. They always make a straight line: half a turn, 180°. So two corners decide the third.

Three corners make one straight line.

↑ Next: because two corners decide the shape, some shapes can be singled out.
↓ Rests on: the half turn.
✋ Try: Tear the corners off a paper triangle. Lay them together.

7 · Five special triangles

Five shapes carry the whole Ladder. The equal-sided triangle. Half a square: its long side is 1.41 times the short (√2). Half an equal-sided triangle: 1.73 (√3). Two triangles from the five-pointed star: 1.62, the golden ratio φ.

Halve a square: √2. Halve a triangle: √3. Cut a star: φ.

↑ Next: these numbers, 1, √2, √3 and φ, come back at the top of the rung.
↓ Rests on: two corners fix a shape.
✋ Try: Fold a square paper along its diagonal; fold an equal-sided triangle in half.

8 · Round a point

Put tiles round a point with no gaps: their corners must make a full turn. Six triangles, four squares or three hexagons fit exactly. Three pentagons leave a gap.

Six, four, three fit. The pentagon leaves a gap.

↑ Next: what happens if we close the gap?
↓ Rests on: the full turn.
✋ Try: Cut out three paper pentagons and put them round a point.

9 · Fold the gap

Close the gap by folding, and the corner stands up out of the paper: a corner of a solid. Less than a full turn folds up; a full turn stays flat.

A gap folds up into a corner.

↑ Next: how many solids can be made of equal faces?
↓ Rests on: tiles round a point.
✋ Try: Tape the three pentagons edge to edge across the gap. The corner rises.

10 · Five solids

With equal faces, exactly five corners fold up, and each closes into one solid. In DICTO order: Dodecahedron (3 pentagons at a corner), Icosahedron (5 triangles), Cube (3 squares), Tetrahedron (3 triangles), Octahedron (4 triangles). There are no more. Euclid ends his book with this.

D·I·C·T·O.

↑ Next: each solid can be seen in mirrors.
↓ Rests on: folded corners.
✋ Try: Make the five corners in paper.

11 · Mirrors

Two mirrors hinged at the right angle turn one bead into a ring: at 90°, four beads; at 60°, six. Three mirrors round one of three triangles make a whole floor.

Halve the turn, double the beads.

↑ Next: three mirrors round a corner in space make a solid.
↓ Rests on: turns, and the special triangles.
✋ Try: Hinge two pocket mirrors and put a coin between them. Close them slowly.

12 · On a ball

Draw a triangle on a ball. Its corners add up to more than a straight line. An eighth of an orange has three right angles.

Flat: a line. On a ball: more.

↑ Next: now let the triangles on the ball be mirrors.
↓ Rests on: the straight line of the corners.
✋ Try: Cut an orange into eighths. Look at the corners of one piece.

13 · The triangle of mirrors: the first rung

The mirrors of each solid cut the ball round it into small equal triangles, each with corners 90°, 60° and one more: 60° for the tetrahedron, 45° for the cube and octahedron, 36° for the dodecahedron and icosahedron. Make that last corner 30° and the triangle lies flat: the floor of tightly packed balls. Each triangle has its number: 1, √2, φ, and √3 for the flat one. Everything golden lives in the φ triangle. This is where the Ladder begins.

Three kinds on the ball, one flat. One, root two, phi; root three on the floor.

↑ Next: Chapter 1 of The Golden Ladder.
↓ Rests on: mirrors, the ball, the five solids and the special triangles.
✋ Try: Find the mirror triangle on a dice: the cube.
