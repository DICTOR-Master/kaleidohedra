The Balance Laws against Stanley and Bayer–Billera
study of 15 November 2026; DICTO: "have we done the Stanley law check against our laws"

DICTO · study note of 15 November 2026 · studies/2026-11-15-stanley-check
DRAFT study note, for DICTO's reading; not for publication. The credit sentences of Section 5 are proposals: nothing in the books or papers has been edited. Stanley 1979 is not yet read (Lead 1).

A slice moving through a four-dimensional solid sees its cells born, trade neighbours and die. DICTO found that the trades balance: as many go one way as the other. This note checks, against the original papers, how much of that law mathematicians already knew, and in what words, and what stays DICTO's.

Verdict (15 Nov 2026): the balance law of #104 Theorem 4 and #108 Theorem 1 is, after an exact and now verified translation (polar sphere, sweep = line shelling), the symmetry h_b = h_(a−b) of the fine h-vector of a balanced simplicial sphere; for one cell of each type at a corner that symmetry is stated in Bayer–Billera 1985, Section 3 (read in full here), and the balanced-complex framework it lives in is Stanley 1979. Stanley 1979 itself could not be read (the publisher's site blocks automated download), and Bayer and Billera do not credit the symmetry to it. The triage of 14 Nov was wrong in two places. Bayer–Billera 1985 is the right source, not the wrong one. And "Stanley 1979's symmetry" is unconfirmed. DICTO's own parts are #108 Theorem 3 (E − V + 1), the rosette balance law (outside the simplicial setting, where symmetry is needed) and the tissue reading throughout.

Floating point only proposes the order of corner heights; each consecutive pair is then checked strictly increasing in exact arithmetic, and a direction that fails is redrawn.

(T5) for every corner v and every set T of its cells, v is the lowest corner of the face T exactly when R(v) ⊆ T: the sweep is the line shelling with restriction faces R(v). (T6) the corner counts by class equal Stanley's fine h-numbers computed from the fine f-vector alone. (T7) DICTO's ledger, read independently from the lowest and highest corner of every 2-face, equals it. (T8) #108's formula is the inversion. (T9) reversal is complementation.

Rosette balance law (Paradigm Lemma 10.2 and Theorem 10.3 in the assembled book, source keys o8.2 and o8.3; holds with Wythoff symmetry, fails without)NEW (outside Stanley's and Bayer–Billera's hypotheses)Stanley's and Bayer–Billera's hypothesis is a simplicial sphere: the polar of a simple polytope. A rosette corner lies in more than four cells, so the polar is not simplicial and no fine h-vector exists. Why symmetry is needed there and not in Stanley's setting: at a simple corner the faces new at v are exactly the interval [R(v), σ_v] of a Boolean lattice. So the face counts satisfy f = M·N with M unitriangular (f_T = Σ_{S⊆T} N_S, with binomials for type a). N is then fixed by the face lattice, and reversing the sweep gives the symmetry with no geometry used. At a rosette corner the new faces are not such an interval (613 corners with more than one maximal new face in the 201 moved-16-cell sweeps). The class contributions are linearly dependent: rank 5 for 8 classes seen. So the per-class counts are not fixed by the face lattice, and balance needs another reason. Wythoff symmetry is one: every edge is parallel to a root, so the ledger depends only on the chamber of u, and W carries u to −u's chamber. The moved 16-cell (same face lattice, no symmetry) is unbalanced under −3x₁ + x₄ (2 corners where four cells are born, 1 where four die). The regular 16-cell balanced in all 50 sweeps. Proposition 3 of the rosette study (the conservation law, the toric h-vector) is the part that does carry over (Stanley 1987, Lee 2011, both already cited there).
Eulerian count of the permutohedron sweep (Paradigm Prop. 8.1, Centre I: 1, 26, 66, 26, 1; Kelvin 1, 11, 11, 1)KNOWN (classical; not Stanley 1979 specifically)The h-vector of the permutohedron is the Eulerian numbers. Its polar is the Coxeter complex of A_(n−1), the order complex of the proper part of B_n, whose flag h-vector is β_n(S) (EC1 Cor. 3.13.2). The swap kinds 6 / 16 / 11 are β_5(S) for |S| = 2 (pure-swap-6d checked all 64 classes in 6D). The reading "an edge goes down when k stands left of k + 1" is the standard descent statistic.

  2. Type a ≠ (1, …, 1): find a statement of h_b = h_(a−b) for balanced spheres of type a in the literature (Björner–Frankl–Stanley 1987; Stanley, Combinatorics and Commutative Algebra, Ch. III).
