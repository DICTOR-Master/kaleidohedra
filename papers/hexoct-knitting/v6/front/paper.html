Abstract. This paper builds a close-packed crystal in two crafts. Knitted (Parts 1–3): one continuous multi-layer knitted fabric whose knitted sheets are the close-packed layers of balls and whose linking yarns join each node to the three nodes of the next sheet that its ball touches, turning on in the same sense at a c layer and back at an h layer. The cells it closes off are the pieces of the HexOct tiles (#75), so the stacking is carried through the fabric's thickness by which nodes the links join, and taut links centre each sheet by themselves. Crocheted (Part 4): a crystal built in crochet from closed crocheted cells, Narcissus in every h layer and cubes in every c layer (the HexOct pieces at λ = 3/8, #98), two cells per ball, fitted whole face to whole face, so that the build is the stacking in three dimensions. Checked exactly for the stackings 3C, 2H, 4H, 6H and 9R: every contact between two layers is the same square, and only three flat face shapes are needed (a square, a regular hexagon, a right triangle), each crocheted in rows of single crochet. Part 4 gives the construction, a test piece and a comparison of knitting and crochet with its sources; the Appendix gives the complete crochet pattern. Part 1 gives the knitted construction; Part 2 tests whether the stacking is a design dial at equal density in the idealised lattice; Part 3 (this front section) adds comfort: vapour transfer, conduction along the yarn and direct radiation. Neither build has yet been made.

Knit the layers of a crystal as layers of fabric and tie them together as the crystal is tied. Every way of stacking the layers weighs the same and squashes the same; what changes is how much you can see straight through it and how easily it shears. Or crochet the crystal's own solid pieces, small closed bricks of yarn, and stack them like building blocks: two kinds of block make every stacking.

Main results

Result 1 (knitted lattice, idealised). In the idealised Knitted HexOct, all close-packed stackings have the same density; through-thickness compression, buckling onset, rigidity, vapour transfer, conduction along the yarn and direct radiation through twelve sheets are the same in every stacking. The stacking changes only two things: the straight see-through area (up to 10.7 times larger in 2H than in 3C at link radius 0.05 a) and, when the sheets are much softer than the links, the free transverse shear (3C up to 17.5 times softer than 2H), which is set by the stack's net turning (its mean Hägg sum), not by the share of c layers.

Result 2 (crocheted solid cells, checked geometrically). Narcissus, a ten-faced solid with one regular hexagon, three squares and six right triangles (#98), fills every h layer, and the cube fills every c layer, two cells per ball. In the stackings 3C, 2H, 4H, 6H and 9R these cells fill space with no gap and no overlap, each face meets one whole face of one other cell, and every contact between two layers is a square of side a/√2 (a the ball spacing). A pattern in single crochet (18 stitches × 20 rows per 10 cm) gives every side within 0.6 % and every corner angle within 0.1° of the true shape. The crocheted cells are not yet made; test piece 1 (two Narcissus and two cubes) comes next.

Part 3. Comfort: vapour, conduction and radiation

On the same voxel model as Part 2 (members as capsules of radius r on the periodic cell, twelve sheets), we computed for seven stackings: the effective diffusivity of vapour through the air spaces (a Laplace solve, Deff/Dair), conduction along the yarn alone (the same solve in the solid, keff/kyarn), and the share of diffuse (cosine-weighted) rays that pass through all twelve sheets without touching a member.

Table 1. Comfort proxies, grid 0.025 a (3C and 2H also at 0.0167 a: 0.8855 and 0.8856, conduction 0.0201). At r = 0.1 a the same holds: Deff/Dair 0.640–0.641 and conduction 0.0898 in every stacking, direct radiation at most 0.04 %.

So in the idealised fabric the stacking does not change how well it breathes or insulates: vapour passes about equally well through every stacking (88 % of open air at this yarn thickness), conduction along the yarn is the same, and with twelve sheets almost no radiation passes straight through in any stacking, so the extra see-through area of 2H along the stacking axis does not carry over to diffuse radiation. Comfort is set by the yarn and the thickness, not by the stacking. The limits are those of Part 2: idealised straight members, no yarn mechanics, no moisture in the fibre, no air flow solved.
