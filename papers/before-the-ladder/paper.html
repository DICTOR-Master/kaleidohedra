Lesson 1 of 6

Looking

Look at a box. It has flat sides, called faces. Where two faces meet there is a line, an edge. Where edges meet there is a point, a corner. Every shape in this course is made of these three.

💡 Face, edge, corner: flat, line, point.

✋ Find a box. Touch one face, run a finger along one edge, press one corner.

↓ Rests on: Nothing: this is the start.↑ Next: Sorting shapes.

Lesson 2 of 6

Sorting

A small die and a big die have the same shape but different sizes. A die and a pyramid have different shapes. Geometry sorts by shape first: grow or shrink a shape and it stays the same shape.

💡 Shape first, size second.

✋ Gather things from the kitchen. Put the same shapes together, whatever their size.

↓ Rests on: Faces, edges and corners.↑ Next: Counting them.

Lesson 3 of 6

Counting

Count a box: 8 corners, 12 edges, 6 faces. Now take corners, subtract edges, add faces: 8 − 12 + 6 = 2. A pyramid: 5 − 8 + 5 = 2. Every solid without a hole through it comes to 2.

💡 Corners minus edges plus faces: always two.

✋ Count a chocolate box with triangle ends: 6 corners, 9 edges, 5 faces. Does it come to 2?

↓ Rests on: Sorting by shape.↑ Next: Directions: dimensions.

Lesson 4 of 6

Dimensions

A point has no size. Slide it: a line, one direction. Slide the line sideways: a square, two directions. Lift the square: a cube, three directions. Each step slides the last shape in a new direction. A fourth direction would give a shape we can only draw as a shadow.

💡 Slide in a new direction to go up one.

✋ Draw a point, a line, a square. Then draw two squares and join their corners: a cube.

↓ Rests on: Counting corners, edges, faces.↑ Next: Turning and mirroring: symmetry.

Lesson 5 of 6

Symmetry

Turn a square a quarter turn: it looks the same. Fold it along a line through its middle: the halves match. Each turn or fold that leaves a shape looking unchanged is a symmetry. A square has 4 turns and 4 mirror lines.

💡 If you can't tell it moved, it's symmetric.

✋ Cut a paper square. Fold it in half every way you can so the halves match: you find 4 folds.

↓ Rests on: Dimensions.↑ Next: Filling a floor.

Lesson 6 of 6

Filling

Cover a floor with one shape, no gaps, no overlaps. With equal-sided shapes only three work: triangles, squares, hexagons, as in a bees' comb. Pentagons cannot: three leave a gap. One step up, filling a room with solids, is where DICTO's work begins.

💡 Three, four, six: the floor-fillers.

✋ Cut out six paper pentagons and try to cover a table with no gaps. Then try squares.
