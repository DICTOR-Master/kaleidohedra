In a simple 4-polytope four cells meet at every corner, and a sweeping hyperplane changes the tissue one move at a time (Chapter 7): a birth, a death, or one exchange of neighbours. Where more than four cells meet at a corner, several contacts change at once; living tissue has the same event, a rosette, where five or more cells meet at a point (Blankenship et al. 2006). Which uniform polytopes give clean tissues and which give rosettes can be read straight from the Coxeter chain.

Theorem 8.1. Let P be the Wythoff polytope of a chain group W with ringed nodes S and unringed nodes U. Then every corner of P lies on
    e = Σi ∈ S |WU| / |WU ∖ N(i)|
edges, where N(i) are the neighbours of node i and WJ is the subgroup generated by the nodes J. P is simple, a clean tissue, exactly when e equals the dimension; otherwise every corner is a rosette of e edges.

Proof. The stabiliser of the corner x is WU. The edges at x of type i ∈ S go from x to its mirror image across node i's mirror, and their directions form the WU-orbit of the root αi; the stabiliser of αi in WU is generated by the unringed nodes orthogonal to it, U ∖ N(i). Summing the orbit sizes gives e. ∎ The count agrees with the measured number of edges at a corner for all 81 polytopes of the Atlas (all seeds of zeros and ones of A3, B3, H3, A4, B4, F4, H4).

Example. For a regular polytope only an end node is ringed, so e = |WU| / |WU ∖ N|: the 600-cell has e = 120/10 = 12 (its corner figure is the icosahedron), the 120-cell e = 24/6 = 4 (simple). For the seed (0, 1, 0, 0) node 2 has unringed neighbours on both sides and e = 2 · 6 / 2 = 6 in B4, F4 and H4: not simple, although each unringed piece alone looks harmless.

8.2 The balance law of the rosettes

Every edge of a Wythoff polytope points along a root, so the height that sweeps it matters only through its chamber, and every sweep has the same ledger of corner events (Lemma 8.2). Reversing the height reverses every event; hence (Theorem 8.3) in every rosette tissue of the Atlas each rosette move is matched by its reverse, class by class, in every direction: the truncated 600-cell trades one contact between truncated tetrahedra for three truncated-tetrahedron–icosahedron contacts at 118 corners and back at 118. The law needs the symmetry: a 16-cell with its corners moved slightly has a sweep with two corners where four cells are born at once and only one where four die. Its face lattice, toric h-vector and cd-index are those of the regular 16-cell, so no combinatorial invariant decides the balance; what every rosette tissue keeps is conservation: each contact is made once and lost once, each cell born once and dies once, and the corner-by-corner sum of these is the toric h-vector of the dual (Stanley; Lee 2011).

Words. At a corner of a generic sweep let D be its edges going down and U those going up. A contact (2-face) at the corner is lost there when both its edges at the corner go down and made when both go up; a cell dies when all its edges at the corner go down and is born when all go up. The event at the corner records |D|, the kinds of contacts lost and made and the types of cells dying and born (kinds and types as in #108); its reverse swaps D and U. The ledger of a sweep is the multiset of its events.

Lemma 8.2 (one ledger per polytope). Let P be the Wythoff polytope of a finite reflection group W. Every edge of P is parallel to a root, so the down edges at every corner depend only on the chamber of the height, and every generic sweep of P has the same ledger.

Proof. The edges at the corner wx are images of the segments from x to six for ringed nodes i, and six − x is a multiple of the root αi. Two heights in one chamber give every edge the same sign. For g in W the event at gv for the height gu is the image under g of the event at v for u, and g keeps cell types and contact kinds, which are W-orbits; W is transitive on chambers. ∎

Theorem 8.3 (the rosette balance law). In every generic sweep of a Wythoff polytope of a finite reflection group every event is matched by its reverse: for every class of event, the corners with that event are exactly as many as the corners with the reverse event. In particular every rosette tissue of the Atlas balances, class by class, in every direction.

Proof. Sweeping by −u turns the down edges into up edges at every corner, so the ledger of −u is the reverse of the ledger of u; by Lemma 8.2 the two ledgers are equal. ∎ For the simple rows this is a second proof of the balance law of Chapter 7 for the Wythoff polytopes (the flow argument of #108 is still needed for simple polytopes without symmetry). Lemma 8.2 and Theorem 8.3 were found stated nowhere in the sources checked; they are elementary, likely folklore for the permutohedron, and new only in the reading as tissues. Checked exactly on all 34 rosette rows of A4, B4, F4 and H4.

Same face lattice means the same toric h-vector (1, 12, 14, 12, 1), flag vector and cd-index (Bayer and Klapper) as the regular 16-cell, which balances in every direction; so balance is geometric, not combinatorial. For every polytope with the face lattice of the 16-cell the counts of the eight kinds of corner obey N1 + N5 = NQ + 2 (corners where four cells are born or die = pure double exchanges + 2), and the sweep balances exactly when N1 = N5 (studies/2026-11-13-rosette-balance, Proposition 6).
