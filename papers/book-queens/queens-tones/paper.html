The Silent Queens: Tones and Rings of the 600-cell and the 120-cell

DICTO · studies of 4 November 2026 · first published in this book
Abstract. DICTO proposed testing a whole four-dimensional solid with the harmonic fingerprint of #94, the 600-cell first. Seen from the centre, the corners of a four-dimensional solid are points on the 3-sphere, and each degree of harmonic on the 3-sphere is a tone. Main theorem. (A) The White Queen (the 600-cell, 120 corners) and the Red Queen (the 120-cell, 600 corners) are silent in every degree from 1 to 11 and sound first at degree 12: both are spherical 11-designs, and so is every orbit of their symmetry group. (B) The White Queen's corners are the 120 unit icosians, a group; its cyclic subgroups of orders 10, 6 and 4 split them into 12 great decagons, 20 great hexagons and 30 great squares, and the Hopf map sends these rings exactly onto the corners of the icosahedron, the dodecahedron and the icosidodecahedron. (C) The number of harmonics of degree k left unchanged by the White Queen's icosians equals, at every degree k from 0 to 120, the number of vibration modes of the Poincaré dodecahedral space given by Aurich, Lustig and Steiner; the White Queen's silence below degree 12 is the same fact as that space's missing low modes. All three are classical (Goethals and Seidel; Coxeter; Sadoc and Mosseri; Klein; Aurich, Lustig and Steiner); what is new here is the reading of the two Queens through them, checked from our own corners. Two further readings are numerical only and are labelled so: the tones of the White Queen's slices, and a station of 19-fold silence on the path between the Queens.

A solid can be listened to: its corners, seen from the centre, are notes on a sphere, and each degree of harmonic is a tone. In our space the icosahedron never sounds the fourth tone. One dimension up, the two Queens are quieter still: nothing sounds until the twelfth tone. The same silence shows when the White Queen is read as rings, and when she is read as the group of a possible shape of the universe.

1. The fingerprint in four dimensions

In #94 the corners of a solid in our space, seen from its centre, are weighted equally on the sphere and expanded in spherical harmonics; the strength Ql of each degree l is the solid's harmonic fingerprint, the bond-orientational order of Steinhardt, Nelson and Ronchetti. DICTO proposed taking the test to a whole four-dimensional structure, the 600-cell first. On the 3-sphere the zonal harmonic of degree l is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind Ul, and for unit directions u1, …, uN we put

Ql2 = (1/N2) Σi,j Ul(ui·uj)/(l + 1),

the mean over all pairs. A single point gives Ql = 1. By the addition theorem Ql2 is a positive multiple of the sum, over an orthonormal basis of the degree-l harmonics, of the squared sums of each harmonic over the points; so Ql = 0 exactly when every harmonic of degree l sums to zero over the points. A set silent in every degree from 1 to t is a spherical t-design, a set of points that averages every polynomial of degree up to t exactly as the whole sphere does.

The test was calibrated on the regular four-dimensional solids (Table 1), whose design strengths are classical.

2. Main theorem, part (A): the two Queens are 11-designs

Theorem 1. The 120 corners of the White Queen and the 600 corners of the Red Queen are silent in every degree from 1 to 11 and sound at degree 12. More generally every orbit of their common symmetry group on the 3-sphere is silent in degrees 1 to 11.

Proof. The two Queens share the symmetry group H4 of order 14 400. For a harmonic f of degree l, the sum of f over an orbit equals the sum over the same orbit of the H4-average of f, which is an H4-invariant harmonic of degree l. The polynomials invariant under H4 are generated by invariants of degrees 2, 12, 20 and 30, and the one of degree 2 is the squared radius; so a harmonic invariant first occurs at degree 12. Below 12 the average is zero, every orbit sum vanishes, and by §1 every Ql with 1 ≤ l ≤ 11 is zero. At degree 12 the computed values Q12 = 0.277 (White Queen) and 0.087 (Red Queen) are not zero. ∎

The 600-cell's corners as an 11-design are classical (Goethals and Seidel 1981 [1]; Bannai's survey [2]); here they serve as the check that the test hears what is known. In our space the icosahedron is silent at degrees 2, 4, 8 and 14 among the even degrees (#94); the Queens are silent in every degree up to 11.

3. Main theorem, part (B): the White Queen in rings

DICTO proposed taking up the tools of the harmonic study that had not yet been used; the first was the Hopf map. The White Queen's 120 corners, with circumradius 1, are the unit icosians: as quaternions they form a group, the binary icosahedral group (checked on our corners).

Theorem 2. The left cosets of a cyclic subgroup of order 10, 6 or 4 split the White Queen's corners into great-circle rings: 12 decagons, 20 hexagons or 30 squares. The Hopf map q ↦ q u q−1, with u the axis of the subgroup's generator, sends each ring to one point of the 2-sphere, and the images are exactly the corners of the icosahedron (12 points, 5 nearest neighbours each), the dodecahedron (20 points, 3 each) and the icosidodecahedron (30 points, 4 each).

Proof. A cyclic subgroup ⟨g⟩ of order n lies on a great circle through 1, and each left coset q⟨g⟩ is its image under the rotation x ↦ qx, again a great circle; the 120 corners fall into 120/n cosets: 12, 20, 30. Right multiplication by a power of g commutes with u, which is the axis of g, so q gk u g−k q−1 = q u q−1, and a whole ring goes to one point. The images are the orbit of u under conjugation by the icosians, which acts as the icosahedral rotation group of order 60. An element of order 10, 6 or 4 is a rotation about a five-fold, three-fold or two-fold axis; the stabiliser of u has order 5, 3 or 2, so the orbit has 12, 20 or 30 points: the corners of an icosahedron, the face centres of an icosahedron (the corners of a dodecahedron), and its edge midpoints (the corners of an icosidodecahedron). ∎

The discrete Hopf fibrations of the 600-cell are classical (Coxeter [3]; Sadoc and Mosseri [4]); the counts, the one-point images and the nearest-neighbour numbers were checked on our corners (study hopf-interlock). Read as rings, the White Queen gives three solids of our space at once: the icosahedron, the dodecahedron and the icosidodecahedron, three stations of the golden cycle of DICTO's book The Golden Cycle (DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23752452).

4. Main theorem, part (C): the White Queen and the Poincaré space

DICTO proposed following up an outside lead, the cosmic microwave background. The Poincaré dodecahedral space is the 3-sphere divided by the 120 icosians; it was proposed as the shape of space by Luminet and colleagues in 2003 [5] because it has no vibration modes of low degree, which would weaken the largest patterns in the microwave sky. Its modes of degree k are the harmonics of degree k on the 3-sphere left unchanged by the icosians.

Theorem 3. (a) For every degree k from 0 to 120, the number of harmonics of degree k on the 3-sphere invariant under left multiplication by the White Queen's 120 corners equals the multiplicity r(β), β = k + 1, of Aurich, Lustig and Steiner [6]: r(β) = β(⌊(β − 1)/10⌋ + ⌊(β − 1)/6⌋ + ⌊(β − 1)/4⌋ − ⌊(β − 3)/2⌋) for β in {1, 13, 21, 25, 31, 33, 37, 41, 43, 45, 49, 51, 53, 55, 57} or β odd and at least 61 (with r(1) = 1), and 0 otherwise. (b) The White Queen is silent in degrees 1 to 11 exactly because the Poincaré space has no mode of degree 1 to 11.

Proof. (a) The harmonics of degree k on the 3-sphere carry the representation Vk/2 ⊗ Vk/2 of left and right multiplication, so the dimension of the left-invariant part is (k + 1) times the mean, over the 120 icosians q = cos θ + u sin θ, of the character sin((k + 1)θ)/sin θ. Computed from our corners this number agrees with r(k + 1) at all 121 degrees k = 0, …, 120 (study pds-modes). Modes occur at k = 0, 12, 20, 24, 30, 32, 36, …: 13 of them at k = 12, 21 at k = 20, 122 at k = 60 and 363 at k = 120. (b) If f is a nonzero left-invariant harmonic of degree k and f(y) ≠ 0, then h(x) = f(xy) is again left-invariant of degree k (left and right multiplication commute), and the sum of h over the 120 icosians is 120 f(y) ≠ 0; so the icosians are not silent at k. Conversely, if there is no left-invariant harmonic of degree k, the average of any harmonic h over left multiplication is zero, and its value at 1, the mean of h over the icosians, vanishes. So the White Queen is silent at degree k exactly when the Poincaré space has no mode of degree k; by (a) the first mode is at k = 12. ∎

The invariant counts come from Klein's invariants of the binary icosahedral group, of degrees 12, 20 and 30 [7], and the mode counts of the Poincaré space are those of Aurich, Lustig and Steiner [6]; what this chapter adds is the cross-check from the White Queen's own corners and the reading of the White Queen as the Poincaré space's group. No claim is made about cosmology: Planck's 2015 search for the topology of space [8] tested flat topologies only, and the Poincaré space remains debated.

5. Numerical readings (not exact)

The two readings of this section are numerical. Their heights and positions are located on grids and are not exact; they are given as observations, with their precision, and are not part of the main theorem.

5.1 The tones of the White Queen's slices (numerical). Each slice of the White Queen, as in Chapter 6, is a solid of our space; its corners, seen from the slice's centre, have a three-dimensional fingerprint as in #94. With circumradius 1, on a grid of 1201 heights, with zeros located to about 0.002 (study harmonic-4d, slices600.py): corner first, every slice has icosahedral symmetry, so Q4 and Q8 are always silent, as they must be; Q6 falls silent as well at the heights |h| ≈ 0.128, 0.396 and 0.638, where the slices sound only from degree 10 up. Cell first, the slices have tetrahedral symmetry; Q4 is silent at |h| ≈ 0.157, 0.330, 0.583 and 0.737, and Q8 at |h| ≈ 0.137, 0.453, 0.829 and 0.854, never at the same height. So no cell-first slice hides icosahedral order in the way Icosaxes does on the cube–octahedron sweep of #94, where Q4 and Q8 vanish together. The exact heights are open.

5.2 A station of deeper silence between the Queens (numerical). The path from the White Queen to the Red Queen of Chapter 7 can be written S(μ) = (600-cell) ∩ μ·(120-cell), with the 600-cell of circumradius 1 and the 120-cell its polar, for μ from r2 ≈ 0.8567 (r = 0.92562, the 600-cell's inradius), where S is the scaled Red Queen, to 1, where S is the White Queen. By Theorem 1 every stage is silent in degrees 1 to 11. For μ between (1 + φ/2)/2 = 0.904508 and 1 only the White Queen's corners are cut: S has 1440 corners, the points v + s(w − v) on her 1440 directed edges vw, with s = (1 − μ)/(1 − φ/2), the cut of Chapter 7. On this stretch Q12 falls to zero at μ* ≈ 0.924165 (Q12 below 9 × 10−5 there, on a scan of 400 points). Since H4 has no invariant harmonics in degrees 13 to 19 either, at μ* the 1440 corners would be silent in every degree from 1 to 19: a spherical 19-design, the deepest silence on the path. The cut there is deeper than the uniform cut 1/3 of the truncated 600-cell, so the station is not that solid. Q20 falls to zero near μ ≈ 0.9540, where Q12 is not zero. The 1440 points are the orbit of the reflection group W(H4) through a point on an edge of the 600-cell (stabiliser of order 10, orbit 14 400/10 = 1440), and such an orbit tuned to kill the degree-12 invariant is the classical route to a 19-design (Goethals and Seidel [1]; Bannai [2]). What is ours is only that this design is a station of the path between the Queens. The exact value of μ* is open.

6. What is known and what is new

Known, credited. Spherical designs and the design strengths of Table 1, the 600-cell as an 11-design and the 19-designs from H4 orbits: Goethals and Seidel [1], Bannai [2]. The invariant degrees 2, 12, 20, 30 of H4, the icosians and the discrete Hopf fibrations of the 600-cell: Coxeter [3], Sadoc and Mosseri [4]. The invariants of the binary icosahedral group: Klein [7]. The Poincaré dodecahedral space as a model of space: Luminet et al. [5]; its mode multiplicities: Aurich, Lustig and Steiner [6]. The fingerprint Ql: Steinhardt, Nelson and Ronchetti, as cited in #94.

Theorems 1–3 as readings of the two Queens, each checked from our own corners: the calibration of the four-dimensional fingerprint (Table 1), the three ring readings of the White Queen, and the agreement with Aurich, Lustig and Steiner at all 121 degrees up to 120. We did not find, in the sources checked, the harmonic fingerprint applied to the Queens, their slices or the path between them.

Numerical only. The heights of §5.1 and the station μ* of §5.2.

7. Conclusion

Heard as tones, the two Queens are silent through degree 11, a silence that every orbit of their symmetry shares. Read as rings, the White Queen gives the icosahedron, the dodecahedron and the icosidodecahedron at once. Read as a group, she is the group of the Poincaré dodecahedral space, and her silence below degree 12 is that space's missing low modes. Between the Queens, a little past the truncated 600-cell, the silence numerically deepens to degree 19. Open: the exact heights of the silent slices, the exact value of μ*, and the fingerprint of the slices in the edge-first and face-first directions.
