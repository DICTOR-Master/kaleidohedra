Epilogue. What has been shown

The way down and back

The book began with twelve dodecahedra round a thirteenth, and the gaps between them. DICTO pictured rays from the centre passing out through those gaps, gaps that should close in the fourth dimension, and the radial cell projection grew from that picture. The lead-up gave the pieces: three small pieces close the gaps between icosahedra (Chapter 1); of Aristotle's five pyramids only the cube's, and the tetrahedron's with the octahedron's, fill space (Chapter 2); face to face the five keep to their own solids, and every face-to-face tiling by them is a tiling by whole Platonic solids cut from their centres (Chapters 3 and 4).

The shadows of four dimensions then gave the shapes that gluing could not: variants of the convex deltahedra that join differently, and deltahedra rebuilt with every edge equal (Chapter 5). They also gave a key that the end of the book used again: every shadow keeps whole the walls that run along its view, flat planes through its centre, and across all the shadows those kept walls join into one lattice reaching all 360 walls of the Queens.

The Queens themselves were mapped completely. The White Queen has 62 slice patterns in her four main directions and four shadows, each resolving into an exact front half and back half, and her 600 cells stand in fifteen shells round one corner, the innermost twenty Cleo-Icos (Chapter 6); Alicia Boole Stott's series of 1900 is the first part of this atlas. Between the Queens lie five stages, built from the White Queen's own corners and mapped in 1402 slices (Chapter 7). The Red Queen is the White Queen's dual; she mirrors her by polarity, shadow for middle slice, holds the dodecahedron's pyramid at her core, and carries ten White Queens on her corners, in two states of five (Chapter 8). Aristotle's five pyramids are the cores of the four-dimensional regular solids, each with exactly its proportions. Heard as tones, both Queens are silent through degree 11, the White Queen as the group of the Poincaré dodecahedral space; and inside her two golden sets of corners are silent where their symmetry expects a sound, by twisted silence (Chapter 9).

The end returns to the centre where the book began. The walls of the Queens are flat slices through that centre, 60 mirrors and 300 cross walls, shared by the two of them; by the Mirror Law every face of both stands on the mirrors, and between them one solid, Valley Charge, has every wall and nothing else (Chapter 10). The silent solids of Chapter 9 stand in the White Queen's walls, every face on a wall, because their faces are of the kinds her walls carry. By corners they are the White Queen; by walls they carry all her mirrors and split her triangles exactly; as solids they overlap and are never her pieces. In the Red Queen the ten White Queens hold 1200 copies of each silent solid, no copy in both states and every copy sharing walls across them, and her own cats are their duals, whose fins cover every one of her pentagons (Chapter 11). The rays that once passed out through the gaps come back as walls, and the shapes found along the way were standing in them.

Open problems

(1) Which tilings of space by the tetrahedron's and the octahedron's pyramids exist besides the octet cut up and its rearrangements (Chapter 2)? (2) Do gluings of five or more pieces with the regular solids, or seven or more of DICTO's pieces alone, give a new deltahedron, and do icosahedra with Antonius fill space (Chapters 3 and 4)? (3) Which of the new deltahedra of Chapter 5 tile space, alone or with others? (4) The atlases of Chapters 6, 7 and 8 cover the four main directions; the slices in other directions are not mapped, and the possible Vacuus of the shadows beyond the four main views are not listed. (5) The exact heights of the White Queen's silent slices, and the exact value of μ* where the path between the Queens is numerically a 19-design (Chapter 9, §5). (6) The inherited-heart rule: is every shared sheet of a shape on a Queen's corners that is not centrally symmetric a wall of the Queen (Chapter 10, §1)? (7) Are the 1200 walls of the ten White Queens that are not the Red Queen's own the cross walls of her 225 inscribed 24-cells? (8) The chambers: the classes and types of the 41,197 pieces that the 1560 walls cut in one chamber of the Red Queen's mirrors, as for the 298 of the White Queen's 360 walls (Chapter 11; Appendix B, §9)? (9) The open problems of twisted silence: the degree-8 sets, the E8 degree-4 unions, an exhaustive dial, and a complete list of the special silences of the icosians by prime-defect label (Appendix A, §10); and the further conjectures and questions of the walls (Appendix B, §9).
