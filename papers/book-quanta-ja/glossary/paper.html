1. How the terms were chosen

Classical words take the standard Japanese term. Polyhedra, four-dimensional solids, packing and crystallography use the words of Japanese mathematics and materials science, checked against Japanese Wikipedia and Japanese technical sources (listed in Section 7). Where usage varies the table gives the pick first and the alternative after it. Two corrections to the first plan came out of the checks: the Archimedean solids are 半正多面体 (準正多面体 is reserved for the cuboctahedron and the icosidodecahedron), and the four-dimensional solids are written 600胞体, 120胞体, with the full forms 正六百胞体, 正百二十胞体 at first use. Where no Japanese term exists (the crystallographers' hexagonality, rectified, the excavated dodecahedron) the pick is a plain rendering and the English stays in brackets at first use.

DICTO's names keep their identity. A coined or proper name (Ogdoecontahedron, Cleo-Icos, Ganesha, Sonsam) is written in katakana, with the Latin name in brackets at its first use in each chapter. A name built from classical words (Belted Triacontahedron, skewed rhombic dodecahedron, Golden TI Star) is translated with the classical terms. Names already Japanese (Kagemusha 影武者, Ritsudō 律動, Nenrin 年輪, Hasu) are written in Japanese; Buddhist names take their established Japanese forms (業, 法, 菩提, 金剛乗, 金剛杵). Names in Latin capitals (AXE, CLEO, FUJI, VAJRA, UNITY, VENUS, DESHI, ATOM), abbreviations (OG, OTT, GTI, KRE …), DICTO himself and the app names stay in Latin. The Alice names follow the Japanese translations of Carroll (帽子屋, 三月ウサギ, ヘイヤ, ハッタ).

Numbers never change. D-numbers (D1 to D105), paper numbers (#8 to #78), finding numbers (F1 to F21), DOIs and formulas are copied as they stand; figure labels stay in English in this first edition.

Readings are given in hiragana for kanji terms; katakana needs none. Notes give the source for any term that is not obvious and the alternatives where usage varies.
