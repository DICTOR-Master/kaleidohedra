The tetrahedral networks of the close-packed stackings (diamond 3C, lonsdaleite 2H, the polytypes 4H, 6H, 9R, and every other stacking, periodic or not) differ in their shells of neighbours from the third shell on. In DICTO's study of stackings (#74) their closed non-backtracking loops turned out not to differ: the numbers of loops of each length from 6 to 34 per site were identical on five stackings, and #74 stated the Loop Equality as a conjecture for all lengths. Theorem 1. On the tetrahedral network of every close-packed stacking, at every site, the number of closed non-backtracking loops of length m is the number for diamond, for every m: 24, 48, 480, 3432, 25704, … for m = 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, …, given by the Ihara–Bass formula from the Domb numbers. The proof is short. In Fourier space over the layers, the stacking only decides whether a step across a bond layer uses a triangle of three bond directions or its point mirror, and a closed walk crosses every bond layer as often upwards as downwards, so the two choices cancel; non-backtracking loops are then reduced to ordinary walks between the two ends of a bond. The closed-walk part was known (Nagle 1966 for ice; Thorpe 1972 for all periodic polytypes). New here are the loops, the statement at every single site, aperiodic stackings, and two corollaries: the same holds on the pyrochlore network of every stacking (spin ice), and on the sphere packing of every close-packed stacking (fcc, hcp, …). A check of exact integer counts on 42 stackings, including 30 random ones, Fibonacci approximants and the aperiodic Fibonacci stacking, agrees at all 686 sites tested.
