Chapter 6 · Four dimensions

Four dimensions

The regular solids of four-dimensional space can be seen only by their shadows and slices. Four works follow two of them, the 600-cell and the 120-cell, which DICTO named the White Queen and the Red Queen, and the stages between them; a fifth (#89) finds the golden cuts of the Platonic solids among the slices of the four-dimensional solids. The full chapters of the four are in the book Alicia in Wonderland: The Red & White Queens (DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23753305).

#62Shadows of the Fourth Dimension

DICTO's idea that the shadows and slices of the four triangle-faced solids of four-dimensional space give new solids with equal triangular faces. Two are variants of known ones that join differently, one closing three round an edge, one filling space alone; four shapes of the 600-cell rebuild exactly with equal edges, among them Kagemusha (first called Shadow-17).

Full chapter: Alicia in Wonderland, Chapter 5. DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23687223

#63The White Queen 600-cell

An exact atlas of every slice and shadow of the 600-cell: 62 slice patterns, twelve made only of triangles, each shadow resolved into two exact halves, and round one corner the 600 cells in fifteen shells, each of one kind of piece, round a core of twenty Cleo-Icos. Alicia Boole Stott saw the first of these slices in 1900.

Full chapter: Alicia in Wonderland, Chapter 6. DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23690856

#76Between the Queens

DICTO's idea of the four-dimensional icosahedron with hexagons, and the stages that lead from one Queen to the other. Cutting the White Queen's corners deeper and deeper passes through five uniform solids, the truncated and rectified 600-cell, the bitruncated 600-cell at the midpoint (its cells truncated icosahedra and truncated tetrahedra) and the rectified and truncated 120-cell, all built from her own corners, with exact cut positions and a complete atlas of 1402 slices and their shadows.

Full chapter: Alicia in Wonderland, Chapter 7. DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23780068

#65The Red Queen 120-cell

The polar of the White Queen, built exactly from her: 120 dodecahedra in nine layers round one. All five of Aristotle's pyramids are the cores of the four-dimensional regular solids, seen in perspective, in polar pairs.

Full chapter: Alicia in Wonderland, Chapter 8. DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23716140

#89All Five Golden Pairs Are Slices of Four-Dimensional Solids

DICTO proposed carrying the golden cuts of #88 beyond the Platonic solids, and finding the remaining golden pairs in four dimensions. Slice the tesseract, the four-dimensional cube, straight across its longest diagonal, and every slice between its two middle layers of corners is a tetrahedron with its corners cut: at heights 1/φ² and 1/φ these are the Greater and Lesser Golden Cut Tetrahedra, either side of the Archimedean truncated tetrahedron at one half. The golden dodecahedra and tetrahedra are slices of the 120-cell as well, so the golden pairs of the dodecahedron, icosahedron, tetrahedron and octahedron are all slices of regular four-dimensional solids. The cubes are the exception, and it is proved: no slice of a regular four-dimensional solid is a cube with its corners cut. They live one step away, in the rectified tesseract, the four-dimensional cube cut back to the middles of its edges, and DICTO's filling by cut cubes and octahedra is a slice of the rectified tesseractic honeycomb. Beyond Plato golden cuts are rare: none on an Archimedean solid; the DICTO Golden-Regular Cuts of the icosidodecahedron and the snub dodecahedron, two each, every face golden or regular (for the icosidodecahedron, Zefiro's stages of 2011, found by DICTO independently); two each on the rhombic dodecahedron and the rhombic triacontahedron; and two on each of the four star polyhedra of Kepler and Poinsot, the Greater and Lesser Golden Cut Great Dodecahedron, Great Icosahedron, Small Stellated Dodecahedron and Great Stellated Dodecahedron. DICTO added the two lesser Golden-Regular Cuts to the DICTO-GTT-Solids, which are now five. Names by DICTO.
