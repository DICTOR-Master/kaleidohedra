The 4D permutohedra fill four-dimensional space, every one a translate of the same cell. Cut that filling by a three-dimensional slice and the cells it crosses fill the slice. In a rational direction the cut repeats; #105 left the golden direction u = (1, φ, φ², φ³, φ⁴) as its second lead.

Theorem 12.1. Cut the permutohedron filling by the hyperplanes u · x = c with u = (1, φ, φ², φ³, φ⁴), at a generic offset c. Then: (i) the cells of the slice are sections of one permutohedron at heights that are dense, so the slice repeats only along a plane, never in a third direction, and its cells come in infinitely many shapes; (ii) they come in exactly 35 combinatorial types, each occurring infinitely often. The 120 corners of the permutohedron lie at only 70 distinct golden heights: 50 coincide because φ² = φ + 1.

Proof. A cell's section is P ∩ {u · x = c − u · λ}, λ in the lattice; since u · λ runs over a set containing integers and integer multiples of φ, the offsets are dense; that the slice repeats along a plane and in no third direction, and that the shapes are infinitely many, is Theorem 12.2 (iii) and (i). The combinatorial type of a section is the set of faces of P that straddle the level, with their containments; it is constant between consecutive corner heights. The heights u · σ over the 120 orderings σ of −2, …, 2 were computed exactly in ℤ[φ] (φi = Fi−1 + Fiφ): 70 distinct values, 69 open bands; their straddling-face graphs fall into 35 isomorphism classes, and density puts cells in every band. ∎ (golden_types.py)

The golden direction sits between the two: it does not repeat in every direction, as a rational cut does, yet it has far fewer kinds than a generic cut, because the golden ratio makes corners meet at the same height. This is the permutohedron's own version of the passage from crystal to quasicrystal, in one direction only: a finite set of kinds, repeating along a plane and never across it. The projection (window) method that gives Penrose-type tilings answers whether the 35 kinds can be cut down to finitely many shapes (studies/2026-11-13-golden-slice-window, slice_window.py, exact in ℚ(φ), 40 checks, FINAL ok):

Theorem 12.2. (i) The golden slice cannot be cut down: in each of its 69 bands the section volume is a non-constant polynomial of the height, and the heights are dense, so every one of the 35 kinds occurs in infinitely many shapes, even up to congruence. (ii) Its window version has finitely many shapes. The corners are the projections of the lattice points whose cells the slice meets (window W = l(P), the interval from −3−7φ to 3+7φ). The tiles are the projected Delaunay tetrahedra of the crossed edges; they fill space face to face (the slice is a power diagram and they are its dual triangulation). There are 60 tile shapes up to translation (30 up to congruence) and exactly 69 corner configurations, one for each region of the window cut by the 70 corner heights. Up to point reflection these are the 35 kinds of Theorem 12.1. (iii) Both fillings repeat along the plane of 5(1,0,−2,1,0) and 5(0,1,0,−2,1), on which l vanishes, and in no third direction; no golden direction avoids this, since l(L) = 5ℤ + φℤ.

Proof. Here l(x) = u · x, P is the permutohedron of −2, …, 2 and L its lattice. (i) By Lawrence's volume formula the section volume in each band is a cubic (a quadratic in the middle band) in the height h with a nonzero coefficient of h³ or h², checked exactly; dense heights then give infinitely many volumes. (ii) For a point x of the slice, |x − λ|² = |x − π(λ)|² + dλ², so the slice is the power diagram of the projected lattice points with weights −dλ², and its dual is the regular triangulation by the projected Delaunay tetrahedra (Aurenhammer); this is the dualisation scheme of Oguey, Duneau and Katz and of Kramer and Schlottmann. The projection is one-to-one on L, so the tile shapes are the 240/4 = 60 translation classes of Delaunay tetrahedra; the tiles at a corner depend only on the band of its height, giving 69 configurations, all distinct, which pair under point reflection into the 35 kinds. Certificates on a patch of radius 50 (11 438 corners, 76 907 tiles): every inner face shared by exactly two tiles, covering number 1 at random points, exact volume balance. (iii) l(λ) = 0 for λ in L exactly on 5ℤ(1,0,−2,1,0) + 5ℤ(0,1,0,−2,1) (since φi = Fi−1 + Fiφ); these vectors map the filling and the slice to themselves. A third independent period of the window tiling would need W − l(μ) = W, so l(μ) = 0; three periods of the slice would leave finitely many shapes, against (i). Over ℚ(φ), l(L) has rank at most 2, so the periods have rank at least 2 in every golden direction. ∎
