The DICTO OG Family on Every Stacking

Twins, polytypes and a Fibonacci crystal: two general rules for the Ogdoecontahedron and its family in the tetrahedral–octahedral honeycomb stacked in any order, with exact kits for the classical polytypes and for a stacking that never repeats

The Ogdoecontahedron in the gyrated octet, the family of solids and their names are DICTO's [3]; taking the family to every stacking was proposed by Claude and adopted by DICTO, and the rules, the polytypes and the Fibonacci stacking are joint work. Computation, checking and drafting with Claude (Anthropic).
Abstract. The tetrahedral–octahedral honeycomb falls into layers, and each layer can be laid on the one below in two ways: as in the octet truss (c) or as its mirror image in the shared plane (h). Any word in c and h is a stacking, exactly as for close-packed crystals, whose polytypes 3C, 2H, 4H, 6H, 9R, 15R … are words of this kind. The DICTO Ogdoecontahedron (OG) sits exactly in the octahedra of the octet [2] and of the gyrated octet [3]; here we place it, and the solids of its family, in every stacking. Two rules decide it. The twin rule: a solid that is centre-symmetric and has a three-fold axis at right angles to the layers has as its mirror image in a layer plane the same solid turned 60°; unless it also has a mirror at right angles to the axis, this is a true twin. The fit rule: a solid that lies in its own layer and does not overlap its copies within the layer fits every stacking, whatever the word. Exactly in ℚ(√5): the OG, the DICTO OTT and the OCD Stella lie in their octahedra and fit every stacking with one cell kit; the DICTO Belted Triacontahedron (DBT) leaves its octahedron but not its layer, so it too fits every stacking (shown in [3] only for the gyrated octet); the golden envelope it was cut from fits the octet but overlaps at every h layer by exactly the volume of the two tips the DBT removes. The shares of space do not depend on the stacking: OG 1025/114 − 145√5/38 ≈ 45.89%, OTT 176/3 − 26√5 ≈ 52.89%, DBT 493/6 − 73√5/2 ≈ 55.02%. For the OG we give the kit per cell for 3C, 2H, 4H, 6H, 9R and 15R, and for the Fibonacci stacking (c → ch, h → c), which never repeats: there exactly (3 − √5)/2 of the layers are twin layers, and a cell needs 5 + √5 caps, (3 − √5)/2 twin caps, √5 − 1 tetrahedra and (3 − √5)/2 bipyramids, all exact.

1. Stackings of the honeycomb

Layers. Frame as in [2, 3]: the octahedron |x| + |y| + |z| ≤ R, R = 2φ/3; a cell of the honeycomb (one octahedron, two tetrahedra) has volume (32 + 16√5)/27. Cut at right angles to the three-fold axis (1,1,1), the honeycomb falls into layers one octahedron thick, the slabs (2k − 1)R ≤ x + y + z ≤ (2k + 1)R. The planes between layers hold the honeycomb's vertices, which are the centres of a close packing of spheres; the layers of the honeycomb lie between the layers of that packing.

Two steps. On a layer the next one lies either as in the octet truss (the step c, a translation) or as the mirror image of the layer in the plane they share (the step h). Across a c plane an octahedron meets tetrahedra; across an h plane octahedron meets octahedron and tetrahedron meets tetrahedron. Any word in c and h is a stacking: all c is the octet (fcc, 3C), all h the gyrated octet (hcp, 2H) [4, 5]. These are the h and c of Jagodzinski's notation for close packings [6] (c is often written k), and periodic words give the polytypes named by Ramsdell [7] and the IUCr [8].

Built and read back (exact). polytype_exact.py builds any word layer by layer with rational maps (a mirrored layer takes its next step downward in the reference frame and is then mirrored up), checks that layer k lies at x + y + z = 2kR, and reads the word back from the geometry: above an octahedron's top face lies an octahedron exactly at the h planes. The A/B/C sequence of the packing follows from the word (c keeps the turn A → B → C, h reverses it) and gives the Ramsdell symbols in Table 2.

2. The twin rule

Rule. Let a solid S, centred at the origin, be centre-symmetric (S = −S) and have a 120° turn about the axis n at right angles to the layers. Then its mirror image in a plane at right angles to n is S turned 60° about n.

Proof. The mirror M in the plane through the centre is the inversion composed with the half turn H about n: M = −H. With S = −S, M(S) = H(S). The half turn is three 60° turns, H = T⁶⁰∘T⁶⁰∘T⁶⁰ = T⁶⁰∘T¹²⁰, and T¹²⁰(S) = S, so H(S) = T⁶⁰(S). A mirror in a parallel plane differs by a translation only. ∎ The twin is a true twin (not S itself) unless S also has the mirror M as a symmetry. This is the spinel law of cubic crystals [9], where the twin is a half turn about ⟨111⟩; for a solid with the 120° turn the half turn and the 60° turn give the same twin.

The rule is checked exactly for the OG, the DICTO OTT, the DBT and the golden envelope (Table 1), with the rational 60° turn that takes (1,0,0) to (2/3, 2/3, −1/3); for the OCD Stella it was checked exactly in [3].

3. The fit rule

Rule. If a solid lies in its own layer, and its copies in the octahedra of one layer do not overlap, then it fits every stacking.

Why. Layers are disjoint slabs, so solids in different layers cannot overlap. Every layer of every stacking is the reference layer moved by a translation or by a mirror; a translated layer carries translated solids, and a mirrored layer carries the mirror images, which by the twin rule are the solids turned 60°. Within a layer nothing depends on the word. ∎ A solid inside its octahedron lies in its layer, so it fits every stacking with the cell kit of [3]: the octahedron cut into the solid and 8 caps by the planes through its centre and edges, the tetrahedra whole; at an h plane two caps join into a twin cap and two tetrahedra into a bipyramid.

The DBT. The DBT pokes out of its octahedron through its six side faces, into the tetrahedra of its own layer, but its two flat poles keep it inside the layer. In [3] it was shown to fill the gyrated octet's octahedra pole to pole without overlap; by the fit rule it fits every stacking. The exact overlap test agrees: with the reference DBT in the middle layer of three and every neighbour within reach, there is no overlap for any of the four pairs of boundaries cc, ch, hc and hh.

The golden envelope. Without its two pole cuts the DBT is the golden envelope of [2], a rhombic triacontahedron. Its two three-fold tips leave the layer. Across a c plane they meet tetrahedra and the envelope fits, as in the octet [2]; across an h plane the tips of two envelopes meet and the envelopes overlap, by exactly (−88 + 40√5)/81 ≈ 0.017811, which is the volume of the two tips the DBT cuts off. So the cut that makes the DBT is exactly what every stacking needs.

4. Polytypes

For the OG, the pieces per cell follow from the share f of h planes. Each cell has one plane between its layer and the next, so per cell there are f twin caps and f bipyramids, and the caps and tetrahedra that did not join: 8 − 2f caps and 2 − 2f tetrahedra, with the OG (or its twin) once. The volumes are those of [3]: cap (277 − 79√5)/1539, twin cap twice that, tetrahedron (16 + 8√5)/81, bipyramid twice that. The twin-cap counts were also counted from the geometry, layer by layer (polytype_exact.py), for every word in Table 2.

5. A Fibonacci crystal of OGs

Take the word grown by c → ch, h → c from c: c h c c h c h c c h c c h …. Its share of h tends to 1/φ² = (3 − √5)/2 (exactly 2584 of the first 6765 letters, both Fibonacci numbers), and since that number is irrational the word never repeats. No two h planes are adjacent and no three c planes follow each other, so the twin planes come 2 or 3 layers apart, in the Fibonacci order. By the fit rule the OG, the OTT, the OCD Stella and the DBT fill their places on this stacking exactly, and by Table 2 the OG crystal needs per cell 5 + √5 caps, (3 − √5)/2 twin caps, √5 − 1 tetrahedra and (3 − √5)/2 bipyramids: golden numbers, from the stacking alone. Each layer is periodic and the order of the layers is not, so the crystal is periodic in two directions and aperiodic in the third, with the same pieces as the octet and the gyrated octet. Fibonacci words are the standard example of aperiodic order in one dimension [10]; Fibonacci chains have also been used to describe heavily faulted stackings of ZnS (web search, Farkas-Jahnke; IUCr congress abstract, not read in full).

6. Prior art and what is claimed

  • Stackings. The two ways to lay one layer of the honeycomb on the next, and the infinitely many stackings this allows, are known [4, 5, 11]; the h/c description and polytype names are classical crystallography [6, 7, 8]; the spinel law is classical [9]. We claim none of these.
  • The twin rule and the fit rule are short consequences of symmetry, and we expect that crystallographers would recognise them; we state them for solids placed in the honeycomb and did not find them stated in this form.
  • Claimed: that the OG, the OCD Stella, the DICTO OTT and the DBT fit every stacking of the honeycomb, exactly, with the shares of space above (the DBT's fit beyond the gyrated octet is new here); that the golden envelope fails at h planes by exactly the volume the DBT removes; the exact OG kits for the polytypes; and the Fibonacci crystal of OGs with its golden piece counts.
  • A note on the DICTO Jewel. [3] reports that the DICTO Jewel, framed by cubes, does not twin; that concerns its packing. As a solid the Jewel is pyritohedral, so the twin rule applies to it too; what fails (float, [3]) is the Jewel's packing with stella octangulas across a (111) plane, which is not a packing in the layers of this honeycomb.

In the sources checked (the full text of the 80 library sources listed in the appendix, and web searches on 20 October 2026) we found the stackings and the notation but not these solids on them.
