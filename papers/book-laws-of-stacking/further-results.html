Locked Layers, the Fibonacci Spectrum and the Rock-Salt Pieces

Abstract. Three results on the tiles and the stackings of this book. (1) A single layer of HexOct c-tiles, or of h-tiles, holds itself together by shape alone: at λ = 1/10, with the exact tiles of Chapter 14 and the outer ring of a plate held fixed, every inner tile is locked against all small slides and twists, in the sense of the topological interlocking of Dyskin and co-workers. The test is calibrated both ways: their layer of tetrahedra is locked, a layer of upright cubes is free. (2) The c/h word of the Fibonacci stacking (Chapter 7) has a pure-point spectrum, every peak at q/φ² with amplitude |sin(πq/φ²)|/(πq), while the crystal it builds, read through the layer positions A, B and C, has no sharp peaks: its strongest features, at l = 1/2 and at l = cos 72°, fade as the crystal grows. (3) In rock salt on any stacking, the pieces of the cubic stretches are regular (cubes, or cubes with their corners cut by regular octahedra), and all the freedom of the stacking is carried by the few pieces next to hexagonal layers, which deform. DICTO proposed this reading of Figure 7.1: the freedom comes from the premise, and the pieces are allowed to deform. Results (1) and (2) are computed; the numbers of (3) are numerical (floating point), with exact forms still to be found.

Lay a floor of HexOct tiles and hold only its edge: no tile inside can be slid or turned out. Read the Fibonacci word as a signal and every one of its notes is sharp; read the crystal it builds and the notes blur. And where cubes and octahedra seem to stack freely, it is because a few other pieces give way.

1. Main result: the HexOct tiles interlock

Result 1 (HexOct tiles interlock; computed). Take the exact c-tile (layer ABC) and h-tile (layer AB) of Chapter 14 at λ = 1/10, each a rigid union of its four pieces, and lay one layer of either kind as a plate over the triangular lattice, with the outer ring of tiles held fixed. Then every inner tile is locked: no small rigid motion of the inner tiles, slide or twist, moves any of them without pressing into a neighbour. This holds for all 7 inner tiles of the c-tile plate and for all 7 inner tiles of the h-tile plate.

The test. It is the kinematic test of topological interlocking (Dyskin et al. 2003 [1]). The contact polygons between neighbouring tiles are computed exactly, by clipping; each inner tile is given an infinitesimal motion, a velocity and a turn (six numbers per tile), and a motion is allowed only if no tile moves into a neighbour across any shared face. A tile is locked when every allowed motion leaves it at rest. This is a first-order test.

Controls. Positive: Dyskin et al.'s layer of tetrahedra (square midsections, neighbours turned 90°): all 21 interior blocks locked, as published. Negative: a layer of upright cubes is free; each cube lifts out. The test separates the two.

Reading. A single HexOct layer of either type holds itself together by shape alone: the HexOct tiles are interlocking tiles, layer by layer, as well as stacking tiles. In Chapter 14 the word "interlocking" names how far the layers reach into each other; here it is the mechanical sense of Dyskin and co-workers, a layer that cannot come apart under small motions once its edge is held.

2. The spectrum of the Fibonacci stacking

Result 2 (computed). Let w be the c/h word of the Fibonacci stacking of Chapter 7, with hexagonal share 1/φ². (a) The spectrum of w is pure point: every peak sits at a frequency q/φ² (mod 1), with amplitude |sin(πq/φ²)|/(πq). (b) The crystal that w builds, read through its layer positions A, B and C on the rods with h − k ≢ 0 (mod 3), has no sharp (Bragg) peaks at its strongest features, l = 1/2 and l = cos 72° = (√5 − 1)/4 (l in units of one over the layer spacing): their amplitudes fall as the crystal grows.

The word. Computed on the word of length F28 = 317 811: the twelve strongest peaks (q = ±1, ±2, ±3, ±4, ±6, ±7) agree with |sin(πq/φ²)|/(πq) to 5 decimals, and the brightest pair sits at the two golden frequencies 1/φ² and 1/φ. A pure-point spectrum with this amplitude is the classical behaviour of a Sturmian word, of which the Fibonacci word is one (Baake and Grimm [2]). The word is harmonized to every degree, each harmonic with a closed-form strength.

The crystal. Besides l = 1/2 and l = cos 72°, the strongest features lie at l = 1 − cos 72°, with a dense cluster accumulating at l = 1/2. Their amplitudes on three crystals of growing length:

Reading. The word that decides straight or twisted is perfectly harmonic; the stacking it produces is not. A Fibonacci-stacked crystal would show structured rods without sharp peaks, strongest at l = 1/2 and at the golden positions cos 72° and 1 − cos 72°. The layer positions are a sum with three values carried along the word, and such a sum can smear a pure-point spectrum, as in the Thue–Morse word. Whether the crystal's spectrum is singular continuous is open (§5).

3. The rock-salt pieces

Figure 7.1 shows rock salt on the 16-layer Fibonacci stacking at weight λ = 1/3. DICTO saw in it cubes, tetrahedra and perhaps octahedra stacking freely together, against the limits that #47 proves for regular cubes, tetrahedra and octahedra, and proposed the answer: the freedom comes from the premise; let the pieces deform. This section works that answer out.

Result 3 (numerical). In a close-packed stacking with rock-salt sites (balls and octahedral holes of weight λ, tetrahedral holes of weight 0): (a) the pieces of the cubic stretches are regular: for λ ≥ 3/8 cubes of edge 1/√2; for λ < 3/8 cubes with their corners cut by regular octahedra of edge 2(3/8 − λ), the pieces of the tetrahedral holes (a non-uniform truncated cubic honeycomb); (b) all the freedom of the stacking is carried by the few pieces that touch a hexagonal layer, which deform, and their shape depends only on the kinds of the layers (the Rule of Halves, Chapter 1); (c) at none of the weights computed is any piece a regular tetrahedron: the tetrahedral holes give regular octahedra in cubic stretches and a 7-cornered piece (4 triangles and 3 squares, all edges equal) next to hexagonal layers.

All numbers of this section are numerical (floating point): power cells with the same sites and weights as Chapter 7, per kind of piece, for pure cubic, pure hexagonal and the 16-layer Fibonacci stacking. The octahedron edge 2(3/8 − λ) is matched at λ = 0.30, 1/3 and 0.35 (edges 0.15, 0.0833 and 0.05). The octahedra vanish at λ = 3/8, where the cubes close: 3/8 is HexOct's own end of the walk, where the c pieces become cubes (Chapter 16).

Against #47. There, regular cubes, tetrahedra and octahedra of edge 1 fill space only in flat slabs and never face to face (#47, Theorem 2). Here cubes and regular octahedra fill space together in every stacking order, face to face in the cubic stretches, because the pieces at hexagonal layers are free to take other shapes: for λ ≥ 3/8 the freedom of the stacking lives in two deformed pieces, the 14-cornered ball piece S:h and the 10-cornered hole piece O:ch. Their names are DICTO's to give.

4. What is known and what is new

Known: the kinematic test of topological interlocking and its layer of tetrahedra (Dyskin et al. 2003 [1]); the pure-point spectrum of Sturmian words such as the Fibonacci word [2]; the smearing of the Thue–Morse spectrum; the truncated cubic honeycomb and the power cells of rock salt; the per-layer split of the pieces, which is the Rule of Halves (Chapter 1). New within the sources checked: that both HexOct tiles lock as single layers (§1); the measured fading of the Fibonacci crystal's strongest features, against the sharp spectrum of its word (§2); the answer to DICTO's premise question, with regular cubes and octahedra in the cubic stretches and all the stacking's freedom in the pieces at hexagonal layers (§3).

5. Leads

  • Interlocking of mixed layers, and of thin plates of several layers; lower λ; the knitted and plate builds of the HexOct plates.
  • Decide whether the Fibonacci crystal's spectrum is singular continuous: a scaling exponent, and the literature on the diffraction of aperiodic polytypes and Fibonacci stackings. Compare with the per-ball c/h fingerprint of #94 (§4), which reads the word exactly.
  • Exact (rational and √2) forms of the rock-salt pieces at hexagonal layers; DICTO's names for the two deformed pieces; whether the 7-cornered piece of the tetrahedral holes has a classical name; prior art.
