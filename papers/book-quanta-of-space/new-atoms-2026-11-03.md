## 1. New space-filling sets

All four are in #89 Section 7. #89 states plainly: "All four start from fillings in Andreini's list [7], and the cut
cells with their holes are the golden members of these truncation patterns; we claim no new filling. No new pair fills
space on its own the way Icosaxes and Unborn Axes do [6]." The book must keep that wording: the new thing is which golden
solids sit in these patterns, not the patterns. Each set comes in a greater and a lesser version, one depth at a time;
the two versions do not mix.

### 1.1 Golden Cut Cubes + octahedra (DICTO's filling)

- **Pieces:** Greater Golden Cut Cubes (x = x_G(4) = (√10 + 2√5 − 3√2 − 2)/4 ≈ 0.347943) with regular octahedra of
  edge √2·x ≈ 0.492066; or Lesser Golden Cut Cubes (x = x_L(4) = (√10 + 3√2 − 2√5 − 2)/4 ≈ 0.233196) with regular
  octahedra of edge √2·x ≈ 0.329788. (Cube edge 1.)
- **Ratio:** 1 cut cube : 1 octahedron (one octahedron per lattice point, its eight parts cut from the eight cubes
  there). Face to face: each corner triangle of a cut cube is a face of its octahedron.
- **Status:** proved, #89 Proposition C, for every depth 0 < x < 1/2; exact at both golden depths (s10: corner
  triangle = octahedron face, volume per lattice point 1 − 8x³/6 + 4x³/3 = 1 exactly, cut cube = Golden Cut Cube corner to
  corner). DICTO proposed this filling, from the Rhombiverse app. Pattern: at x = 1/(2 + √2) it is Andreini's 1905 filling by
  truncated cubes and octahedra, so the pattern is Andreini's; the golden members are DICTO's.
- **Four dimensions:** #89 Corollary D: the section y1 = h of the rectified tesseractic honeycomb (rectified tesseracts and
  16-cells) is this filling at twice the size, x = h/2; the golden fillings are its sections at h = 2x_G(4) and 2x_L(4),
  exact (s10). No section of the three regular honeycombs contains a cut cube (Theorem A).
- **Source:** #89 Proposition C, Corollary D, Section 7; DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23917931.
- **Plain line:** Cut every cube in a stack of cubes at its corners and the eight cut-off corners meeting at each point
  make one small octahedron; at the two golden depths this is a slice of a four-dimensional honeycomb.

### 1.2 Golden Cut Rhombic Dodecahedra + cubes + tetrahedra

- **Pieces:** Greater Golden Cut Rhombic Dodecahedra (a = 0.477001, b = 0.294803) with cubes and regular tetrahedra of
  edge a; or Lesser Golden Cut Rhombic Dodecahedra (a = 0.322952, b = 0.522548) with cubes and regular tetrahedra of
  edge a. (Rhombic dodecahedron edge 1; a = corner sides, b = original sides of the golden octagons.)
- **Ratio:** 1 cut rhombic dodecahedron : 1 cube : 2 tetrahedra (the 6 four-fold corners close into cubes, 6 cells
  each; the 8 three-fold corners into tetrahedra, 4 cells each).
- **Status:** volume-checked (s6, floating point: 6 sharp caps = cube a³, 4 blunt caps = tetrahedron, both depths).
  Pattern from Andreini 1905 (the filling by rhombic dodecahedra, truncated).
- **Source:** #89 Section 7 (Table 2 for a, b); DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23917931.
- **Plain line:** Cut Kepler's rhombic dodecahedra at their golden depth and the holes are small cubes and tetrahedra.

### 1.3 Golden Cut Tetrahedra + Icosaxes (or Unborn Axes) + cuboctahedra

- **Pieces:** Greater Golden Cut Tetrahedra + Icosaxes + cuboctahedra of edge x = 1/φ² ≈ 0.381966; or Lesser Golden
  Cut Tetrahedra + Unborn Axes + cuboctahedra of edge x = 1/(φ√5) ≈ 0.276393. (Tetrahedron and octahedron edge 1; both
  have triangle faces, so their golden cuts have the same x.)
- **Ratio:** per lattice point 2 cut tetrahedra : 1 Icosaxes (or Unborn Axes) : 1 cuboctahedron (8 tetrahedron caps +
  6 octahedron caps close into one cuboctahedron).
- **Status:** volume-checked (s6, floating point, both depths). Pattern from Andreini 1905 (the tetrahedra-octahedra
  filling, truncated).
- **Source:** #89 Section 7; DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23917931.
- **Plain line:** Cut the octet's tetrahedra and octahedra at the same golden depth and DICTO's Icosaxes (or Unborn
  Axes) stand between golden tetrahedra, with a cuboctahedron at every meeting point.

### 1.4 Truncated octahedra with one face kind golden + tetragonal disphenoids

- **Pieces:** Archimedean truncated octahedra (edge 1) cut so that one kind of face is golden, four cuts (#89 Table 1):
  squares golden at x_G(4) ≈ 0.347943 or x_L(4) ≈ 0.233196; hexagons golden at x_G(6) ≈ 0.325683 or
  x_L(6) ≈ 0.208224; with tetragonal disphenoids of edges √2·x and √3·x.
- **Ratio:** 1 cut truncated octahedron : 6 disphenoids (24 corners, 4 cells at each).
- **Status:** volume-checked (s6, floating point, all four cuts: 4 caps = one disphenoid). Pattern from Kelvin's filling
  by truncated octahedra (Andreini 1905).
- **Source:** #89 Section 7; DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23917931.
- **Plain line:** Cut Kelvin's cells so their squares, or their hexagons, are golden, and the gaps are small wedge-like
  tetrahedra.
- **Note:** these four cut truncated octahedra have no names in #89; they are not proposed as atoms here.

### 1.5 Further fillings in #88 and #89

- **#88:** none new. #88 Section 5 restates that Icosaxes and Unborn Axes fill space together, which is #48's Theorem 4
  (T(x) and T(1/x) fill space for every x > 0; DICTO DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23596362), already in the book under #48 The
  Golden Split. #88 adds that this pair is exactly the octahedron's two golden cuts, and announces a third family for the
  pair, the DICTO Equilibrium Space-Fillers, "to be set out in a later paper" (not yet published; do not list as a family
  in the book until then).
- **#89:** the only further filling is the four-dimensional one, the rectified tesseractic honeycomb (Corollary D), which
  is the 4D home of 1.1 rather than a new 3D set. #89 states that no new pair fills space on its own.

**DICTO Golden-Regular Cuts (new family; every face golden or regular), six solids:**
- Greater Golden Cut Icosidodecahedron (x = 1/3: pentagons golden, triangles regular hexagons). Status: Zefiro 2011's

  credited. In the catalogue: rediscovered.
- Lesser Golden Cut Icosidodecahedron (x = 1/(φ√5): triangles golden truncated triangles, pentagons regular decagons).
  Status: Zefiro 2011's regular-decagon stage, as above; rediscovered. Also a DICTO-GTT-Solid. Not White Knight (same

- Lesser Golden Cut Snub Dodecahedron, left and right (x = 1/(φ√5)). Status: new within #89's checks. Also a
  DICTO-GTT-Solid.
  (Two hands: the catalogue's practice for chiral shapes decides one entry or two.)

**DICTO-GTT-Solids, now five:** Icosaxes, Unborn Axes, White Knight, Lesser Golden Cut Icosidodecahedron, Lesser Golden

## 3. Where in the book each would go

The book has six chapters (Atoms, Bonds, Shells, Cycles, Stacking, Four dimensions), then Findings and apps; works
from #52 on are told in short (short-entries.html). Proposed places, following that practice:

- **Chapter 1, Atoms, "All the atoms" (atoms-catalogue.html, "DICTO's new atoms"):** the eight new #88 solids and the
  ten new #89 solids above (4 rhombic, 4 snub in two hands, 2 icosidodecahedral as rediscovered), in order of
  publication (#88 before #89), D·I·C·T·O order within #88. Family notes on Icosaxes, Unborn Axes and White Knight.
  The two Golden Cut Icosidodecahedra could instead go in the book's closing list "Known and rediscovered shapes in the
  work" (Zefiro 2011); the catalogue's status column already handles "rediscovered".
- **Chapter 1, Atoms, short entry #88 "Every Platonic Solid Has Exactly Two Golden Cuts":** after #49 The Polyhedral
  Stretching Theorem, before the catalogue. Plain line: each regular solid has exactly two corner cuts that make every
  face golden; ten solids, the octahedron's two being DICTO's Icosaxes and Unborn Axes.
- **Chapter 2, Bonds, short entry for the fillings of #89 (sets 1.1 to 1.4):** after #50 One Move Each (the classical
  cut-and-glue fillers), with #89's own caveat that the patterns are Andreini's and the golden members are new; set 1.3
  also links back to #48 The Golden Split.
- **Chapter 6, Four dimensions, short entry #89 "All Five Golden Pairs Are Slices of Four-Dimensional Solids":** after
  #65 The Red Queen 120-cell. Plain line: the golden pairs of the dodecahedron and icosahedron are slices of the 120-cell,
  the tetrahedra of the tesseract, the octahedra (Icosaxes, Unborn Axes) of the 24-cell, and the cubes, which no regular
  four-dimensional solid has as a slice (Theorem A), of the rectified tesseract; DICTO's cube-and-octahedron filling is a
  slice of the rectified tesseractic honeycomb.
- **Introduction, "Between the repeating and the never-repeating" or "What the pieces show"** (optional, one sentence):
  the golden heights 1/φ² and 1/φ return in four dimensions.
