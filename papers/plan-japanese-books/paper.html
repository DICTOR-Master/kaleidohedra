2. Decisions (picks; change any)

  • Glossary first. One table of every term and name before any chapter: standard Japanese terms for the classical words (正多面体, 菱形十二面体, 最密充填, 積層, 六方晶 …), checked against Japanese crystallography and geometry usage; DICTO's names in katakana with the Latin name at first use (e.g. クレオ・イコス Cleo-Icos), D-numbers unchanged; the law as 「六方性の法則」 (Hexagonality Law), with the crystallographers' term for hexagonality checked. The glossary is the one document DICTO reads before the chapters.
  • Title. 『空間の量子 ― マニフェスト』 (Quanta of Space: A Manifesto); the English title on the title page too.
  • Translation. Chapter by chapter: draft by the local model on dicto-big-brother (gpt-oss-120b, already installed), with the glossary as fixed input; then my check of every sentence against the English (numbers, formulas, names, claims, nothing added or lost); formulas and figures are not translated by the model.
  • Native reader. Before release, a native Japanese reader reads the whole book once (DICTO's choice of reader: himself or a friend). Machine-drafted Japanese can be correct and still sound foreign; this read fixes the tone.
  • Credit lines. As in the English Quanta: "DICTO is the artist name of (Alan) J. Baker" and "For my father, Alan Baker" stay in Quanta only, translated; every other Japanese book says DICTO only.
  • Layout. Horizontal writing (横書き), A4 like the English, same figures and plates; text in Noto Serif JP, headings in Noto Sans JP; formulas unchanged. Figure labels stay in English in this first edition, with Japanese captions; text-heavy figures (the catalogue cards) get Japanese labels.
  • Release. A separate Zenodo record per Japanese book, linked to the English concept record as its translation, CC BY-SA 4.0, ORCID, community dicto; a 日本語 link on the book's card on dictospheres.com (Japanese texts stay on dictospheres.com). Amazon KDP (Japan) joins the held final set.
