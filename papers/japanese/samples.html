#57 The Rule of Halves: the Holes of Every Close-Packed Stacking

二分則：全ての最密充填積層における空隙

English (live on Zenodo)日本語（案）
Stack layers of balls and the gaps between them are small octahedra and tetrahedra. Stack them in the cubic way (ABCABC…) or the hexagonal way (ABAB…) and these gaps are arranged differently; crystals also use countless mixed stackings, such as ABAC or ABCACB. In papers #51, #53 and #56 we slid one number, a weight, between the two kinds of gap and followed how space is shared out among them: the web of bonds. Here we follow it on every stacking at once, and find a simple rule. A tetrahedral gap always takes the piece it would have in the pure stacking of the layer it sits closest to. An octahedral gap sits half-way between two layers, and its piece is two halves: the top half is the top half of the cubic or the hexagonal piece, as the layer above decides, and the bottom half likewise from the layer below. So every stacking, of any length or pattern, is built from the two pure ones, and the kinds of piece are finite: we list them all, with exact volumes. Where a cubic layer meets a hexagonal one, octahedral pieces appear that are half cubic and half hexagonal, which DICTO calls the Janus c/h pieces ; two of them he named Chucky and Fang ; and its volume is exactly the mean of its two parents'. The proof is exact, with one bound done by hand. The rule was not found in the sources checked; its classical counterpart for the balls themselves, that each ball's surroundings are set by its own layer, is the reason the h/c notation of crystallography exists. The ideas are DICTO's: following the web of bonds onto the other close-packed stackings (25 October 2026), and the names Chucky, Fang and Triskelion Tetrahedron, and the set name Janus c/h pieces. #59 The Icosaxes Hexa: Bloom, Bud and Seed

Icosaxes Hexa：Bloom、Bud、Seed（開花・蕾・種）

English (live on Zenodo)日本語（案）
The DICTO Hexa (#18) is eight DICTO Jewels that pass into each other until they close into one block. DICTO asked for the same move with icosahedral symmetry. Twenty Icosaxes, DICTO's golden truncated octahedra (#48), placed on the corners of a dodecahedron and slid towards the centre first touch as the Icosaxes Bloom, with twelve windows (at distance φ²√3/2); at exactly 1/φ of that distance the windows close into the Icosaxes Bud, sealed round a hollow; at exactly one third the hollow fills: the Icosaxes Seed, one solid (with Lotus Jewel Axes: at 1/φ³). Proved exactly. In the lattice, eight Icosaxes round an Unborn Axes make one solid (the same as nine Icosaxes passing through each other, along the whole golden bond from Kelvin onwards), and these blocks form a Kagome network in which every Icosaxes is shared by exactly two blocks; keyed with Tricaps, its piece has volume exactly φ². The idea is DICTO's; the names are DICTO's working names. Computation and proof with Claude (Anthropic); a first draft by a local open model (gpt-oss-120b), checked line by line. #41 The DICTO Jewel

DICTO Jewel（DICTO ジュエル）

English (live on Zenodo)日本語（案）
Put Euclid's dodecahedron on a cube, as roofs on its six faces, and carve away the stella octangulas of the six neighbouring cubes: what remains is the DICTO Jewel , with 38 corners and 60 faces, twelve of them thick Penrose rhombi (the windows) and 48 triangles, and volume exactly 3/16 at the scale where a 2 × 2 × 2 block of cells (the Hexa's cube, §4) has edge 1, or 12 on cells of edge 2. Jewels and stella octangulas on the other cells fill space in the rock-salt pattern, 3 : 1 by volume [2]; we prove this exactly: each stella's cube is the stella and six carved roofs of exactly 1/96 each. DICTO's clusters [2, 10], named here as single solids: the DICTO Jewel-Tetra (4 Jewels, 228 faces, 3/4), whose copies share out all Jewels, and the DICTO Jewel-Octa (6 Jewels and the stella that exactly fills their hole, 19/16), which tiles space with loose stellas (stellas inside no piece) on exactly 12 lattices of translations, all one tiling up to its symmetry. Together they sit in the pattern of the octet truss, eight Jewel-Tetras round each Jewel-Octa, but as overlapping solids, not as a tiling: without overlap at most one stella in six can be inside a Jewel-Octa, and the 12 lattices reach that bound. The third, the DICTO Jewel-Rhombi , is eight Jewels on the corners of the face-centred cubic cell, meeting on 18 windows, with the stella sealed inside (396 faces, 25/16). Each of the three tiles space with loose stellas (on 4, 12 and 25 lattices of translations; 2, 1 and 9 up to symmetry); none can without stellas, since Jewels are only three quarters of space. The DICTO Hexa and DICTO Hexa-Key [12] (5/4 and 3/4, filling space 5 : 3, two windows between neighbouring Hexas), checked numerically in [12], are proved exactly. As a solid the Jewel is pyritohedral, so it twins like the Ogdoecontahedron [5]; its packing does not twin by a (111) mirror, because that mirror does not keep the cube lattice.
