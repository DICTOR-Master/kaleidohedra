The Hexagonality Law panel

Five decisions before building · 30 October 2026

The task: on 28 October you proposed that the papers open in a Hexagonality Law app, and decided it should be a panel in Kaleidohedra's Stackings world, with the design decided before building. Each decision below has a recommended answer. A reply such as "1A 2A 3A 4A 5A" (or "all recommended") is enough to start.

1. What the panel shows

A (recommended): one chart: a material's measured quantity against h, the share of hexagonal layers, with the law's straight line and a dot for each real stacking of that material. Tapping a dot builds that stacking in the world beside it.

B: a table of materials and their values, no chart.

2. Which materials

A (recommended): one chart from each paper of the law, the same charts as the site cards, so the panel and the papers match:

PaperChart

#68volume per atom pair of silicon carbide, silicon and germanium stackings

B: start with silicon carbide (#68) only, add the rest later.

3. How it opens

A (recommended): a "Law" button in the Stackings world's panel opens the chart above the stacking; a row of buttons, one per paper, picks the chart. Nothing already in the world changes.

B: a separate view mode replacing the column of holes while the chart is shown.

4. Your own stacking on the chart

A (recommended): the stacking built in the world (including layers you flip) shows as a gold marker on the line at its own h, moving as you flip layers: the law's prediction for that stacking.

B: real materials' dots only.

5. Links from the papers

A (recommended): the cards of #66–#74 on dictospheres.com get "View in app" buttons that open the world with that paper's chart showing.

B: no links for now.

All data come from the papers' own study folders, unchanged; no new claims. Built and tested on phone and iPad before "into the deploy queue?".
