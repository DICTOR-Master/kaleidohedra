# Candidate (a): #102 v6, Section 5, as it stands
(Setting in common.md.)

Theorem A (half-turn twist bijection). Cut G_s at the vertical bonds of bilayer k. For a polygon through v that uses exactly two of these bonds, at crossing points p, q, replace the part of the polygon on the far side of the cut by its image under the half-turn about (p+q)/2 (more generally any involution in the toggle group fixing the crossing set and far pairing). This is a length- and site-preserving bijection onto the corresponding polygons of the stacking with bilayer k's letter flipped (c <-> h). Corollary: for every length l and every site, the polygons crossing every bilayer at most twice (with a fixed crossing profile) are equinumerous in every close-packed tetrahedral stacking. Proof: short (lattice coset argument plus Whitney-type surgery).

Theorem B (exact local flip formula). Flipping bilayer k changes p_l(v) by Delta = -1/2 * sum over (n, S, M, M') of A_root(S, M, R) * A_far(S, M', F), R + F + n = l, where A measures the failure of one half-network's linkage counts to be invariant under a toggle half-turn. For n = 4 crossings A depends only on pairs of arcs that meet, so it is a finite local table; six-crossing terms vanish for l <= 20 by hand.

Theorem 5 (now proved, up to diamond's constants). At every site of every stacking, p_l equals diamond's count for l <= 16; at l = 18 and 20, p_l = diamond's value minus an exact linear form in the c/h letters of four nearby bilayers (e.g. upper-atom form p_18 = 349236 - 24 h[-1] - 42 h[0] - 30 h[1] - 12 h[2]). Proof: Theorem B plus finite tables (about 1000 arcs / 7700 meeting pairs at l = 18; 6756 / 74493 at l = 20), checked for all neighbouring letter patterns. The reason 18 is first: the first half-network asymmetries (6 bonds) all sit on one pairing, so the first nonzero product needs 6 + 8 + 4 bonds.
