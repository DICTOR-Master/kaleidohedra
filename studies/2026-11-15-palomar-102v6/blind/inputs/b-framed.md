# Candidate (b): the same results, framed against an outside question
(Setting in common.md; theorems exactly as in candidate (a), repeated here.)

Theorem A (twist bijection, all lengths): polygons crossing every bilayer at most twice are equinumerous, length by length and site by site, in every close-packed tetrahedral stacking (diamond, lonsdaleite, all polytypes, aperiodic). Theorem B: exact local formula Delta = -1/2 sum A_root * A_far for the change under one c/h flip. Theorem 5: polygon counts through a site agree with diamond's for l <= 16 and first differ at l = 18 by an exact linear law in the letters of four nearby bilayers (also at l = 20).

Framing offered by the submitter. The question whether stacking (hexagonal vs cubic) affects lattice statistics of tetrahedral and close-packed networks is stated in print by others:
- Xu, Lin, Zhang, arXiv:2511.22477 (v3, June 2026), Introduction: "Despite the long history of studies on ice-type models, one of the long-standing unsolved problems concerns whether the residual entropy of hexagonal ice (Sh) equals that of cubic ice (Sc)." Abstract: "While analytical studies have established the inequality Sh >= Sc, numerical investigations suggest that the two values are very close."
- Benito, Urrutia, Karayiannis, Laso, Crystals 13 (2023) 1316, abstract: comparing HCP and FCC (both coordination 12), "once a critical number of steps is reached, one of the crystals shows a higher number of SAWs compatible with its geometry. We explain the observed trends in terms of the bending and torsion angles as imposed by the geometric constraints of the crystal lattice." (Search summaries of the paper report the counts agree up to N = 5 and that an assumption of equal hcp/fcc counts was shown false.)
The submitter's claim: Theorems A, B and 5 give the first exact, structural account of where and why stacking first enters cycle statistics of the tetrahedral (ice/diamond) network. Note honestly: the results concern simple cycles (polygons) of the diamond-type network, not the ice-rule entropy itself and not SAWs on sphere packings; they do not decide Sh = Sc.
