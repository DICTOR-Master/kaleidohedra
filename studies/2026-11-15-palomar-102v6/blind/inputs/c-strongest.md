# Candidate (c): the strongest version reachable soon
(Setting in common.md; Theorems A, B, 5 as in candidate (a).)

Added statement C (sphere packings; proof sketch, not yet computer-checked). Let P_s be the contact graph of the close-packed sphere packing (Barlow packing) of stacking s: layer k is the coset s_k, each sphere has 6 in-layer and 3 + 3 interlayer neighbours (s = ABC... is fcc, AB... is hcp). Cut P_s between layers k and k+1. For a self-avoiding walk from v (or a polygon through v) crossing this gap exactly twice, by edges (x1, y1), (x2, y2) with x_i in layer k, the half-turn g about (x1+x2)/2 maps the coset s_k to itself and swaps the other two cosets, so applying g to the far part flips the c/h letter of layer k only, and g(y1) = x1 + x2 - y1 is a neighbour of x2 and g(y2) a neighbour of x1; for walks crossing exactly once, use the half-turn about x1. This gives a length-preserving involutive bijection. Corollary C: for every length N, the SAWs from a site (and the polygons through it) that cross every interlayer gap at most twice are equinumerous on fcc, hcp and every Barlow packing; the whole fcc/hcp difference in SAW counts is carried by walks crossing some gap at least three times.

Outside context: Benito, Urrutia, Karayiannis, Laso, Crystals 13 (2023) 1316, abstract: "once a critical number of steps is reached, one of the crystals shows a higher number of SAWs compatible with its geometry. We explain the observed trends in terms of the bending and torsion angles as imposed by the geometric constraints of the crystal lattice." (Summaries: counts agree up to N = 5; the assumed equality of hcp/fcc counts is false.) Also: Xu–Lin–Zhang arXiv:2511.22477 call Sh = Sc for ice "one of the long-standing unsolved problems". Statement C plus a Theorem-B-type local formula for SAWs would give an exact account of the first hcp/fcc SAW difference; that local formula is NOT yet done, and none of this decides Sh = Sc.
