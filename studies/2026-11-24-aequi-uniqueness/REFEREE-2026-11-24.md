## 1. Is the g-test Alexandrov's condition here? Yes.

Checked independently of #112, against the Lean definition:
`AlexandrovCondition n κ` says, for every nonempty proper index set I, Σ_{i∈I} κ_i < area of
{u : n_i·u > 0 for some i in I}. That region is the sphere less the polar of I.

- I in an open hemisphere: the polar of I is the polar polygon of the spherical hull, of area 2π − L (sides ↔
  angles, Girard; a point gives a hemisphere, L = 0; an arc of length ℓ gives a lune of area 2π − 2ℓ, L = 2ℓ).
  With κ = 4π/N the condition is 4πa/N < 2π + L, which is g(A) < 0. ✓
- I in no open hemisphere: then 0 is a convex combination of points of I, so the polar lies in a plane and has
  area 0; the condition is a < N, true for proper I. ✓ (This is where "not in a closed hemisphere" is needed for
  the theorem itself, not for the condition.)
- Hulls that contain further directions: no loss, since the proof bounds g(A) for **every** subset A in an open
  hemisphere, and a larger A with the same hull is such a subset. ✓

So Proposition 2 is right and quantifies over the right sets; a, L, A are as the proof uses them.

## 2. Step by step

**Step 1 (centre).** t = max_c min_{n∈A} n·c > 0, R = arccos t ∈ [0, π/2). Correct. KKT: stationarity gives
Σλ_n = 1 and Σλ_n n = 2νc; ν > 0 because a nonnegative combination of points in an open hemisphere with Σλ = 1 is
nonzero, and |c| = 1 at the optimum because t > 0. So c is a positive combination of active points. Correct.
One active point ⇒ c = p ⇒ every n in A has n·c ≥ 1 ⇒ A = {p}. Correct (uses distinctness).
*Gap 1.*

**Step 2 (balance).** b ≥ |Σ_{n∉A} n| = |Σ_{n∈A} n| ≥ Σ_{n∈A} n·c ≥ a cos R. Correct, for every A and every R.
(a−b)/(a+b) is decreasing in b, so (2a−N)/N ≤ (1−cos R)/(1+cos R) = tan²(R/2). Correct.

**Step 3 (perimeter ≥ 4R).**
- Two active points: c is the midpoint of the minor arc pq (2R < π), the hull of A contains that arc, so
  L ≥ 4R. Correct; equality holds exactly when the hull is the arc.
- Three: cos s = cos²R + sin²R cos φ, so sin(s/2) = sin R sin(φ/2). ✓ h'' recomputed:
  h'' = α(α²−1) sin x / (1−α² sin²x)^{3/2} ≤ 0 on [0, π/2]. ✓ The feasible set {0 ≤ φ_i ≤ π, Σφ_i = 2π} is the
  triangle with vertices the permutations of (π, π, 0). ✓ Minimum of a concave sum at a vertex: 2R + 2R + 0. ✓
- Perimeter is monotone under inclusion of convex sets in an open hemisphere (polars nest the other way, area
  of polar = 2π − perimeter). ✓ Also valid when the smaller set is an arc or a triangle with c on a side.
*Gap 2.*

**Step 4.** a = 1: g = 2π(2−N)/N < 0. ✓ a ≥ 2 ⇒ two distinct points ⇒ R > 0; open hemisphere ⇒ R < π/2. So
only R in the open interval (0, π/2) occurs: **R ≥ π/2 never has to be treated**, because such A lie in no open
hemisphere and fall under the second bullet of §1. f(R) = 2π tan²(R/2) − 4R has f(0) = f(π/2) = 0 and is
strictly convex, so f < 0 inside. ✓ (Grid check, 2·10⁶ points: max f = −4·10⁻⁶ at the left end.)
Equalities b = a cos R (regular tetrahedron, a = 3) and L = 4R (every a = 2) do occur, and do no harm: the strict
sign comes from f, not from Steps 2–3.

**Closing line.** Balanced and not on one great circle ⇒ in no closed hemisphere. ✓ (Conversely, balanced and on
a great circle fails, so the hypothesis is exactly right.) N ≥ 4 is implied (three balanced unit vectors are
coplanar), so it is harmless but redundant.

**From the condition to the die.** Existence: the condition gives a polytope with corners r_k n_k, r_k > 0,
curvature 4π/N at each; positive curvature makes each a true corner; its polar is the die, every face present,
0 inside, Ω_k = 4π/N by the polar identity. Uniqueness up to scale: Alexandrov's uniqueness on the polars. The
derivation in #112 Theorem A (a) is correct as written. *Gap 4.*

## 3. Gaps and fixes

1. **Step 1, the "two or three active points" split is asserted, not derived.** Fix: add "by Carathéodory's
   theorem for cones in R³, c is a positive combination of at most three linearly independent active points;
   one, two or three give the cases." (With three, c is strictly inside their cone, so the three gaps are < π;
   ≤ π is all the proof uses.)
2. **Step 3, the three-point case does not say that s(0) = 0 is allowed though the points are distinct.** The
   bound per ≥ 4R is taken over the closed polytope, so it holds a fortiori; one clause ("the minimum over the
   closed set bounds the value at any admissible gaps") closes it. Also state that perimeter monotonicity is
   used for nested convex sets inside one open hemisphere (true here, since the triangle lies in the hull of A).
3. **Remark ("sharp in the limit only: f → 0 as R → π/2") is incomplete.** g also tends to 0 as R → 0: for
   N = 4, two close pairs (±cos α, ·, ·) with α → 0 off a great circle give g = −4R → 0 (found numerically,
   g = −4·10⁻⁴). So there is no uniform margin at either end. The theorem is unaffected (strict for each finite
   set); reword the remark, and do not claim a margin anywhere in #112.
4. **Citations.** The theorem numbers (Oliker Theorems 4 and 6; Bertrand Theorem 1.2) and the exact form of the
   condition in those papers were not re-checked by this referee, and Alexandrov (1942) was not opened by the
   authors. Fix: at the point of use, cite the Lean statements as the primary support (`fair_die_iff`,
   `fair_die_unique`, 0 sorry by grep; their condition is verified in §1 to be the g-test), and keep Alexandrov
   as "cited via Oliker and Bertrand". Lean hypotheses match: unit, injective, and "for every x ≠ 0 some
   n_i·x > 0" (= in no closed hemisphere).

Not gaps, but worth a word in the paper: Corollary B1 needs the directions distinct and "not in one plane
through the centre"; the orbit argument (gP = λP, equal volume, λ = 1) is correct.

## 4. Attempts at a counterexample (all failed)

Double precision, exhaustive over **all** subsets A (N ≤ 12), each with its exact max-min centre (best of all
point, pair-midpoint and triple-circumcentre candidates), hull perimeter by gnomonic projection. For each A in an
open hemisphere the three claims were tested separately: b − a cos R ≥ 0, L − 4R ≥ 0, g < 0.

| family | sets | largest g | least b − a cos R | least L − 4R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| regular tetrahedron; triangular prism (N = 5) | 2 | −2.59 | 0 (equality) | 0 (rounding) |
| N = 4, two-parameter family, down to 10⁻⁵ from a great circle | 20 | −4.0·10⁻⁴ | +1·10⁻⁸ | −2·10⁻¹² (rounding) |
| random balanced, N = 5 … 10 | 315 | −1.40 | +0.066 | rounding |
| three tight clusters (spread 10⁻³), N = 6, 8, 10 | 70 | −1.19 | +0.055 | rounding |
| within 10⁻⁴ of a great circle, N = 6, 8, 10 | 70 | −0.96 | +0.080 | rounding |
| most points in one cap, N = 7, 9 | 80 | −1.74 | +0.24 | rounding |
| extremal: regular a-gon at colatitude R with b near the opposite pole, a cos R = b cos ε, N ≤ 12 | 15 | −1.02 | 0 (equality) | rounding |
| unions of two D₃ orbits (not even), N = 12 | 12 | −3.27 | +2.18 | rounding |
| **control, unbalanced** (7 of 10 in a small cap) | 1 | **+1.99** (condition fails, as it should) | | |

"Rounding" means |value| ≤ 3·10⁻¹², on a = 2 sets where L = 4R exactly.

End to end: for random balanced, non-even sets with N = 5, 7, 9, 12, 20 the polar was solved directly
(curvature = angle defect; multiplicative update of the radii). Each converged to all curvatures 4π/N within
10⁻¹², depth ratios 1.2–1.5, every corner present. The unbalanced control diverges (radius ratio > 10⁶).
Note for anyone repeating this: with too large a step the iteration oscillates and reports a missing corner;
that is the solver, not the theorem.

Not done: exact or interval arithmetic for the numerics; exhaustive subsets beyond N = 12.
