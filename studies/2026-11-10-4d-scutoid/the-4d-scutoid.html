The 4D Scutoid

A vertical cut through a stacking of close-packed holes (ACABC, layer spacing s = 1/5). In the cut every hole's cell is a flat polygon. Standing those polygons up along λ, the weight that lifts the tetrahedral holes into the 4th dimension, turns every hole into a column with flat faces. Move the cut up through λ and watch the gold cell gain two neighbours at the wall.

What is exact. Every power-diagram plane moves linearly with λ, so each column is a polyhedron with flat faces, and its cut at any λ is the hole's cell there. The wall at λ = −5s/8 is proved for every spacing s from 1/8 to 1/4 (#61): below it octahedral holes still touch across a whole layer; at the wall they let go and tetrahedral holes meet instead. In this cut the gold cell keeps its six neighbours and gains two at the wall: the green columns begin as wedges exactly there, the scutoid's Y-corner one dimension up.
