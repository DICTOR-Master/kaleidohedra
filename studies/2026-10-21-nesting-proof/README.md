# Golden nesting of the OG tower: theorem, proof, exact constants (#43 DICTO Kyushindo Cores, 2026-10-21)

Setting (paper #38/#39/#43). S a convex polytope with the centre O inside. Faces n_i.x <= h_i (unit normals, h_i > 0), foot points
f_i = h_i n_i, reverse step P(S) = hull{f_i}, forward step T(S) = {x : v.x <= v.v for all corners v}. R(S) = largest corner
distance, r(S) = smallest face distance (inradius about O), H(S) = largest face distance. Downward family D_0 = OG, D_k = P(D_{k-1});
golden tower S_k = phi^k D_k. Nesting S_{k-1} inside S_k  <=>  D_{k-1} inside phi P(D_{k-1})  <=>  t_min(D_{k-1}) <= phi, where

    t_min(S) = least t with S inside t.P(S) = max over corners v of S and faces G of P(S) of (m_G.v)/h_G.

## Theorem A (containment constant, any convex polytope)

    max_v |v|^2 / h_P(v)  <=  t_min(S)  <=  R(S)/r(P(S))  <=  (R(S)/r(S))^2,        h_P(v) = max_i f_i.v / |v| (support of P(S)).

Equality throughout for the five Platonic solids: 9 (tetrahedron), 3 (cube, octahedron), 15 - 6 sqrt5 = 1.58359 (dodecahedron,
icosahedron). No constant works for all polytopes, not even centrally symmetric ones: the square bipyramid of height e has
t_min = (R/r)^2 -> infinity as e -> 0 (3, 13.1, 102, 1113 for e = 1, .3, .1, .03; tmin-general.log).

Proof. Upper bound: for a unit u let s.u be the boundary point of S in direction u, on face i; s <= R and n_i.u = h_i/s >= h_i/R, so the
support of P(S) in direction u is >= f_i.u = h_i (n_i.u) >= h_i^2/R >= r^2/R. A closed convex set whose support is >= rho in every
direction contains the ball rho.B, so P(S) contains (r^2/R).B, i.e. r(P(S)) >= r^2/R; and S lies in R.B, so S lies in
(R/r(P(S))).P(S) and in (R^2/r^2).P(S). Lower bound: P(S) lies in the slab v.x <= |v| h_P(v), so the gauge of the corner v is at least
|v|/h_P(v). Platonic: all |v| = R, all h_i = r, and the face normal nearest a corner makes the angle arccos(r/R) with it, so
h_P(v) = r.(r/R) and both bounds are R^2/r^2.  QED

## Theorem B (icosahedral nesting; the general statement DICTO asked for)

Let S be any convex polytope (any convex body) with icosahedral rotational symmetry about O. Then

    S  inside  (15 - 6 sqrt5) . P(S)  inside  phi . P(S),          15 - 6 sqrt5 = 1.583592 < phi = 1.618034  (29 < 13 sqrt5: 841 < 845).

The constant 15 - 6 sqrt5 is best possible (icosahedron and dodecahedron attain it). Hence for EVERY icosahedral start the golden tower
phi^k P^k(S) is nested for every k, with no computation needed; phi beats the sharp constant by only 0.0344.

Proof. By Theorem A it suffices to show r(S) >= c R(S) with c = sqrt((5 + 2 sqrt5)/15) = 0.794654 = r/R of the icosahedron (then
(R/r)^2 <= 1/c^2 = 15/(5 + 2 sqrt5) = 15 - 6 sqrt5). Let v be a farthest corner, |v| = R. S is convex and I-invariant, so it contains
hull(I.v), and a face plane with unit normal w and support h has h >= max_g (g v).w. So it is enough to prove the

Covering lemma. For all unit u, w there is a rotation g in the icosahedral group I with (g u).w >= c, i.e. the orbit I.u comes within
theta* = arccos c = 37.3774 deg of every direction.

Proof of the lemma. The full group I_h = I x {+-1} is generated by reflections; its fundamental domain is the (2,3,5) Moebius
triangle T with vertices on a 2-fold, 3-fold and 5-fold axis, angles 90, 60, 36 deg and sides A2A3 = 20.905, A2A5 = 31.717,
A3A5 = 37.377 deg; 120 copies h T (h in I_h) tile the sphere. Two facts: (i) a spherical convex polygon with vertex distances < 90 deg
has diameter = largest vertex distance (if p is within theta of every vertex v_i then for x = sum l_i v_i / |sum l_i v_i|, l_i >= 0,
x.p >= cos(theta) sum l_i / |sum l_i v_i| >= cos theta; apply twice); so diam T = A3A5 = theta*. (ii) Let s be the reflection in the
great circle of the side A3A5 and K = T u sT, the kite A3 A2 A5 A2' (angles 120, 90, 72, 90 deg, convex). Its diagonals are A3A5 =
theta* and A2A2' = 2x the altitude of T from the right angle onto A3A5; by the spherical sine rule sin(alt) = sin(A2A3) sin(A2A5)
/ sin(A3A5) = sin 18 deg exactly, so A2A2' = 36 deg < theta* and, by (i), diam K = theta*.
Now take u, w. Moving u by I_h (the orbit I.u is replaced by itself or by its antipodal image -I.u, which has the same covering
radius) we may assume u in T. Pick h in I_h with h w in T. If h is a rotation, u and h w both lie in T, so d(u, h w) <= theta*, i.e.
d(h^-1 u, w) <= theta* with h^-1 in I. Otherwise h = s g with g in I (I has index 2, so I_h = I u sI), so g w lies in sT, u in T, both in
K, d(u, g w) <= diam K = theta*, i.e. d(g^-1 u, w) <= theta*. Equality holds for u on a 5-fold axis and w on a 3-fold axis
(icosahedron) or the other way round (dodecahedron).  QED

Numerical confirmation (covering_lemma.py, covering-lemma.log): the sides, the 18 deg altitude, the kite diameter, and the minimum
over a grid of u in T of the inradius of hull(I.u) = 0.794654, attained only at the 3-fold and 5-fold axes. The same kite argument
FAILS for the octahedral group (altitude 30 deg, kite diagonal 60 > 54.74 deg), although numerically every octahedral orbit hull has
inradius >= 1/sqrt3, which would give S inside 3 P(S) for octahedral symmetry (cube/octahedron sharp): left as a conjecture.

## What remains conjecture / open

- The downward family's own behaviour: t_min(D_k) is NOT monotone (1.134, 1.117, 1.103, 1.146, 1.042, 1.087, 1.026, 1.060, 1.138, ...);
  whether it tends to a limit (1? something above?) is open. Theorem B makes the nesting independent of this.
- "Reverse undoes forward" (T(P(S)) = S) holds exactly for k <= 5 only (and again at D_9): from D_6 on some foot points of D_{k-1} lie
  strictly inside hull(D_k) (60, 60, 90 of them at k = 6, 7, 8), so T(D_k) is strictly larger than D_{k-1} there. The tower and
  Theorem B are unaffected (they only use the hull).
- Octahedral analogue S inside 3 P(S) (numerical only). Tetrahedral analogue would be 9.
