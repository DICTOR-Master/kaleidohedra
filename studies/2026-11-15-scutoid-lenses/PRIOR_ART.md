# Prior art: small-aperture vision aids, lenslet spectacle lenses, compound eyes and scutoids (scutoid lenses study)

Date: 2026-11-15. Free sources only; nothing was bought. "Full text" means the text was read. "Abstract only" means the full text was not read. "Snippet only" means only a search-engine summary was seen, so the number should be checked before anyone quotes it. Question: DICTO (15 Nov 2026) asks whether lenses (spectacles, camera lenses) can give the near-vision benefit of a small pupil without losing too much light, using arrays of tiny apertures or lenslets on a curved surface, and whether the scutoid geometry of cells packed between two curved surfaces has a role.

## (b) What each source gives (credited at the point of use in README)

### Optics of small apertures
- **Colicchia G, Hopf M, Wiesner H, Zollman D (2008)** "Pinhole glasses", The Physics Teacher 46, 26-28, doi 10.1119/1.2823996. Full text. Commercial pinhole glasses: 0.9 mm holes in front, 1.2 mm behind, spaced 3 mm. Airy-disc estimate for a 1 mm hole (radius about 14 µm on the retina, a few receptor spacings). Two holes closer than the pupil diameter give two retinal images; "too large of a separation will result in dead spots in the field, while too small of a separation will produce multiple images"; "impossible to produce a separation that is optimum for all refractive defects and pupil sizes". Single-hole glasses useless in dim light; no research supports pinhole glasses to reduce or prevent refractive errors. Cites Wittenberg (1993) J Am Optom Assoc 64, 112-116 and Young (1989) Phys Teach 27, 648 on the pinhole camera (not read).
- **"Clinical feasibility of pinhole glasses in presbyopia" (2019)**, Chung-Ang University repository record and PubMed 30460857; 32 presbyopes, NCT02722291 (Seminars in Ophthalmology, per the publisher link; authors not seen). Snippet only: better uncorrected distance and near acuity, accommodation demand 15% lower, but lower legibility, reading speed down, more eye fatigue (tear break-up time shorter).
- **Pinhole soft contact lenses**, 20 presbyopes, two weeks: distance-corrected near and intermediate acuity better, contrast sensitivity lower at some frequencies. Snippet only (authors not seen).
- **AVEH (African Vision and Eye Health) 75(1) article 318**, doi 10.4102/aveh.v75i1.318: 45 presbyopes, pinholes of several sizes in bright and dim light; more than 40% acuity gain in about 67%; "not a substitute for proper correction". Snippet only.
- **Wang B, Ciuffreda KJ (2006)** Depth-of-focus of the human eye: theory and clinical implications, Surv Ophthalmol 51, 75-85. Seen only as a citation. The geometric relation used here (blur angle = pupil diameter × defocus) is standard textbook optics, from memory.
- **Pinhole magnifier**: Exploratorium exhibit page "Pinhole magnifier" (snippet only): a pinhole lets the eye come closer than its near point and acts as a magnifier.
- **Optimal pinhole** d = sqrt(2.44 λ f) (geometric blur equal to the Airy diameter): Petzval and Rayleigh's classical pinhole-camera result, from memory (also discussed by Young 1989, not read).
- **Scheiner (1619)**: two pinholes inside the pupil double a defocused point (the Scheiner disc). From memory.

### Small-aperture implants
- **KAMRA inlay (AcuFocus)**, FDA PMA P120023 (device summary via innolitics; snippet only): 3.8 mm ring, 6 µm thick, PVDF with carbon black, 1.6 mm aperture, 8,400 micro-perforations (5-11 µm) for nutrient flow. Labelling: glare, halos, night-vision trouble, blur, dryness; visual symptoms in more than 30% of subjects.
- **KAMRA explants, 10-year single-site review (2022)**, PubMed 36237487 (snippet only): 22 explants for blur at near, poor night/dim vision, halos, haze, double vision; residual haze after explant in 16 of 22; hyperopic shift.
- **Persistent corneal fibrosis after explantation of a small aperture corneal inlay** (case report, QxMD 30584010; snippet only).
- Market status of KAMRA: the search did not confirm a withdrawal (the Raindrop inlay was withdrawn in 2018, 43% haze). Not stated in README.
- **IC-8 Apthera IOL (AcuFocus)**, FDA PMA P210005, approved 22 July 2022 (FDA summary via innolitics, AcuFocus press release on Business Wire, Ophthalmology Times, Optometry Times; snippet only): 1.36 mm aperture in a 3.23 mm opaque mask; one eye only (monovision partner); 453 subjects, 12 months; better binocular uncorrected intermediate and near acuity, distance non-inferior; monocular contrast sensitivity lower; sponsor's figure of 0.91 D more range than monofocal at 0.2 logMAR.

### Lenslet spectacle lenses (myopia control)
- **Lam CSY et al. (2020)** DIMS spectacle lenses slow myopia progression: a 2-year randomised clinical trial, Br J Ophthalmol 104, 363-368. Abstract only (via PolyU repository listing and trade summaries): 52% slower myopia progression, 62% less axial elongation versus single vision.
- **Jaskulski M, Singh NK, Bradley A, Kollbaum PS (2020)** Optical and imaging properties of a novel multi-segment spectacle lens designed to slow myopia progression, Ophthalmic Physiol Opt 40, 549-556, doi 10.1111/opo.12725. Abstract only: hexagonal array of 1 mm lenslets, 0.5 mm nearest-neighbour separation, central single-vision zone, +3.50 D add; multiple PSFs and poor image quality at the lenslet focal plane. The often-quoted "396 lenslets, 1.03 mm, 33 mm zone" was NOT confirmed in any source opened here.
- **Bao J et al. (2022)** Spectacle lenses with aspherical lenslets for myopia control vs single-vision spectacle lenses, JAMA Ophthalmol, doi 10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2022.0401. Abstract and trade summaries: lenslets on 11 concentric rings (Essilor gives 1,021 lenslets, not found in the abstract); HAL slowed refraction change by 0.80 D and axial growth by 0.35 mm over 2 years.

### Pinhole arrays in displays
- **Huang F-C, Wetzstein G, Barsky BA, Raskar R (2014)** Eyeglasses-free display: towards correcting visual aberrations with computational light field displays, ACM Trans Graph 33(4) (SIGGRAPH). Project page and press: printed pinhole mask, 75 µm holes at 390 µm pitch, on a phone screen, with prefiltering; works for one viewer position.
- **Slim near-eye display using pinhole aperture arrays** (NVIDIA Research, 2015; snippet only): minimum pinhole spacing set by the pupil diameter and the eye-array-screen distances.

### Compound eyes and curved lens arrays
- **Kim S, Cassidy JJ, Yang B, Carthew RW, Hilgenfeldt S (2016)** Hexagonal patterning of the insect compound eye: facet area variation, defects, and disorder, Biophys J 111, 2735-2746, doi 10.1016/j.bpj.2016.11.004. Abstract (Europe PMC): packing defects (non-hexagonal facets) rise with local facet-size variation in Drosophila mutants; Voronoi model.
- **Lee KC, Yu Q, Erb U (2016)** Mesostructure of ordered corneal nano-nipple arrays: the role of 5-7 coordination defects, Sci Rep 6, 28342 (PMC4916435). About 10% of nipples in 5-7 grain-boundary positions on each facet of Nymphalis antiopa; curvature named as the frustration. (Nano-nipples on a facet, not ommatidia.)
- **Song YM et al. (2013)** Digital cameras with designs inspired by the arthropod eye, Nature 497, 95-99, doi 10.1038/nature12083. Abstract and press: about 160° hemispherical camera, 180 artificial ommatidia; lens and detector arrays made flat and then stretched into a dome by pneumatic pressure.
- **Jeong K-H, Kim J, Lee LP (2006)** Biologically inspired artificial compound eyes, Science 312, 557-561, doi 10.1126/science.1123053. Abstract and press: about 8,700 hexagonal microlenses on a polymer dome, each with a light-guiding cone and a self-aligned waveguide written by UV light through the lens.
- Compound-eye curvature reviews seen as snippets (UCL Discovery review; PolyU review): optical performance depends on facet density, size and curvature. No source on scutoids in compound eyes was found (queries 6, 15).

### Scutoids
- **Gómez-Gálvez P, Vicente-Munuera P et al. (2018)** Scutoids are a geometrical solution to three-dimensional packing of epithelia, Nat Commun 9, 2960, doi 10.1038/s41467-018-05376-1. Abstract: cells with different apical and basal neighbours in curved (tubular) epithelia, Voronoi model.
- **Mughal A, Cox SJ, Weaire D, Burke SR, Hutzler S (2018)** Demonstration and interpretation of "scutoid" cells formed in a quasi-2D soap froth, Phil Mag Lett 98, 358-364, arXiv 1809.08421. Abstract: scutoids "induced by curvature of the bounding surfaces", found in simulation and experiment in a dry foam.
- DICTO's own scutoid work: #104 (Scutoids: Layers, Shells and Cycles), #108, and ledger entries [106]-[113] (4D scutoids).
