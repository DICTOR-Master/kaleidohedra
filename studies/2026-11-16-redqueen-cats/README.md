# The Red Queen's cats: golden copies inside the 120-cell (16 Nov 2026)

**Conclusion (exact in Q(√5)): the 120-cell holds exactly 1200 golden truncated 5-cells (A-type) and 1200 golden cantellated 5-cells (B-type), all of them inside its 10 inscribed 600-cells (120 + 120 in each, one W(H4) class each), and every one of its 600 corners is shared by exactly two inscribed 600-cells, one from each of the two whole states. Together the copies use 1560 walls (60 + 300 + 1200), which cut the 120-cell into 593,236,800 chambers, none of them a cat.**

DICTO's question (16 Nov 2026), in his words: "can we conjecture any of the crossover or framing to hierarchical understanding of the 600 cells ... would the 120 cells survey help clarification of the meat/cat paradox ... similar to the cutting the cat in half zen mondo and Schrödinger's cat paradox ... it seems there are many half-alive cats coexisting". The koan (Nansen cuts the cat, Mumonkan case 14) and Schrödinger's cat are DICTO's images for corners that belong to two wholes at once. No physics is claimed here. Computation was done with Claude (Anthropic).

Background: `studies/2026-11-16-queens-construct` (the exact builders, qlib, the 600-cell = A + −A + C3 + B + −B), `studies/2026-11-16-600cell-walls` (every plate of A and B is a 600-cell wall), `studies/2026-11-16-queens-chambers` (the 360 walls make 298 regions per Coxeter chamber), `studies/2026-11-16-queens-vicinity-vacuus` (heart kind; the diminished 600-cells).

Names used: P = the 600-cell, Q = the 120-cell, both in sum-zero R⁵ where S5 permutes the coordinates. A = hull of the 20 permutations of (φ², φ², φ², φ, 0), B = hull of the 30 permutations of (φ², φ², 1, 1, 0), C3 = the remaining 20 corners of P (the uniform expanded 5-cell). A *copy* is a set of corners congruent to A, B or C3. A *wall* is a central hyperplane; a *plate* is the central hyperplane spanned by a 2-face.

## Proposition (the hierarchy, exact)

1. **Corners.** Q has exactly 10 inscribed 600-cells. Every corner of Q lies in exactly 2 of them. They fall into two whole states of 5 disjoint 600-cells, and the two 600-cells of each corner are in different states. Two 600-cells of the same state are disjoint; two from different states share exactly 24 corners, and those 24 form a 24-cell. Every H4 mirror swaps the two states.
2. **The S5s.** Inside one 600-cell, the S5s that split it into A, −A, C3, B, −B are exactly the 60 subgroups generated by an A4 set of its mirror reflections. All 60 are conjugate in its symmetry group W(H4) (normaliser order 240 = S5 × {±1}). Each gives one A, one −A, one C3, one B, one −B; so a 600-cell holds 120 A-type, 120 B-type and 60 C3 copies, and an exhaustive congruence search finds no others.
3. **Copies in the 120-cell.** Q holds exactly **1200 A-type, 1200 B-type and 600 C3 copies** (120, 120 and 60 in each inscribed 600-cell, using that 600-cell's own 60 S5s, 600 S5s in all). An exhaustive search over all of Q's corners finds no copy of A or B outside the inscribed 600-cells (40 A-type and 60 B-type copies through each corner). Under Q's symmetry group (order 14400) each type is **one congruence class**: the stabiliser of an A or B copy has order 12, of a C3 copy order 24.
4. **Walls.** Every plate of every copy lies in a wall of its own 600-cell. All the copies together use **1560 walls**:
   - 60 = Q's own mirrors (which are also Q's cell-parallel sections). Each is a cross wall of all 10 inscribed 600-cells.
   - 300 = the sections perpendicular to Q's corner axes. Each is a mirror of exactly the 2 inscribed 600-cells through that corner.
   - 1200 = further cross walls of the inscribed 600-cells, each shared by exactly 2 of them; they are not among P's 360 walls.
   - A copies use only the 300; B copies use all 1560; C3 copies use the 60 and the 1200. In each inscribed 600-cell, its A and B copies use all 360 of its own walls (its 60 mirrors + 300 cross walls).
5. **Half-cats (the overlap rule).** Fix an S5 in a 600-cell of the first state. Of its 5 shared 24-cells (one with each 600-cell of the second state), **one is split 0 A-type, 12 C3, 12 B-type and the other four 10 A-type, 2 C3, 12 B-type**. So every shared 24-cell is exactly half B-type in each of its two 600-cells, whatever S5 is chosen. Over both choices (60 × 60 per pair) there are 18 patterns (table below); at least 4 corners are B-type in both halves, and in 1 choice in 60 every corner has the same group in both halves.
6. **Two whole states.** No copy belongs to both states. Copies from different states share 0 to 5 corners (A with A; never 4), 0 to 3 (A with B), 0 to 6 (B with B), but always share walls: A with A at least 2, A with B at least 1, B with B at least 5 (up to 20). Both states use the same 1560 walls.
7. **Chambers.** The 1560 walls cut each H4 Coxeter chamber into **41,197** regions (exact Zaslavsky count = float splitting count), so Q into **14400 × 41,197 = 593,236,800 chambers**. Every chamber is a cone from the centre, while A and B contain the centre inside them, so no chamber is a copy of A or B. The cat-and-steak converse fails one level up, as it failed for the 600-cell.

## The Queens' skeletons: are they made of Vacuus fins?

DICTO (16 Nov): "the skeletons of the white and red queens are in question as to whether vacuus form the skeletons". A *fin* is the cone from the centre over one 2-face of a shape. Two readings, both exact: the **finite** fin (cut off at the face) and the **radial** fin (the same cone carried outward to the Queen's surface). Coverage is the fraction of area (of length, for edges) inside the union of the fins.

**Verdict: the Vacuus fins form the 600-cell's skeleton, and partly form the 120-cell's.**

Each Queen has the full symmetry of the copy set, so one triangle, one pentagon and one edge settle "all" (the union of all copies' fins is invariant).

- **600-cell, finite reading, exactly.** Each A-type copy has 20 of the 600-cell's triangles as faces (its 5 tetrahedral cells are 600-cell cells), and each B-type copy has 10. W(H4) is transitive on the triangles, so every triangle is a face of exactly 2 A-type copies (120 × 20 / 1200) and of exactly **1** B-type copy (120 × 10 / 1200). **The 120 B-type copies split the 600-cell's 1200 triangles between them exactly**, and the A-type copies cover them twice.
- **Is each Queen's own Vacuus exactly the union of the inner shapes' fins?** No: the union contains it and is larger. For the 600-cell, of the five pieces' 290 fins, 150 lie on cross walls, outside its Vacuus, and of the 140 on mirrors only 60 lie inside the triangle cones; 60 are half inside and 20 are 0.335 inside. For the 120-cell, of the 300 copy fins in a pentagon's plate, 240 lie wholly outside its pentagon cones and 60 are 0.22 inside.
- **Minimal completion.** For the 600-cell nothing is missing once all S5s are used: the B-type copies alone (finite) or the A-type copies alone (finite) already cover every triangle. For the 120-cell's pentagons, the A and B fins never reach them; the C3 fins complete them radially. Literally (finite), no golden copy reaches the 120-cell's surface, because every copy lies inside an inscribed 600-cell whose faces are chords of Q.

## Three kinds of Vacuus: heart, inherited heart, non-heart

Kinds (the name "inherited heart" is DICTO's, 16 Nov 2026):
- **heart**: the shape is centrally symmetric;
- **inherited heart**: not centrally symmetric, but some plate carries at least 2 faces;
- **non-heart**: every plate carries exactly 1 face.

**Lemma.** If a polytope X with the centre inside is centrally symmetric, every plate carries at least 2 faces. *Proof.* Let f be a 2-face. Then −f is a 2-face too. The plate of f is span(0, f), a linear subspace, so it contains −f. And f ≠ −f: f lies in a supporting hyperplane n·x = h with h > 0 (the centre is inside), while −f lies in n·x = −h. ∎ (Checked on every centrally symmetric shape below: minimum 2 or more faces per plate.)

**Tentative conjecture tested:** "a shape is inherited heart exactly when it inherits its walls from a heart parent but lacks the parent's central symmetry". *Inherits* = every plate is a wall of the parent (P's 360 walls; for copies in Q, their own inscribed 600-cell's 360 walls).

What does hold on every case tested: **removing corners from a heart parent (the diminished 600-cells) gives inherited heart, and twisting a parent's corners into a new orbit (A, B, the 5-cells) gives non-heart.** So the conjecture needs the parent's *faces*, not only its walls. The vicinity study's table calls the diminished 600-cells non-heart; under the three kinds they are inherited heart (that study had only two kinds).

## Dual cats: the Red Queen's own cats are polar duals

DICTO asked (16 Nov): "why the red queen but not the white, is there perhaps a false underlying premise ... something previously too elegant and perfect to question?" The premise: we built the 120-cell's cats from 600-cells by *inclusion*. An inscribed 600-cell uses 120-cell corners, but its edges are diagonals of the 120-cell, so its pieces never make the 120-cell's edges or pentagons. The relation between the two Queens is *duality*. DICTO's hypothesis (confirmed by him on 16 Nov 2026): the Red Queen's own cats are the polar duals A*, B*, C3* of A, B, C3, placed so that the polarity carries the White Queen onto the Red Queen.

**Verdict: YES.** The false premise explains the asymmetry. Under duality, A* and B* alone cover every pentagon and every edge of the 120-cell whole, both finite and radial, with their fins on the 120-cell's own mirrors, just as A and B cover the 600-cell's triangles. The inclusion copies, dualised or not, never reach a pentagon. Two limits: (i) the cover does not need the golden form, because any orbit of the same S5 type gives the same fins (control below). The golden form gives the fit: cells on the 120-cell's cell walls, and A*'s corners on its corners. (ii) No counterpart of the exact B-type partition: no set of copies covers each pentagon exactly once.

**Construction (exact in Q(√5)).** P is the 600-cell whose corners point at Q's cell centres (Q's corners are the centroids of P's cells). Polarity is taken about the common centre with a sphere of radius² r² = |Q corner|². Then X* = {y : x·y ≤ r² for every corner x of X}. A*, B*, C3* are the polars of A, B, C3 and of all their copies in P (the 60 S5s of P: 120 A*, 120 B*, 60 C3*). Because A ⊂ P, A* ⊃ Q, and every cell of A* lies in a cell wall of Q (it is polar to a corner of A, which is a corner of P). Since P = A ∪ −A ∪ C3 ∪ B ∪ −B (corners), **Q = A* ∩ −A* ∩ C3* ∩ B* ∩ −B***: the five duals of one S5 use Q's 120 cell walls once each.

- **All or nothing.** Each copy covers a pentagon either whole or not at all (no partial fractions occur). Each copy covers exactly 60 pentagons: 6 of the 12 on each of its S5's 10 mirrors. - **Fewest copies.** Every mirror needs at least 2 copies, so at least 12. An exact-cover search (complete) finds no 12-copy cover in any selection. - **White Queen, for comparison** (section above): the A and B fins cover all 1200 triangles and 720 edges of the 600-cell. Each triangle is a face of exactly 2 A-type and 1 B-type copy. In the dual picture each pentagon is covered by 10 A* and 10 B* copies.

**Placement II: dualising the inclusion copies** (all 3000 copies of the 600 S5s of the 10 inscribed 600-cells). Their fins still cover Q's edges whole, radially (3840 fins through one edge; by symmetry every edge). (The polar of an inscribed 600-cell has its cells facing Q's corner directions, not Q's cell centres, so it is never Q.) So the inclusion picture, dualised or not, misses the pentagons.

**Corner membership and the two states on the dual side** (`data/dual_cats.json`)
- Each cell of Q (one per corner of P) is a cell wall of 20 A*, 30 B* and 10 C3* copies.
- **Each corner of Q is a corner of exactly one A* copy**: the 120 A* copies split Q's 600 corners into 120 sets of 5.
