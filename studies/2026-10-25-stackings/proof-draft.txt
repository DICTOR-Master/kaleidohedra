THE RULE OF HALVES FOR CLOSE-PACKED STACKINGS (draft proof for referee attack, DICTO with Claude, 25 October 2026)

SET-UP. Unit balls, centres in close-packed layers (triangular lattices, spacing 1) at heights k·d, d = sqrt(2/3); layer k sits on one of
the positions A, B, C (no two consecutive layers equal); the stacking is any such sequence (periodic or not). Each layer is h (hexagonal)
if its two neighbours sit on the same position, c (cubic) otherwise. Sites: in the gap between layers k (position X) and k+1 (position Y)
there is one octahedral hole over the third position Z at height (k+1/2)d, a tetrahedral hole over X at (k+3/4)d and one over Y at
(k+1/4)d. Weights: w_oct = 0, w_tet = λ. The power diagram assigns to site p the cell {x : |x−p|^2 − w_p ≤ |x−q|^2 − w_q for all q}.
We consider −11/24 ≤ λ ≤ 3/8 (outside this range one kind of cell is empty). Pure stackings: ABC... (all c, "cubic octet") and AB...
(all h, "twinned octet").

THEOREM. For every stacking and every λ in the range:
(a) the cell of a tetrahedral site is congruent to the cell of a tetrahedral site of the pure stacking of the type (c or h) of the layer
    nearest to the site (the tetrahedral hole over X at (k+3/4)d is nearest to layer k+1, and vice versa);
(b) the cell of an octahedral site, cut by the horizontal plane through the site, consists of an upper half congruent to the upper half of
    an octahedral cell of the pure stacking of the type of layer k+1, and a lower half congruent to the lower half of an octahedral cell of
    the pure stacking of the type of layer k.

PROOF.
Step 1 (locality). Claim: the cells of the sites of gap k are determined by the six layers k−2, ..., k+3.
 (i) Computation (exact, rational arithmetic): for each of the 16 possible type-patterns (h/c) of layers k−1..k+2 (which with relabelling
     fix layers k−2..k+3) we build a periodic stacking containing those six layers, and compute the power diagram exactly on the whole λ-range
     (it changes combinatorially only at finitely many λ; on each open stretch every cell corner is an affine function of λ; the program
     verifies on each stretch that its finite site list suffices and that cell volumes add up to the lattice cell). We check: every corner of
     every cell of gap k lies within squared distance 1/2 of its site (it suffices to check the stretch ends, since the squared distance of an
     affine path is convex). Result: holds in all 16 cases, every stretch.
 (ii) Hand bound: a site q of a gap three or more gaps away from gap k is at height distance ≥ 2.5 d from every site p of gap k, so
     |q−p| ≥ 2.5d, and |w_p − w_q| ≤ 11/24. The bisector (radical plane) of p and q is at distance (|q−p|^2 + w_p − w_q)/(2|q−p|) ≥
     ((2.5d)^2 − 11/24)/(2·2.5d) from p (the function t ↦ (t^2 − c)/(2t) is increasing), whose square is 23763/28800 > 1/2.
 (iii) Convexity: let C_local be the cell of p computed from the sites of gaps k−2..k+2 only, C_W its cell in the periodic test stacking,
     C_S its cell in an arbitrary stacking with the same six layers. All far constraints miss the closed ball of radius sqrt(1/2) about p.
     C_W lies in that ball (i). Inside the ball C_W and C_local coincide; C_W ⊆ C_local; since ∂C_W ⊆ ∂C_local within the ball and both
     are convex, C_W = C_local. The same argument gives C_S = C_local. Hence the cells of gap k depend only on the six layers.
Step 2 (the 16 windows). For each window, each stretch, each site of gap k: one symmetry g of the layer lattice about the site's vertical
 axis (the 12 plane symmetries of the triangular lattice, with or without the reflection z ↦ −z: 24 maps) carries the corner functions
 (affine in λ) of the cell (a) onto those of a pure tetrahedral cell, of the stated type: 176 cases out of 176 (all sites, all stretches,
 vanished cells skipped); (b) for octahedral sites, the corner functions above, on and below the mid-plane, and the points where edges
 cross the mid-plane (exact rational functions of λ), onto those of the stated pure half: 160 out of 160. Equality of affine corner
 functions gives equality of the cells (convex hulls) for every λ in the stretch; equality of corners on one side plus the section gives
 equality of the halves.
Step 3 (stops). At the finitely many stop values the cells are the limits of the cells on the adjacent stretches (checked: the limit equals
 the cell computed directly at the stop), so the congruences persist.

LIMITS STATED. The range −11/24 ≤ λ ≤ 3/8. In Step 2(b) the side of each corner is compared at one λ per stretch; no stretch needed to be
split (no corner crossed the mid-plane inside a stretch for the mixed cells). Floating-point arithmetic is used nowhere in Steps 1(i),
2, 3. COROLLARY: Fang and Chucky (new pieces of the double-hexagonal stacking ABAC) are each a cubic half glued to a hexagonal half;
the tetrahedral piece TT is the cubic piece; the catalogue of all pieces of all close-packed stackings is finite (21 combinatorial types).
