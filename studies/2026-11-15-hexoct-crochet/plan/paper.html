HexOct in crochet: cells that fill space

A plan for a three-dimensional crocheted HexOct crystal · DICTO · dictospheres.com · 15 November 2026
DRAFT plan of 15 November 2026 for DICTO's reading (study folder 2026-11-15-hexoct-crochet); not for publication as it stands. Nothing has been crocheted yet. All geometry is checked exactly on the compute host (FINAL OK); sources are free and listed in the study.

The result is three-dimensional. Each cell is a closed, solid crocheted polyhedron; the cells fit whole face to whole face and stack into c and h layers, so the build is the crystal in 3D, not a flat fabric and not a pattern on a surface. The cells are DICTO's Narcissus (every h layer) and the cube (every c layer), the HexOct pieces at the end of the walk (λ = 3/8, #98), two cells per ball in every layer. Checked on periodic blocks of 3C, 2H, 4H, 6H and 9R: no gaps, no overlaps, every face met by exactly one whole face, and every contact between two layers is the same square. Only three face shapes are needed: a square of side L, a regular hexagon of side L√(2/3), a right triangle with legs L/√3 and L√(2/3) (hypotenuse L), with L = a/√2 for ball spacing a.

Firmness. Stuffing alone lets faces bulge (AmiGo: the shell takes its shape from the stuffing), and bulging faces cannot meet face to face. So each cell is crocheted over a folded core from the same net (card or PET bottle sheet), slightly undersize; its folds are 90°, 120° and 135° only. Seams: whip stitch through back loops, on the inside.

Build. Test piece 1: two Narcissus + two cubes (one h unit on one c unit). Test piece 2: a 4H block, 2 × 2 balls × 4 layers (16 Narcissus, 16 cubes).

Conclusion, at its true strength: the sources show that crochet makes closed polyhedral shells from flat faces joined at any edge, holds shape better than knitting and grows from one live loop in any direction: what a space-filling cell needs. No source shows crocheted cells that fill space (precedents: Taimiņa's hyperbolic planes from 1997, Osinga & Krauskopf's Lorenz manifold 2004, crocheted Platonic solids, the Wertheims' Crochet Coral Reef from 2005, belcastro & Yackel's books). So: well founded, untested; test piece 1 decides.
