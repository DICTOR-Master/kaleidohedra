Covering a slab of the golden gap

OCDs joined hexagon to hexagon on the bcc lattice, each in its golden envelope (RT). Between second neighbours the gap is a flat slab over one RT face (a golden rhombus, 63.43°), 0.1049 thick. Picture first, for DICTO

  • Each tile becomes a piece by pushing it along a third five-fold axis: rhombohedra on the rhombi, golden bricks (parallelepipeds with edges e and e/φ along five-fold axes) on the rest.
  • Exact: four choices of that third edge give a layer exactly half the slab (0.05246): two of length e, two of length e/φ.
  • Exact: in each length the two choices lean opposite ways, so two layers, one leaning each way, end exactly on the neighbour's face. Between them they spill a little past the slab's sides into the wedges (red), leaving an equal hollow on the other side (hatched).
  • Open: whether the spills and hollows of neighbouring slabs and wedges can meet up; that is the next step.
