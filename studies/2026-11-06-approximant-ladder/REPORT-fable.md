# Approximant ladder: prior art, proofs, open points (Fable, 2026-11-06)

Setting: 3D icosahedral cut-and-project from Z^6; physical directions e_i golden, perpendicular directions g_i with the rational q = m/n in place of 1/phi; window W = zonotope of the g_i (Kepler's rhombic triacontahedron in the golden case); generic shift gamma. Tile edge |e_i| = sqrt(2+phi) = 1.9021.

## Known (with citations)

1. The rational-perpendicular construction of cubic approximants is Elser and Henley's (Phys. Rev. Lett. 55, 2883, 1985: "a modified version of the projection method" giving the alpha-AlMnSi 1/1 structure; abstract checked online, full text not in the library). The lattice-parameter rule a(F_{n+1}/F_n) = 2(F_n + tau F_{n+1}) a_6D / sqrt(2+tau) is quoted in Ishimasa 2019 (library, equation after Fig. 1, his ref. 17); in the units here it gives exactly the sides found: 2phi^2, 2phi^3, 2phi^4, 2phi^5. Body-centred 1/1 and primitive (Pa3) 2/1 cells are the standard experimental cases (Ishimasa 2019: 1/1 bcc a = 14.343 A, 2/1 Pa3 a = 23.271 A; Fujita et al. 2013: 1/1, 2/1 and a Pa3 3/2 superstructure). The general rule "both Fibonacci numbers odd gives body-centred, otherwise primitive" (matching bcc, sc, sc, bcc here) I know from Elser-Henley / Goldman-Kelton (Rev. Mod. Phys. 65, 213, 1993) but could not verify in the library; the 10 + 6 rhombohedra per primitive 1/1 cell (20 + 12 per cubic cell) I recall from Elser-Henley, likewise unverified. Rational cuts as approximants in general: Kleman 2013 (library, section "3D approximants").
2. The cut-and-project tiling itself: Kramer and Neri, Acta Cryst. A40, 580 (1984), window = Kepler's triacontahedron, edge sqrt(2+tau), volume 20 tau^3, tiles of volume 2 tau^2 and 2 tau (Baake and Grimm 2012, library, section 4.5 and Fig. 17-19); the neighbour criterion "z_F +- w_i in V" and the partition of the window into vertex-configuration regions (Dietl and Eschenburg 2016, library, eq. 2.11); 24 vertex geometries, 39 with matching-rule marks (Hann, Socolar and Steinhardt 2016, library, Table I, after Katz).
3. Vertex density = tile density for a parallelepiped tiling: known in the plane (Euler count, every face a quadrilateral). For 3D no statement was found in the sources checked; it is folklore-level (tile-type frequencies are volumes of complementary parallelepipeds in the window, Katz-Duneau 1986, not in library). Proved below with an exact count formula.
4. Icosahedral vertex frequency: Baake and Grimm 2012 (library, p. 13, Fig. 19): the only icosahedrally symmetric vertex star of the Kramer-Neri tiling is 20 acute rhombohedra (the triacontahedron), and these vertices form a model set whose window is the tau^-3-scaled triacontahedron, so their share of all vertices is tau^-9 = 0.01316. (Original: Henley, Phys. Rev. B 34, 797, 1986, not in library.) The weaker "all 12 edges present" star has share tau^-6 = 0.05573 (window tau^-2 W); not found stated, derived below and confirmed numerically.
5. Kepler nest: the icosahedron with its 12 corners at the golden points of the octahedron's 12 edges is classical (Wikipedia "Regular icosahedron", checked: "divide each edge in the golden section", "five disjoint icosahedra"); the dodecahedron on a cube is Euclid XIII.17. The identity "the cube among the dodecahedron's corners is the octahedron's dual cube" is not stated in any source checked; it is a one-line corollary: eight faces of Pacioli's icosahedron lie in the octahedron's face planes (each octahedron face contains three golden points), so I and O share their insphere and the eight face centres on the 3-fold axes; D's corners are I's face centres, hence D's cube corners = O's face centres = corners of O's dual cube. Pacioli 1509 and Kepler 1619 are in the library only as unreadable OCR; Heath's Euclid vol. 3 only as a scan.

## New within the sources checked
The exact counting formula and its consequences (points = tiles on every rung, 576 settled, 8/5 and 13/8 predicted); seat = triacontahedral vertex; the finite proof of the seat spacing sqrt3 phi^2 on every rung; the exact 1/3 nesting of the Dodeca-13; the tau^-6 share of 12-edge stars. The diffraction ladder is a numerical observation only.

## Proved

(i) Points per cell = tiles per cell = sum over the 20 triples {l,m,n} of |det(g_l, g_m, g_n)|, divided by det(Lambda_perp), where Lambda_perp = Z-span of g_1..g_6.
Proof. Lambda_perp is a lattice (the g_i are rational) and x -> x.G maps Z^6 onto it with kernel exactly the period lattice L, so points per cell = #(Lambda_perp + gamma) in W. The tile with corner x and edges e_i, e_j, e_k exists iff its eight corners project into W, i.e. iff p_x + Z_ijk lies in W, Z_ijk the parallelepiped of g_i, g_j, g_k. For convex bodies {p : p + A in A + B} = B (support functions), and W = Z_ijk + Z_lmn with {l,m,n} the complementary triple, so tiles of type ijk per cell = #(Lambda_perp + gamma) in a translate of Z_lmn. A translate of a parallelepiped spanned by three lattice vectors holds, for generic gamma, exactly |det|/det(Lambda_perp) points (it is a fundamental domain of the sublattice they span). Summing over triples gives the tile formula. For points: W, a zonotope on lattice vectors, is dissected into translates of the same 20 parallelepipeds (Shephard's zonotopal dissection, 1974), each again holding exactly its index of points; the sum is the same. QED. (Degenerate triples at 1/1, det 0, give no tiles and no volume.)
Values (integer arithmetic): 1/1 16 = 10 + 6; 2/1 136 = 84 + 52; 3/2 576 = 356 + 220 (the README's 577 is a boundary double count); 5/3 1220 = 754 + 466; 8/5 10336 = 6388 + 3948; 13/8 43784. Cell volumes 10*2phi^2 + 6*2phi = 71.777 etc. agree with tiles.py.

(ii) Seats. A point is a Q-seat (12 neighbours within 2.0, Q4 = 0, Q6 = 0.6633) iff it has the full 12-edge star and no further point at distance exactly 2 (the 30 vectors +-e_i +- e_j with an obtuse angle have length 2; the ball of radius 2.0 includes them, and rc = 2.05 gives identical counts). A rhombohedron meeting the point at a corner with an obtuse face angle puts the far corner of that face at distance 2; so a bare star has every surrounding rhombohedron prolate with the point at its acute apex: solid angle pi/5 each, twenty of them, the triacontahedron. Seat = triacontahedral vertex = Baake-Grimm's icosahedral vertex star. The README's "no full triacontahedra on any rung" came from a bug in tiles.py: the corners are computed as Epar[ijk] @ b instead of b @ Epar[ijk]; fix the line and withdraw the sentence.
Window form: star points have perp image in W12 = {p : |p.n_k| <= h_k - max_i |g_i.n_k|} over the 30 facet normals of W; in the golden case every facet distance shrinks from phi to 1/phi, so W12 = phi^-2 W and the share is phi^-6 (numerically 0.055728). The seat window W_RT shrinks W by the whole triacontahedral configuration; share phi^-9 = 0.013156 in the golden case (numerically identical), and 0.0101, 0.0118, 0.0130, 0.0131 on 2/1, 3/2, 5/3, 8/5. Expected seats per cell = N times this share: 1.38, 6.8, 15.8, matching the observed 1, 6, 16; the exact integers depend on gamma (W_RT is not a lattice parallelepiped), so 1, 6, 16 are generic values, not theorems. At 1/1 the seat window has no interior: one 12-edge star per cell but no seat (seats.py rerun at R = 3: 0 seats, the star has 15 neighbours within 2, Q4 = 0.15); the README's "1/1: 1 seat" should read "1 star, 0 seats".
Spacing. Two seats x, y have perp images in W_RT, so v = y - x in Z^6 has v.G in the interior of 2 W_RT and |v.E| = their distance; [E G] is invertible, so |v.E| < sqrt3 phi^2 and v.G in 2 W_RT bound every coefficient (|v_i| <= 3 on all rungs), and the finite enumeration finds no admissible v with |v.E| < sqrt3 phi^2 on any rung (golden, 1/1 to 8/5), while v = delta_i + delta_j + delta_k (acute triple: the prolate rhombohedron's long body diagonal, length sqrt(6 + 9phi) = sqrt3 phi^2 = 4.5346) is admissible from 3/2 on and in the golden limit. So the seat spacing is at least sqrt3 phi^2 everywhere and attained generically from 3/2 on; on 2/1 the one seat per cell sits on the simple cubic lattice of side 2phi^3. For mere 12-edge stars the bound fails: adjacent stars (distance 1.9021) are admissible from 3/2 on and occur (3/2 at R = 4: 24 stars per cell, 6 of them bare). The spacing is a seat property, not a star property.

(iii) Dodeca-13 at 1/3. Let r be the face distance of the central dodecahedron and n_k its 12 unit face normals. Each outer dodecahedron is the mirror image of the central one in a face plane, so its centre is 2r n_k and its far face lies in the plane p.n_k = 3r: the cluster's support in direction n_k is at least 3r. Conversely, for outer dodecahedron j and direction k the support is at most 2r (n_j.n_k) + R, R the circumradius; for j = k it is exactly 3r (mirroring preserves the support along n_k), and for j != k, n_j.n_k <= 1/sqrt5, so support <= 2r/sqrt5 + R = 2.3972 < 3r = 3.3405 (edge 1: r = 1.11352, R = 1.40126). The central dodecahedron contributes r, and the 30 wedges and 20 needles lie in the convex hull of the 13 dodecahedra. So the support of the whole cluster in every face direction is exactly 3r, and the largest copy inside the central dodecahedron (the intersection of the 12 slabs |p.n_k| <= r) has scale exactly 1/3, attained on all 12 faces at once. Checked on c13-parts-pieces-unit.json: max support/r = 3.000000001 for all 12 normals, reached only by the outer dodecahedra.

## Not proved
The bcc/sc alternation in general (observed; the gcd of the perp minors is 2 exactly at 1/1 and 5/3, consistent with body-centring). The exact seat integers 1, 6, 16 (shift-dependent; expected values proved). Attainment of the seat spacing on 5/3 in a full cell (README's R = 9 run; my R = 6 run found no periods with the default search). The diffraction scores (numerical). Rung-1 statements (icosahedra on bcc) were not reviewed. The two literature recollections flagged in Known 1.
