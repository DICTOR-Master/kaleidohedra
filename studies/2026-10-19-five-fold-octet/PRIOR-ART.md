The reference library was searched first, then the web. Bergman 1988 has been added to the library (history/, refs c882c3d).

## Closest prior art

- **Bergman, US 4,719,726 (1988), "Continuous spherical truss construction".** A central icosahedron of **twenty tetrahedral pyramids** with an **endless sequence of space-filling icosahedral-spherical layers** of octahedra and tetrahedra, all of equal thickness and made from two strut lengths (ratio 0.951). Each layer has 12 pentagonal rosettes. **This is close to the UP idea as a whole: a Cleo-Ico-like core plus octet-like spherical shells without end.** It must be cited, and UP must not claim that idea as new.
  - Differences: Bergman's shells are all the same kind (tet-oct triangles, equal thickness, linear growth). UP's shells are **different solids in a cycle**: ID → RT → golden truncated icosahedron → Q → ID × 10/φ⁴. UP's pieces include pentagons, hexagons and rhombi on the shell surfaces, the five-sided Vajras and the hexagonal Cleos, and UP is exact.
  - Read so far through Google Patents' text only; the scan in the library still needs a page-by-page read.
- **Kramer, Acta Cryst. A38 (1982) 257–264, "Non-periodic central space filling with icosahedral symmetry using copies of seven elementary cells".** A central icosahedral filling by 7 cells that reassemble into copies τ³ larger, i.e. self-similar. Library: link-only, still to read. UP's scale factor 10/φ⁴ (≈ 1.459) is **not a power of φ**, so UP is not a golden self-similar inflation in Kramer's sense. That needs checking against the paper itself.
- **Lalvani, US 4,723,382 (1988).** Polygonal members with edges along the 15 two-fold axes, building tet/oct/rhombohedral cells that fill space periodically or non-periodically. Related in spirit but not central shells. First page read.
- **Polar reciprocation and Hart's canonical polyhedra** (Hart 1997; Antiprism `canonical`, `pol_recip`). When all corners lie on one sphere, UP's step **is** polar reciprocation, so ID → RT is the classical dual pair. Plain reciprocation has period 2 (ID ↔ RT). **UP's rule reciprocates each corner about its own sphere**, so from the RT (corners at two radii) it goes on to a golden truncated icosahedron rather than back to the ID, and **closes only after 4 steps, with a gain of 10/φ⁴**. No source found for this rule or this cycle so far.
