# The rolling law (Lean 4 + Mathlib) — 2026-11-24

Project V SF A. For the three 3D reflection groups A3 (24), B3 (48), H3 (120)
the dual of the permutahedron is a fair die with one face per group element
(tetrakis hexahedron, disdyakis dodecahedron, disdyakis triacontahedron).
Rolling across an edge = multiplying by one simple reflection; going round a
corner of valence 2m = the braid relation of order m.

**Rolling law (Matsumoto's theorem for these groups):** any two shortest rolls
between the same two faces are joined by corner moves (braid moves), and every
roll is brought to a shortest one by corner moves plus cancelling a
back-and-forth roll. Longest shortest roll = 6, 9, 15.

**Status 2026-11-24: M1–M6 built clean.** The roll tables are now derived
from the dice's exact coordinates (`gen/dice.py`), so every theorem below is
about the actual dice.

### How the proof is split
* `RollingLaw/Basic.lean` (general, no data): a `RollModel` is faces, base
  face, rolls `r i`, distance `d` with local conditions: rolls are involutions;
  each roll changes `d` by exactly 1; only the base face has `d = 0`; every
  other face has a roll towards the base; and the **corner condition** — if two
  different rolls `a, b` both lead towards the base, then rolling back round the
  corner `a b a …` keeps leading towards it for `m(a,b)` steps and both ways
  round meet. From these alone: `d` is the shortest-roll length, Matsumoto, and
  word reduction (induction on length; the corner case is the classical one).
* `RollingLaw/Coxeter.lean` (general): if the rolls also satisfy the Coxeter
  relations, `W → faces` is a bijection, Mathlib's length is `d`, and
  Matsumoto / reduction hold for Mathlib's `CoxeterSystem` (the bijection
  itself uses Matsumoto on the faces).
* `RollingLaw/Dice.lean` (computed): the three dice; every condition is a
  finite statement about the tables, checked by the kernel.
* `RollingLaw/Q5.lean`: exact numbers a + b√5, their real value, and a
  proved decision procedure for `≤`.
* `RollingLaw/Die.lean`: M4 (geometry of each die) and M5 (rolling law for
  each die between any two faces).
* `RollingLaw/Mirrors.lean`: M6 (mirror count, each crossed once).
* `RollingLaw/Data/*.lean`: exact data generated by `gen/dice.py` (data only;
  nothing in the generator is trusted).

The statements of `A3.rolling_law` etc. mention only the die (faces, rolls,
corner moves), not the group.

Exact coordinates (`gen/dice.py`): face normals `n x = g_x u` (the orbit of a
generic point u: the permutahedron), vertex directions = orbits of the three
axis vectors (A3: (1,1,1), (1,0,0), (−1,−1,−1); B3: (1,0,0), (1,1,0),
(1,1,1); H3: (0,φ,1), (0,0,1), (1,1,1)), heights c = max n·V; the die is the
polar of the permutahedron.
