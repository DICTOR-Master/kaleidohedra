# Shadow forms of the simple cubic icosahedron networks (2026-10-23)

DICTO's idea (2026-10-23): complementary ("shadow") ways of breaking up the AXE networks' space, to find new pieces; aim (DICTO): unit pieces without odd protrusions, and flat top/bottom fills. Float computations unless marked; exact check of the golden split still to do.

Scripts run from this folder's `scripts/` with ~/k-mix study data (sc-star-H-unit.json) and ~/axe-gap geometry.

1. **Fusions** (fuse1–5): face-sharing pairs convex only Tricap+Unborn and Unborn + k Tricaps; 16 chunky 2–3-piece fusions (fill ≥ 85%, elongation ≤ 1.8), best AXE+icosahedron 95.7%.
2. **Two-piece AXE assignment** (fuse6): each AXE to its lower icosahedron (or Unborn) gives icosahedron+6 AXE / Unborn (and twin); fills (point test 0/0) but spiky: 68% / 49% of hull.
3. **Flat layers** (flat1–5): no lattice plane misses every piece; the icosahedron-equator plane cuts only icosahedra + AXE. Best single layer unit 84.0% of hull (sliding copies), turned twins no better (84.0%; C 78.5%). Note: layer A turned-twin groups mostly skipped by an orbit-count check (possible bug).
4. **Golden split of the AXE** (axesplit1–4): cone each AXE from a point p on its axis (t = 0 long-edge midpoint … 1 short-edge midpoint) into 4 pyramids, each joining the piece across that face. Icosahedron + 12 pyramids is convex for t ≤ 1/φ, Unborn + 12 pyramids for t ≥ 1/φ: **at t = 1/φ both are convex** (float: fill − 1 ≈ 4e-16 and −1.6e-10). Both are **truncated octahedra** (24 corners, 8 hexagons, 6 squares): the same hexagon (sides alternating 1/√2 and 1/(φ√2)), big squares 1/√2 on the padded icosahedron, small squares 1/(φ√2) on the padded Unborn; volumes 2.490712 + 1.745356 = φ³. Body-centred two-piece fill, point test 500,000 points: 0 uncovered, 0 doubled. Pictures: ~/Documents/papers-to-read/golden-split/.

EXACT (exact/golden_split_exact.py → golden-split-exact.txt, ALL PASS, ℚ(√5) arithmetic, no floats in any test): both padded pieces convex (union volume = hull volume), each 24 corners / 14 faces (8 hexagons, 6 squares), volumes 1 + 2√5/3 and 1 + √5/3, sum φ³ = 2 + √5; all 16 hexagons congruent (15 corner distances), squared sides 1/2 and (3 − √5)/4; big squares side² 1/2, small (3 − √5)/4. The fill itself follows exactly from the network's fill (axe-gap study) since the split only re-assigns AXE parts. Still float only: that t = 1/φ is the ONLY convex point (sweep).

5. **Golden split in the Lotus Jewel network** (jewelsplit1–2; exact check in golden_split_exact.py): the same cones; the 8 Tricaps move to the dodecahedron side, so the big truncated octahedron gets 8 Tricap-shaped dents and the small one 8 Tricap keys: a keyed pair, volumes **√5 and 2 exactly** (sum φ³). Each fills ≈ 89.8% / 89.4% of its hull (the keys). Any Tricap assignment still fills, so mixed keyings (some hexagons keyed one way, some the other, some not) all fill. Pictures 04–06 in golden-split/.

## Prior art (thorough check, 2026-10-23)
Sources checked: DICTO's reference library (kaleidohedra-references: MANIFEST, READING-LIST, Fedorov ch. 13 — nothing on this); web (9 searches): Koca et al. arXiv:1506.04600 (read in full, text saved in exact/), Polytope Wiki "Truncated octahedron" and "Pyritohedral icosahedron", Ozdes Koca et al. 2021 Acta A (Mosseri–Sadoc dodecahedral structures), Wikipedia bitruncated cubic honeycomb / truncated octahedron, α-AlMnSi (Pm-3 1/1 approximant) literature, Laguerre/power-diagram tessellation papers, quadibloc "Filling Space with Polyhedra", ProofWiki icosahedron in octahedron.

**Known (cite):**
- Each piece's SHAPE. Koca, Koca, Al-Ajmi, Al-Sheriani 2015 (arXiv:1506.04600; SQU J. Sci. 21(2) 2016), §4.3 eq. (49): the union of a pseudoicosahedron and its mirror image is a non-regular truncated octahedron, the B3 orbit a1(1, x, 0), whose pseudoicosahedra (eq. 39) have corners ±(1 + x)e1 ± x e2 cyclic. Our big piece is x = τ = φ (icosahedron ∪ mirror icosahedron), the small x = 1/τ (Unborn ∪ mirror Unborn; coordinate ratio φ²) — proven exactly here (corner orbits; the 12 pyramid tips are exactly the mirror image's corners).
- Polytope Wiki: the semi-uniform truncated octahedron o4y3x (8 ditrigons, 6 squares, full octahedral symmetry); its alternation is a pyritohedral icosahedron, regular when the ditrigon–ditrigon edge is (√5 − 1)/2 of the square–ditrigon edge (our big piece). The small piece has the reciprocal ratio φ.
- Unweighted bcc Voronoi cell = truncated octahedron; unequal truncated octahedra on bcc arise as weighted Voronoi (Laguerre) cells — general, standard.
- Context, not the same claim: α-AlMnSi is a Pm-3 1/1 approximant with icosahedral clusters at cube corners and body centres; Mosseri–Sadoc tiles (AXE = t6) are used aperiodically (Koca et al. 2021).

**Not found in any source checked:**
- The two-piece fill by the golden pair (x = φ at lattice points, x = φ² at body centres) — though since the two have the same hexagon with edge lengths swapped, the fill itself may be regarded as an easy member of the Laguerre family; we do not claim the bare tiling.
- Its derivation: icosahedra + Unborns (+ Lotus Jewel / Tricap variants) with the AXE gap, and the golden split of the AXE at t = 1/φ as the unique point where both padded pieces are convex; the AXE tips landing exactly on the mirror corners; the keyed √5 / 2 pair of the Lotus Jewel network and its free keyings.
"Not found" ≠ "new"; limited to the sources listed.

## Tetrahedron, octahedron, cube (DICTO 2026-10-23: "can we do something similar with tetrahedron octahedron cube?")
- **Octet family** (octet/octet1.py → octet-family.txt; picture golden-split/07): in the octet (regular tetrahedra + octahedra, fcc vertices), cut every tetra–octa double cone (tetra centre → shared face ← octa centre) by a plane parallel to the face at fraction t from the tetra centre (a power diagram, so every piece is convex). t = 0: octahedron pieces only = rhombic dodecahedra (fcc Voronoi cells; tetrahedra vanish); 0 < t < 1/3: small regular tetrahedra + rhombic dodecahedra with their 8 three-fold corners cut (8 triangles, 12 hexagons); t = 1/3: the octet; 1/3 < t < 1: cubes with 4 alternate corners cut (4 triangles, 6 hexagons) + small regular octahedra; t = 1: cubes. Volumes add to the cell at every t. The endpoints are classical dissections (RD = octahedron + 8 quarter-tetrahedra; cube = tetrahedron + 4 corner pieces); the octet is the middle of a one-parameter family of mixed space-fillers from Kepler's RD to the cube. Float only so far; prior art not checked (power diagrams of the octet's centres — likely known).
- **Factor x ends** (picture golden-split/08): T(x) → octahedron and T(1/x) → cuboctahedron as x → 0 — the rectified cubic honeycomb (octahedra + cuboctahedra, classical); x = 1 Kelvin; x = τ golden.
