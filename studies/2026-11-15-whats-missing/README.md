# What's missing: hidden swaps, unused involutions and the periodic points of the hops (15 Nov 2026)

**Verdict (15 Nov 2026, corrected after the independent referee the same day): no new law-giving fixed point was found. Every law forced today is classical: Brunn–Minkowski concavity together with a symmetry that negates the slicing direction (the fixed slices of evacuation and of the longest element w0), the evenness of a differentiable mirror-invariant quantity in the twist family of Narcissus (the simplest case of Palais's symmetric criticality), and an orbit lemma of the icosahedral group (the regular hulls of the pentakis cycle). Of the 17 laws that had no swap, 1 is in fact forced at a swap's fixed point (Kelvin's half of the Dehn test, already proved as the CsCl parity), 6 are carried by a swap as an invariance or a swapped pair (Hexagonality, Window, width, one-way-only, the all-golden 4D pair, the 4D Vacuus heart), 1 has only an algebraic swap (valley rule), and 9 have none. Gale and Alexander duality force nothing; the registry holds no Alexander fixed point. Only the Fibonacci hop has no periodic points beyond its two fixed points; the other cycles are periodic on shapes only through polarity or symmetry (Part 3 lists periods 2 and 4). On solids with all corners on one sphere about the centre, DICTO's growth rule T is the polarity swap up to scale.**

DICTO approved this study on 15 Nov 2026. ## Candidates for an independent referee, with the referee's verdicts

Each is stated so that it can be attacked; the wording below is the corrected one.

1. **#91 pair** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION). On K ∩ μK* with K = [−1, 1]⁴, 2 < μ < 3, the map μ ↦ 5 − μ is the B4 seed reversal (0, 1 − a, a, 0) ↦ (0, a, 1 − a, 0), a = μ − 2: a parameter pairing, geometric on the cube cells only, where it is the CsCl site swap. It swaps the two all-golden polytopes μ = φ² and μ = 2 + 1/φ²; its fixed point is the bitruncated tesseract, the uniform member, whose degree-4 tone is −45 ≠ 0; on this line the tone 48a²(a² − 4) vanishes only at the 24-cell end (a = 0), forced there by extra symmetry.
2. **#100 reclassified** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION). Theorem 1.1's zero at Kelvin's cell is forced by the CsCl swap: Dehn = −24√2(3 − 2μ) ⊗ β = 12√2(3 − 2μ) ⊗ arccos(−1/3) is odd, because the complementary cell in the cube tiling is the (3 − μ) cell and one of each fills a period. The swap pairs the cube (μ = 3) with the empty cell (μ = 0), so the zero at the cube is not at any swap's fixed point.
3. **Evacuation slice** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION; credit fixed). The slice of the n-permutohedron by the fixed space of x ↦ (n + 1)𝟙 − w₀x equals its projection (true for every convex body and every affine involution preserving it) and is, up to scale, the type-B permutohedron: the signed permutations of (n − 1, n − 3, …) in the coordinates u_i = x_i − x_{n+1−i}, that is (1, 3, …, 2k − 1) for n = 2k and (2, 4, …, 2k) for n = 2k + 1. This is the standard folding of A_{n−1} to B_k (Steinberg; Stembridge). At n = 6, 7 it is the silent-zonohedron line at a = 1/2, 1, where nothing in the battery is forced.
4. **Bells** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION; credit fixed). On an even number of bells every row of the plain hunt is fixed by reversing the row and replacing b by n + 1 − b; on an odd number the move maps the plain hunt to itself rung in the opposite order, and exactly two rows (rounds, back rounds) are fixed. Plain Bob Minimus goes to Reverse Bob Minimus (started from its half-lead): the ringers' reverse of a method.
5. **Brunn at the heart** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION). The DICTO 1:2 Truncated Octahedron (volume 122/3 in the coordinates x₁, x₂, x₃) is the largest slice x₅ = const of the 4D permutohedron (205/6 at t = ±1, 1835/48 at ±1/2). More generally, every slice fixed by a symmetry that negates the offset directions is the largest in its parallel family: Brunn 1887 for hyperplane slices, the general Brunn–Minkowski inequality for slices of higher codimension (the 6-permutohedron's fixed 3D slice, volume 87 against 307/4 and 64).
6. **Gale** (FAILS as first written, "every hexahedron"; stands for cube type). The corners of every hexahedron of cube type with planar faces are self-associated (for stable sets, Eisenbud–Popescu 2000, Cor. 7.4); a tetrahedron with two corners cut off is not; the octahedron frustum shows that self-association forces neither tiling nor Dehn zero; the hexagonal discs of the cuboctahedron, tetrakis hexahedron, elongated square bipyramid and bilunabirotunda lie on conics.
7. **Alexander** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION; credit fixed). No registry solid is a fixed point of S ↦ (E ∖ S)* (which needs a chosen self-duality; V = F is the screen, and half the edges on discs). Planar duality: Whitney 1932.
8. **Twist family** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION). In the family of two Narcissus joined on the hexagon and turned by θ, every differentiable mirror-invariant quantity is critical at the straight join and at Covenant (an even differentiable function; the simplest case of Palais 1979); the straight join is achiral.
9. **T is a swap** (STANDS WITH CORRECTION). On solids with all corners on one sphere about the centre, DICTO's growth rule is polar reciprocation up to scale; T² returns a scaled copy only when the image is again sphere-inscribed (the Platonic solids); the tetrahedron is a fixed point on shapes, up to the point reflection.
10. **Icosaxes and Galois** (STANDS; credit fixed). Icosaxes is fixed by √5 ↦ −√5 up to similarity, through a ↦ 1/a, which preserves Q₄ and is the identity on shapes; no Möbius involution preserving Q₄ on the cube–octahedron line fixes it; its silence is the 5-design property of its two icosahedra (Delsarte, Goethals and Seidel 1977).
11. **Pentakis regularity** (STANDS). The regular twin hulls of the pentakis cycle follow from icosahedral symmetry and the facet counts 12 and 20.
