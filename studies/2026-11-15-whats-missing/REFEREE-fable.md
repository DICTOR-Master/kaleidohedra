# Referee report: "What's missing" (15 Nov 2026)

Independent referee pass by Claude (Anthropic) on the README of this study, its script `scripts/missing.py` and its log. Each statement was re-derived by hand or read off the script's exact computation; three small confirmations were run on the compute host. The README and the books were not edited.

## The three most important issues

**1. The Brunn volumes are wrong: 140/3, 259/6 and 138 are not the volumes of those slices.** The theorem (the fixed slice is the largest) is true, but the printed "exact" numbers that support it are inflated.

- Cause. `rat_measure` (dimension 3) adds one pyramid per row of the inequality list, and `perm_planes` emits the same plane twice whenever two subsets give the same inequality. For the slice x₅ = 3 + t this happens for S and S ∪ {5} exactly when t = |S| − 2, so at t = 0 the six squares are counted twice, at t = 1 the triple facets, at t = −1 the single facets. For the 6-permutohedron it happens for S and S ∪ {i, 7 − i}.
- Correct values, in the script's own coordinates (y₁, y₂, y₃):

| slice | README | correct | how checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| x₅ = 3 (the DICTO 1:2 Truncated Octahedron, permutohedron of 1, 2, 4, 5) | 140/3 | **122/3** | by hand: cube ∩ μ·octahedron has volume 4μ³/3 − 4(μ − 1)³ = 244/81 at μ = 4/3, scale 3, projection factor 1/2; 140/3 − 122/3 = 6 is exactly the six squares counted twice; hull volume 40.6667 on the host |
| x₅ = 3 ± 1 (permutohedra of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 1, 3, 4, 5) | 259/6 | **205/6** | hull volume 34.1667 on the host |
| x₅ = 3 ± 1/2 | 1835/48 | 1835/48 (no coincident planes; consistent, not recomputed) | cube roots 3.245, 3.368, 3.439 are concave |
| evacuation-fixed slice of the 6-permutohedron, sperm(1/2, 3/2, 5/2) | 138 | **87** | the script's own formula V = 4(2a³ + 12a² + 21a + 8) at a = 1/2; hull volume 87.0000 on the host |
| the same, offsets (±1/4, ∓1/4, 0) and (±1/2, ∓1/2, 0) | 89, 212/3 | **not established** | 89 > 87 would contradict Brunn, so 89 is wrong; both must be recomputed |

- The README's own numbers already showed the fault: 1835/48 ≈ 38.2 at t = ±1/2 lies below 259/6 ≈ 43.2 at t = ±1. Volumes of parallel slices of a convex body cannot dip and rise again (Brunn). The checks passed only because they test "centre larger" and "even", not concavity.
- Correction: remove duplicate rows before `rat_measure` (or build each facet once), recompute all P5 and P6 slice volumes, add a concavity check of volume^(1/3), and replace the numbers in the README, the draft paper and the JSON. The octagon areas 7, 23/4, 4 use the 2D polygon route and are correct (7 re-derived by hand).

**2. Candidate 6 fails as written: not every hexahedron with 8 corners is self-associated.** There are two combinatorial types of convex hexahedron with 8 corners. The claim holds for the combinatorial cube (three pairs of opposite faces, whose plane pairs give three quadrics through the 8 corners). It fails for the tetrahedron with two corners cut off (2 triangles, 2 quadrilaterals, 2 pentagons): the quadric rank is 8 for two exact examples checked on the host, so the corners are not self-associated. Correction: "the corners of every hexahedron of cube type (combinatorial cube) with planar faces". The seven registry solids tested are all of cube type, so the registry statement stands.

**3. Brunn is credited for more than his hypothesis covers, and two headline sentences are looser than the tables.**

- Brunn's theorem concerns parallel hyperplane slices: volume^(1/(n−1)) is concave. A maximum at the middle needs, in addition, a symmetry of the body that negates the slicing direction. Full central symmetry is sufficient but not necessary: the tetrahedron's square slice and the 5-cell's slice x₁ − x₅ = 0 are correct examples although neither body is centrally symmetric, because w₀ negates the direction. Candidate 5 should say "every slice fixed by a symmetry that negates the offset directions", not "of a centrally symmetric permutohedron".
- For n ≥ 6 the evacuation-fixed slice has codimension 2 or more in the permutohedron (3D slice of a 5D body at n = 6). Brunn 1887 does not cover that; the general Brunn–Minkowski inequality does (slices by parallel k-flats, volume^(1/k) concave in the offset). Credit "Brunn–Minkowski" there.
- The verdict says "the hops have no periodic points beyond their fixed points". Part 3 itself lists period 2 for T (cube, dodecahedron), period 4 for the pentakis cycle and "every member" for the golden cycles. The sentence is true only of the Fibonacci hop. Correction: "the Fibonacci hop has no periodic points beyond its two fixed points; the other cycles are periodic on shapes only through polarity or symmetry".
- 8 of the 108 checks are `check(True, …)`: statements asserted in the script, not computed (the #91 seed reversal, the #45 duality invariance, the #85 fixed point, the identification with the silent-zonohedron line, the tiling and Dehn remark, the icosahedral core, "T is the polarity swap", the pentakis orbit lemma). The count of computed checks is 100. Each of the eight is true (see the table), but they are proofs by argument and should be labelled so.

## Headline verdict

"No new law-giving fixed point; every forced law is a known one": **STANDS.** Nothing in the eleven candidates is a new law. Every forced statement reduces to (a) evenness plus Brunn–Minkowski concavity, (b) evenness of a smooth function at a mirror-fixed parameter, (c) orbit sizes of the icosahedral group, or (d) Dehn additivity in a two-tile tiling. The corrections above and below change numbers, wording and credits, not the verdict.

## The corrections, exactly

**(A) #91.** The B4 diagram has no symmetry, so "seed reversal" is not an automorphism of the group and μ ↦ 5 − μ is not a geometric involution of the family; the README's table says so ("not a similarity" on the tetrahedral cells), the candidate should too: "a parameter pairing, geometric on the cube cells only". Its fixed point is singled out by a different, checkable property: it is the uniform member (both edge lengths equal). Missed fact: on this line the degree-4 tone is proportional to a²(a² − 4), so its only zero on 2 ≤ μ ≤ 3 is a = 0, the 24-cell, forced there by extra symmetry (F4 has no invariant of degree 4). That is a known forced zero at an endpoint, not at the fixed point; it belongs in the row.

**(B) #100.** Re-derived: for 1 < μ < 2 the cell has 24 edges of length (μ − 1)√2 with dihedral π − β′ and 12 edges of length (2 − μ)√2 with dihedral 2β′, β′ = arccos(1/√3), so Dehn = 24√2(3 − 2μ) ⊗ β′ = 12√2(3 − 2μ) ⊗ arccos(−1/3). The candidate's "12√2(3 − 2μ)" is right only with the angle arccos(−1/3); the control row prints the same quantity with twice the size and the opposite sign (−72√2/5 at 6/5); and the README uses β for arccos(1/√3) in Part 1 and for arccos(1/3) in the Gale row. Fix one angle unit and one name throughout. The forcing is a real proof and should be written as one: the complementary cell of cube ∩ μ·octahedron in the cube tiling is a copy of cube ∩ (3 − μ)·octahedron about the cube corner, one of each fills a period, so D(μ) + D(3 − μ) = 0. The swap extends to 0 ≤ μ ≤ 3 and pairs the cube (μ = 3) with the empty cell (μ = 0): the cube's zero is the image of nothing, which agrees with "not at a fixed point".

**(C) Evacuation slice.** (i) "Slice = projection" holds for every convex body and every affine involution preserving it (x ↦ (x + σx)/2 maps the body into the fixed slice and is the identity on it); the 30 random directions are not needed. (ii) The corners, by a one-line rearrangement argument, are the signed permutations of (n − 1, n − 3, …) in the coordinates u_i = x_i − x_{n+1−i}; so "(1, 3, …, 2k − 1)" and "(1, …, k)" are two different scalings (u for even n, u/2 for odd n). Say "up to scale". (iii) Credit: this is the folding of the Coxeter diagram A_{n−1} by its symmetry to B_k; the fixed-point set of the diagram automorphism on the type-A permutohedron being the type-B permutohedron is standard (Steinberg's folding; see Stembridge's note on folding by automorphisms). Not new. (iv) RSK of the reverse-complement word giving (evac P, evac Q): Schützenberger 1963; a textbook statement is Stanley, Enumerative Combinatorics 2, Appendix 1 (Theorem A1.2.10). (v) Self-evacuating tableaux and domino tableaux: this was Stanley's conjecture, proved by Stembridge 1996; bijective proof by Berenstein and Kirillov. "Stembridge 1996; Stanley 2009" is acceptable only if Stanley 2009 is cited as the survey.

**(D) Bells.** Proof: with x = (12)(34)… and y = (23)(45)…, conjugation by w₀ fixes x and y when n is even (so every row of the hunt is fixed; w₀ is the central element (xy)^(n/2), back rounds) and exchanges x and y when n is odd. Correction for odd n: the two hunts are the same set of 2n rows rung in opposite order, so the move maps the plain hunt to itself reversed, and exactly two of its rows are fixed (rounds and back rounds). Credit fix: the move is what ringers call the reverse of a method. The image of Plain Bob Minimus (x14x14x14x12) is x14x14x14x34, which is **Reverse Bob Minimus** started from its half-lead (the method library confirms Reverse Bob as the reverse of Plain Bob; its place notation x14x34x14x14 is from memory and should be checked against the library); the README should name it. "8 of its 24 rows are fixed" says nothing about the method: its rows are all of S₄ and 8 = 2²·2! is the number of centrally symmetric rows.

**(E) Gale.** Besides issue 2: (i) the test "rank ≤ 7 means self-associated" is a theorem only for stable point sets (for 8 points in space: no two equal, no four on a line, no six in a plane): Coble 1929; Dolgachev–Ortland 1988; Eisenbud–Popescu 2000, Corollary 7.4. The solids tested satisfy it (at most 4 or 5 corners in a plane), but the script does not check it; state it. Without stability the criterion is Eisenbud–Popescu Theorem 7.1(b): every 7-point subset must impose as many conditions as all 8. (ii) Self-association is a projective notion (Coble's association). Gale's 1956 duality for polytopes also carries signs; "Gale fixed point" should be read as "self-associated in Coble's sense". (iii) The frustum: Dehn = −3√2 ⊗ arccos(1/3) re-derived by hand (edges √2, √2/2, √2/2; dihedrals π/2 − β/2, π/2 + β/2, π − β); correct. (iv) Six points of a plane are self-associated exactly when they lie on a conic (classical; Eisenbud–Popescu, introduction and Theorem 7.1). For the cuboctahedron the hexagons are regular, so the conic is a circle: true and without content.

**(F) Alexander.** The count stands (4 of 14 and 12 of 26 are not half). Two corrections. The condition "V = F" is necessary but not the real hypothesis: S ↦ (E ∖ S)* needs a chosen self-duality of the solid; V = F is only the screen. Credit: the correspondence between an edge set on the sphere and the dual of its complement is planar (matroid) duality, Whitney 1932; Alexander 1922 is the homological statement behind χ(S) = 1. Say "no fixed point in the registry", never "no fixed point exists" (the README's last column says the latter).

**(G) Twist family.** The proof is: Narcissus has a vertical mirror, so the join at −θ is the mirror image of the join at θ; with the 120° period, f(θ) = f(−θ) and f(60° + θ) = f(60° − θ); a differentiable even function has derivative zero. That is elementary; Palais 1979 applies (the fixed set of Z/2 is two isolated points) but is far heavier than needed. Cite it as "the simplest case of". The hypothesis "smooth" matters and is not checked for the battery: hull volume, Mahler volume, roundness and wall area can have a corner (like |θ|) at exactly these symmetric angles, where the hull changes type; for those, evenness gives an extremum or a kink, not f′ = 0. The two tones tested are polynomials in cos θ and sin θ, so they are fine. The candidate should read "every differentiable mirror-invariant quantity".

**(H) T.** T(S) = {x : v·x ≤ v·v} equals R² × (polar of S) only when every corner v has the same length R. For a solid with corners at two or more distances from the centre it is not a scaled polar, and T² returns a scaled copy only if T(S) again has all corners on one sphere (true for the five Platonic solids; false for an Archimedean solid, whose T is a Catalan solid). The script's own table row says "every sphere-inscribed solid whose image is inscribed"; the candidate and the verdict must carry that restriction. The three computed cases are right: T(tetrahedron) = −3 × tetrahedron, T²(cube) = 3 × cube, T²(dodecahedron) = (R/r)² × dodecahedron = (15 − 6√5) × dodecahedron. "The" fixed point should be "a" fixed point: every self-polar solid with corners on one sphere is one, and the tetrahedron is fixed only up to the point reflection.

**(I) Icosaxes.** Checked: σ(1/φ) = −φ and sperm(1, φ, 0) = φ × sperm(1, 1/φ, 0); Q₄ on the line is proportional to a⁴ − 3a² + 1, zero only at a = φ^(±1); the unique real Möbius involution fixing 1/φ and keeping {±i} is a ↦ (2 − a)/(2a + 1) (derived independently), and it moves φ off the zero set. One addition: a ↦ 1/a preserves Q₄ exactly and is the identity on shapes (its fixed point a = 1 is the cuboctahedron); it is why every unit of norm ±1 is Galois-fixed up to similarity, and the README should say so. Credit fix: spherical designs and the icosahedron as a (tight) 5-design are Delsarte, Goethals and Seidel 1977; Goethals and Seidel 1981 is the later paper on orbits of reflection groups. Cite 1977 first.
