# Vacuus, Scutoid Frusta & Aequus: the first two studies (24 November 2026)

DICTO's top-priority project (his page "Vacuus, Scutoid Frusta, & Aequus"; aim: "the exploration classification and possible
combinatory interdependencies & utilization of these three completely unique discoveries"). Classification items 1 and 2 of his page.
Names Vacuus (heart, inherited heart, non-heart), Aequus/Aequi, Scutoid Frusta, amber cell, Fullerene Star: DICTO's.

**Verdict, study 1: all 1232 Aequi classified with exact symmetry checks.** Every centrally symmetric die is a heart (276: Ih 4, Oh 57,
Th 215); every die without central symmetry but with at least one pair of parallel faces is an inherited heart (951: O 31, Td 92, T 828);
**only 5 Aequi are non-heart** (no two faces parallel: Td 2, T 3), all built from one tetrahedron half O8/4a plus halves of the
triacontahedron directions. Only the 4 Ih dice have icosahedral symmetry.

**Verdict, study 2: 15 amber cells (5 Aequi x sc, bcc, fcc moulds at 6/5 s0), all valid exact partitions of the resin.** In 14 of the
15 cells **every scutoid frustum keeps its side count** (no Y vertex anywhere). The one exception: TRT-16 (T 3) in the sc mould, where
**all 16 scutoid frusta change their side count** (12 Y vertices). The Th 60-face die (RD12 + D12 + I12′ + RT24′) gives 60 scutoid frusta
in 4 kinds, all keeping their side count, in all three moulds.

Method (exact):
- The 122 face directions are matched one to one with exact vectors in Q(φ)³ (six base patterns, all permutations and signs).
- Every die is invariant under the frame rotation group T (checked). A finite group of isometries containing this T is one of T, Th, Td, O,
  Oh, I, Ih (I and Ih in two placements), each generated by T and some of: −I, the mirror x↔y, the quarter turn about z, a 5-fold
  rotation of either icosahedron. So the full group is fixed by which of these five maps carry the die to itself. Each map acts on the
  122 directions exactly (exact parallelism in Q(√5)); a face may go only to a face of the same depth. Same orbit: equal depth by
  construction. Different orbits: the 60-digit depths differ by at least 3.1e-5 wherever this was needed.
- One die has two orbits at the same depth to 60 digits: Aequus CIRT-30, T 26 (its C6 and RT24/12a faces both at depth 1). Its
  group was checked both ways (depths equal / unequal): T either way. The equality is now proved exact (next section).
- Vacuus kind: heart = centrally symmetric; inherited heart = not centrally symmetric, yet some plate carries a face and its parallel
  opposite face; non-heart = no two faces parallel. Plates = one per line through the centre carrying face normals = N − (parallel pairs):
  heart N/2; non-heart N.
- Icosahedral core: the 4 Ih dice (placement A) share the symmetry of the dodecahedron, icosahedron, icosidodecahedron and Fullerene Star.
  The column parallel_core lists the cores whose faces are all parallel to faces of the die: DI-32 and D·I·RT-62 take all of them,
  DRT-42 the dodecahedron, IRT-50 the icosahedron. Every other die: no icosahedral symmetry; the core sits in a subgroup.

## CIRT-30: C6 and RT24/12a at exactly the same depth (scripts/cirt30_exact.py; logs/cirt30_exact.log)

**Theorem (exact).** The fair die Aequus CIRT-30, T 26 has its 6 C6 faces and its 12 RT24/12a faces at exactly the same distance from
the centre. Its 12 I12 faces sit at x/√3 times that distance, where x is the real root near 1.758 of 16x⁴ − 40x³ − 100x² + 130x + 145.

Certificate. (1) The candidate die (C6 and RT24/12a at depth 1, I12 at x/√3) has every vertex in K = Q(√5, x). Its 44 vertices are
verified exactly: zero in K on their own planes, and strictly inside all other planes (arb enclosures). (2) For a face cone with
corners v_k, cos Ω = (−1)ⁿ Re ∏(α_k + i√G_k)/M, with α_k, G_k and M in K (interior angles of the spherical polygon). (3) β = cos Ω − cos(2π/15)
is a root of a polynomial P in Q[B], obtained exactly by resultants in PARI (eliminating the √G_k, cos(2π/15), x and √5). P = B^k·Q
with Q(0) ≠ 0, so a nonzero β would have |β| ≥ |q₀|/(|q₀| + max|qᵢ|) (Cauchy). The arb enclosure of |β| lies far below that bound,
so β = 0. Ω lies in (0, π), so Ω = 2π/15 = 4π/30. Results: C6 hexagon, P of degree 512, |β| < 4e-1186 against the bound 3.5e-181; RT24/12a pentagon, P of degree 2048, |β| < 3e-1193 against the bound 1.8e-729 (logs/cirt30_exact.log, FINAL ok). The I12 faces then also have 4π/30, since the 30 cones fill the
sphere. (4) The fair die of an orbit set is unique up to scale (Alexandrov's uniqueness for given normals and curvatures; the search
found exactly one), so the fair CIRT-30 is this die. The polynomial q came from an integer relation on the 60-digit depth. It serves
only to name the candidate; the proof does not depend on how it was found.

## Study 2: amber cells (A), first set (scripts/aequus_solid.py, amber_aequus.py, run_cells.sh; data/cells; logs/cells)

Amber cell (A): the die as core at every point of a cubic lattice (sc, bcc, fcc), mould = its Voronoi cell at 6/5 s0 (s0 = smallest
scale with the die inside), resin = mould minus die, divided by DICTO's combined method exactly as in study 2026-11-21-amber-cell-sweep
(outer pattern = Voronoi diagram of the face centroids on the mould; Y vertex where the two patterns differ). amber.py is reused
unchanged except: the core is the die; orbits are taken under the frame rotation group T (every Aequus has it); each piece is also
tested for convexity exactly; geometry is saved for the renders.

### What is exact, and for which die

The fair depths are not known in closed form in general (CIRT-30, above, is an exception), so each die is built from its face planes
u·x ≤ D, with u exact in Q(φ)³ and D rational. Claims:

- **Exact for the rational stand-in** (D within 1e-14 of d·|u|, d the 60-digit fair depth; data/cells/stand, logs/cells): the
  stand-in's face lattice equals the fair die's (face count, corners, side counts). Every piece is a closed oriented embedded surface,
  and wall triangles cancel in pairs. Piece volumes are exact and add to mould − die exactly (gap 0 in Q(√5)). Convexity of each piece
  is decided exactly. Piece counts, side counts, kinds and Y vertices are exact for this polytope.
- **Certified for every depth vector in the box B** of radius 1e-18 around a rational centre within 1e-20 of the 60-digit fair
  depths (scripts/certify.py, run_certify.sh; logs/certify). The amber code runs once with every number carried two ways: exactly at the
  centre, and as an arb ball enclosing its value over all of B. Every nonzero sign the code tests is certified to keep that sign over
  the whole box: in all 15 cells, 0 nonzero signs uncertified, out of 36 507 to 291 800 sign tests per cell (logs/run_certify.log, FINAL ok); the box runs give the same counts as the stand-in in every cell. Every sign that is zero at the centre was tested again, in exact arithmetic, at two random rational
  points of B. Both gave the same sign sequence (equal sha256) and the same counts. So the code takes one path on all of B, and the
  combinatorics (pieces, side counts, kinds, Y vertices, convexity) is the same everywhere in B. One part of this is randomised: that
  the zero tests vanish on all of B, not only at the centre and the two random points, rests on a Schwartz–Zippel identity test
  (64-bit random offsets). It is not a deterministic proof.
- **Transfer to the real fair die** needs its depth vector to lie in B, that is, within 1e-20 of the 60-digit values. The search
  studies confirmed the depths to 80 digits (residual 1e-100) but gave no interval proof of where the root lies. That step is the one
  link not yet certified (LEAD 7).
- Not claimed: the volumes of the real die's pieces (transcendental in general). Only their combinatorics carries over.

Checks on every cell (all 15 pass): each piece is a closed oriented embedded surface; every wall triangle is used by exactly two pieces
with opposite orientation; every piece has positive exact volume; **piece volumes add to mould − die exactly (gap 0 in Q(√5))**;
winding-number sampling 3729 points, none covered other than once. FINAL ok (logs/run_cells.log).

## What this shows

- Heart, inherited heart and non-heart split the Aequi by symmetry: heart exactly when the group contains −I (Ih, Oh, Th); without −I,
  nearly every die still has parallel face pairs. Non-heart Aequi are rare (5 of 1232).
- A fair die's own face pattern carries over to the mould almost unchanged: with face-centroid seeds the outer pattern had the same
  adjacency as the die in 14 of 15 cells, so the resin divides into scutoid frusta that keep their side count. Side changes need a die
  whose faces are far from the mould's shape (the small tetrahedral TRT-16 in the cube).

## The Vacuus cut (26 Nov 2026; scripts/vacuus_cut.py, logs/vcut; idea proposed by Claude, tested at DICTO's "yes test the vacuus cut")
Cut the resin along the die's own Vacuus planes (through O and each die edge): the piece over face F is cone(O, F) ∩ mould beyond F.
8 dice (heart 377, DI-32, CO-14; non-heart 61, 62, 236, 237, 499) x sc, bcc, fcc at 6/5 s0, exact Q(√5); volume gap 0 in all 24 cells, FINAL ok.
- By construction (proof in two lines: an intersection of half-spaces; the cones over the die's faces meet only along Vacuus planes):
  every piece is convex, its neighbours are exactly the die's face neighbours, one lateral face per die edge, NO Y vertex, never a
  side change. The Vacuus cut is the cut that never makes a scutoid: the pieces are frusta of pyramids, with a flat outer end (one
  mould face) or a bent one (several mould faces, across mould edges).
- Flat vs bent outer ends depend on die and mould, not on Vacuus kind (e.g. CO-14: 6 flat in sc, 8 in bcc, 0 in fcc; 236 and 237:
  24 flat in sc and fcc, 4 in bcc; 61 and 62: none flat in any mould).
- Not new as mathematics (the central fan over a convex polytope's faces); it gives the baseline: scutoid behaviour (side changes,
  Y vertices) needs a cut that is NOT the Vacuus. No main point linking Vacuus and Scutoid-Frusta from this; at most one line in the
  Scutoid-Frusta paper's definitions, if DICTO wants it.
