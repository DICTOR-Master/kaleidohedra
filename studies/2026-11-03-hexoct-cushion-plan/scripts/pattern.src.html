Knitted HexOct Patch: Pattern

One small three-sheet patch, A-B-C, knitted in step with its link cords knitted in: the trial piece c of the cushion plan

DICTO, dictospheres.com, 3 November 2026, study 2026-11-03-hexoct-cushion-plan (scripts/pattern.py), pattern, not published
Status. Pattern for DICTO; nothing knitted yet. Open points:
  1. The in-step method and the cord catches are untried; this patch is their first test.
  2. The press and shear checks need something that pushes the sheets apart. The pattern uses a loose wool test fill (about {{fill}} g), pushed in for the checks and taken out afterwards; the patch itself has no fill, side walls or cover.
  3. The gauge (22 stitches and 22 ridges per 10 cm) is the plan's assumption and must be measured on the swatch.
  4. There are no expected values for sink and shift: these are the first measurements. Comparing c with h needs the h patch (section 7).
  5. Whether an overhand stopper knot in 1 mm cord holds in a DK garter stitch is not tested; the fall-back is a second overhand on top.
  6. The card gauge must be printed at 100 % (check bar beside it, section 3.3).

DICTO proposed the Knitted HexOct (#83): knitted sheets as close-packed layers, each node joined to 3 nodes of the next sheet by a link of length a at {{tilt}} degrees, which holds the sheets root(2/3) a apart. After the cushion plan DICTO chose to start with one patch. This is the plan's trial piece c: three garter sheets knitted at the same time on three needles, joined by {{NL}} link cords that are knitted in at both ends, nothing sewn. Every number below comes from scripts/pattern.py, on the plan's lattice (checks in patch/data/check.out).

Gauge

Garter stitch on 3.5 mm needles: 22 stitches and 22 ridges to 10 cm (one ridge = two rows). Cast on 30 stitches, knit 30 ridges, lay the swatch flat without stretching and count over 10 cm. More stitches than 22: change to a larger needle; fewer: a smaller one. The stitch gauge must match, because the card sets the link length: 8 stitches must span the card's length a = {{a}} mm. For the ridges, 22 gives a node-row spacing {{e22}} from the ideal, 23 gives {{e23}}, 21 gives {{e21}}; write your count in the results table (section 6).

How to read the chart and the tables

The chart (Figure 8) shows the three sheets on one grid, as you hold the work for row 1: one square is one stitch by one ridge, st 1 at the right, the cast-on edge at the bottom; circles, squares and triangles are the node stitches of A, B and C; teal lines join the two ends of each link. The ridge table (section 4) says what to do on every ridge, row by row, sheet by sheet; a ridge listed as "knit" is plain knitting. The link table (section 5) lists every cord piece with a box for each catch.

3. Techniques

3.1 Three needles in step

  1. Set-up. Each sheet has its own needle and its own ball. Hold the three needles as a stack: A in front, then B, then C, with the working yarns at the right. Put a locking marker in st 1 of each sheet: this edge is where row 1 starts.
  2. Row 1. Knit across A, then tip A forward out of the way; knit across B and tip it forward; knit across C (Figure 3, panels 1 and 2).
  3. Turn. Lift the three needles together and turn the stack round as one, the way you turn any knitting at the end of a row: C is now in front (panel 3). Turn one way after row 1 and back the other way after row 2, so that the three yarns do not twist round each other.
  4. Row 2. Knit across C, then B, then A, tipping each forward in turn (panel 4). Turn back: A is in front for the next ridge.
  5. Which row takes a cord. A cord always lies at the back of the work. On row 1 the back of each sheet faces the sheet above it, so cords going up are caught on row 1; on row 2 the back faces the sheet below, so cords going down are caught on row 2. The ridge table gives the row for every catch.
  6. Counting. Row 1 runs from st 1 to st {{W}}, row 2 from st {{W}} back to st 1; for row 2 the ridge table also gives the count from the start of the row.

3.2 The node stitch with the cord (CK)

3.3 Catching a cord at the card length

The first catch of a piece (the fold of a V, or the first end of a single with a 2.5 cm tail at the back) needs no measuring. Every later catch is made at the card length, so that every link is exactly a long between its two stitches: equal lengths matter more than anything else in this pattern.

4. The patch, ridge by ridge

Cast on {{W}} stitches on each of the three needles (long-tail), each with its own ball, and set up the stack (section 3.1). Ridge 1 is the first two rows after the cast-on. Work {{H}} ridges as in the table, all three sheets in step; every catch is a CK (section 3.2), every catch marked "at card" is measured (section 3.3), and every catch is knotted (section 3.4). When you reach a stitch that holds a cord loop from the row before, lock it (section 3.2, step 4). Bind off after ridge {{H}}, in the row-1 position: bind off A knitwise, loosely, then B, then C. Cut the yarns leaving 15 cm.

5. Link table

Tick each box as the catch is made and knotted. "B st 8, r 6/2" = sheet B, stitch 8, ridge 6, row 2. A V piece has two far catches, one for each arm; take the arm on the side of that stitch.

6. Finishing and the checks

  1. Ends. Weave each yarn end into its own sheet. Check that all {{NC}} boxes of the link table are ticked and every knot sits snug.
  2. Test fill. Tease about {{fillgap}} g of carded wool per gap into thin layers and slide them between A and B and between B and C from the open sides, until every cord is just taut and the sheets lie flat. The fill is for the checks only.
  3. Measure (Figure 9, panel 1): lay the patch on a table and read the values in the table below. Look along a row of nodes: the cords run straight on through sheet B; a thin knitting needle laid along an A-B cord lines up with the B-C cord on the other side of the same B stitch.
  4. Press (panel 2): the patch on the non-slip mat, the board on top with a piece of mat between board and patch. Read the height at the middle of one side with the board only, then with the 5 kg bag in the middle of the board ({{kpa}} kPa; the plan gives about 1 kPa for a head and 6 kPa for sitting). Take the bag off, wait one minute, read again.
  5. Shear (panel 3): tape a stop (a strip of wood) to the table against the bottom sheet's far edge. With the 5 kg bag on, hook the luggage scale to the board and pull level, slowly. Mark the board's start on a strip of tape and read its shift at 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 kg on the scale.
  6. After the checks, pull the test fill out, trim the cord tails to 5 mm beyond the knots, and keep the patch flat.

7. The h patch (A-B-A), if DICTO wants to compare

The h version differs only in sheet 3's offsets: its nodes sit over sheet A's, on the ridges and stitches below. Its links follow the same rules (each node to the three nearest nodes of the next sheet; the node met first takes the fold of a V or the first end of a single; up on row 1, down on row 2): {{nlh}} links from {{ph}} cord pieces, and no link pair runs straight on through B.
