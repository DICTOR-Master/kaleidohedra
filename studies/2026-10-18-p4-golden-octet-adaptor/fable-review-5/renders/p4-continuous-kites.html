Depth transition: section across the seam, and the depth law

Measured on the OCD itself: its kites fold by 23.8° (dihedral 156.2°) and the star centre rises 1.12 mm above the ring of its neighbours — the imprint's own relief is about 1.1 mm, i.e. DICTO's number. A kite roof folding like the OCD's facets (23.8°) over one row spacing is 0.9 mm deep. So "imprint depth fading to 1.1 mm" would be flat; for a visible transition the rows nearest the socket must be deeper than the imprint. Drawn: 2.0 mm at the socket rim fading linearly to 1.1 mm at 37 mm from the OCD centre (the fade spans the inner 11 mm of each face; the far corners stay at 1.1 mm). Alternative if the far surface should be fainter: 1.1 mm at the rim fading to 0.5 mm. The depth field depends only on the distance from the nearer OCD centre, so it is continuous across every seam and fold (the piece's E0T-type edges lie on the bisector of the two centres).

(b) The 8 flat faces of one W (each drawn in its own frame: A at the fan centre, E along the edge, T at the apex)

All eight faces carry the same picture in their own (A, E, T) frame; the four bump faces (A0E0T±, A1E1T±) with the signs shown, the four dent faces (A0E1T±, A1E0T±) with every sign reversed. Across the fold A0T+ (faces 1 and 3) the two pictures are related by the 3-fold turn about that edge, across A0E0 (faces 1 and 0) by the mirror x = 0, across E0T+ (faces 1 and 4) by the 3-fold turn about that edge, across the piece (faces 1 and 7) by the inversion through the edge midpoint — each fixes the shared edge point by point, so every kite line that reaches a fold continues on the next face.

How the pattern is derived, and what it does

  • Tile. The OCD's star kite itself: long edge 9.4 mm, short edge 7.8 mm, length 14.7 mm, apex angles 55.7° / 68.6° (60 mm octahedron edge). Same scale as the star in the socket.
  • Tiling. The only way congruent kites tile a plane edge to edge: rows of kites nose to tail, neighbouring rows pointing the opposite way and sitting in each other's notches (row spacing 4.4 mm). The rows run parallel to the piece's seam edge A0E0 and one row axis lies on the seam.
  • Why that orientation is forced. Across the seam A0E0 the next face in the same plane belongs to another W, placed by the half-turn about the A0E0 axis. That turn reverses the face normal, so the height field must be anti-symmetric across the seam (zero on it) and the kite lines must be mirror-symmetric about it. The kite tiling's only mirror lines are its row axes, hence rows parallel to A0E0 with an axis on the seam. The seam kites are then split exactly in half: the upper half rises as a bump of one W, the lower half sinks as the dent of the W behind — DICTO's "kite split between neighbouring W".
  • Heights. Row axes alternate: seam 0, then +d/2, −d/2, +d/2 … outward (anti-symmetric on the other side), with d the local depth of the transition law above (deepest at the socket, 1.1 mm far out). Each kite becomes a shallow roof (ridge along its long diagonal) or trough; every raised kite row on a bump face faces a sunk row on the neighbour. Within one face the rows alternate raised / sunk, so the pattern is decorative on its own and the whole face is a key.
  • One identical piece. The eight faces pair bipartitely across the structure (bump faces A0E0T±, A1E1T±; dent faces A0E1T±, A1E0T±; the neighbour across any face presents a face of the other class). The pattern on one face is carried to the other seven by the piece's own 2/m symmetry and by the 3-fold turns about the OCD centres, as in the first keyed W, so every W is the same solid and every bump meets a dent. This is exact, not approximate.

Problems, stated plainly

  • Flat tilted kites are impossible. A periodic plane tiling by one flat tilted kite is a tilted plane: there is no non-trivial way to give every kite a flat facet. The kites here are creased along their long diagonal (two triangles, fold of 10° at 1.1 mm); at that depth the crease reads as a soft ridge, not a break, but it is not the OCD's flat facet.
  • The star does not continue into the rows. Five-fold order cannot run into a periodic row tiling. The socket rim is a visible join, as on the OCD where the W planes cut its faces. Of the two star edges reaching each face (dashed), one lies within 1° of a row direction but aligning it would tilt the rows off the seam and break the mirror; the rows stay parallel to A0E0.
  • The piece's oblique edges. Along A0T, E0T (and their A1 twins) the relief must return to the face plane so that the faces meet in a closed solid: a fade band about one row wide along those edges (not drawn as such; the clipped kites there will be flattened in 3D). Along the seam edges A0E0, A1E1 nothing fades — the zero line is the seam itself.
  • Corners. The piece corner E0 falls at 2.9 kite lengths from A0, not on a kite vertex; the pattern is simply cut there, and beyond E0 the next W's rows run the other way (inversion through E0). Pattern lines meet at E0 but the kites do not continue straight through it.
  • The transition and the sockets. The deepest rows sit next to the socket rim, exactly where the OCD's shadow (grey) overhangs the face plane: raised rows there must stay clear of the OCD (the first keyed W kept 1.3 mm). The transition may need to start a few mm outside the rim; to be checked in 3D.
