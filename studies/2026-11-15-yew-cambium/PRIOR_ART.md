# Prior art: counting cambial file events in conifers (yew study)

Date: 2026-11-15. Free sources only; nothing was bought. "Abstract only" means the full text was not read. "Snippet only" means only a search-engine summary was seen, so the number should be checked before anyone quotes it.

Question: a stem cross-section records the cambium (a sheet of fusiform initials) as radial files of tracheids. Files split (pseudotransverse/anticlinal divisions), end (loss or elimination of initials) and shift sideways (intrusive growth, domains, rearrangement). What counts of these events exist already, and in what units?

Bannan's unit, as the abstracts give it: **divisions per centimetre of xylem increment** (radial growth). The abstracts do not spell out whether this is per lineal series of cells, i.e. per radial file, but that is how the method works (he followed lineal series through serial tangential sections). This should be checked in one full text before we rely on it. On that reading, 1.3 divisions per cm with 1 mm rings means about 0.13 divisions per file per ring, and 0.8 per cm with 2 mm rings means about 0.16 per file per ring (our conversion). In narrow rings the rate per cm rises, so this conversion does not carry over.

### 2. Bailey 1923: whole-circumference counts
Bailey, I. W. 1923. The cambium and its derivative tissues IV. The increase in girth of the cambium. Am. J. Bot. 10(9): 499-509. https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1537-2197.1923.tb05747.x (full OCR read: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.37524)
- Pinus strobus, one-year-old stem: cambium circumference 12,566 microns; "Number of fusiform initials in a cross section of stem 724"; ray initials 70; fusiform initial length 870 microns, tangential diameter 16 microns.
- Sixty-year-old stem: circumference 1,256,640 microns; fusiform initials 4,000 microns long, 42 microns wide; "from 724 to 23,100 fusiform initials and from 70 to 8,796 ray initials" over "the 59-year interval".
- Our arithmetic: a net gain of 22,376 fusiform initials in one transverse plane over 59 years, about 380 per year for the whole stem. Density falls from about 576 per cm of circumference (year 1) to about 184 per cm (year 60). Those are net figures; gross divisions and losses are larger.

### 5. Other quantitative cambium studies (for units)
- Bossinger, G. and Spokevicius, A. V. 2018. Sector analysis reveals patterns of cambium differentiation in poplar stems. J. Exp. Bot. 69(18): 4339-4348. https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/ery230 (PMC6093462). Read (page reader). Transgenic sector tracing in poplar: "approximately one in four cells that gave rise to a cambial initial lineage" "was purged from the cambial zone across a growing season"; 581 sectors, 221 traced to single initials, 51 "lost initial" sectors; sector width typically "6 (±1.1) cells". This is the only **per-initial-lineage-per-season** loss figure found, and it is for an angiosperm.
- Barlow, P. W., Brain, P., Powers, S. J. 2002. Estimation of directional division frequencies in vascular cambium and in marginal meristematic cells of plants. Hybrid aspen, about "2071 ± 152" initials around the stem; "one new cell file is gained following the anticlinal division of a cambial initial cell"; division counts expressed as sets of periclinal productions per doubling of initials (19-28 sets per doubling). No per-year rate.
- Rao, K. S., Menon, A. R. S., Dave, Y. 1989. Developmental and dimensional changes in the cambial fusiform initials of Holoptelea integrifolia. Biol. ### 8. Topological or "tissue event" framing
- **Scutoids**: Gómez-Gálvez, P., Vicente-Munuera, P., Tagua, A., Forja, C., Castro, A. M., Letrán, M., Valencia-Expósito, A., Grima, C., Bermúdez-Gallardo, M., Serrano-Pérez-Higueras, Ó., Cavodeassi, F., Sotillos, S., Martín-Bermudo, M. D., Márquez, A., Buceta, J., Escudero, L. Defined for curved animal epithelia: cells "have different neighbours in their basal and apical surfaces". Plants, wood and cambium are not mentioned.
- Searches for "scutoid" with plant, cambium or xylem returned only animal-epithelium work plus two cross-kingdom reviews whose abstracts do not mention scutoids or cambium: Hamant, O. and Saunders, T. E. 2020, Annu. Rev. Cell Dev. Biol. 36: 385, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-cellbio-012820-103850 ; Harrison, R., Hamant, O., Saunders, T. E. 2026, Development 153, https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.205774. Full texts not read. **No scutoid framing of wood or cambium was found.**
- DICTO's own record: DICTO 2026, "Scutoid Tissues in Four Dimensions", Zenodo, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24111864. This is our own prior work, not outside prior art.
- **Neighbour exchange in cambium described in cell-biology terms, not topology**: Jura-Morawiec et al. 2006, Włoch et al. 2009, 2013, Miodek et al. 2022 and Bossinger and Spokevicius 2018 all describe an initial's tip intruding between a neighbour and its derivative, with that neighbour's file eliminated. In vertex-model language this is a neighbour exchange (T1) followed by a cell leaving the sheet (T2). **None of them uses T1/T2, topology or Euler-type counting.** Krawczyszyn 1971, "Unidirectional splitting and uniting of rays in the cambium of Platanus" (https://doi.org/10.5586/asbp.1971.004), counts ray splitting and uniting events by name. Not read.
- **Wave / field models**: Hejnowicz 1971, 1974 and Zagórska-Marek and Hejnowicz 1980 treat Z/S domains as travelling "morphogenetic waves" of orientation (a continuum field, not a cell-sheet census). Hejnowicz 1980 explains intrusion through tensional stress. A growth-tensor model for cambium specifically was not found (Hejnowicz's growth-tensor work concerns apices; not checked further).
- **Simulations**: Wilson and Howard 1968 (one file, day steps); Forest, L. and Demongeot, J. 2006. Cellular modelling of secondary radial growth in conifer trees: application to Pinus radiata. Bull. Math. ## (c) Verdict
1. Known counts: Bannan measured **pseudotransverse divisions per cm of xylem increment** (radial growth along a lineal series): 1.3 for Douglas-fir and 0.8 for lodgepole pine at normal ring widths, rising steeply in narrow rings. Bannan 1950, as quoted later, gives survival of new initials as **8 of 65, 2 of 26 and 6 of 36 divisions** (about 13%). Bailey 1923 gives **net whole-circumference counts** (Pinus strobus, 724 to 23,100 fusiform initials in 59 years). Hejnowicz 1971 gives **domain drift per mm of xylem** (0.7 mm per 1 mm). The only **per-initial-per-season** loss figure (about 1 in 4 lineages) is for poplar, from Bossinger and Spokevicius 2018.
2. Missing: no per-file-per-ring census of file starts, ends and sideways shifts, no study on yew, and no QWA tool that reports these events. RAPTOR and Brunel discard or repair broken files. The Hejnowicz 1961, 1966 (Thuja) and 1967 papers and Larson 1994 chapter 6 probably hold per-initial-per-year rates but could not be read today.
3. Topology: the cell events (intrusion between neighbours, file elimination, Z/S domains) are well described. **No source frames them as T1/T2 events, a topological census or scutoids;** the nearest are cross-section cellular automata (Forest and Demongeot 2006) and Hejnowicz's orientation-wave model.
