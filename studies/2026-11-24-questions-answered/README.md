# 1. The packets theorem: what it answers, and who to cite

**The theorem (Lean).** In any tiling of 3-space by convex polytopes (not necessarily face-to-face) using similar copies of a lead piece (lead 1: truncated icosahedron / dodecahedron centre pyramid; lead 2: truncated dodecahedron / icosahedron centre pyramid), plus helpers (dodecahedron, icosahedron, truncated octahedron, truncated cuboctahedron, truncated icosidodecahedron) plus any convex pieces with all dihedral angles rational multiples of π, no tile is a copy of a lead piece. Frame: *it can be proved by hand; we offer the Lean proof.*

## 1a. Published questions and results it touches

1. **Which Platonic solids tile together.** Gabbrielli, Jiao and Torquato (Phys. Rev. E 86, 041141, 2012) state: "From simple considerations based on their dihedral angles, no pair of regular polyhedra can completely fill R³ apart from tetrahedra and octahedra, either periodically or nonperiodically" (cubes of two sizes aside). Their argument is the edge-angle sum (π or 2π around an edge, non-face-to-face allowed). Source: <https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevE.86.041141> (read, p. 3). **Our theorem extends this hand argument** from *pairs of Platonic solids* to an open-ended mix: any number of helpers, any scales, plus every rational-angle convex piece, with an icosahedral Archimedean lead piece.
2. **Space-fillers have Dehn invariant zero.** Debrunner (1980), "Über Zerlegungsgleichheit von Pflasterpolyedern mit Würfeln", Arch. Math. 35, 583–587; generalised by Lagarias and Moews, "Polytopes that fill Rⁿ and scissors congruence", Discrete Comput. Geom. 13 (1995) 573–583 — tiles need not meet face-to-face, and aperiodic tiles are included. Sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehn_invariant>, <https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF02574064>, and arXiv 1602.04668 (which describes Debrunner's result as the codimension-2 Dehn invariant for every tiling polytope) <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04668> *(snippet)*. **Relation:** these results are about tilings by *congruent copies of one tile*; our theorem is about *one piece inside a mixed tiling* and gives the conclusion "no such tile occurs", which the single-tile Dehn results do not state.
3. **The 28 convex uniform honeycombs** (Andreini 1905, 25; Johnson 1991 and Grünbaum 1994, all 28) contain no icosahedral-symmetry cell. Source: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_uniform_honeycomb>. Our theorem gives a reason that covers far more than uniform, face-to-face tilings.
4. **Rational-angle tiles.** Chentouf and Sun, "Tetrahedra tiling problem", arXiv 2312.01654 (2023): of the tetrahedra with all dihedral angles rational multiples of π (Kedlaya–Kolpakov–Poonen–Rubinstein classification), all Hill-family members tile, exactly one member of the new family does, at most 40 of 59 sporadics do; so the converse of Debrunner's theorem fails. Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01654>. **Relation:** our "rational-angle convex pieces" class is the same class these authors study for tetrahedra; our theorem admits all of them as helpers.
5. **Grünbaum–Shephard, Conway–Burgiel–Goodman-Strauss, Senechal, Goldberg, Croft–Falconer–Guy.** No free full text was found stating the question "which Platonic/Archimedean mixtures tile space non-face-to-face" as an open problem. Found only bibliographic pointers: Danzer–Grünbaum–Shephard (1983) "Does every type of polyhedron tile three-space?" and Grünbaum–Shephard (1980) "Tilings with congruent tiles" (cited in arXiv 1005.3836, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3836>); Senechal, "Which tetrahedra fill space?", Math. Mag. 54 (1981) 227–243, and Goldberg, "Three infinite families of tetrahedral space-fillers", J. Combin. Theory A 16 (1974) 348–354 (<https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Space-FillingTetrahedron.html>). Croft–Falconer–Guy and *The Symmetries of Things*: not available free; not checked in text. **Honest outcome: no published open question is answered; known results are extended and formalised.**

## 1b. Prior art for the hand proof (who to cite)

- **Edge-angle sum argument for regular solids:** Gabbrielli–Jiao–Torquato 2012 (above) — cite at the point of use.
- **Rational independence of the dihedral angles involved** (dodecahedron arccos(−1/√5), icosahedron arccos(−√5/3), and the Archimedean angles built from them): Conway, Radin and Sadun, "On angles whose squared trigonometric functions are rational", Discrete Comput. Geom. 22 (1999) 321–332, arXiv math-ph/9812019 <https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/9812019>. Their Table 3 gives Dehn invariants of Platonic and non-snub Archimedean solids in a rational angle basis (per <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehn_invariant>). This is the natural citation for "these angles are not rational multiples of π and are rationally independent", the step our Niven/edge-ring lemmas formalise.
- **Dehn invariant and tiling:** Debrunner 1980; Lagarias–Moews 1995 (above).
- **Statement that only the cube (Platonic) and truncated octahedron (Archimedean) tile alone:** Torquato–Jiao, "Dense packings of polyhedra: Platonic and Archimedean solids", arXiv 0909.0940 <https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.0940> *(snippet)*.

## 1c. Existing formal proofs of tiling impossibility via angles

- **Niven's theorem is in Mathlib** (`Mathlib.NumberTheory.Niven`, theorem `niven`: rational cos at rational multiples of π lies in {−1, −1/2, 0, 1/2, 1}). Source: <https://nasqret.github.io/fineqs/docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Niven.html> (doc mirror). Mizar has a sine version (Naumowicz–Korniłowicz 2016, Formalized Mathematics) *(snippet)*.
- **Formal tiling impossibility proofs found:** mutilated chessboard (Isabelle, Paulson, <https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-394.pdf>); Keller's conjecture in dimension 8 (Lean, Clune, <https://joshclune.github.io/pdfs/Keller_Reduction.pdf>); 2D triangle tilings with Lean 4 formalisations (arXiv 1206.2229, 1812.07014). **None uses dihedral angles in 3D.** No formal proof that regular tetrahedra, dodecahedra or icosahedra fail to tile was found in Lean, Isabelle (AFP search page gave no hits text), Coq or HOL.
- **Conclusion:** to our search, the packets theorem would be the first machine-checked 3D tiling-impossibility result by dihedral angles.

1. Cite Gabbrielli–Jiao–Torquato 2012 and Conway–Radin–Sadun 1999 in the packets paper at the hand-proof step; cite Debrunner 1980 and Lagarias–Moews 1995 for context.
2. Smallest next Lean milestone: **Aristotle's error** (regular tetrahedra do not tile, any scale, non-face-to-face) from Mathlib's `niven` and our edge-ring lemma.
