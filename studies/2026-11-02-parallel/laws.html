  10. #49 The Polyhedral Stretching Theorem
    The Polyhedral Stretching Theorem. Let P be a convex polyhedron whose edges all have length 1, and slide it a distance 1 along a direction u. The swept shape (the convex hull of P and P moved by u) has every edge of length 1 unless u is parallel to an edge of P. Its faces are: each face of P not parallel to u, kept whole (one copy, at the end it faces); each n-sided face parallel to u, stretched into an (n+2)-gon, with one side fewer for each of its edges parallel to u; and one parallelogram for every edge of P's outline seen along u, a rhombus when the slide is 1.

  15. #54 One Turn Is a Gnomon: the Golden Cycle Step by Step, and the Polar of the Keyed Pair
    Theorem A (the classical gnomon of the golden cycle; exact, computer-verified in exact arithmetic). Let the twelve stations be as in Table 2, repeated in every turn at scale φ 3m .   22. #63 The White Queen 600-cell
    Lemma (one pattern per gap). Let h₁ < h₂ be two neighbouring vertex heights. At every height strictly between them the slice has the same pattern of faces. Proof. An edge of the 600-cell crosses the cut at height h exactly when its two ends lie on opposite sides of h. No vertex has a height strictly between h₁ and h₂, so as h moves through the gap no vertex changes side, and the same edges cross the cut. Inside the gap each face of the slice is the cross-section of one tetrahedral cell, with its corners at the crossing points on that cell's edges, and two faces share an edge of the slice exactly when their cells share a triangle that is cut. Which cells and triangles are cut, and through which edges, is therefore the same throughout the gap, and so is the pattern of faces. Hence the cuts at the vertex heights and one cut inside each gap give every slice pattern. The lemma is about the pattern only: edge lengths and angles change with the height.

    Theorem (Hexagonality Law). Let a role have, in the gap between layers of types x (below) and y (above), a piece of volume F(x, y), the same in every stacking.
