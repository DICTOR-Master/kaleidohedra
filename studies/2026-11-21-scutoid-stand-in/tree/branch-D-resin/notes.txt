Branch D, DICTO's idea, which he names "the resin solution" (tree search, 21 Nov 2026).
Build a mould round C1 that tiles space by itself, fill the gap between C1 and the mould (the resin), and map the resin by division.
Run: scripts/resin.py and scripts/overlap.py on the compute host; data/resin.json (every piece kind: vertices, faces, dihedrals, neighbours);
log.txt (ends FINAL ok).

Setting. C1 = truncated icosahedron (edge 1) with a dodecahedron centre pyramid on each pentagon; apices exactly at ((5+sqrt5)/4)(0, 1, phi)
and its images (lateral edges sqrt3 phi/2 checked exactly). vol C1 = 35 + 25 sqrt5/2 = 62.9508. C1 at every point of a cubic lattice with
its T_h axes on the cube axes (both T_h settings are equivalent under the lattice's O_h). Mould = Voronoi cell. All coordinates in Q(sqrt5).

1. Moulds (exact smallest scale with C1 inside its Voronoi cell; s = cube edge of the lattice; set by the spike apices)

2. Division rules (one convex flat-faced piece per C1 face, 80 per cell)
   R1 central: piece(F) = mould cap {beyond F's plane} cap the cone from C1's centre over F. Exact in Q(sqrt5). It is a partition for
      every mould and scale: C1 is star-shaped from its centre and all 80 face planes lie at positive offset (exact), so the cones
      tile space outside C1. Checked: sum of piece volumes = mould - C1 exactly (Q(sqrt5)); no overlaps (LP).
   R2 normal/mitre (DICTO's segments along face normals, with each edge's dihedral bisector plane as the wall): 50 digits (the
      hexagon-triangle bisectors carry sqrt3). It is a partition only if the walls reach the mould before they meet.

   Per kind (faces, vertices, neighbours on the C1 side and the mould side, Y vertices, every dihedral with its basis form): log.txt.
   - R1 never gives a scutoid, proved: each lateral face is a plane through the centre and one C1 edge, so the piece across it is the
     same all the way out; two lateral planes meet only along the ray through a C1 vertex, so no Y vertex. The pieces are frusta
     whose tops break over the mould's edges (1 to 3 top facets).
   - R2: the hexagon pieces' walls on the three hexagon-triangle (valley) edges lean in over the hexagon at 79.5474 deg = 5pi/8 - beta/2.
     Where the mould is far enough they close over the hexagon: the hexagon-hexagon walls end at Y vertices (Y = 3, shell-side
     neighbours = the three spike triangles only), a true scutoid shape. But exactly there the local mitre regions of neighbouring
     faces overlap, so these scutoid pieces are not a valid division; the valid one needs the full straight skeleton (new walls between
     faces that are not neighbours on C1). Not computed.

4. Rings. Every valid division is a tiling, so all edge rings close. Dihedrals on the C1 side are in the basis: R1 pi/4 + beta (hexagon),
   pi - 2beta and 2pi/3 (triangle); R2 the half angles 5pi/8 - beta/2, pi/4 + beta, 2pi/3. Lateral-lateral angles are outside
   {pi, alpha, beta, gamma} (R1 124.3094 / 55.6906 on the vertex rays; R2 113.2410 / 66.7590); several lateral-mould angles too, a few of
   them are lambda* shifted (R2 sc: 102.3087 = lambda* - pi/8 - beta/2). The 157.7612 barrier (lemma A1) never appears as a dihedral:
   each piece sits on one whole C1 face and nothing asks it to close a ring of C1 faces alone.

Score. Fewest kinds: 5 in every arrangement (the five T_h face orbits; T_h is the most of I_h a lattice keeps). Scutoids: none in any
valid division. Rings: close in all valid divisions, no 157.76 barrier. Best: bcc at s0 (truncated-octahedron mould, the tightest: the
resin is 62.95, just under C1's own 62.95), where R1 and R2 are both valid; R1 is exact in Q(sqrt5).
Verdict: the resin solution works as a construction (5 piece kinds, periodic, exact) but gives no scutoid pieces in its valid forms.
