Polyhedraverse: claims audit

Every claim of novelty, proof, verification or exactness in the public text of ~/polyhedraverse, checked against the code and tests

DICTO · audit of 31 October 2026 · for DICTO's decision · not for publication
Repository: github.com/DICTOR-Master/polyhedraverse (archived), local HEAD 567b37f. Most of the text was written in September 2026 by Claude Sonnet. Audit by Claude (Anthropic). Nothing in the repository was changed.
Verdict. 88 claims checked in 10 groups of files. 41 A (accurate and supported), 28 B (overclaim), 19 C (cannot be checked as written; most are numbers with no test, or stale text). The 4D write-up (docs/radial-cell-projection.md) was already corrected on 24 September (commit e212ac4). Its first version called RCP "a computational method conceived and developed by" DICTO, under his legal name. It now credits Coxeter and Schlegel and claims no new mathematics. What is left there is smaller: some tests check fewer cases than the text says. The tetrahedron's three closures are classical, but the text presents them as found. The provenance paragraph does not give DICTO's dated contributions. The serious overclaims are in other files. Classical stellations sit under the heading "DICTO's Zometool discoveries". The RVCMG spec separates itself from edge collapse without citing the classical vertex split. One report says "proved" for a check over finitely many cases. And "uniquely, in four" is in the README.

1. The most serious overclaims

  1. Classical stellations under "discoveries". README.md:201 lists "true stellations of the Catalan and Platonic solids" among DICTO's Zometool discoveries, and docs/dicto-zometool-discoveries.md:1 is titled that way. These stellations are classical (Kepler 1619; Coxeter, Du Val, Flather and Petrie, The Fifty-Nine Icosahedra, 1938; Wenninger, Dual Models, 1983; Escher's solid). Proposed: title "DICTO's Zometool finds"; README: "…and, from DICTO's idea of stellating rhombic and kite faces, stellation pieces for the classical stellations of the Catalan and Platonic solids."
  2. RVCMG is set apart from edge collapse, with no citation. docs/RVCMG.md:443–472 says RVCMG "should not be reduced to" polygon simplification and that edge collapse "is not the primitive". Moving two vertices together until they meet, and splitting them again, are Hoppe's edge collapse and vertex split (Progressive Meshes, 1996). They are also Garland and Heckbert's vertex-pair contraction (1997). L534 says the duality is "proven". It is shown on worked examples with tests. Proposed: add to §27 "edge collapse and vertex split (Hoppe 1996), vertex-pair contraction (Garland–Heckbert 1997)". Change L534 to "checked on the 3- to 8-vertex states by tests". Keep DICTO's contribution: the hemi-RD hex connector treated as one reversible state system.
  3. "Proved" for a finite check. docs/composite-seed-rcp/REPORT.md:58–64 and :127–129 say εpos(L) = 0 is "proved combinatorially". The code (krp-core/…/positionalResidual.js:57–97) computes each L separately, to a tolerance, for L = 1…12 (cube, octahedron) and 1…10 (tetrahedron). Proposed: "checked exactly for L = 1…12 (cube, octahedron) and L = 1…10 (tetrahedron); a proof for every L is open."
  4. "Every adjacent pair" checked on one pair. docs/radial-cell-projection.md:525, :533 and :660 say the dihedral angle and the shared-face coincidence are tested for adjacent cells, and for "every adjacent pair" of the 5-cell. scripts/verify-radial-projection.ts:96 and :131 test only complex.adjacency[0], the first pair of each closure. Proposed: "measured on one adjacent pair per closure" (or extend the test to every pair).
  5. Classical closures presented as found. docs/radial-cell-projection.md:637–658: the tetrahedron "has three real closures", "re-examining… found" them, and §21.2 is headed "a new θ". Three, four or five tetrahedra round an edge give the 5-cell, the 16-cell and the 600-cell. This is Schläfli's list ({3,3,3}, {3,3,4}, {3,3,5}; 1852). A positive angle defect is a necessary condition, not a proof of closure. Proposed: "The tetrahedron is the cell of three regular polytopes (Schläfli): the 5-cell, the 16-cell and the 600-cell. The app first supported only the 16-cell; on 15 September the other two θ values were added and checked."
  6. "Uniquely, in four". README.md:24. Other programs build or show regular 4-polytopes cell by cell (e.g. Stella4D by R. Webb, Jenn3d, 4D Toys). Proposed: "…in three dimensions, and in four."
  7. "Duoprism" for a polyhedral prism. README.md:162, changelog.ts:160, docs/guide.md:114, app/page.tsx:796 and the code itself. A duoprism is the product of two polygons. The construction here is P × interval, a polyhedral prism. The code comment (duoprism.js:6) also says it "embeds in ordinary 3D". A 4D prism is projected into 3D, not embedded. Proposed: "4D Prism (polyhedral prism, P × interval)".
  8. "Densest packing". changelog.ts:29: the Dogstar "is the hole regular dodecahedra leave in their densest packing". Hart's alternation gives dodecahedron density (5+√5)/8 ≈ 0.9045. This is the densest lattice packing (Betke and Henk, 2000). Densest among all packings is not established here. Proposed: "…in their densest lattice packing (Hart 1996)".
  9. "Four are new". changelog.ts:47, of the Regular 9. The 60° rhombohedron (octahedron plus two tetrahedra) and the leaning prisms are classical space-fillers. The i18n credit (i18n.ts:68) already says "new here". Proposed: "Four are new to the app."
  10. "Every claim above is tested … in CI". docs/dicto-zometool-discoveries.md:206. Line 213 of the same file says the sheared-FCC proof was a standalone calculation. The counts 15 and ~66 and the "closest to a true RD" claim have no script either. Proposed: "The claims in sections 1–3 and 5 are tested by …; the sheared-FCC map, the count of 15 and the ~66 census were standalone calculations and are not in the repository."
  11. The Kepler–Poinsot solids "among" the stellations. app/layout.tsx:18 (meta description and JSON-LD, read by search engines). The Stellations family reaches the third stellation. It builds three of the four; the great icosahedron is not there. Proposed: "(three of the Kepler–Poinsot solids among them)".
  12. "True place in the 4D structure". docs/guide.md:108, README.md:38–40. The Closed view shows each cell where the perspective (Schlegel) projection puts it, not its place in 4D. Proposed: "Closed shows each cell where it falls in the perspective projection of the closed 4D polytope."
