## 1. The statement

The Challenge (`package/Challenge/GrunbaumK10.lean`; 194 lines, 10 KB, under Palomar's preferred 300 lines / 32 KiB) is
the reviewed Challenge of studies/2026-11-17-get-palomar. Only the witness docstring changes, from GET A to GET B. No
definition or statement is changed. It has four theorems:
- `grunbaum_k10`: there exist P, F : Fin 30 → Parallelogram and a basis b such that P is of spherical type, F is rhombic
  monohedral with golden rhombi, every zone has 10 faces, b is linearly independent, and the lattice tiles by translates,
  face to face.
- `grunbaum_k10_parallelohedron`: the same result in Grünbaum's own terms.
- `cube_everyZoneHas` (4) and `rhombicDodecahedron_everyZoneHas` (6): the calibration theorems.

## 2. The proof with GET B

`package/scripts/make_witness_b.py` writes `package/lean-B/` from the GET A development of the GET study, which is left
unchanged. GET B has the same lattice basis and the same covolume 20 + 32φ as GET A, so the lattice part carries over
word for word. Only the witness data change:
- **Data and Checks:** the GET A data and pilot checks are removed, and the GET B ones are kept.
- **Certs.lean** (faces XB, zone certificate ZB, matching MB) is regenerated by the study's own `gen_certs.py`, run on
  the B data.
- **SphereData.lean** (435 cone-separation normals SPB) is regenerated by the study's own `gen_sphere.py`, run on the B
  data, from the same centre c₀ = (0, φ, 1 + φ).
- **The centre:** c₀ lies in cell 15 of GET B with parameters (2 − φ, φ − 1, 2 − φ), found exactly in ℚ(φ). It is the
  only cell that contains it.
- **Names:** every witness name is renamed from A to B by identifier segments. The module SphereA becomes SphereB.

The kernel re-decides every exact fact. The first build passed with no change to any proof.

The library is the 23 modules of `lean-B/GET`, 3,806 lines. All files together come to 4,152 Lean lines, of which 806
are generated (Data, Certs, SphereData, Statement).

**Tried and dropped:** splitting the six heaviest `decide +kernel` checks into chunks, to ease con-ron. The kernel checks
became slower: Lean's kernel no longer finished within the 280 s limit. The change was removed, and the entry uses the
unsplit checks.

## 3. Comparator, kernels, negative controls (logs in `package/logs/`)

Every command ran in the foreground under 280 s. The template `verify-comparator.sh` does not finish in one 280 s call
for this entry; an earlier run on the GET A version stopped during NanoDa. So the same judge was run in parts with
`package/scripts/run_comparator_split.sh`:
- **Export:** `leanexport` wrote the challenge and solution exports, with exactly the declaration list that
  `lake comparator` exports (sha256 in `logs/exports-sha256.txt`).
- **Comparator:** `lake comparator --challenge-from-export --solution-from-export` ran with the template's generated
  configuration and no external kernel. It did the statement comparison, the axiom check and Lean's kernel. Result:
  **"Your solution is okay!"**, 2 min 40 s, cgroup peak 6.19 GB.
- **NanoDa:** run directly on the solution export, with exactly the arguments and config file that `lake comparator`
  gives it (from the toolchain's Lake/CLI/Check.lean). Result: **accepts**, 1 min 13 s, peak 7.11 GB.
- **con-ron:** run directly with its default worker count, under a 40 GB cap. Result: **accepted 46,286 declarations
  (`--verified`)**, 2 min 07 s, peak 28.7 GB.

The axioms (`logs/axioms.log`) of all four theorems are `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. No `sorry` or
`native_decide` appears in the library or the Solution. `check-lean-sources.py` passes.

**Negative controls** (`scripts/run_negative_controls.sh`). Each ran through the unchanged template comparator, in a
scratch copy, and **all three were rejected**:
- (a) `EveryZoneHas F 10` → `12` in `grunbaum_k10`: "Challenge and solution theorem statement do not match:
  'GrunbaumK10.grunbaum_k10'" (35 s);
- (b) `IsRhombus` with ‖u‖ ≤ ‖v‖: "Const does not match between challenge and target
  'GrunbaumK10.Parallelogram.IsRhombus'" (33 s);
- (c) the proof of `grunbaum_k10` replaced by `sorry`: "Illegal axiom detected: 'sorryAx'" (35 s).

## 4. Fit check (4 cores, 14 GB, no swap; `logs/fit/`)

All runs used CPUs 0–3, `LEAN_NUM_THREADS=4`, and a cgroup capped at 14 GB with no swap.
- **Build** from an empty build folder, in three calls: 1:22, 0:55 and 0:27, about 2 min 44 s in all. The cgroup peak
  was 6.21 GB; the largest process reached 8.1 GB RSS (ContactData). At most 2 Lean processes ran at once. **Fits.**
- **Lean's kernel** (`leanchecker --from-export`): accepts, 2 min 29 s, peak 5.65 GB. **Fits.**
- **NanoDa:** accepts, 1 min 15 s, peak 7.14 GB. **Fits.**
- **con-ron with 2 workers (Palomar's count): does not fit.**
  - It checks 45,000 of 45,355 records in 47 s.
  - On the last heavy `decide +kernel` records its memory climbs to the 14 GB cap (sampler: 14.9 GB, with memory pressure
    rising). It is still running at 280 s.
  - At 16 workers it needs 28.7 GB. The GET A version behaved the same on 17 November.
  - The heaviest records are the contact checks, the zone certificate, the translate separation, the adjacent-span
    check and the sphere cover.
  - In Palomar's pipeline (PalomarSubmission 14aa8d6, `verify_submission.py`), con-ron runs after the mandatory Lean and
    NanoDa checks, and a capacity failure is recorded as "declined" with a warning ("mandatory Lean and NanoDa checks
    passed"), not as a rejection. On a 16 GB runner the likely outcome is that warning.
