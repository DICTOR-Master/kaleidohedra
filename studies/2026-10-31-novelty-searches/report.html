1. The leaning hexagonal prism and its two blocks

A prism on a regular hexagon, every edge one medium blue strut: two squares and four 72° rhombi round the sides. Its axis leans by θ with sin θ = (φ − 1)/√3, θ ≈ 20.905°; volume 3φ/2. It fills space by translation (Fedorov's hexagonal-prism type) and splits into three equal-edged parallelepipeds, two of one kind and one all-rhombus, each of volume φ/2. (The brief named blocks of volumes φ/2 and ½; in DICTO's record both prism blocks are φ/2 and the ½ block is the flattened rhombohedron of item 2.)

2. DICTO FCC and the flattened rhombohedron

A rhombic-dodecahedron-type cell of medium blue struts: six 60° and six 72° rhombi, no squares; volume φ²; built from two all-rhombus blocks (φ/2) and two flattened rhombohedra (two 60° and one 72° face pairs, volume ½). It tiles space on a sheared FCC lattice but is not an affine image of the rhombic dodecahedron.

The rhombic-dodecahedron form (4 squares, 8 rhombi of 60°) and the regular-hexagon elongated dodecahedron (4 regular hexagons, 4 squares, 4 rhombi of 60°) were already credited as known. 4. The Euclid–Kepler–Pacioli cell and network

One cubic unit (period φ²) holding, about one centre, Pacioli's three golden rectangles, the icosahedron, octahedron, Dogstar, stella octangula, cube, great stellated dodecahedron and dodecahedron, each inside the next; copies join across faces into a crystal of space group Pm3̄ with icosahedra at the 12j sites. The network: great stellated dodecahedra on the even (FCC) cells and icosahedra on the odd ones, sharing corners. Earlier searches (6 and 8 October) were web-level only.

5. RHOMBITURE's keyed dowels

Mark one corner of every face: on the prolate block the sharp corner at a tip, on the oblate block the blunt corner at a pole. In the true 3D Penrose (Ammann–Kramer–Neri) tiling, same-kind joins always meet marked corner to marked corner (1871/1871 prolate, 509/509 oblate). A two-hole pattern then takes 2 dowels on a true same-kind join, 1 on a prolate–oblate join and 0 on a same-kind join turned the wrong way. This is not a full matching rule.

6. RVCMG

DICTO's proposed framework: the hexagonal cut face of half a rhombic dodecahedron is a connector whose vertices can coalesce (and split again) into 5-, 4- and 3-vertex states, forming a reversible state graph; "vertices morph, faces connect". RVCMG §27 already disclaims edge collapse, vertex split (Hoppe 1996) and vertex-pair contraction (Garland–Heckbert 1997).

A. HexOct: two tiles for every stacking (#75)

Each close-packed layer with its share of the holes is one of only two tiles, c or h, placed by one motion; any tile sits on any tile, so every stacking is a string of two tiles.
