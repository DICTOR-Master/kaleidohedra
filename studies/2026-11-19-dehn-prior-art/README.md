# Prior art: formal proofs of Dehn's theorem and Dehn invariants of named solids (19 November 2026)

**Verdict.** Closest prior work: FormalBook chapter 10 (mo271/FormalBook PR #157, merged 6 October 2026), a complete Lean proof of Hilbert's third problem by Bricard's condition, stated for an abstract segment-and-angle decomposition model that is not linked to decompositions of sets in ℝ³; next, xiangyazi24/proof_in_the_book (Lean, decompositions of concrete solids, with the Pearl-Lemma count balance left as a hypothesis); and facebookresearch/atlas-lean (Lean, Dehn invariant of an edge list, cube zero, tetrahedron nonzero with the irrationality of arccos(1/3)/π assumed). On paper, Conway–Radin–Sadun (1999) give the Dehn invariants of the Platonic and Archimedean solids and the ℚ-independence modulo π of angles with rational squared cosine, including arccos(1/3), arccos(1/√5), arccos(√5/3). **What is not found:** a machine-checked proof of Dehn's theorem in any system with the invariant defined from the solid as a set in ℝ³ (no edge list as input) together with a proved bridge "scissors congruent ⇒ equal invariant" for convex polytopes and finite unions; machine-checked Dehn invariants of the RD, TO, golden rhombohedra, rhombic triacontahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron or octahedron; a machine-checked ℚ-independence of two angles modulo π; the independence modulo π of arccos(1/√5) and the angle β (cos² β = 1/3 − 2√5/15) in any source, formal or not (β is outside the Conway–Radin–Sadun class because cos² β is irrational); any result on CLEO, AXE, FUJI.

## Where ours stands against these

- The Platonic, RD, TO, golden-rhombohedron and triacontahedron values are classical on paper (CRS 1999, Debrunner, Calegari). Ours are the Lean versions, read from the solid as a set through study 5's edge reading.
- arccos(1/3) and arccos(1/√5) each irrational in π: also in FormalBook ch. 10 (Lean) and Mathlib's Niven file. Their joint independence with arccos(√5/3) is in CRS on paper. Joint independence of α = arccos(1/√5) and β, checked or on paper: not found.
- FormalBook ch. 10's Minkowski–Weyl theorem is used, and credited, in our `Dehn2Hull.lean` (optional file).
