# Swaps and fixed points: DICTO's body of work realigned round one move (13 Nov 2026)

**Conclusion (13 Nov 2026): the proposition "geometry gives laws at the fixed points of its swaps" is FALSE as a universal law. Of 45 proved laws in DICTO's work, 9 sit at the fixed point of a swap, 1 at the fixed axis of a rotation, 1 near a fixed point (proved off it), 14 are carried by a swap in another way (4 as invariance, 10 as a swapped pair), 3 are fixed points of hops, not swaps, and 17 involve no swap found. The lower true outcome: a swap plays some role in 24 of 45 laws (53 %; 28, 62 %, counting the rotation axis and the hops, fixed points of moves that are not swaps); a fixed point carries a law when the swap forces it (an odd quantity must vanish there, a symmetric concave one peaks there) or when the fixed object gains a symmetry its partners lack.**

DICTO, 13 November 2026 (spelling tidied, words his): "One of the biggest lids I have blown was with scutoid cells in 4D; it was unprecedented, but we haven't realigned the whole universe to fit in with it." And: "duals are swaps, so many swaps everywhere at every level: faces and vertices etc."

Working frame (agreed with DICTO): a **swap** is an involution, a move s with s(s(x)) = x. Its **fixed points** are the x with s(x) = x. The proposition to test: geometry gives laws at the fixed points of its swaps. DICTO's founding insight of his node work, that a DICTOsphere's **faces** (not its corners) direct the network, is itself a swap: duality, faces ↔ corners. It is the same move as the bridge of aim 2: the canonical form of a solid is the volume of its polar.

## 3. The test of the proposition

**(a) Does every proved DICTO law sit at the fixed point of some swap? No.** The counterexamples:
1. The **Hexagonality Law** (#66, #67), the **Window Law** (#84) and the **Laves locality** theorem: straight lines in the shares of layer words; the c ↔ h swap sends h to 1 − h, and nothing happens at its fixed point 1/2.
2. The **Silent Golden Station** (#94): Q₄ is silent at μ = φ on the cube–octahedron line, not at the turn's fixed point √3; φ is not the fixed point of any swap of that line found here.
3. **#43 Theorem 4** (octahedral constant 3): the bound is sharp at the swapped ends, cube and octahedron, while the fixed point √3 gives only 5 − 2√3. The same happens with Mahler's product: least at the swapped pair (cube, octahedron: Iriyeh–Shibata), largest off the fixed point (μ ≈ 1.6312).
4. **Star polytopes** (ledger [74]): the turn identity holds, but the peak at the fixed point fails for 12 of 14; for the small stellated and great dodecahedra the fixed point is the minimum.
5. The **canonical-form tones** on five non-self-dual lines (cube, icosahedral, B₅, B₆, 600-cell): proved off the fixed point.
6. The space-filling and Dehn laws (#44, #45, #47, #77, #85, #92, #93, #100, #101, #106) and the counting laws of #105: no swap found at all.
7. **Hops** (#69, #80, #99): golden fixed points of moves of infinite order. Every Möbius map is a product of two involutions (classical), so these are fixed points of a product of swaps, not of one swap; the proposition does not cover them as stated.

**What the swaps do instead.** Three roles, all common: (i) the law lives AT the fixed point (9); (ii) the law is an INVARIANCE: the quantity is the same on both partners (4); (iii) the law is about the PAIR or the orbit, often relating it to the fixed points, as the Swap Rule does (10).

**When a fixed point carries a law: two mechanisms, both known in general.**
- **Parity.** If a quantity f is odd under the swap (f ∘ s = −f), it must vanish at every fixed point. This is near-but-never-on Theorem A (eps = −1), and it is K4 here: the Dehn coefficient of the CsCl cell is 12√2(3 − 2μ), odd under μ ↔ 3 − μ, so Kelvin's cell is the only Dehn-zero member. A general version: when two cells of a lattice tiling are swapped, Dehn(P) + Dehn(Q) = 0 (a lattice fundamental domain is equidecomposable with a parallelepiped; Dehn 1901, Debrunner 1980), so at the fixed point 2·Dehn(P) = 0 and the cell tiles alone with Dehn invariant zero.
- **Symmetry plus concavity.** If f is even under the swap (f ∘ s = f), the fixed point is critical (the principle of symmetric criticality, Palais 1979; Michel 1971 for group orbits); it is the maximum when f is also concave. K3: the CsCl wall area W(μ) = W(3 − μ) with A″ < 0, so Kelvin's foam has the most wall per cell; Turn Theorem: log t_min is concave in log μ; K1, K2: the isoperimetric maximum is Lindelöf's theorem (1869: among polytopes with given face normals the one circumscribed about a sphere is the most isoperimetric).
- **Extra symmetry of the fixed object.** When the fixed object has more symmetry than its partners, everything that symmetry kills vanishes there (near-but-never-on Theorem B: B₄'s fixed solid is the 24-cell). Where it has no extra symmetry, the special points sit NEAR the fixed point, displaced by −f(0)/f′(0).

None of these three mechanisms is DICTO's discovery; they are general principles, credited above. What is DICTO's: the swaps themselves on his lines and structures (the Turn, the beat turn, the core turn, the yantra turn, the CsCl pair, the Swap Rule, the scutoid swap in 4D, the Mirror Law), and the insight that duality runs through all levels.

**The lower true outcome, as a candidate law (not proved):** "A swap's fixed point carries a law exactly when the swap forces one: an odd quantity vanishes there, an even concave one peaks there, and the fixed object's extra symmetry silences what it kills. Elsewhere the swap still organises the law, as an invariance or a pair." It is a reading of the 45, not a theorem.
