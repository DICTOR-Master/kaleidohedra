# RT centre pyramids and the Dehn test (gap 9), 4 Nov 2026
For a centre pyramid of a face-transitive (isohedral) convex solid, every lateral dihedral is 2π/k (k pyramids round a corner axis) and the base dihedral is half the solid's
dihedral angle θ. So the pyramid's Dehn invariant vanishes iff θ/π is rational (Dehn: Σ length ⊗ angle mod Qπ).
Among the 5 Platonic and 13 Catalan solids (standard dihedral tables), θ/π is rational only for the cube (1/2), the rhombic dodecahedron (2/3) and the rhombic triacontahedron (4/5).
=> exactly three isohedral solids have Dehn-zero centre pyramids: cube (pyramids tile: whole cubes), RD (tile freely, #93), RT (OPEN: edge-to-edge search alive at 31–60 pieces;
overnight queue run rt-pyramids-night). Dehn zero is necessary (Debrunner 1980) for tiling; by Sydler 1965 each of the three pyramids is scissors-congruent to a cube.

## Space-filling (Dehn) test on the sweeps' own solids, 4 Nov — fills the last column of Concordance 6.1
- Cube–octahedron sweep: for 1<μ<2 the two dihedrals satisfy θ_sh ≡ −θ_hh/2 (mod π), so Dehn = (L_hh − L_sh/2) ⊗ θ_hh: zero only at μ = 3/2 (Kelvin, which tiles); for 2<μ<3 the
  octagon–triangle angle is unbalanced: only the cube passes. The Dehn test picks out exactly the sweep's two lone tilers.
- CO–RD sweep: all irrational angles are multiples of β = arccos(1/√3): Dehn = (L(triangle–rhombus) − L(triangle–square)) ⊗ β. Zero exactly at μ = 5/3:
  the section is the B3 permutohedron, corners all permutations of (±1, ±2, ±3) (48 corners, 6 octagons, 8 hexagons, 12 rectangles; edges √2 and 2 in ratio √2 — NOT the
  Archimedean truncated cuboctahedron). It is the orbit of (3, 2, 1) = ρ of Sp(6) (C3) — a weight shape again (Golden Ladder ch. 2).
  Its hexagons are not centrally symmetric, so it is not a parallelohedron: no lattice tiling. Whether it tiles space alone in another way is OPEN — the sweeps' only Dehn-zero
  candidate besides the known tilers (and the RD at μ = 1).
