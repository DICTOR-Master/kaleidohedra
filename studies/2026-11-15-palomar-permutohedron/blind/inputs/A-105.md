# Candidate group A: paper #105, "DICTO 1:2 Truncated Octahedron: The 4D Vacuus of the Permutohedron" (Zenodo preprint, not peer reviewed, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24121177)

## Abstract (as published)
Write 1 to 5 in every order and link two orders that differ by exchanging two neighbouring values: the web of exchange, 120 knots and 240 exchanges, is the skeleton of the 4D permutohedron. Its 4D Vacuus is the set of central slices that carry its faces. Theorem 1: the 150 two-dimensional faces lie in exactly 65 central slices, in four congruence classes (30, 20, 10, 5). Theorem 2: the five slices that hold the middle value fixed are the permutohedron of 1, 2, 4, 5; any two cross in a regular hexagon through the centre. Theorem 3: the number of central hyperplanes spanned by the ridges of the permutohedron of 1..n, for every n. Theorem 4: the one-parameter family T(x) of truncated octahedra (hull of signed permutations of (1+x, x, 0)) is the family of permutohedra of four values symmetric about 0. Version 4 adds Lemma 2a and Theorem 2b (the heart in every odd dimension).

## Definitions
Pi_n = conv{ (s(1),...,s(n)) : s a permutation of 1..n }, a simple (n-1)-polytope in the hyperplane sum x_i = n(n+1)/2, centre c = ((n+1)/2,...,(n+1)/2). A ridge (face of dimension n-3) of Pi_n is an ordered set partition of [n] into three blocks. Each ridge F does not contain c, so aff(F ∪ {c}) is a hyperplane (within the ambient (n-1)-space) through c: the "central slice" of F.

## Statements to score (each separately)
A1 (Theorem 3, the count). The ridges of Pi_n span exactly N(n) distinct central hyperplanes, where
N(n) = sum over a,b,c >= 1, a+b+c = n, a < c, of n!/(a! b! c!)  +  T(n),  with T(n) = 2^(n-1) - 1 for n odd and 2^(n-2) - 1 for n even.
Values n = 3..9: 3, 15, 65, 225, 770, 2443, 7761. (Proof: the normal of the slice of the split (B1,B2,B3) with sizes (a,b,c) takes the values bc(b+c), -ac(a+2b+c), ab(a+b) on the blocks; distinct three-valued normals identify only a split and its reverse; two-valued ones (a = c) are the hyperplanes sum_{i in S} x_i = 0 (centred), with the extra coincidence for n even.) Sequence submitted to the OEIS.
A2 (Theorem 1, n = 5). The 150 2-faces of Pi_5 lie in exactly 65 central 3-dimensional slices, each face in exactly one; up to the symmetry group (S_5 x central inversion) four classes of slice, of sizes 30, 20, 10, 5, with explicitly computed shapes.
A3 (Lemma 2a + Theorem 2 + Theorem 2b). Lemma 2a: for the permutohedron of values v1 > ... > vn and v_p >= t >= v_{p+1}, the section x_i = t is the permutohedron of the n-1 values obtained by replacing v_p, v_{p+1} by v_p + v_{p+1} - t. Theorem 2b: for n = 2m+1 and c = m+1, the intersection of k of the central slices x_i = c is the permutohedron of n-k values: for k odd, 1..n with the middle k values removed; for k even, 1..n with the middle k+1 values removed and c put back. In particular n-3 of them meet in a regular hexagon (orders of 1, c, n). (Theorem 2 is the case n = 5: two slices x_i = 3, x_j = 3 meet in a regular hexagon.) Proof by majorization (Rado).
A4 (Theorem 4). For every x > 0, T(x) = conv of signed permutations of (1+x, x, 0) is, up to scale, the permutohedron of -q, -p, p, q with x = (q-p)/(2p).
