# Candidate group B: paper #104, "Scutoid Tissues in Four Dimensions" (Zenodo preprint, not peer reviewed, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24111864)

## Abstract (as published)
A scutoid is a cell between two surfaces whose outline changes on the way through: two cells that touched stop touching, and two others start. Slice a four-dimensional polytope with a moving hyperplane: its 3-dimensional cells cross the slice as polygons, and as the hyperplane moves the polygons trade neighbours. Each cell is then a flat-faced scutoid, and the boundary of the polytope a closed scutoid tissue. Theorem 1: in a generic sweep of a simple 4-polytope the tiling changes only by births, scutoid swaps (the classical 2-2 flip) and deaths, h1, h2 and h3 of them, in every direction. Theorem 1' gives every dimension. Theorem 2: the 120-cell, truncated 120-cell and bitruncated 600-cell sweep with (116, 366, 116), (716, 966, 716), (716, 2166, 716). Theorem 4 (balance law). Theorem 3: a computer-assisted exact proof of the Swap defect of close-packed stackings below s = 1/4 (power diagrams of the holes).

## Statements to score (each separately)
B1 (Theorem 1). In a generic linear sweep of a simple 4-polytope P, the cell-tiling of the slice boundary changes only at vertices; at a vertex with i downward edges: i = 1 a cell appears, i = 2 one pair of cells stops sharing a 2-face crossing the slice and one pair starts (a "scutoid swap"), i = 3 a cell disappears. Their numbers are h1, h2, h3 of P (independent of the direction).
B1' (Theorem 1'). For a simple d-polytope, d >= 4: passing a vertex of index i is the bistellar i -> (d-i) move on the dual triangulation of the slice boundary; facet contacts change only for i = 1 (birth), 2 (contact gained), d-2 (contact lost), d-1 (death); the number of index-i vertices is h_i.
B2 (Theorem 2). The 120-cell, truncated 120-cell and bitruncated 600-cell are simple, with (h1, h2, h3) = (116, 366, 116), (716, 966, 716), (716, 2166, 716).
B3 (Theorem 3, computer-assisted). For layered sphere packings with layer spacing d, d^2 = (2/3)s, s in [1/8, 1/4], with space divided among octahedral (weight 0) and tetrahedral (weight lambda) holes by power distance, the "Swap defect" D = V(c|h) + V(h|c) - V(c|c) - V(h|h) of hole-cell volumes keyed by the c/h types of four layers is independent of context and given by explicit piecewise closed forms in (s, lambda) (cubic and linear-shift regimes; D = 0 for -5s/8 <= lambda <= 3s/8), proved exactly for six stackings by 35,561,184 exact sign checks, and for all stackings under a "four-layer condition".
B4 (Theorem 4, balance law). Let a simple 4-polytope have cells of two kinds W, R with exactly 2 W and 2 R cells at every vertex. In every generic sweep, the swaps exchanging a W-W contact for an R-R contact equal those exchanging R-R for W-W, and both equal (#R-R 2-faces) - (#R cells) + 1; all other swaps exchange W-R for W-R. For the bitruncated 600-cell: 601 each way.
B5 (Lemma 5). At every vertex of the truncated 600-cell, in every generic direction, at least three facet contacts change at once (max cut of the wheel W5 has 7 of 10 edges).
