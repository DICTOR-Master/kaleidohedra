# Candidate group D: theorems in the book "The Permutohedron Paradigm: Every Possible Order" (DICTO, Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24239019; not peer reviewed) that are not in the separate papers

## Abstract (as published, excerpt)
The book follows one move, two neighbouring values changing places, from a hexagon through four-dimensional scutoid tissues to a family of solids and back to the bells: orderings of three and four values, the truncated octahedron family, sweeps of simple 4-polytopes as scutoid tissues counted by their h-vector, uniform polytopes as "clean" or "rosette" tissues read from Coxeter diagrams, the Vacuus count in every dimension, a golden quasiperiodic slice of the permutohedron honeycomb, the permutohedron's deformation cone and its equal-edged members, and change ringing (bells) as walks on permutohedra.

## Statements to score (each separately)
D1 (Ch. 10, Theorem 8.1 + Lemma 8.2 + Theorem 8.3, rosette balance law). (a) For the Wythoff polytope of a chain group W with ringed nodes S and unringed nodes U, every vertex has degree e = sum_{i in S} |W_U| / |W_{U \ N(i)}| (N(i) = neighbours of node i in the diagram); P is simple iff e = dimension. (b) Every edge of a Wythoff polytope is parallel to a root, so the multiset of vertex "events" (which faces are lost/made, which facets born/die) of a generic sweep depends only on the Weyl chamber of the functional, hence is the same for all generic directions; reversing the direction reverses every event, so every event class occurs exactly as often as its reverse (also for non-simple Wythoff polytopes). An explicit perturbed 16-cell (same face lattice) shows the balance fails without the symmetry.
D2 (Ch. 13, Theorems 12.1-12.2, golden slice). Cut the tiling of R^4 by translates of the permutohedron of -2..2 by the hyperplanes u.x = c, u = (1, phi, phi^2, phi^3, phi^4), generic c. The 3D slice tiling has cells in exactly 35 combinatorial types (69 bands of height), infinitely many shapes up to congruence; it and its "window" (dual Delaunay) version repeat along exactly a rank-2 lattice and in no third direction; the window version has 60 tile shapes up to translation and 69 vertex configurations.
D3 (Ch. 14, Theorem 14.3). In the deformation (type) cones of the Coxeter permutohedra of A3, B3, H3, the members with all edges of length 1 are exactly the 0/1 wall labellings closing round every face; up to symmetry there are 17 (A3), 101 (B3), 4427 (H3).
D4 (Ch. 14, Proposition 14.4). Every full-dimensional graphical zonotope tiles its hyperplane by translations for any edge lengths; for n = 4 the five connected graphs on 4 vertices give Fedorov's five parallelohedra.
D5 (Ch. 14, Theorem 14.1). On the Postnikov line P(t), 0 <= t <= 1, from the permutohedron to Loday's associahedron, P(t) keeps the permutohedron's labelled face lattice for t < 1, all non-interval facets vanish together at t = 1; P(t) tiles by translations only at t = 0; on the "equal-speed" line, facet S survives exactly for s < D_S = sum_{b<b'} k_b k_b' over its blocks.
D6 (Ch. 15, Theorems 13.3, 13.5-13.7). The truncated octahedron has exactly 44 Hamiltonian cycles (4 classes under its 48 symmetries); the change-ringing graph R4 on 4 bells has exactly 5396 Hamiltonian cycles in 162 classes, automorphism group of order 48; 15 classes have periodic change words; the most symmetric tours have symmetry D3d (order 12), exactly three classes (Double Bob, Double Court, Double Canterbury Minimus).
