## Theorems (all for Mathlib's `CoxeterMatrix.A 4`; Mathlib has no separate `A₄`)

Faces `Fin 120`; `A4.r i` = rolling across the ridge of type `i` (`i = 0..3`).

| statement | Lean name | kind |
|---|---|---|
| face `x` is dual to the corner `corner x` of the permutahedron; the 120 corners are distinct permutations of (5,4,3,2,1), face 0 ↔ (5,4,3,2,1); rolling across the ridge of type `i` swaps the entries `5−i`, `4−i` (one edge of the permutahedron) | `A4.corner_zero`, `A4.corner_perm`, `A4.corner_injective`, `A4.roll_corner` | kernel check |
| the rolls form a roll model (involutions, distance changes by 1, corner condition) and satisfy the Coxeter relations of `A 4` | `A4.model`, `A4.lifts` | kernel check |
| the Coxeter group has 120 elements; every element has length ≤ 10; exactly one has length 10 | `A4.card_group`, `A4.length_le`, `A4.existsUnique_length_eq` | general + kernel check |
| Matsumoto's theorem; word reduction | `A4.matsumoto`, `A4.reduce` | general |
| **rolling law** between any two faces: two shortest rolls are joined by corner moves; every roll → a shortest roll by corner moves and cancellations | `A4.rolling_law`, `A4.rolling_reduce` | general |
| **10 mirrors** (Mathlib's `IsReflection`) | `A4.card_mirrors` | general + kernel count |
| **every longest shortest roll (10 rolls), from any face, crosses every mirror exactly once** | `A4.longest_crosses_each_once` | general |
| **transitivity**: for any faces `x, y` there is a permutation `σ` of the 120 faces with `σ x = y` and `σ ∘ r i = r i ∘ σ` for every `i`; such a `σ` is determined by one value | `A4.transitive`, `A4.transitive_unique` | general (new: `RollModel.transitive`, `RollModel.transitive_unique`) |

Exact statements:
```lean
theorem A4.card_mirrors : Nat.card {t : M.Group // cs.IsReflection t} = 10
theorem A4.longest_crosses_each_once {ω : List (Fin 4)} (hω : cs.IsReduced ω) (hl : ω.length = 10)
    (w : M.Group) {t : M.Group} (ht : cs.IsReflection t) : (mirrorsCrossed cs w ω).count t = 1
theorem A4.transitive (x y : Fin 120) :
    ∃ σ : Fin 120 ≃ Fin 120, σ x = y ∧ ∀ i z, σ (r i z) = r i (σ z)
```
`transitive` is the combinatorial half of the 3D theorem `fair`. The other half (each `σ` is an isometry of ℝ⁴
mapping the die onto itself) needs the exact die and is part of the geometric step.

## Next milestone: the local-to-global lemma for M4 (stated, not proved)

Checking every face against every vertex of the die (`support` in 3D) does not scale to H4
(14 400 faces × 2 640 vertices). M4 for 4D needs this lemma, after which only face–neighbour checks remain.

**Setting.** A finite set `F` of faces; for each face `x` a vector `n_x ∈ ℝ⁴` and four vertices
`v_x,0, …, v_x,3 ∈ ℝ⁴` (`v_x,k` of type `k`), linearly independent, with `n_x · v_x,k = 1`. Let
`C_x = cone(v_x,0, …, v_x,3)` and `T_x = conv(v_x,0, …, v_x,3)`. Rolls `r_i : F → F` (involutions) with
`v_(r_i x),k = v_x,k` for every `k ≠ i`, and `v_(r_i x),i ≠ v_x,i`.

**Lemma (local-to-global).** Suppose
1. *(ridges are two-sided)* for every `x, i`, the vertices `v_x,i` and `v_(r_i x),i` lie strictly on opposite
   sides of the hyperplane spanned by the three shared vertices `{v_x,k : k ≠ i}`;
2. *(local convexity)* for every `x, i`: `n_x · v_(r_i x),i < 1`;
3. *(one point covered once)* some point `p ≠ 0`, not on the boundary of any `C_x`, lies in exactly one `C_x`;
4. the roll graph is connected.

Then (a) the cones `C_x` cover ℝ⁴ and have pairwise disjoint interiors (a complete simplicial fan), and
(b) for every face `x` and every vertex `v` of every face, `n_x · v ≤ 1`, with equality exactly when `v` is a
vertex of `x`. So `D = {y : n_x · y ≤ 1 for all x}` is a convex polytope whose facets are exactly the
tetrahedra `T_x`, adjacent across their ridges as the rolls say.

*Proof plan.* (a) is a degree argument: by 1 the cones form a closed pseudomanifold around 0 whose radial map
to the 3-sphere is a local homeomorphism off codimension-2 faces, hence a covering of constant degree; 3 makes
the degree 1. (b) is the wall criterion for piecewise-linear functions on a complete fan: the function
`φ(y) = n_x · y` on `C_x` is convex if and only if it is convex across every wall, and 2 is exactly convexity
across the wall between `C_x` and `C_(r_i x)`; then `φ = max_x n_x · y`, which gives (b). The wall criterion is
standard in toric geometry (D. Cox, J. Little, H. Schenck, *Toric Varieties*, AMS 2011, §6.1); the exact
statement used must be checked against that source before it is cited in a paper. For the reflection dice
the cones `C_x` are the Weyl chambers, so 3 holds at any interior point of the fundamental chamber.

Finite checks this leaves per die: 1 and 2 (120 × 4 checks for A4, 14 400 × 4 for H4), 3 at one point, 4 from
`down`. Everything else is proved once, for all dice.
