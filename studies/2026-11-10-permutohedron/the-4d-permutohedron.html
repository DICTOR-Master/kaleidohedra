The 4D Permutohedron

All 120 orderings of five numbers are the corners of a four-dimensional solid: the 4D permutohedron. Its 30 cells are 10 three-dimensional permutohedra (the orderings of four values; classically truncated octahedra) and 20 hexagonal prisms, four meeting at every corner. Like the truncated octahedron in 3D, it fills its space without gaps.

Corners 120, edges 240, cells 30. Every corner lies in four cells, so the solid is simple.
Build. Seen from just outside one 3D permutohedron: that cell becomes the outer shell and the other 29 sit inside it, every face still flat (a Schlegel view).
Sweep. A flat 3D cut moves through the 4D solid. Its surface is tiled by the cells' cross-sections, and they change only by 26 births, 26 deaths and 66 scutoid swaps, as the h-vector (1, 26, 66, 26, 1) predicts in every direction.
